Sentence correction gave you four versions and asked which was right. Fill in
the blanks gives you one sentence with a gap and asks what belongs in it, and
the first job is to work out what the gap is testing.

## Why it matters

Fill in the blanks is one of the most common verbal question types. TCS NQT,
Wipro, Accenture, Cognizant, Capgemini and AMCAT-style papers all use it,
usually three to six questions per section, and it appears again inside cloze
tests. Check the current pattern.

Every blank tests one of two things: **grammar** (the options are forms of the
same word, or small words like prepositions) or **meaning** (the options are
four different words that all fit grammatically). Recognising which in five
seconds tells you the method.

## What to study

| # | Blank type | How you recognise it | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Preposition or article | Options: in, on, at, for | High |
| 2 | Verb form or tense | Options: go, goes, went, has gone | High |
| 3 | Vocabulary, single blank | Four different words, same part of speech | High |
| 4 | Double blank | Pairs of words; both must fit | Medium |
| 5 | Connector | Options: although, because, however, therefore | Medium |
| 6 | Confused words | Options: affect, effect | Medium |

## The clue words

The sentence usually tells you the direction of the missing word.

```text
Contrast: the blank is the OPPOSITE of the other half
  although, though, but, however, despite, yet, while, whereas, instead

Continuation: the blank AGREES with the other half
  and, also, moreover, furthermore, similarly, because, since, as

Cause and effect
  therefore, so, hence, thus, as a result, consequently

Example / definition
  such as, for example, that is, a colon, a dash, "in other words"
```

## The method

::steps
### Read the sentence and say "blank"
Understand what the sentence is about before you look at the options.

### Decide: grammar or meaning?
Look at the options. Forms of one word, or small words: grammar. Four
different words: meaning.

### If grammar, apply the rule
Tense from the time words, agreement from the subject, preposition from the
fixed pair.

### If meaning, predict first
Circle the clue word. Put your own word in the blank, positive or negative,
before looking. Then pick the option closest to yours.

### Eliminate by part of speech
If the blank needs an adjective, every noun and verb in the options goes.

### Read the sentence with your answer
Out loud in your head, once. If it sounds wrong, check the second-best option.
::

::tip
For double blanks, test the easier blank first. It usually removes two
options, and you only have to think hard about the remaining two.
::

## A worked example

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="A grammar blank" icon="i-lucide-text-cursor-input"}
  *By the time the results were announced, most candidates ____ the venue.*

  (a) leave (b) left (c) had left (d) have left

  Options are forms of "leave": a grammar blank. Two past actions, and leaving
  came first: past perfect.

  **Answer: (c) had left.**
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="A meaning blank with a contrast" icon="i-lucide-arrow-left-right"}
  *Although the written round was ____, most of the candidates cleared it
  comfortably.*

  (a) easy (b) simple (c) demanding (d) short

  "Although" signals contrast with "cleared it comfortably". The blank must be
  something that makes clearing it surprising. Predict: "hard".

  **Answer: (c) demanding.**
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="A double blank" icon="i-lucide-columns-2"}
  *His answers were so ____ that the panel could not ____ what he meant.*

  (a) clear, understand (b) vague, understand (c) vague, doubt (d) precise,
  follow

  "So ... that the panel could not" means the first word is negative. (a) and
  (d) go. Between (b) and (c), "could not doubt what he meant" makes no sense.

  **Answer: (b) vague, understand.**
  :::
::

## Common mistakes

- **Looking at the options first** and picking the word that sounds most
  impressive.
- **Missing the contrast word.** "Although" and "despite" flip the meaning,
  and they are often at the start where the eye skips.
- **Choosing a correct word with the wrong preposition after it.** "He was
  accused ____ cheating": "of", not "for".
- **Testing only one blank** of a double blank.

## How to practise

- **Daily:** 10 questions from the fill-in-the-blank sets on IndiaBix and PrepInsta, and the vocabulary sets in R.S. Aggarwal's *Objective
  General English*.
- **Target:** 25 seconds for a grammar blank, 35 for a meaning blank.
- **The habit:** write your predicted word next to each question before
  looking at options, for the first week. It feels slow and it is the thing
  that makes you fast.

## Practice questions

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. 'She has been working here ____ 2019.' (for / since / from / by)"}
  **Since.** A starting point.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. 'Despite his ____ preparation, he could not clear the test.' (poor / thorough / little / no)"}
  **Thorough.** "Despite" means the preparation should have led to success.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. 'The new policy will ____ every fresher who joins after April.' (effect / affect)"}
  **Affect.** The verb.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. 'He was ____ of the change in venue, ____ he went to the old address.' (aware, so / unaware, so / aware, and / unaware, but)"}
  **Unaware, so.** Going to the old address is the result of not knowing. "But"
  would need a contrast, and "aware" makes the second half illogical.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="5. 'The candidate was ____ for being late; the panel did not mind.' (punished / excused / blamed / fined)"}
  **Excused.** "The panel did not mind" continues the idea.
  :::
::
