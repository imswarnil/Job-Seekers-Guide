Sentence completion gave you one sentence and its signal words. A **cloze
test** gives you a whole paragraph with several words taken out, and each blank
has to agree with the grammar of its own sentence and the meaning of the
passage around it.

## Why it matters

Cloze tests are a fixed format in several papers: TCS NQT and Capgemini have
used them regularly, and they turn up in Accenture, Cognizant and AMCAT-style
verbal sections too. One passage usually carries four to eight questions, so a
cloze passage can be a quarter of your verbal score. Check the current pattern.

It is also the question type that rewards grammar most directly. Every rule in
the grammar chapter (tenses, articles, prepositions, agreement) shows up here.

## What to study

| # | What each blank is testing | How to spot it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Grammar: tense, agreement, article, preposition | The options are forms of the same word |
| 2 | Vocabulary: the word with the right meaning | The options are different words of the same type |
| 3 | Connectors: however, therefore, although | The blank starts a clause or sentence |
| 4 | Collocation: words that go together | "Make a decision", not "do a decision" |

## The method

::steps
### Read the whole passage once, fast
Skip the blanks. Twenty seconds. You want the topic and the tone: positive,
negative, neutral, formal.

### Classify each blank
Look at its options. Same word in different forms means grammar. Different
words means meaning.

### Grammar blanks first
They have a right answer you can prove. Check tense against the other verbs,
singular or plural against the subject, the preposition the verb takes.

### Meaning blanks next
Predict a word, then match. Use the tone from step one to decide between two
close options.

### Reread the finished sentence
Read each completed sentence once. A wrong choice usually sounds wrong in
context even when it looked fine alone.
::

::tip
The passage is always consistent in tense. If the first sentence is in the past
tense, a blank verb in the middle is almost certainly past tense too.
::

## A worked example

> Every year thousands of graduates **(1)** ______ to Bangalore in search of
> their first job. Most of them **(2)** ______ little idea of what the written
> round involves. They spend months learning to code, **(3)** ______ they
> ignore the aptitude test that comes first. As a **(4)** ______, many are
> rejected before anyone looks at **(5)** ______ skills.

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="Blank 1: (a) moves (b) move (c) moving (d) moved"}
  **(b) move.** Subject is "thousands of graduates", plural, and "every year"
  signals the present simple for a habit. (a) is singular, (c) is not a finite
  verb, (d) is past where the passage is present.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Blank 2: (a) has (b) have (c) having (d) is having"}
  **(b) have.** "Most of them" is plural. "Have little idea" is the fixed
  phrase.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Blank 3: (a) because (b) so (c) but (d) therefore"}
  **(c) but.** Learning to code versus ignoring the test is a contrast.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Blank 4: (a) result (b) reason (c) cause (d) matter"}
  **(a) result.** "As a result" is the fixed phrase, and rejection is the
  result of ignoring the test.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Blank 5: (a) its (b) their (c) his (d) our"}
  **(b) their.** It refers back to "many", the graduates.
  :::
::

Two of those were grammar, two were connectors and fixed phrases, one was a
pronoun. That mix is typical.

## Common mistakes

- **Solving blank by blank without reading the passage.** The tone and tense of
  the whole passage decide half the blanks.
- **Choosing what sounds good alone.** Test the word in the full sentence.
- **Ignoring collocations.** "Take an exam", "make a mistake", "pay attention",
  "raise a question". These pairs are tested constantly.
- **Spending two minutes on one vocabulary blank.** Mark your best guess and
  move on; the grammar blanks are the sure marks.

## How to practise

- **Daily:** one cloze passage from IndiaBix "Closet Test" (their spelling) or
  PrepInsta's TCS and Capgemini verbal pages.
- **Target:** 30 seconds a blank, plus 20 seconds for the first read.
- **Keep a collocation list** in the back of your vocabulary notebook. Every
  fixed pair you get wrong goes in it.

## Practice questions

Passage: *The walk-in (1) ______ at nine, but the queue had formed (2) ______
seven. Candidates (3) ______ résumés in plastic folders and (4) ______ nervous
conversations about the test. By noon, only a (5) ______ of them were still
there.*

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. (a) start (b) started (c) starting (d) starts"}
  **(b) started.** The passage is in the past ("had formed").
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. (a) since (b) on (c) by (d) for"}
  **(c) by.** "Had formed by seven" means the forming was complete before that
  time.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. (a) carry (b) carried (c) carries (d) carrying"}
  **(b) carried.** Past tense, plural subject.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. (a) made (b) did (c) had (d) took"}
  **(c) had.** "Had a conversation" is the collocation.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="5. (a) many (b) handful (c) much (d) plenty"}
  **(b) handful.** "Only a handful of them." The article "a" rules out "many"
  and "much", and "a plenty of" is not English.
  :::
::
