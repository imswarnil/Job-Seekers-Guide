Voice and speech closed the grammar chapter. This chapter is about the question
types, and the first is the one that tests every grammar rule at once: a
sentence cut into parts, and you name the part with the mistake.

## Why it matters

Error spotting is the most common grammar question type in the written round.
TCS NQT, Wipro, Accenture, Cognizant, Capgemini and AMCAT-style papers all use
it, usually three to six questions per section. Check the current pattern.

The format is always the same:

```text
The number of candidates (A) / who cleared the test (B) / were higher (C) /
than last year. (D) / No error (E)
```

You pick the part containing the error, or "No error" if there is none.

The error is almost always one of these, roughly in this order of frequency:

## The rules not covered yet

```text
Pronoun case: after a preposition, the object form
  between you and me          NOT: between you and I
  He is taller than I (am).   (the verb is understood)

Pronoun owner: one possible noun only
  The dean told the registrar that his decision would be reviewed.  (whose?)

Parallelism: items in a list take the same shape
  responsible for shortlisting, collecting fees and publishing results
  NOT: shortlisting, fee collection and to publish results

Comparatives
  more better → better;  most cleverest → cleverest
  senior / junior / superior / prefer + TO, not than
  "the more you practise, the faster you get"

Redundancy (the extra word is the error)
  return back, repeat again, revert back, cousin brother,
  each and every one of them are, the reason is because

Confused pairs
  fewer (countable) / less (uncountable)
  between (two) / among (more than two)
  affect (verb) / effect (noun)
  its (belonging) / it's (it is)
  than (comparison) / then (time)
  lose / loose,  advice (noun) / advise (verb),  principal / principle
```

## The method

::steps
### Read the whole sentence once, for meaning
Do not hunt part by part on the first read. You need to know who the subject is
and when the action happens.

### Find the subject and the main verb
Cross out anything between them. Check they agree. This alone answers a large
share of questions.

### Check the verb's tense against the time words
Circle "since", "for", "yesterday", "in 2018", "if", "when". Each one fixes the
tense.

### Scan the small words
Every article, preposition and pronoun. Is one missing, wrong or extra?

### Only then choose "No error"
"No error" is a real answer, maybe one question in five. Choose it after the
checklist, never as a guess because you are tired.
::

::warning{icon="i-lucide-alert-triangle"}
Some papers apply negative marking to the verbal section too. If you cannot
find the error after the checklist and you have spent 45 seconds, mark it for
review and move on. Coming back with fresh eyes finds more errors than staring.
::

## A worked example

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="The number of" icon="i-lucide-search"}
  *The number of candidates (A) / who cleared the test (B) / were higher (C) /
  than last year. (D) / No error (E)*

  Subject: "the number". Verb: "were". "The number" is singular.

  **Answer: C.** "Was higher".
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="A redundancy" icon="i-lucide-copy"}
  *After the interview (A) / I returned back (B) / to my PG (C) / in BTM Layout.
  (D) / No error (E)*

  Subject and verb agree, the tense is right, the prepositions are right. The
  small-word scan finds "back": "return" already means go back.

  **Answer: B.** "I returned".
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="No error" icon="i-lucide-check"}
  *Neither the trainer nor the students (A) / were aware (B) / of the change
  (C) / in the schedule. (D) / No error (E)*

  "Neither ... nor": the nearer subject, "students", is plural, so "were" is
  right. Everything else checks.

  **Answer: E.**
  :::
::

## Common mistakes

- **Reading by ear only.** The sentence often "sounds fine" because the error
  is one you hear every day.
- **Hunting part by part** before understanding the sentence.
- **Never choosing "No error"**, or choosing it too often. It is one real
  answer among five.
- **Missing the error in part (A).** People start looking from the verb and
  forget the opening words.

## How to practise

- **Daily:** 15 questions from IndiaBix "Spotting Errors", running the
  checklist on paper for the first week.
- **Target:** 40 seconds a question by the end of week one, 30 by week three.
- **Your mistake page:** every wrong answer goes in the notebook with the rule
  it broke. After a week you will see that three or four rules cause most of
  your misses, and those are what you revise.
- **Books:** R.S. Aggarwal's *Objective General English* for volume; company
  previous papers on PrepInsta for style.

## Practice questions

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. Each of the candidates (A) / were asked (B) / to bring (C) / a pen. (D) / No error (E)"}
  **B.** "Each" is singular: "was asked".
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. I have been waiting (A) / for the results (B) / since two weeks (C) / now. (D) / No error (E)"}
  **C.** "For two weeks". Two weeks is a length of time.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. The offer letter was sent (A) / to him and I (B) / on the same day (C) / last month. (D) / No error (E)"}
  **B.** After "to", the object form: "him and me".
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. There are less vacancies (A) / this year (B) / than there were (C) / in 2018. (D) / No error (E)"}
  **A.** Vacancies are countable: "fewer vacancies".
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="5. The trainer asked us (A) / to revise the notes, (B) / practise daily (C) / and taking mocks. (D) / No error (E)"}
  **D.** Parallelism: "and take mocks".
  :::
::
