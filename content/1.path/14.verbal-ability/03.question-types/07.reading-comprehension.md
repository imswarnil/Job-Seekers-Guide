Para jumbles asked you to see how sentences connect. Reading comprehension (RC)
gives you the connected passage and asks what it says, and it is the question
type where the way you read matters more than how well you read.

## Why it matters

RC is in almost every verbal section: TCS NQT, Infosys, Wipro, Accenture,
Cognizant, Capgemini and AMCAT-style papers usually include one or two passages
with three to six questions each. Check the current pattern, but plan for RC to
be a large share of your verbal marks.

In college I read these passages the way school taught me: start at the top,
understand everything, then answer. That approach does not finish in time. The
round is not testing whether you understand the passage in general. It is
testing whether you can **find a specific fact in unfamiliar text under time
pressure**, and that is a trainable skill.

## What to study

The five question types, in the order you should answer them:

| # | Type | Sounds like | Where the answer is |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Fact (locate) | "According to the passage, what..." | One sentence. Find it |
| 2 | Vocabulary in context | "The word *X* in paragraph 2 means..." | The sentence around the word |
| 3 | Inference | "It can be inferred that..." | Two facts put together, nothing added |
| 4 | Main idea or title | "The best title for the passage is..." | The first and last paragraphs |
| 5 | Tone or attitude | "The author's tone is..." | The adjectives the author chooses |

## The method

::steps
### Read the questions first (30 seconds)
You are loading what to look for. Every sentence of the passage now gets
scanned against specific targets instead of being understood in general.

### Skim for structure (40 seconds)
First sentence of each paragraph. Build a map: "paragraph 2 is the reasons,
paragraph 3 is the objection, paragraph 4 is the numbers".

### Answer the fact and vocabulary questions
Go to the paragraph your map points at, read those few sentences properly,
answer, move on. These are the cheap marks.

### Then inference, main idea and tone
These need the whole shape, which you now have.
::

::tip
Timing: about **one minute per question** for the whole set, reading included.
A passage with five questions gets five to six minutes. If you are past that,
guess the tone question (see below) and move on.
::

## A worked example

> In 2019 the college replaced its admissions cut-off with a composite score.
> Previously an applicant was shortlisted on marks alone; a candidate scoring 78
> was admitted and one scoring 77 was not, regardless of anything else. The new
> formula weights the qualifying examination at 60 per cent, an aptitude test at
> 30 per cent and an interview at 10 per cent.
>
> The registrar's office defended the change on two grounds. First, that marks
> alone rewarded students from schools with generous internal assessment, which
> varied by as much as fourteen percentage points between boards. Second, that
> the aptitude component predicted first-year performance more reliably than
> the qualifying marks did, a finding drawn from four years of internal data.
>
> Critics within the faculty were unconvinced. One professor argued that the
> interview component, though small, introduced a subjective element that could
> not be audited, and that a 10 per cent weight was enough to move a borderline
> candidate across the line.
>
> The office has since agreed to publish interview rubrics, though not
> individual scores. In the first year under the new formula, applications from
> rural districts rose 4 per cent, and applications overall fell 2 per cent.

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. What weight does the aptitude test carry? (fact)"}
  **30 per cent.** Paragraph one, last sentence.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. What happened to overall applications in the first year? (fact, with a trap)"}
  **They fell 2 per cent.** The trap is the 4 per cent rise, which is true but is
  about rural districts only.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. Which is presented as a finding from data, not an argument? (inference)"}
  **That the aptitude component predicted first-year performance more
  reliably.** It is the only claim the passage attaches to "four years of
  internal data". Everything else is an argument or a policy.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. The word 'audited' in paragraph 3 most nearly means (vocabulary)"}
  **Checked by someone independent.** The sentence contrasts it with
  "subjective": the objection is that nobody can check the interview marks
  afterwards.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="5. The author's tone is best described as (tone)"}
  **Neutral, or balanced.** The passage gives the office's case and the
  critics' case and endorses neither.
  :::
::

## The trap answer

::compare
  :::compare-side{label="Reads for an impression" verdict="wrong"}
  Absorbs the whole passage, feels that the policy broadly worked, and picks
  "applications rose 4 per cent" for question 2.
  :::
  :::compare-side{label="Reads for the target" verdict="right"}
  Has the word "overall" loaded from the question, scans the last paragraph,
  finds "overall fell 2 per cent", answers. Ten seconds.
  :::
::

The wrong option in RC is almost always **a true sentence from the passage
that does not answer the question asked**. It feels right because you
recognise it.

## Common mistakes

- **Reading the passage first, slowly.** Questions first.
- **Answering from your own knowledge.** Only what the passage says counts,
  even if you know better.
- **Extreme options in inference questions.** "Always", "never", "completely"
  are usually wrong; passages are cautious.
- **Tone answers like "angry" or "sarcastic".** Exam passages are written to be
  unarguable. Neutral, analytical, objective or mildly critical are the usual
  answers.

## How to practise

::steps
### Every day
Two passages from IndiaBix "Comprehension" or PrepInsta's verbal pages, timed
at one minute per question.

### Every week
Read one long newspaper editorial (The Hindu or Indian Express opinion pages)
and write its main idea in one sentence. That is the main-idea question,
practised without options.

### Keep a log
For every wrong answer, write its type (fact, inference, tone...). After two
weeks the log shows which type is costing you, and that is where to spend
time.
::

## Practice questions

Using the same passage:

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. What weight does the interview carry?"}
  **10 per cent.**
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. What did the office agree to publish?"}
  **Interview rubrics**, not individual scores.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. A suitable title: (a) Why Marks Do Not Matter (b) A Change in Admissions and Its Critics (c) The End of Interviews (d) Rural Students Win"}
  **(b).** It covers the change, the reasons and the objections. (a) and (c)
  are extreme, (d) is one detail.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. Can it be inferred that the new formula increased total applications?"}
  **No.** The passage says overall applications fell 2 per cent.
  :::
