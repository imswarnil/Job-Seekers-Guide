Error spotting asked you to find the mistake. Sentence correction asks you to
choose the fix, which is harder in one way: two of the four options usually
look right, and only one is.

## Why it matters

Sentence correction (the whole sentence rewritten four ways) and sentence
improvement (one underlined part, with "no improvement" as an option) appear in
TCS NQT, Infosys, Accenture, Cognizant, Capgemini and AMCAT-style papers,
usually two to five questions per section. Infosys in particular likes long
correction questions where every option is fluent English. Check the current
pattern.

## What to study

| # | Format | What it looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Sentence improvement | A part is in bold or underlined. Four replacements, one is "No improvement" |
| 2 | Sentence correction | Four complete versions of the same sentence. Pick the correct one |
| 3 | Choose the correct sentence | Four different sentences, one correct |
| 4 | Choose the incorrect sentence | The reverse. Read the question twice |

The errors are the same ones as error spotting: agreement, tense, articles,
prepositions, modals, pronouns, parallelism, modifiers, redundancy. What
changes is the method.

## The method: eliminate, then compare

::steps
### Find the error before you read the options
Run the error-spotting checklist on the original. If you know what is wrong,
you know what the right option must fix.

### Remove every option that keeps the error
If the problem is "were" for "was", any option with "were" goes. Usually this
removes two options in ten seconds.

### Compare the survivors on three things
Did it fix the error without adding a new one? Did it keep the meaning? Is it
the shortest clear version?

### "No improvement" last
Choose it only if you found no error in step one and every option introduces
one.
::

## The modifier rule

This one is tested mostly in correction questions, and it is worth its own
section because it makes sentences say something absurd.

::compare
  :::compare-side{label="Wrong" verdict="wrong"}
  *Having failed the written round, the HR sent me home.*

  An opening phrase describes the noun that comes straight after it. As
  written, the HR failed the written round.
  :::
  :::compare-side{label="Right" verdict="right"}
  *Having failed the written round, I was sent home by the HR.*

  Or: *Because I had failed the written round, the HR sent me home.*
  :::
::

**The rule:** after an opening "-ing" or "-ed" phrase, the next noun must be
the one doing that action. This is called a **dangling modifier** when it is
wrong.

## What the wrong options do

Setters build the three wrong options from a small set of tricks. Knowing them
makes elimination faster.

```text
1. Fix the error and add a new one
   "The list were published" → "The lists was published"

2. Change the meaning
   "She nearly cleared all rounds" → "She cleared nearly all rounds"
   (different facts)

3. Wordy but grammatical
   "due to the fact that" for "because", "in spite of the fact that"
   for "although". Grammatical, but a shorter correct option beats it.

4. Correct but in the wrong register
   Slang or contractions ("gonna", "can't") in a formal sentence.
```

::tip
When two options both look correct, choose the shorter one that keeps the
meaning. Setters rarely make the wordier version the answer.
::

## A worked example

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="Sentence improvement" icon="i-lucide-pen-line"}
  *The manager, along with his team members, **are attending** the training
  today.*

  (a) is attending (b) were attending (c) have attended (d) No improvement

  The subject is "the manager"; "along with his team members" does not count.
  Singular verb. Today, still going on: present continuous.

  **Answer: (a) is attending.**
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Choose the correct sentence" icon="i-lucide-list-checks"}
  (a) Walking to the venue, the rain started.
  (b) Walking to the venue, I was caught in the rain.
  (c) While walking to the venue, the rain caught me.
  (d) Walking to the venue, it began to rain on me.

  The rain was not walking. Only (b) puts the walker straight after the
  phrase.

  **Answer: (b).**
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Two options survive" icon="i-lucide-scale"}
  *He could not attend the interview **due to the fact that he was unwell**.*

  (a) because of he was unwell (b) because he was unwell (c) due to he was
  unwell (d) No improvement

  (a) and (c) are ungrammatical: "because of" and "due to" take a noun, not a
  clause. (d) is grammatical but wordy. (b) is correct and shortest.

  **Answer: (b).**
  :::
::

## Common mistakes

- **Reading the options first.** Four fluent sentences confuse your ear.
  Decide what is wrong before you look.
- **Choosing an option that fixes one error and adds another.**
- **Missing a changed meaning** because the new sentence is grammatical.
- **Answering "choose the incorrect" as if it said "correct".** Underline the
  question word.

## How to practise

- **Daily:** 10 questions from IndiaBix "Sentence Correction" and "Sentence
  Improvement", writing the error you expect before looking at options.
- **Target:** 40 seconds each; Infosys-style long questions up to 60.
- **Weekly:** one set of Infosys previous-paper correction questions from
  PrepInsta. They are the hardest, and practising on them makes the others
  feel quick.

## Practice questions

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. Improve: 'Neither of the answers **are correct**.'"}
  **Is correct.** "Neither" is singular.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. Improve: 'I prefer coding **than** testing.'"}
  **To.** "Prefer" takes "to".
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. Improve: 'She is one of those candidates who **has** cleared every round.'"}
  **Have.** "Who" refers to "candidates", which is plural.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. Choose the correct one: (a) Being a holiday, the office was closed. (b) It being a holiday, the office was closed. (c) Being holiday, office was closed."}
  **(b).** In (a) the office is "being a holiday". (c) drops both articles.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="5. Improve: 'The reason for my delay **is because** the bus broke down.'"}
  **Is that.** "The reason is because" is redundant.
  :::
::
