One-word substitution packed a phrase into a word. Idioms go the other way: a
few ordinary words that together mean something none of them means alone.
"Burn the midnight oil" has nothing to do with oil.

## Why it matters

Idioms and phrases are tested in Wipro, Cognizant, Capgemini, Accenture and
AMCAT-style verbal sections, usually two to four questions when present, and
they appear inside cloze passages and sentence completion too. Check the
current pattern. They also matter after the test: interviewers and colleagues
use them, and "the ball is in your court" in an HR call is not about sport.

## Idioms that keep appearing

| Idiom | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| break the ice | start a conversation in an awkward situation |
| hit the nail on the head | describe something exactly right |
| a piece of cake | very easy |
| burn the midnight oil | work or study late into the night |
| once in a blue moon | very rarely |
| cost an arm and a leg | be very expensive |
| beat around the bush | avoid the main point |
| call it a day | stop working for now |
| bite off more than you can chew | take on more than you can manage |
| in hot water | in trouble |
| let the cat out of the bag / spill the beans | reveal a secret |
| cry over spilt milk | regret something that cannot be changed |
| get the ball rolling | start something |
| under the weather | slightly ill |
| a blessing in disguise | something good that first seemed bad |
| back to square one | back to the start after a failure |
| pull someone's leg | tease someone |
| the ball is in your court | it is your turn to act or decide |

## Phrasal verbs

A **phrasal verb** is a verb plus a small word (up, off, into, down) that
changes its meaning. These are tested in error spotting and fill in the blanks
as well.

| Phrasal verb | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| put off | postpone | The drive was put off until Monday. |
| call off | cancel | They called off the interview. |
| turn down | reject | She turned down the offer. |
| look into | investigate | HR will look into the complaint. |
| come across | find by chance | I came across the walk-in notice online. |
| give up | stop trying | Do not give up after one rejection. |
| carry on | continue | Carry on with the next section. |
| make up for | compensate | Extra practice made up for the lost week. |

::tip
Put off and call off are the pair papers love. *Put off* means later; *call
off* means never. One word decides whether the interview happens.
::

## The method, when you do not know it

::steps
### Picture it literally
"Bite off more than you can chew": a mouthful too big. Most idioms are a
picture of their meaning.

### Decide the charge
Is the picture good or bad? "In hot water" is uncomfortable, so the meaning is
negative.

### Eliminate the literal option
One option always takes the words literally ("to eat too quickly"). It is
almost never right.
::

## A worked example

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="'After the third rejection he was back to square one.' (a) happy (b) at the start again (c) in a new city (d) in the first round"}
  Picture: a board game, sent back to the first square. Negative charge. (d) is
  the literal trap ("one", "first").

  **Answer: (b) at the start again.**
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="'The HR manager said the ball was in my court.' (a) I had to play a game (b) I had lost (c) it was my turn to decide (d) the meeting was over"}
  Picture: in tennis, when the ball is on your side, you must play it. (a) is
  literal.

  **Answer: (c) it was my turn to decide.**
  :::
::

## Common mistakes

- **Choosing the literal meaning.**
- **Confusing similar phrasal verbs:** put off and put out, call off and call
  on, look into and look after.
- **Learning idioms without an example sentence.** You will recognise the words
  and not the meaning.
- **Using idioms in formal writing.** In the email and essay sections, plain
  words score better than "a piece of cake".

## How to practise

- **Daily for two weeks:** 10 questions from IndiaBix "Idioms and Phrases",
  **15 seconds** each.
- **Notebook:** one page for idioms, one for phrasal verbs, each with your own
  sentence.
- **Listening:** when you hear an idiom in a video or a conversation, write it
  down that day. Idioms are learned faster by hearing than by lists.
