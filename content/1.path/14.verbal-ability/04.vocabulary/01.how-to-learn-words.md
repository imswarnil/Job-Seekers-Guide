Reading comprehension, cloze and sentence completion all got harder the moment
a word was unfamiliar. This page is not a word list. It is a method for
learning words so that they are still there on the day of the test.

## Why it matters

Vocabulary is tested directly (synonyms, antonyms, one-word substitution,
idioms, the next three pages) and indirectly in every other verbal question.
Directly, expect roughly three to eight questions across Infosys, Wipro,
Accenture, Cognizant, Capgemini and AMCAT-style papers; indirectly, it decides
some of every RC and cloze set. Check the current pattern.

The standard advice is "memorise a 1,000-word list". It is a poor use of a
month: the papers do not draw from one list, and words memorised in a column
are gone in a week. What works is fewer words, met in context, reviewed on a
schedule.

::note
Vocabulary is exposure, not intelligence. It depends mostly on what you
happened to read growing up, and unlike intelligence it can be bought with a
month of daily practice. If this section has beaten you before, that tells you
about your reading so far, not about your ability.
::

## What to do

::steps
### Collect 10 words a day, from reading
Read one newspaper editorial a day (The Hindu or Indian Express opinion pages
are the usual choice). Underline words you cannot define. Take 10. Words from a
real sentence stick far better than words from a list, because you remember
where you met them.

### Write each one properly, once
In one notebook, used only for this.

### Review on a schedule
Not every day. On the days below.

### Use five a day
In a message, in your spoken practice, in a sentence you say to yourself on
the bus. A word you have used once is a word you own.
::

## The notebook entry

Every word gets the same five lines. It takes about a minute a word, ten
minutes a day.

```text
Word:        meticulous
Meaning:     very careful about small details
My sentence: She checked every answer on the OMR sheet; she is meticulous.
Similar:     careful, thorough, painstaking
Opposite:    careless, sloppy
```

::tip
The line that matters most is **your own sentence**, about your own life. A
dictionary example is someone else's memory. "My PG roommate is meticulous
about his Java notes" is yours.
::

## The review schedule

Forgetting is fast at first and slow later, so reviews start close together
and spread out. This is called **spaced repetition**.

| Review | When | What you do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Next day | Cover the meaning, say it from the word |
| 2 | Day 3 | Same, and say your sentence aloud |
| 3 | Day 7 | Cover the word, recall it from the meaning |
| 4 | Day 21 | Mixed with other weeks' words |
| 5 | Day 45 | Only the ones you missed on day 21 |

Mark a small tick beside a word each time you get it right. Three ticks in a
row and it moves to the "known" page at the back.

::note
If you prefer your phone, Anki is a free flashcard app that runs this schedule
for you. The method matters, not the tool. What does not work is rereading the
notebook front to back every night: it feels productive and it is not.
::

## Roots: words you have never seen

For words you meet for the first time in the test itself, break them into
parts. A small number of prefixes and roots build a large share of the words
in these papers.

| Part | Means | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `bene-` | good | benefit, benevolent |
| `mal-` | bad | malpractice, malnutrition |
| `circum-` | around | circumference, circumvent |
| `contra-`, `anti-` | against | contradict, antisocial |
| `in-`, `im-`, `un-` | not | invalid, impossible, unfair |
| `pre-` | before | predict, precaution |
| `retro-` | backwards | retrospect |
| `sub-` | under | subway, submerge |
| `trans-` | across | transport, transfer |
| `spect` | look | inspect, spectator |
| `dict` | say | predict, dictate |
| `vert` | turn | reverse, divert |
| `-phobia` | fear | claustrophobia |
| `-cide` | killing | pesticide, homicide |

## A worked example

**Choose the word closest in meaning to *circumvent*: (a) surround (b) avoid
(c) complete (d) invent.**

`circum-` is "around", `vent` is "come". To come around something: to find a
way round it. **(b) avoid.** (a) is the trap: it matches "around" but not
"come around an obstacle". The root narrows it to two, and meaning in a
sentence decides ("he circumvented the rule").

## Common mistakes

- **Learning from lists with no sentence.** No context, no memory.
- **Too many words a day.** Thirty a day for a week, then nothing, loses to ten
  a day for a month.
- **Skipping reviews.** The review days do the remembering; the first day only
  introduces the word.
- **Never using the words.** Passive recognition fades; one use fixes it.

## How to practise

- **Daily:** 10 new words (10 minutes), today's reviews (10 minutes), and 10
  synonym or antonym questions from IndiaBix.
- **Weekly:** a 20-word self-test from the previous week, meanings covered.
- **Book:** Norman Lewis's *Word Power Made Easy* is built on roots and is the
  usual recommendation for good reason. One session a day.
- **Over 30 days:** about 300 words, most of which will still be there on the
  day of the test.

## Practice questions

Work each one out from its parts before you open it.

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. retrospective"}
  **Looking back at the past.** `retro-` backwards, `spect` look.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. benevolent"}
  **Kind, wishing good to others.** `bene-` good, `vol` wish.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. incontrovertible"}
  **Impossible to deny.** `in-` not, `contro-` against, `vert` turn: cannot be
  turned against.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. malleable"}
  **Easily shaped; also, easily influenced.** From a root meaning "hammer",
  plus `-able`: able to be hammered into shape.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="5. precaution"}
  **A step taken beforehand to prevent harm.** `pre-` before, `caution` care.
  :::
