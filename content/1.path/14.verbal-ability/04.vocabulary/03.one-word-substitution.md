Synonyms swapped one word for another. One-word substitution swaps a whole
phrase for one word: "a speech made without preparation" becomes
*extempore*. It is vocabulary again, but the words come in families, and
learning the family is faster than learning the words.

## Why it matters

One-word substitution appears in Wipro, Cognizant, Capgemini and AMCAT-style
verbal sections, and in government-style tests, usually two to four questions
when present. Check the current pattern. The same small pool of words repeats
across papers more than any other vocabulary type, which makes a starter list
genuinely useful here.

## What to study

Learn them by group, not alphabetically. Each group shares a root or an idea.

| # | Group | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | People by belief or attitude | atheist, optimist, misanthrope |
| 2 | Fears (`-phobia`) | acrophobia, claustrophobia |
| 3 | Rule and government (`-cracy`, `-archy`) | democracy, oligarchy |
| 4 | Killing (`-cide`) | regicide, pesticide |
| 5 | Study of (`-logy`) | ornithology, psychology |
| 6 | "All" words (`omni-`) | omniscient, omnipresent |
| 7 | Cannot be ... (`in-`, `il-`, `-ible`) | illegible, inevitable, insolvent |
| 8 | Places and groups | apiary, fleet |

::tip
Put these in your vocabulary notebook as groups: one page for `-phobia`, one
for `omni-`, one for people. When a new one appears in practice, it goes onto
its group's page, and the group makes it easier to recall.
::

## The method

::steps
### Find the key idea in the phrase
"Cannot be read" is about reading. "Knows everything" is about "all".

### Match it to a root
`omni-` all, `-logy` study of, `-cide` killing, `poly-` many, `il-` / `in-` not.

### Eliminate options with the wrong root
If the phrase is about fear, only a `-phobia` word survives.
::

## A worked example

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="A person who knows many languages: (a) linguist (b) polyglot (c) translator (d) bilingual"}
  `poly-` is "many", `glot` is "tongue". **(b) polyglot.**

  (a) linguist is the trap: a linguist studies language, and may speak only one.
  (d) bilingual is exactly two. (c) is a job, not the ability.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Handwriting that cannot be read: (a) illegal (b) illegible (c) illiterate (d) invisible"}
  `il-` "not", `leg` "read", `-ible` "able to be". **(b) illegible.**

  (a) is the look-alike (not lawful). (c) describes a person who cannot read,
  not the handwriting.
  :::
::

## Common mistakes

- **Describing the person when the phrase describes the thing**, or the other
  way round (illiterate versus illegible).
- **Choosing a nearby profession** (linguist for polyglot).
- **Look-alikes:** illegible and illegal, eminent and imminent, epitaph and
  epithet.
- **Learning alphabetically.** Groups stick; alphabetical lists do not.

## How to practise

- **Daily for two weeks:** 10 questions from IndiaBix "One Word Substitutes".
  Target **15 seconds** each.
- **Add** every new word to its group page in your notebook, with your own
  sentence.
- **Book:** R.S. Aggarwal's *Objective General English* has a long list;
  work through it one group at a time.

## Practice questions

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. Fear of closed spaces?"}
  **Claustrophobia.**
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. One who is present everywhere?"}
  **Omnipresent.**
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. That which cannot be avoided?"}
  **Inevitable.**
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. Study of birds?"}
  **Ornithology.**
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="5. A person who is new to a job: (a) veteran (b) novice (c) expert (d) amateur"}
  **(b) novice.** (d) amateur means someone who does something for pleasure, not
  pay, which is a different idea.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="6. Unable to pay one's debts?"}
  **Insolvent** (bankrupt is also accepted in most papers).
  :::
