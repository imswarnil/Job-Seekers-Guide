The last page gave you a way to learn words so they stay. This page is where
most of them get tested: a word in capitals, four options, find the one that
means the same (**synonym**) or the opposite (**antonym**).

## Why it matters

Synonyms and antonyms appear in Wipro, Accenture, Cognizant, Capgemini, Infosys
and AMCAT-style verbal sections, sometimes as standalone questions (two to five)
and sometimes inside a reading passage ("the word X in paragraph 2 means").
Check the current pattern. Each takes 15–25 seconds, or none at all if you do
not know the word and have no method, so the method below is what turns them
into marks.

## What to study

| # | Type | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Synonym of a word in isolation | High |
| 2 | Antonym of a word in isolation | High |
| 3 | Synonym or antonym of a word in a sentence | High |
| 4 | Word in a passage, meaning in context | Medium |

## The elimination method

You rarely need to know the answer. You need to remove the wrong ones, and
wrong options are wrong in predictable ways.

::steps
### Decide the charge
Is the word positive, negative or neutral? Even a word you half know usually
has a feel: "lethargic" feels negative, "diligent" positive. Remove options
with the wrong charge.

### Remove the opposite
In a synonym question, one option is usually the antonym, put there for people
who read too fast. In an antonym question, one option is usually the synonym.

### Remove the look-alike
An option that looks or sounds like the question word ("eloquent" and
"elegant") is often a trap.

### Use roots on what is left
The prefixes and roots from the last page decide most of the remaining pairs.
::

::warning{icon="i-lucide-alert-triangle"}
Read the instruction twice. "Choose the word **opposite** in meaning" and
"choose the word **similar** in meaning" sit in the same section, and switching
between them mid-section is the most common way to lose a mark you knew.
::

## Pairs worth knowing

These come up again and again. Learn them as pairs, with the notebook method.

| Word | Synonym | Antonym |
| --- | --- | --- |
| abundant | plentiful | scarce |
| candid | frank | evasive |
| diligent | hard-working | lazy |
| obsolete | outdated | modern |
| lethargic | sluggish | energetic |
| transparent | clear | opaque |
| humble | modest | arrogant |
| hostile | unfriendly | friendly, amicable |
| ambiguous | unclear | clear, explicit |
| frugal | thrifty | extravagant |
| verbose | wordy | concise |
| reluctant | unwilling | eager |
| benevolent | kind | cruel, malevolent |
| temporary | short-lived | permanent |
| augment | increase | reduce, diminish |

## A worked example

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="Synonym of ELOQUENT: (a) silent (b) articulate (c) elegant (d) loud"}
  - Charge: positive, about speaking.
  - (a) silent is the opposite. Out.
  - (c) elegant is the look-alike. Out.
  - (d) loud is about volume, not skill. Out.

  **Answer: (b) articulate**, able to express ideas clearly. You could reach it
  by elimination alone.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Antonym of FRUGAL: (a) thrifty (b) careful (c) extravagant (d) poor"}
  Frugal means careful with money. (a) and (b) are synonyms, the trap in an
  antonym question. (d) is related but not opposite: a poor person can be
  frugal or not.

  **Answer: (c) extravagant.**
  :::
::

## Common mistakes

- **Answering a synonym question with the antonym** because the instruction
  changed.
- **Choosing a related word, not an equal one.** "Poor" relates to "frugal"
  but does not mean it or its opposite.
- **Picking the look-alike.**
- **Ignoring the sentence** when a word is given in context. Many words have
  two meanings; the sentence picks one.

## How to practise

- **Daily:** 10 synonym and 10 antonym questions from IndiaBix, 20 seconds
  each.
- **Every wrong answer** goes into your vocabulary notebook as a full entry,
  with both its synonym and its antonym.
- **Book:** R.S. Aggarwal's *Objective General English* has long synonym and
  antonym exercises for volume.

## Practice questions

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. Synonym of CANDID: (a) sweet (b) frank (c) secret (d) shy"}
  **(b) frank.**
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. Antonym of SCARCE: (a) rare (b) abundant (c) small (d) hidden"}
  **(b) abundant.** (a) rare is the synonym trap.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. Synonym of OBSOLETE: (a) outdated (b) obvious (c) broken (d) expensive"}
  **(a) outdated.** (b) is the look-alike.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. Antonym of VERBOSE: (a) wordy (b) loud (c) concise (d) verbal"}
  **(c) concise.** (a) is the synonym, (d) the look-alike.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="5. In 'The manager was reluctant to approve the leave', reluctant means: (a) eager (b) unwilling (c) quick (d) angry"}
  **(b) unwilling.**
  :::
::
