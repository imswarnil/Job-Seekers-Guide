The reasoning plan ended with a timed full section. The last section of the
same paper is English, and this page answers what it contains, how many
questions to expect, and where the marks come from.

## Why it matters

Almost every written round I sat in 2018 had an English section, and so does
almost every one I have seen since. It is usually the section freshers prepare
for least, because it does not feel like something you can study. You either
"know English" or you do not.

That belief cost me. In college I answered grammar questions by what sounded
right, and what sounded right to me was often wrong. I did not know the rules
the questions were built on, so I had no way of checking my ear. Once I learned
the rules in Bangalore, the section stopped being a lottery.

::note
Verbal ability is also the section where the cut-off is often a separate
number. Some companies set a minimum per section, so a strong quant score does
not rescue a weak English one. Check how your company scores it.
::

## The section, company by company

These are rough figures from the papers I sat and the ones I have seen since.
Patterns change every year, so check the current one on the company's own
page or PrepInsta before you sit it.

| Company or test | Roughly | What it leans on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TCS NQT | 20–25 questions, about 25–30 minutes | Error spotting, fill in the blanks, sentence completion, reading comprehension |
| Infosys | 20–40 questions, about 20–35 minutes | Reading comprehension, sentence correction, para jumbles. The hardest verbal section of the service companies |
| Wipro | 20–25 questions, plus a short essay in some years | Grammar, synonyms and antonyms, comprehension, then a written essay |
| Accenture | 20–25 questions in the English ability part | Grammar, vocabulary, comprehension. A separate spoken communication test in some years |
| Cognizant | 20–25 questions | Grammar, comprehension, sentence ordering. Email writing in some years |
| Capgemini | 25–30 questions | Grammar, vocabulary, comprehension, para jumbles |
| AMCAT-style | 18–25 questions, about 25 minutes | Comprehension, grammar, vocabulary. An optional written English (essay) module |

::warning{icon="i-lucide-alert-triangle"}
Some papers add a writing task: an email or a short essay, marked on format,
grammar and whether you answered the prompt. It is worth practising even if
your target company did not include one last year, because it takes an evening
to learn and costs you the whole task if you meet it cold. The writing chapter
covers both.
::

## What to study

In priority order, by how many marks each type carries across these papers:

::steps
### Grammar rules
Tenses, articles, prepositions, subject-verb agreement, modals, voice and
speech. Every error-spotting and sentence-correction question is built on one
of these.

### Error spotting and sentence correction
The biggest single block of grammar marks. Usually 5–8 questions together.

### Fill in the blanks and sentence completion
Grammar plus vocabulary. Usually 3–6 questions.

### Reading comprehension
One or two passages with 3–5 questions each. Often the largest block in one
place.

### Vocabulary
Synonyms, antonyms, one-word substitution, idioms. 2–5 questions, sometimes
hidden inside other types.

### Para jumbles and cloze tests
2–5 questions. Slower than the others, so learn the method.

### Email and essay
Only some papers, but a whole task when they appear.
::

## How to spend the time

Most verbal sections give you roughly a minute per question, and the questions
are not equal.

| Question type | Target time |
| --- | --- |
| Synonym, antonym, one-word substitution | 10–20 seconds. You know it or you do not |
| Fill in the blank | 20–30 seconds |
| Error spotting, sentence correction | 30–45 seconds |
| Para jumble | 60–90 seconds |
| Reading comprehension | 3–4 minutes to read, then 30 seconds a question |

::tip
Do the vocabulary and grammar questions first, fast, and bank the time for the
passage. The mistake I watched people make at every drive was starting with
the passage because it came first on the screen, then running out of time on
five grammar questions they could have answered in three minutes.
::

## A worked example

Here is a mini-section of four questions, the way a paper mixes them.

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. Error spotting" icon="i-lucide-search"}
  *The list of selected candidates (A) / have been displayed (B) / on the notice
  board (C) / since Monday. (D)*

  **Error in B.** The subject is "list", which is singular, so "has been
  displayed". Rule: subject-verb agreement.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. Fill in the blank" icon="i-lucide-text-cursor-input"}
  *She has been preparing for the test ____ three months.*

  (a) since (b) for (c) from (d) by

  **(b) for.** "For" takes a length of time, "since" takes a starting point.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. Synonym" icon="i-lucide-book-a"}
  *Choose the word closest in meaning to DILIGENT.*

  (a) lazy (b) hard-working (c) clever (d) careless

  **(b) hard-working.**
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. Sentence improvement" icon="i-lucide-pen-line"}
  *If I would have known about the walk-in, I would have gone.*

  **"If I had known".** The "if" half of a past condition takes the past
  perfect, never "would have".
  :::
::

Four questions, four different rules, about two minutes if you know them.
That is what this track teaches.

## Common mistakes

- **Answering by ear.** Your ear is trained on the English around you, which
  may repeat the exact errors the paper tests. Check against a rule.
- **Starting with the passage** and running out of time.
- **Leaving the writing task to the end** with five minutes left. If there is
  an email or essay, give it its full time.
- **Not reading all four options** in error spotting. "No error" is a real
  answer, and so is the part you skimmed.

## How to practise

- **Daily:** 20 verbal questions, mixed types, after your reasoning set.
- **Weekly:** one timed full verbal section from a company's previous paper
  (PrepInsta and IndiaBix both have them).
- **Books:** a grammar book you can finish, such as Wren and Martin's *High
  School English Grammar* for the rules, and R.S. Aggarwal's *Objective General
  English* for question practice.
- **Every day:** read one article of English news out loud. It feeds the
  vocabulary, the comprehension and the speaking at once.

## Practice questions

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. Which is correct: 'Each of the students have a hall ticket' or 'has'?"}
  **Has.** "Each" is singular.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. Fill in: 'He is ____ honest man.'"}
  **An.** "Honest" begins with a vowel sound; the h is silent.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. Antonym of SCARCE?"}
  **Plentiful** (or abundant).
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. Find the error: 'I am working here since 2019.'"}
  **"Am working" should be "have been working".** An action that started in the
  past and continues now takes the present perfect continuous.
  :::
::
