Subject-verb agreement made sure the verb matched its subject. Modals are the
helper verbs that go in front of a main verb to add ability, permission,
possibility or duty, and they come with one rule that the papers test again
and again.

## Why it matters

Modals turn up in fill in the blanks, error spotting and sentence improvement
in TCS NQT, Accenture, Cognizant, Capgemini and AMCAT-style papers, roughly one
or two questions per section. Check the current pattern.

They matter more than their question count in the writing task and the
interview, because they are how English is polite. "I want to reschedule" and
"Could I reschedule?" ask for the same thing, and only one of them sounds right
in an email to HR.

## The rules

```text
1. Modal + base verb. No "to", no -s, no -ed, no -ing.
   She can code.           NOT: She can codes / can to code / can coding
   He must submit it.      NOT: He must submits / must to submit

2. A modal never changes for the subject.
   He can, she can, they can.   NOT: he cans

3. Two modals never sit together.
   You will be able to join.    NOT: You will can join

4. Past possibility / regret: modal + have + past participle
   I should have practised.     (I did not, and I regret it)
   He must have missed the bus. (I am almost sure)
   She might have left already. (possibly)

5. Ought takes "to":  You ought to prepare.
   Used to (past habit): I used to study at night.
```

| Modal | Main meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| can | ability, informal permission | I can solve this in a minute. |
| could | past ability, polite request | Could you share the venue address? |
| may | formal permission, possibility | May I come in? It may rain. |
| might | weaker possibility | The results might come out today. |
| must | strong obligation, near certainty | You must carry ID. He must be tired. |
| should | advice, expectation | You should revise the formulas. |
| would | polite request, conditional | I would appreciate a reply. |
| will | future, promise | I will send the documents. |
| shall | suggestion (I/we), formal rules | Shall we start? |
| ought to | moral duty, advice | You ought to inform HR. |

::tip
"Must not" and "need not" are different. "You must not use a phone" means it
is forbidden. "You need not bring a printout" means it is not necessary. The
papers put these two side by side as options.
::

## A worked example

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="Modal + base verb" icon="i-lucide-search"}
  *Every candidate (A) / must brings (B) / two photocopies of (C) / the degree
  certificate. (D)*

  **Error in B.** After "must", the base verb: "must bring".
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Past regret" icon="i-lucide-rewind"}
  *I should practise more before the drive last week.*

  The drive is over, so this is regret about the past: should + have + past
  participle.

  **Correct: I should have practised more before the drive last week.**
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Choosing the meaning" icon="i-lucide-list-checks"}
  *The office is closed today. It ____ be a holiday.*

  (a) can (b) must (c) should (d) would

  **(b) must.** A strong conclusion from evidence.
  :::
::

## Common mistakes

- **"Can able to".** Either "can" or "am able to", never both.
- **"Should to" and "must to".** Only "ought" takes "to".
- **Two modals: "will can", "might could".**
- **"Could of" and "should of".** It is "could have", "should have"; the "of"
  comes from how it sounds when spoken.
- **"Must not" for "need not".**

## How to practise

- **Day one:** the rules block and the table. Write one sentence per modal
  about your job search.
- **Day two:** 15 questions from IndiaBix "Sentence Improvement" and "Spotting
  Errors", looking for modals. Target **25 seconds**.
- **Ongoing:** write your practice emails with "could", "would" and "may" for
  requests. It will feel formal at first; that is the point.

## Practice questions

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. Correct it: 'I can able to join from Monday.'"}
  **I can join from Monday** (or "I am able to join").
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. Fill in: 'You ____ bring a printout; the e-ticket on your phone is enough.' (must not / need not)"}
  **Need not.** It is not necessary, not forbidden.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. Correct it: 'He should has told us earlier.'"}
  **He should have told us earlier.**
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. Fill in: '____ you please send the offer letter again?' (Can / Could)"}
  **Could.** Both are grammatical; "could" is the polite form a formal email
  expects, and it is the answer the test wants.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="5. Correct it: 'You will can see the results tomorrow.'"}
  **You will be able to see the results tomorrow.**
  :::
