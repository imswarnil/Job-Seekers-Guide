Articles and prepositions were the small words. This page is the rule that
produces more error-spotting answers than any other: the verb must agree with
its subject, and the test always tries to make you look at the wrong noun.

## Why it matters

Subject-verb agreement is the most tested single grammar rule across TCS NQT,
Wipro, Accenture, Cognizant, Capgemini and AMCAT-style papers. I would expect
one to three questions per section to turn on it, most often in error spotting
and sentence correction. Check the current pattern.

It is also one of the fastest rules to apply. Once you know the technique, a
question takes 20 seconds.

## What to study

| # | Pattern | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | A phrase between subject and verb | High |
| 2 | Each, every, either, neither, everyone: singular | High |
| 3 | Either/or, neither/nor: the nearer subject decides | High |
| 4 | "One of the ... who" and "one of the ... " | High |
| 5 | "The number of" vs "a number of" | Medium |
| 6 | Collective nouns: committee, team, jury | Medium |
| 7 | Along with, as well as, together with: ignored | High |
| 8 | Nouns that look plural: news, mathematics, series | Medium |

## The technique

The test always separates the subject from the verb with a phrase, because
that is what makes it hard.

::compare
  :::compare-side{label="Wrong" verdict="wrong"}
  *The list of shortlisted candidates **were** published on Friday.*

  The verb has agreed with "candidates", the nearest noun, not with the
  subject.
  :::
  :::compare-side{label="Right" verdict="right"}
  *The list of shortlisted candidates **was** published on Friday.*

  Cross out "of shortlisted candidates" and read what is left: "the list was
  published".
  :::
::

**Cross out everything between the subject and the verb, then read what is
left.** That is the whole technique, and it works on most questions.

## The rules

```text
1. Phrases in between do not count
   The quality of the answers IS poor.

2. Each, every, either, neither, everyone, anybody, nobody → singular
   Each of the candidates HAS a hall ticket.

3. Either X or Y / neither X nor Y → verb agrees with the NEARER one
   Neither the manager nor the interviewers WERE available.
   Neither the interviewers nor the manager WAS available.

4. Along with, as well as, together with, in addition to → ignore them
   The candidate, along with her parents, WAS waiting outside.

5. X and Y joined by "and" → plural
   Java and SQL ARE on the syllabus.

6. The number of → singular.  A number of → plural
   The number of applicants HAS doubled.
   A number of applicants HAVE left.

7. One of the + plural noun + singular verb
   One of the candidates IS late.
   (But: "one of the candidates who ARE late": "who" refers to "candidates")

8. Collective nouns (committee, team, jury, staff) → singular when acting as one
   The committee HAS announced the results.

9. Plural-looking singulars → singular
   The news IS good.  Mathematics IS my weak subject.

10. Amounts as one quantity → singular
   Five thousand rupees IS a lot for a fresher.   Three months IS enough.
```

::tip
Rule 3 is the one people get wrong most often. Put your finger on the "or" or
"nor", and look at the noun directly after it. That noun decides the verb.
::

## A worked example

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="Hidden subject" icon="i-lucide-search"}
  *The performance of the candidates (A) / in the written round (B) / were
  better (C) / than last year. (D)*

  Cross out "of the candidates in the written round". What is left: "the
  performance were better".

  **Error in C.** It should be "was better".
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Neither-nor" icon="i-lucide-split"}
  *Neither the HR executive nor the panel members (A) / was informed (B) / about
  the change (C) / in venue. (D)*

  The nearer subject is "panel members", which is plural.

  **Error in B.** It should be "were informed".
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="As well as" icon="i-lucide-link"}
  *The trainer, as well as the students, are going to the drive.*

  "As well as the students" does not count. The subject is "the trainer".

  **Correct: The trainer, as well as the students, is going to the drive.**
  :::
::

## Common mistakes

- **Agreeing with the nearest noun** instead of the real subject.
- **"Each of them are".** Each is singular, always.
- **"The number of candidates are".** "The number" is one number.
- **Treating "along with" like "and".** Only "and" makes a plural.
- **"The news are".** News is singular.

## How to practise

- **Day one:** learn the ten rules. Write one sentence of your own for each.
- **Days two and three:** 15 error-spotting questions a day from IndiaBix
  "Spotting Errors", doing the cross-out on paper every time. Target **25
  seconds**.
- **Then:** whenever an error-spotting question stumps you, try this rule
  first. It is the answer more often than any other.

## Practice questions

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. 'The set of attendance records for all first-year students are missing.' Correct it."}
  **The set ... is missing.** Cross out "of attendance records for all
  first-year students".
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. 'Either the students or the teacher ____ (have / has) the key.'"}
  **Has.** The nearer subject is "the teacher".
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. 'A number of freshers ____ (was / were) waiting outside.'"}
  **Were.** "A number of" means many, so plural.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. 'One of my friends have cleared the TCS test.' Correct it."}
  **One of my friends has cleared the TCS test.**
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="5. 'Ten kilometres ____ (is / are) a long way to walk to the venue.'"}
  **Is.** A distance as one quantity.
  :::
::
