The overview showed that almost every grammar question is about a verb. This
page is the verb's timing: which form says when something happened, and the
handful of tense mistakes that the papers are built around.

## Why it matters

Tense errors are the single most common thing tested in error spotting,
sentence correction and fill in the blanks. Across TCS NQT, Wipro, Accenture,
Cognizant, Capgemini and AMCAT-style papers, I would expect two to four
questions per section to turn on a tense, even when the question does not say
"tense" anywhere. Check the current pattern, but this is the first grammar
topic to learn.

It matters after the test too. "I have completed my graduation in 2018" is the
sentence I heard most often in interview queues, and it is wrong.

| # | Topic | Priority |

| 7 | Verbs that do not take -ing (know, understand, belong) | Medium |

## The twelve tenses, one table

Take the verb "study".

| | Simple | Continuous | Perfect | Perfect continuous |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Present** | I study | I am studying | I have studied | I have been studying |
| **Past** | I studied | I was studying | I had studied | I had been studying |
| **Future** | I will study | I will be studying | I will have studied | I will have been studying |

You do not need to name them in the test. You need to know which one a
sentence needs.

## The rules that carry the marks

```text
1. Simple past + a finished time (yesterday, in 2018, last week, ago)
   I completed my degree in 2018.            NOT: I have completed ... in 2018

2. Present perfect + no finished time, or up to now (already, yet, ever, just)
   I have already submitted the form.

3. Since + a point in time; for + a length of time
   I have been studying since March.  I have been studying for three months.

4. Past conditional: If + had + past participle, would have + past participle
   If I had practised, I would have cleared it.  NOT: If I would have practised

5. Time clauses about the future use the present
   I will call you when the results come out.   NOT: when the results will come out

6. Two past actions: the earlier one takes the past perfect
   The test had started when I reached the hall.

7. Sequence: a past reporting verb pulls the next clause into the past
   He said that he was tired.   NOT: He said that he is tired (in most test answers)

8. Stative verbs (know, believe, belong, understand, want) avoid the -ing form
   I know the answer.   NOT: I am knowing the answer
```

::tip
The signal words do half the work. "Yesterday", "ago", "in 2018", "last year"
mean simple past. "Since" and "for" with something still going on mean the
present perfect continuous. Circle the signal word before you look at the
options.
::

## A worked example

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="Present perfect with a finished time" icon="i-lucide-calendar"}
  *I have passed out from college in 2018.*

  "In 2018" is a finished time, so the verb must be simple past.

  **Correct: I passed out from college in 2018.** (Or "I graduated in 2018".)
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Since and the continuous" icon="i-lucide-clock"}
  *She is waiting in the queue since eight o'clock.*

  The waiting started in the past and is still going on, and "since" gives a
  starting point.

  **Correct: She has been waiting in the queue since eight o'clock.**
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="The if-clause" icon="i-lucide-git-branch"}
  *If he would have carried his résumé, they would have interviewed him.*

  "Would have" never goes in the "if" half.

  **Correct: If he had carried his résumé, they would have interviewed him.**
  :::
::

## Common mistakes

- **"Have done" with a date.** A finished time takes the simple past.
- **"Since three months".** Three months is a length: "for three months".
- **"Would" in both halves** of a conditional.
- **"Will" after when, before, after, until, as soon as, if** when talking
  about the future.
- **"I am understanding" and "I am having a car".** Stative verbs stay simple.
  ("I am having lunch" is fine, because there "have" means eat.)

## How to practise

- **Days one and two:** learn the rules block. Write two sentences of your own
  for each rule, about your own week.
- **Days three to five:** 15 tense questions a day from IndiaBix "Sentence
  Correction" and "Spotting Errors", and the tense chapter of Wren and Martin.
  Target **30 seconds** each.
- **Every day after that:** when you read your news article aloud, stop at
  three verbs and ask why that tense.

## Practice questions

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. Correct it: 'I am living in BTM Layout for six months.'"}
  **I have been living in BTM Layout for six months.** Still going on, with a
  length of time.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. Fill in: 'The interview ____ (start) before I reached the office.'"}
  **Had started.** The earlier of two past actions.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. Correct it: 'I will inform you as soon as I will get the offer letter.'"}
  **As soon as I get the offer letter.** Future time clauses take the present.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. Correct it: 'Did you ever visited Bangalore?'"}
  **Have you ever visited Bangalore?** "Ever" with experience up to now takes
  the present perfect. ("Did you ever visit" is possible in some varieties, but
  "did" never takes "visited".)
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="5. Fill in: 'If I ____ (know) the venue, I would have reached on time.'"}
  **Had known.**
  :::
::
