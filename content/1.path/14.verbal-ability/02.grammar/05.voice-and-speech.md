Modals finished the verb rules. This page puts all of them to work: turning a
sentence from active to passive, and from direct speech to reported speech.
Both are pure transformation, and both use every tense rule you have learned.

## Why it matters

Voice and speech questions appear in AMCAT-style tests, Capgemini, Wipro and
many smaller company papers, and in older TCS and Cognizant patterns, roughly
one or two questions per section when present. Some years they vanish from a
company's pattern completely; check the current one. They are worth learning
anyway, because they are mechanical: follow the table and you get the mark.

Two definitions. In the **active voice** the doer comes first: *HR shortlisted
the candidates.* In the **passive voice** the thing done to comes first: *The
candidates were shortlisted by HR.* **Direct speech** quotes the exact words:
*He said, "I am ready."* **Indirect** (reported) **speech** reports them: *He
said that he was ready.*

## Passive voice: the table

The passive is always a form of **be** + the **past participle**. The tense
lives in the "be".

| Tense | Active | Passive |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Simple present | HR checks the forms. | The forms are checked by HR. |
| Present continuous | HR is checking the forms. | The forms are being checked by HR. |
| Present perfect | HR has checked the forms. | The forms have been checked by HR. |
| Simple past | HR checked the forms. | The forms were checked by HR. |
| Past continuous | HR was checking the forms. | The forms were being checked by HR. |
| Past perfect | HR had checked the forms. | The forms had been checked by HR. |
| Simple future | HR will check the forms. | The forms will be checked by HR. |
| Modal | HR must check the forms. | The forms must be checked by HR. |

```text
Steps:
1. The object becomes the subject.
2. Keep the tense; write it on "be".
3. Add the past participle.
4. The old subject goes after "by" (or is dropped if it adds nothing).
5. Pronouns change case: I → me, he → him, they → them.

Commands:  Close the door.  →  Let the door be closed.
Perfect continuous tenses have no common passive. Papers rarely ask them.
```

## Indirect speech: the rules

When the reporting verb is in the past ("said", "asked"), the tense inside
moves one step back. This is called **backshift**.

| Direct | Indirect |
| --- | --- |
| am / is → | was |
| are → | were |
| do / does → | did |
| did → | had done |
| have / has → | had |
| will → | would |
| can → | could |
| may → | might |
| must → | had to (or must) |

| Word in direct speech | Becomes |
| --- | --- |
| now | then |
| today | that day |
| yesterday | the previous day / the day before |
| tomorrow | the next day / the following day |
| here | there |
| this / these | that / those |
| ago | before |

```text
Statements: said that + backshift
  He said, "I am tired."          → He said that he was tired.

Questions (yes/no): asked if / whether, no question mark, statement word order
  She asked, "Are you ready?"     → She asked if I was ready.

Questions (wh-): asked + wh-word, statement word order
  He asked, "Where is the venue?" → He asked where the venue was.

Commands and requests: told / asked / ordered + to + verb
  She said, "Bring your ID."      → She told me to bring my ID.

No backshift when the reporting verb is present ("says"),
or for a universal truth: He said that the sun rises in the east.
```

## A worked example

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="Active to passive" icon="i-lucide-arrow-left-right"}
  **Change to passive: *The panel is interviewing the candidates.***

  Object "the candidates" becomes the subject. Present continuous, so "are
  being" + past participle.

  **Answer: *The candidates are being interviewed by the panel.***
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Direct to indirect, with every change" icon="i-lucide-message-square-quote"}
  **Change to indirect: *The recruiter said, "I will call you tomorrow."***

  ```text
  said → said that
  I → he or she (the recruiter)
  will → would
  you → me (the listener, if it is me)
  tomorrow → the next day
  ```

  **Answer: *The recruiter said that she would call me the next day.***
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="A reported question" icon="i-lucide-circle-help"}
  **Change to indirect: *He asked me, "Have you cleared the written round?"***

  Yes/no question, so "if". Statement word order. "Have" backshifts to "had".

  **Answer: *He asked me if I had cleared the written round.***
  :::
::

## Common mistakes

- **Changing the tense in the passive.** Past active stays past passive.
- **"Was being" for "has been"** and similar tense slips inside "be".
- **Keeping the question order** in reported questions: *asked where was the
  venue* is wrong.
- **Forgetting the time words:** "tomorrow" left as "tomorrow".
- **Keeping the question mark** in reported questions.
