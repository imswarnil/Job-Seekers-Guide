Idioms and phrases finished the vocabulary chapter, and that is the last of the
multiple-choice English. Some papers then ask you to write something yourself,
and the most common thing they ask for is an email. This page is how to write
one that scores.

## Why it matters

Email writing turns up in the written rounds of some service companies. It has
appeared in Accenture's communication assessment and in Cognizant-style and
AMCAT-style papers, usually as **one email in about 10–15 minutes**, often with
a word limit around 100–150 words and sometimes with a list of words or phrases
you must use. Patterns change between hiring seasons, so check the current
pattern for the company you are sitting.

It is also the first thing you will write at work. In the job, a clear email
gets a reply the same morning. A long, polite one with the request at the
bottom often gets none.

::note
The marking is usually partly automated and partly a rubric. Either way, it
rewards the same things: the right format, every point in the prompt covered,
correct grammar, a polite and formal tone, and staying inside the word limit.
::

## What to study

In priority order:

- [ ] **The format**: subject, greeting, opening line, body, closing, sign-off.
- [ ] **Covering every point in the prompt.** Markers tick them off one by one.
- [ ] **Formal tone**: no SMS spelling, no "Hi dude", no emojis.
- [ ] **Using the given words** when the prompt supplies them, each one in a
      sentence that makes sense.
- [ ] **Grammar you already know**: tenses, articles, subject-verb agreement.
- [ ] **The common email types**: request, apology, complaint, information,
      leave application, reschedule.

## The format

| Part | What goes in it | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Subject | The point of the email, in a few words | Request to reschedule interview on 14 March |
| Greeting | Formal, by name if given | Dear Ms Rao, |
| Opening | Who you are and why you are writing, in one or two lines | I am writing to request... |
| Body | The details the prompt asks for, one idea per paragraph | Reason, what you propose, what you will do |
| Closing | What you want them to do next, and thanks | I would be grateful if you could confirm... |
| Sign-off | Formal, then your name | Regards, / Yours sincerely, |

## The template

Fill this in and you have covered the format before you think about content.

```text [email-template.txt]
Subject: <what this email is about, in 5 to 8 words>

Dear <Mr/Ms Surname, or Sir/Madam>,

I am writing to <request / inform you / apologise for / enquire about> <topic>.

<Paragraph 2: the details. Reason, facts, dates. One idea per paragraph.>

<Paragraph 3: what you propose or what you are asking them to do.>

I would be grateful if you could <the action you need> by <date, if any>.
Thank you for your time.

Regards,
<Your full name>
<Phone number, if it helps>
```

::tip
Put the request in the first sentence. The reader should know what you want
before they know why. The old habit of three lines of "hope you are doing well"
before the point costs marks in the test and replies at work.
::

## A worked example

**The prompt:** *You have an interview with a company on 14 March at 10am, but
you have a university exam the same day. Write an email to the HR manager, Ms
Anjali Rao, asking to reschedule. Use these words: inconvenience, available,
confirm. (100–120 words)*

::compare
  :::compare-side{label="What freshers often write" verdict="wrong"}
  > Subject: interview
  >
  > Hi mam,
  >
  > Hope you are doing well. I am very happy to get the interview call from
  > your esteemed company. But I have exam on that day so I cant come. Pls
  > give me another date. Sorry for the inconvenience.
  >
  > Thanks

  Vague subject, "mam", SMS spelling, missing articles, no date proposed, only
  one of the three required words, no name.
  :::
  :::compare-side{label="What scores" verdict="right"}
  > Subject: Request to reschedule interview on 14 March
  >
  > Dear Ms Rao,
  >
  > I am writing to request a new time for my interview, currently scheduled
  > for 14 March at 10am.
  >
  > I have a university examination on the same morning, which I cannot move. I
  > apologise for any inconvenience this may cause.
  >
  > I am available on 15, 16 or 17 March at any time, and can also attend
  > online if that is easier for the panel.
  >
  > I would be grateful if you could confirm a new slot at your convenience. I
  > remain very keen on the role.
  >
  > Regards,
  > Rahul Verma
  > +91 98xxx xxxxx

  About 105 words. Every point covered, all three words used, a clear request.
  :::
::

## Common mistakes

- **Missing a point from the prompt.** If it says "mention your availability",
  the markers are looking for dates.
- **Forgetting a required word**, or using it in a sentence that does not make
  sense to tick a box.
- **Going far over or under the word limit.** Count roughly: a line of normal
  handwriting or typing is about 10–12 words.
- **SMS English**: "pls", "u", "cant" without the apostrophe, "mam".
- **Informal greetings and sign-offs**: "Hi", "Cheers", "Thanks & Regards"
  written as one shouted line.
- **No subject line**, or a subject that is one word.
- **Writing a letter format** (addresses, date at the top) when the prompt says
  email.

::warning{icon="i-lucide-alert-triangle"}
Do not memorise one email and paste it into every prompt. A rubric checks that
you answered this prompt. A beautiful email about the wrong thing scores low.
::

## How to practise

::steps
### Week one: the format
Write one email a day from a prompt, on paper, using the template. Check it
against the format table and the prompt, point by point.

### Week two: against the clock
One email a day in **12 minutes**, typed, with a word count at the end. Use
the email-writing prompts on PrepInsta's Accenture and Cognizant pages, or
make your own from situations in your week: asking a PG owner for a receipt,
asking an institute for a demo class, rescheduling a walk-in.

### Before a drive
Two emails from that company's previous prompts. Read each one back aloud once;
you will hear the missing article that your eye skipped.
::

## Practice prompts

Write each one in 12 minutes, then compare with the points listed.

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. Ask your training institute for a certificate of completion you need for an interview next week."}
  **The points a marker looks for:** subject naming the certificate; who you
  are (name, course, batch dates); what you need and by when; why (the
  interview); a polite closing asking them to confirm when it will be ready.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. You missed a scheduled online assessment because of a power cut. Write to the recruiter explaining and asking for another chance."}
  **Points:** subject with the assessment name and date; a short, factual
  apology; the reason in one line (no long story); a request for a new slot;
  your availability; thanks.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. Use the words: accommodation, deposit, refund. Write to your PG owner asking for your deposit back when you move out."}
  **Points:** subject about the deposit refund; the date you are leaving; the
  deposit amount and when it was paid; a request for the refund and how you
  would like it paid; each of the three words used naturally.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. Inform a company that you have accepted another offer and are withdrawing from their process."}
  **Points:** subject stating the withdrawal; thanks for their time; a clear,
  polite statement that you are withdrawing (no need to name the other
  company); hope to stay in touch; formal sign-off.
  :::
::
