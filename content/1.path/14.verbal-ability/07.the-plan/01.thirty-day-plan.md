The fear of speaking was the last topic. This page puts the whole track into
thirty days you can actually follow, alongside the quant and reasoning plans if
you are running all three.

## Why it matters

A verbal section is usually 20–25 questions in 20–30 minutes, plus an email or
essay in some papers (check the current pattern). You do not need to be
brilliant at English to clear it. You need the rules behind the questions, a
method for each question type, a steady supply of new words, and enough timed
practice that the clock stops surprising you. Thirty days is enough for that
if you do it every day.

## The daily shape

About 60–75 minutes a day, split like this:

| Block | Time | What |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Words | 10 min | 10 new words, plus the review pile from [How to learn words that stay](/verbal-ability/vocabulary/how-to-learn-words) |
| The day's topic | 30–40 min | Read the page, then its practice questions, then 15–20 more from IndiaBix or PrepInsta |
| Reading | 10 min | One editorial, read aloud. Doubles as speaking practice |
| Speaking | 10 min | One recorded 60–90 second answer, and the step of the speaking ladder you are on |

::tip
Keep one notebook for verbal: grammar rules in the front, mistakes in the back
with the rule you broke. Revise the back pages every Sunday.
::

::warning{icon="i-lucide-alert-triangle"}
Do not skip the mocks to "finish topics". The mocks are where you learn your

but never timed is not ready.
::

## Common mistakes

- **Doing vocabulary in one week and then stopping.** Words need the daily
  review or they are gone in a month.
- **Leaving speaking until after the written round.** The interview can be the
  same afternoon.
- **Reading pages without doing questions.** The practice is the plan; the
  reading is preparation for it.
- **Not logging mistakes.** The same five rules cause most wrong answers, and
  only the log shows you which five are yours.

## How to practise

- **Where:** IndiaBix's verbal ability and verbal reasoning sections, PrepInsta's
  company-wise pages, and a grammar book such as Wren and Martin or S.P. Bakshi's
  *Objective General English* for extra exercises.
- **When the plan slips:** do not restart. Move the missed day to the next
  revision day and carry on.
- **After day 30:** keep 20 minutes a day (words, one RC passage, one recorded
  answer) until you have an offer.

## Check yourself

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. Which question type did your diagnostic on day 1 show as weakest?"}
  Give that topic an extra 15 minutes on each revision day (7, 21, 29).
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. Can you write an email and plan an essay inside the time?"}
  12 minutes for an email, 5 minutes for an essay plan. If not, add one of each
  to days 25 and 27.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. Did your mock score go up between Mock 1 and Mock 3?"}
  If it did not, look at the mistake log. It is usually one or two rules, or
  running out of time on reading comprehension.
  :::
::
