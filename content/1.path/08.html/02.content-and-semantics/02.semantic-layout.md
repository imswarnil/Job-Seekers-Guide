Headings, text, links and images fill a page, but a page also has regions: a
banner at the top, navigation, the main part, a sidebar, a footer. HTML5 added an
element for each, and choosing between them is one of the most asked HTML
questions there is.

## Why this matters in a job

Screen reader users move around a page by **landmarks** ("skip to main
content", "next region"). A page built from `div`s has none, so they hear the
navigation read out on every single page. Search engines also weight the content
inside `<main>` and `<article>` above the footer and sidebar. And in interviews,
"what is the difference between `div`, `section` and `article`?" is asked so
often that it is worth having a crisp answer ready.

## A page has regions

Think of a printed college newsletter. The masthead at the top with the college
name, a strip of section names, the lead story, a column of short notices down
the side, and the address at the bottom. Nobody labels them, and everybody
recognises them. The semantic tags are those regions, labelled so a machine can
recognise them too.

::feature-list{columns="2"}
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-panel-top" title="header"}
  Introductory content: the site banner, or the heading block of an article. A
  page can have several (one per article).
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-menu" title="nav"}
  A block of major navigation links. Not every group of links; the main menu,
  a table of contents, pagination.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-square" title="main"}
  The unique content of this page. **Exactly one visible `main` per page**, not
  inside header, footer or aside.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-newspaper" title="article"}
  Self-contained content that would make sense on its own, copied to another
  site: a news post, a notice, a comment, a student profile card.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-layers" title="section"}
  A thematic group within something, which normally has its own heading:
  "Eligibility", "Fees", "Hostel".
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-panel-right" title="aside"}
  Related but not central: a sidebar, a "did you know" box, related links.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-panel-bottom" title="footer"}
  Closing information for its section or page: contact details, copyright,
  secondary links.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-image" title="figure and figcaption"}
  A self-contained illustration, chart or code listing with its caption.
  :::
::

### Choosing between div, section and article

::flow{numbered direction="vertical" caption="Ask these in order."}
  :::flow-step{label="Would it make sense on its own, somewhere else?" icon="i-lucide-newspaper"}
  Yes: `article`. A news item, a notice, a faculty profile.
  :::
  :::flow-step{label="Is it a themed part of something, with its own heading?" icon="i-lucide-layers" highlight}
  Yes: `section`. The "Fees" part of the admissions page.
  :::
  :::flow-step{label="Is it only there to be styled or grouped for layout?" icon="i-lucide-box"}
  Yes: `div`. A wrapper for a grid, a card's padding. No meaning is intended,
  and that is fine.
  :::
::

## How it works

A department home page, marked up with landmarks:

```html [department.html]
<!doctype html>
<html lang="en">
  <head>
    <meta charset="utf-8">
    <title>Computer Science · LNCT</title>
  </head>
  <body>
    <header>
      <p><a href="/">LNCT Bhopal</a></p>
      <nav aria-label="Main">
        <ul>
          <li><a href="/admissions">Admissions</a></li>
          <li><a href="/departments">Departments</a></li>
          <li><a href="/placements">Placements</a></li>
        </ul>
      </nav>
    </header>

    <main>
      <h1>Department of Computer Science</h1>

      <section aria-labelledby="programmes">
        <h2 id="programmes">Programmes</h2>
        <p>B.Tech (120 seats) and M.Tech (18 seats).</p>
      </section>

      <section aria-labelledby="notices">
        <h2 id="notices">Notices</h2>
        <article>
          <h3>Semester 3 results published</h3>
          <p>Results are on the portal from 10 am on Monday.</p>
        </article>
        <article>
          <h3>Guest lecture on operating systems</h3>
          <p>Seminar hall B, Thursday at 2 pm.</p>
        </article>
      </section>

      <figure>
        <img src="/cse/placements.png" width="640" height="360"
             alt="Offers per year: 2023 88, 2024 97, 2025 112">
        <figcaption>Placement offers, 2023–2025</figcaption>
      </figure>
    </main>

    <aside aria-label="Quick links">
      <a href="/timetable">Timetable</a>
    </aside>

    <footer>
      <p>Raisen Road, Bhopal · admissions@college.edu</p>
    </footer>
  </body>
</html>
```

```text [The landmarks a screen reader lists]
banner            (the top-level header)
navigation, Main
main
  region, Programmes
  region, Notices
complementary, Quick links
contentinfo       (the top-level footer)
```

That list is what a screen reader user sees when they press the "landmarks"
key. A `div` page produces an empty list. Note that a `section` only becomes a
named region when it has an accessible name, which is what
`aria-labelledby="programmes"` gives it by pointing at the heading's `id`.

::warning{icon="i-lucide-alert-triangle"}
`lang="en"` on `<html>` is one attribute and decides which voice a screen reader
uses. Without it, an English page can be read with another language's
pronunciation rules. It is the cheapest accessibility fix there is and it is
missing from many pages.
::

## Real use case

The "skip to content" link, found at the top of almost every government and
university site that has been through an accessibility audit, depends on
`<main>` existing:

```html [skip-link.html]
<body>
  <a href="#main" class="skip-link">Skip to content</a>
  <header>... forty navigation links ...</header>
  <main id="main">
    <h1>Fee payment</h1>
  </main>
</body>
```

It is hidden until it receives keyboard focus, so a keyboard user's first Tab
shows it, and Enter jumps past forty links. Search engines benefit from the same
structure: the text in `<main>` and `<article>` is treated as the page's
content, and the repeated header and footer are recognised as boilerplate.

## What confuses people here

- **`section` is not a fancy `div`.** If you are adding it only to hang a class
  on, use a `div`. A section without a heading is usually a sign of that.
- **`header` is not only for the top of the page.** An `article` can have its
  own `header` with its title and date.
- **`article` does not mean "a blog article".** It means self-contained. A
  comment, a product card, a student profile card are all articles.
- **Semantic tags do not change the look.** They are all block elements with no
  default styling beyond that. The benefit is entirely in meaning.

## Interview questions

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="Difference between div, section and article?" icon="i-lucide-message-circle"}
  `div` is a generic container with no meaning, used for styling and layout.
  `section` is a thematic grouping of content, normally with a heading.
  `article` is self-contained content that would make sense on its own, such as
  a news item or a comment. Test: could it be syndicated elsewhere? Article.
  Is it a titled part of something? Section. Neither? Div.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Name the HTML5 semantic elements." icon="i-lucide-message-circle"}
  `header`, `nav`, `main`, `article`, `section`, `aside`, `footer`, `figure`,
  `figcaption`, `time`, `mark`, `details` and `summary`. Then say why: they give
  screen readers landmarks, search engines structure, and developers readable
  markup.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="How many main elements can a page have?" icon="i-lucide-message-circle"}
  One visible `main`. It holds the content unique to that page and must not be
  inside `header`, `footer`, `aside` or `article`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Does semantic HTML help SEO?" icon="i-lucide-message-circle"}
  Yes, indirectly: headings and landmarks tell a crawler what the page is about
  and which part is content versus navigation. It is not a ranking trick; it
  makes the page easier to understand, for machines and people.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="What is a landmark?" icon="i-lucide-message-circle"}
  A region a screen reader can jump to: banner (`header`), navigation (`nav`),
  main, complementary (`aside`), contentinfo (`footer`), and named regions.
  They let a user skip straight to the part they want.
  :::
::
