You know the shapes and the minutes. What is left is the wording, and a question
you could solve but could not parse is worth the same zero as one you could not
solve at all.

## Quantitative

::field-group
  :::field{name="Percentage point" type="critical"}
  The arithmetic difference between two percentages. 68% to 76% is eight
  **percentage points** and an 11.8% **rise**. Confusing these two is the most
  frequent error in both exams and real reports.
  :::
  :::field{name="Of" type="wording"}
  Means multiply. "30% of 400" is `400 × 0.30`. Trivial until it appears inside
  a sentence with three other numbers.
  :::
  :::field{name="Successive" type="wording"}
  One change applied to the result of the last. Multiply the multipliers; never
  add the percentages.
  :::
  :::field{name="Ratio" type="concept"}
  A shape, not a quantity. `22:3` says nothing about size until one real number
  is supplied, and then everything follows from one multiplier.
  :::
  :::field{name="Proportion" type="concept"}
  Two ratios that are equal. "Directly proportional" means one doubles when the
  other does; "inversely" means one halves.
  :::
  :::field{name="Average speed" type="critical"}
  Total distance divided by total time. Never the average of the speeds unless
  the times are equal. For equal distances, `2ab ÷ (a + b)`.
  :::
  :::field{name="Rate" type="concept"}
  How much of a job per unit of time. Rates add; times do not. The single idea
  underneath all time, work, pipes and relative-speed questions.
  :::
  :::field{name="Simple interest" type="formula"}
  `P × R × T ÷ 100`. Interest on the original amount only, every period.
  :::
  :::field{name="Compound interest" type="formula"}
  `P × (1 + R/100)^T − P`. Interest on interest, which is the successive-change
  multiplier wearing a different name.
  :::
  :::field{name="Mean, median, mode" type="statistics"}
  Average, middle value when sorted, most frequent. A question asking for the
  median of an even-length list wants the average of the middle two.
  :::
::

## Reasoning

::field-group
  :::field{name="Syllogism" type="logic"}
  Statements plus a conclusion, asking whether it necessarily follows. It
  follows only if true in **every** arrangement consistent with the statements.
  :::
  :::field{name="Follows" type="critical"}
  Means "must be true", not "is probably true" and not "is true in the real
  world". Real-world knowledge actively hurts here.
  :::
  :::field{name="Only" type="wording"}
  Carries entire questions. "The only son", "the only daughter". Never
  decoration, always the key. Circle it.
  :::
  :::field{name="Immediate" type="wording"}
  Adjacent, with nobody in between. "To the left of" allows a gap; "immediately
  to the left of" does not.
  :::
  :::field{name="Facing the centre" type="critical"}
  In a circle, their left is anticlockwise as you look down at your drawing.
  "Facing outward" reverses it. Write which one at the top of the drawing.
  :::
  :::field{name="Between" type="wording"}
  "Three people between A and B" means exactly three. "A is third to the left of
  B" counts from B. Fix your convention before starting.
  :::
  :::field{name="Coding-decoding" type="pattern"}
  A letter substitution, usually a fixed shift, sometimes a reversal or a
  position swap. Write the alphabet with positions in the margin first.
  :::
  :::field{name="Affirming the consequent" type="logic"}
  All A are B, X is B, therefore X is A. Invalid, and the most common wrong
  answer in syllogism questions. Also the shape of most bad debugging.
  :::
::

## Data interpretation

::field-group
  :::field{name="Biggest against fastest" type="critical"}
  "Largest increase" is absolute. "Fastest growth" is relative. The smallest
  category usually wins the second and never the first.
  :::
  :::field{name="Denominator" type="concept"}
  What a percentage is a percentage of. Half the traps in this section are a
  swapped denominator.
  :::
  :::field{name="Lakh and crore" type="units"}
  One lakh is 100,000. One crore is 10 million, or 100 lakh. A table headed "in
  lakhs" with the answer options in crores is a deliberate trap.
  :::
  :::field{name="Cumulative" type="wording"}
  A running total rather than a per-period figure. A cumulative chart never goes
  down, and reading one as per-period is a guaranteed wrong answer.
  :::
::

## The exam itself

::field-group
  :::field{name="Negative marking" type="scoring"}
  A deduction for a wrong answer, usually 0.25. Check the actual scheme in the
  first thirty seconds.
  :::
  :::field{name="Expected value" type="scoring"}
  The average marks a decision earns over many attempts. With one option
  eliminated and 0.25 negative marking, guessing is worth `+0.165`, so it is
  always worth doing.
  :::
  :::field{name="Sectional cutoff" type="scoring"}
  A minimum you must score **in each section**, not only overall. Where it
  exists, skipping an entire section is fatal no matter how well you did
  elsewhere. Find out before the day.
  :::
  :::field{name="Proctored" type="format"}
  Watched, usually by camera and screen monitoring. Rough paper is often
  restricted to one sheet you must show at the start, which changes how you
  plan your working.
  :::
::

## The four that decide most marks

If you remember nothing else from this glossary:

::feature-list{columns="2"}
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-percent" title="Percentage point is not percentage"}
  Appears in the quantitative section and again in data interpretation, and in
  every dashboard you will ever build.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-gauge" title="Average speed is not the average of speeds"}
  One of the four options will be the midpoint, put there because it is the
  expected mistake.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-trending-up" title="Fastest is not biggest"}
  Relative against absolute. The question always says which, and the eye always
  reads the other.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-compass" title="Facing the centre reverses left"}
  The highest-frequency single error in the reasoning section.
  :::
::
