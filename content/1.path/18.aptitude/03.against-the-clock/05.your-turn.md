Eight questions where the number was the easy half. This is the last thing in
the track and it is the only one that tells you anything, because everything
until now has been practice without the clock that does the actual rejecting.

## Set it up properly

::steps
### Sixty minutes, one timer, visible
Not a rough hour. A timer where you can see the minutes going.

### One sheet of rough paper
This is what a proctored exam allows. If you have been using three, find out now
rather than on the day.

### No interruptions, no phone
The point is to reproduce the conditions, and the conditions include not being
able to look anything up.

### Write down your pass-one finish time
You are measuring your triage as much as your answers.
::

## The paper

Forty questions, sixty minutes. Fewer than a real paper, proportionally the same
time pressure.

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="Section A: Quantitative (15 questions)" icon="i-lucide-calculator"}
  1. A department's intake fell from 180 to 144. Percentage decrease?
  2. A fee rises 8% then 12%. Net rise?
  3. Seats split 6:4:5 over 600. Largest share?
  4. 42% of 850 applicants cleared. How many?
  5. Two clerks take 15 and 10 hours. Together?
  6. A tank fills in 4 hours, drains in 12. Time to fill?
  7. Trains 420 km apart, speeds 60 and 80 km/h, approaching. Time to meet?
  8. Out at 15 km/h, back at 10 km/h. Average speed?
  9. Students to staff 32:3. 42 staff. Students?
  10. Of 900 applicants, 40% shortlisted, 35% of those accepted. Accepted?
  11. A 240m train passes a 360m bridge in 30 seconds. Speed in km/h?
  12. Three staff take 15, 20 and 12 hours. Together?
  13. A price rises 20%. What fall returns it to the original?
  14. 55% study Java, 40% study SQL, 25% study both. Percentage studying
      neither?
  15. Attendance moves from 64% to 72%. Percentage rise?
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Section B: Reasoning (15 questions)" icon="i-lucide-puzzle"}
  16. `4, 9, 19, 39, 79, …`
  17. `3, 6, 18, 72, …`
  18. `2, 6, 12, 20, 30, …`
  19. `D, G, K, P, …`
  20. Odd one out: `27, 64, 100, 125, 216`
  21. **All engineers are graduates. Some graduates are teachers.**
      *Conclusion:* Some engineers are teachers.
  22. **No student is a lecturer. All lecturers are staff.**
      *Conclusion:* Some staff are not students.
  23. **All who applied paid a fee. Anita paid a fee.**
      *Conclusion:* Anita applied.
  24. Five sit in a row facing north. C is third from left. A is immediately
      right of B. E is at the left end. D is not at an end. Who is at the right
      end?
  25. Books stacked: History directly above Geography. Maths at the bottom.
      Science directly below English. Geography above Science. Order from top?
  26. "She is the daughter of my father's only son." Relation to the speaker?
  27. "P is the brother of Q. R is the father of P. S is the sister of R." How
      is S related to Q?
  28. If `MARKS` is `NBSLT`, what is `GRADE`?
  29. Walk 5 km east, turn left, walk 4 km, turn left, walk 5 km. Distance and
      direction from start?
  30. If today is Wednesday, what day is it 100 days from now?
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Section C: Data interpretation (10 questions)" icon="i-lucide-table"}
  Fee collection, in lakhs, across four departments:

  | Department | 2024 | 2025 | 2026 | Students 2026 |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Computer Science | 240 | 288 | 360 | 800 |
  | Electronics | 180 | 171 | 162 | 400 |
  | Mathematics | 80 | 100 | 132 | 300 |
  | Management | 100 | 118 | 160 | 340 |
  | **Total** | **600** | **677** | **814** | **1,840** |

  31. Percentage increase in total collection 2024 to 2026?
  32. Which department grew fastest 2024 to 2026?
  33. Average fee per student in Computer Science in 2026?
  34. Which department had the highest fee per student in 2026?
  35. Electronics fell each year. At the 2025 to 2026 rate, what is 2027?
  36. What percentage of 2026 collection was Computer Science?
  37. Ratio of Mathematics to Management collection in 2025?
  38. If Mathematics grows 30% in 2027, what will it collect?
  39. Total collection per student across the university in 2026?
  40. Which department had the largest absolute increase 2025 to 2026?
  :::

## The answers

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="Section A" icon="i-lucide-check"}
  **1.** `36 ÷ 180 = 20%`. **2.** `1.08 × 1.12 = 1.2096` → 20.96%.
  **3.** Parts 15, multiplier 40 → largest is `6 × 40 = 240`.
  **4.** `850 × 0.42 = 357`. **5.** `1/15 + 1/10 = 2/30 + 3/30 = 5/30 = 1/6` →
  **6 hours**. **6.** `1/4 − 1/12 = 3/12 − 1/12 = 2/12 = 1/6` → **6 hours**.
  **7.** `420 ÷ 140 = 3 hours`. **8.** `2 × 15 × 10 ÷ 25 = 12 km/h`.
  **9.** Multiplier 14 → `32 × 14 = 448`. **10.** `900 × 0.40 × 0.35 = 126`.
  **11.** `600m ÷ 30s = 20 m/s = 72 km/h`.
  **12.** `1/15 + 1/20 + 1/12 = 4/60 + 3/60 + 5/60 = 12/60 = 1/5` → **5 hours**.
  **13.** `1 ÷ 1.2 = 0.8333`, so a fall of **16.67%**.
  **14.** `55 + 40 − 25 = 70` study at least one → **30% neither**.
  **15.** Eight percentage points = `8 ÷ 64` = **12.5%**.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Section B" icon="i-lucide-check"}
  **16.** `× 2 + 1` → **159**. **17.** `× 2, × 3, × 4`, next `× 5` → **360**.
  **18.** `n(n+1)`: 2, 6, 12, 20, 30 → **42**.
  **19.** Positions 4, 7, 11, 16: gaps 3, 4, 5, next 6 → 22 = **V**.
  **20.** 27, 64, 125, 216 are cubes. **100** is not.
  **21. Does not follow.** The teachers could all be non-engineer graduates.
  **22. Follows.** Lecturers are staff and no lecturer is a student.
  **23. Does not follow.** Affirming the consequent. Someone else could pay a
  fee.
  **24.** `E _ C _ _` with A immediately right of B, and D not at an end. B and A
  take slots 2 and... slot 2 alone is free before C. So B=4, A=5 fails because D
  cannot be at an end and slot 2 would be D, which is valid: `E, D, C, B, A`.
  Right end is **A**.
  **25.** From the top: **History, Geography, English, Science, Maths**.
  **26.** Father's only son is the speaker, so she is his **daughter**.
  **27.** S is R's sister, R is P's and Q's father, so S is their **aunt**.
  **28.** Each letter +1 → **HSBEF**.
  **29.** East 5, north 4, west 5. East and west cancel → **4 km north**.
  **30.** `100 ÷ 7 = 14` remainder 2. Wednesday + 2 = **Friday**.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Section C" icon="i-lucide-check"}
  **31.** `214 ÷ 600 = 35.67%`.
  **32.** Maths: `80 → 132` is +65%. CS is +50%, Management +60%. **Maths**.
  **33.** `360 lakh ÷ 800 = 0.45 lakh = ₹45,000`.
  **34.** CS ₹45,000; Electronics `162 ÷ 400 = ₹40,500`; Maths
  `132 ÷ 300 = ₹44,000`; Management `160 ÷ 340 = ₹47,059`. **Management**.
  Note this is not the department collecting the most.
  **35.** `171 → 162` is a fall of 9, which is 5.26%. Another 5.26% off 162 →
  **153.5 lakh**. And it is two points, not a trend.
  **36.** `360 ÷ 814 = 44.2%`.
  **37.** `100 : 118 = 50 : 59`.
  **38.** `132 × 1.30 = 171.6 lakh`.
  **39.** `814 lakh ÷ 1,840 = 0.4424 lakh = ₹44,239`.
  **40.** CS, `288 → 360`, up 72 lakh. Largest absolute, though not fastest.
  :::
::

## Reading your result

Two numbers matter, and the second one matters more than people expect.

| | What to look at |
| --- | --- |
| **Marks** | Out of 40. Most written rounds cut somewhere around 55 to 65% |
| **Pass-one finish time** | Should be around 25 minutes with 24 to 28 questions answered |

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="What the combinations tell you" icon="i-lucide-check"}
  **High marks, pass one finished around 25 minutes.** You are ready. Sit one
  more in a fortnight to stay sharp and stop practising this.

  **High accuracy, few attempted.** The commonest profile, and mine. You know
  the material and you are being rejected by the clock. The fix is entirely the
  fifteen-second rule, and it is a habit rather than knowledge, so it needs
  repetition rather than study.

  **Many attempted, low accuracy.** You are rushing past the reading. Slow down
  on the final sentence of each question, because most of these will be right
  method answering the wrong question.

  **Low on one section only.** Go back to that chapter. Do not do another full
  paper until it improves, because a mock tells you nothing you do not already
  know.
  :::
::

## The honest note to end on

::pull-quote
This round is learnable, it is mechanical, and it is not a measure of whether
you can do the job. Clearing it is a tax you pay to get into the room where
somebody finally looks at what you built.
::

I took four years to pay it, because for three of them I was preparing for the
wrong round. Do not do that.
