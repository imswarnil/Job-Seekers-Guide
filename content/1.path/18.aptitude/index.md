It is built, tested, deployed and defensible. Now the round that will reject you
before anybody opens it.

## The thing nobody tells you

I failed to get a job for four years, and for most of that time I believed the
problem was that I was not good enough at programming. I was wrong, and it took
me an embarrassingly long time to work out why.

I was never being rejected for my code. I was being rejected at 10:15am, in a
hall, by a multiple-choice test, before a single technical person had seen
anything I had made.

::pull-quote
The written round is the largest filter in the Indian hiring pipeline, and it is
the one candidates prepare for least, because it does not feel like real
engineering.
::

## The numbers, said plainly

At a typical campus or mass-hiring drive:

::feature-list{columns="2"}
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-users" title="Who sits it"}
  Everybody who applied. At a large drive that is 2,000 people in a hall or on a
  proctored laptop, and the company has maybe 40 roles.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-filter" title="Who clears it"}
  Often under a third. The written round exists to make the rest of the process
  affordable, and it succeeds by rejecting most people quickly.
  :::
::

It is not a fair filter and it is not trying to be. It is a cheap one, and
understanding that is what stops it feeling personal.

## Why this is not as bad as it looks

Here is the good news, and it is genuinely good: **this is the most learnable
round in the entire process.**

A technical interview can go anywhere. An HR round depends on a person's mood.
The written round is drawn from a small, stable, public set of question shapes
that have barely changed in fifteen years. There are about eight of them in
quantitative aptitude, and maybe six in reasoning.

You are not learning mathematics. You are learning to recognise a shape and
apply a method, quickly, without a calculator.

::note
Four weeks, about an hour a day, is enough to go from failing this round to
clearing it comfortably. That is a smaller investment than any other track here
and it has a higher immediate return, which is exactly why it sits at this point
in the path rather than at the start: you now have something worth getting to
the next round **for**.
::

## What this track refuses to do

There are books with 900 practice questions. This is not one.

Volume without pattern recognition is how people spend six months and improve by
nothing. Every lesson here teaches a shape, shows the method on one worked
example, names the trap, and then gives you questions to attempt. If you can
recognise the shape, the arithmetic was never the hard part.

::warning{icon="i-lucide-alert-triangle"}
One habit matters more than any technique in this track: **do the arithmetic on
paper, not in your head.** Almost every wrong answer I have watched somebody
give in practice was a correct method with a slipped digit, and the digit
slipped because they were trying to look fast.
::

## The three chapters

::steps
### The numbers
Percentages, ratios, averages, time and work, speed and distance, and the
profit-and-loss family. Six shapes that cover most of the quantitative section.

### Reasoning
Series, blood relations, seating arrangements, syllogisms, coding-decoding and
the direction problems. Less arithmetic, more careful reading.

### Against the clock
Data interpretation, and then the part that actually decides your score:
deciding which questions to skip, in what order to attempt, and how to manage
sixty questions in sixty minutes.
::

The third chapter is the one that changes scores the most, and it is the one
nobody practises.
