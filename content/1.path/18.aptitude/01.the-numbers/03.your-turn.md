Two shapes learned. This is the first time you sit them together under a clock,
which is a different skill from solving them, and the gap between the two is
what the written round is actually measuring.

## The rules

::steps
### Twenty minutes
A timer, visible. Not "about twenty minutes".

### Paper and pen
No calculator. Write the working down, every time. The single most common cause
of a wrong answer in practice is correct method plus a slipped digit, and the
digit slips when you try to do it in your head.

### Attempt in order, but skip freely
If a question has not resolved into a shape within fifteen seconds, mark it and
move on. Come back. This is a scoring strategy, not an admission of anything,
and it is covered properly in the last chapter.
::

## The twenty

1. A department had 250 students and now has 200. Percentage decrease?
2. Applications rose 20% then fell 20%. Net change?
3. Seats split 7:5:3 across three programmes, 450 seats total. Seats each?
4. 65% of 480 applicants cleared the written round. How many?
5. A fee of ₹40,000 rises 12% then 15%. Final fee?
6. Attendance goes from 72% to 81%. Percentage rise?
7. Two clerks take 9 and 18 hours. Together?
8. A tank fills in 6 hours, leaks empty in 9. Time to fill?
9. Trains 300 km apart approach at 70 and 80 km/h. Time to meet?
10. Cycle out at 12 km/h, walk back at 4 km/h. Average speed?
11. Students to faculty 25:2. 48 faculty. Students?
12. Of 600 applicants, 45% are shortlisted, 60% of those accepted. Accepted?
13. A 180m train passes a pole in 9 seconds. Speed in km/h?
14. Three staff take 12, 18 and 36 hours. Together?
15. A price falls 25%. By what percentage must it rise to return to the original?
16. Two buses leave in the same direction at 50 and 65 km/h. Gap after 4 hours?
17. 30% of students take Maths, 45% take Physics, 15% take both. What percentage
    take neither?
18. A 200m train passes a 300m platform in 25 seconds. Speed in km/h?
19. Ravi does a job in 8 days, works 3 days, leaves. Meera finishes the rest in 5
    days. How long would Meera take alone?
20. The ratio of boys to girls is 9:7. There are 96 more boys than girls. Total
    students?

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="Answers 1 to 10" icon="i-lucide-check"}
  **1.** `50 ÷ 250 = 20%` decrease.

  **2.** `1.2 × 0.8 = 0.96` → **down 4%**. Never zero.

  **3.** Parts total 15, multiplier `450 ÷ 15 = 30` → **210, 150, 90**.

  **4.** `480 × 0.65 = 312`.

  **5.** `1.12 × 1.15 = 1.288` → `40,000 × 1.288 = ₹51,520`.

  **6.** Nine percentage points, which is `9 ÷ 72 = 12.5%`. If you answered 9%,
  reread the percentages lesson, because this distinction appears in real
  reports weekly.

  **7.** `1/9 + 1/18 = 2/18 + 1/18 = 3/18 = 1/6` → **6 hours**. Under 9, passes
  the check.

  **8.** `1/6 − 1/9 = 3/18 − 2/18 = 1/18` → **18 hours**.

  **9.** Closing at 150 km/h. `300 ÷ 150 = 2 hours`.

  **10.** `2 × 12 × 4 ÷ 16 = 96/16 = 6 km/h`. Not 8.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Answers 11 to 20" icon="i-lucide-check"}
  **11.** Multiplier `48 ÷ 2 = 24` → `25 × 24 = 600` students.

  **12.** `600 × 0.45 × 0.60 = 162`.

  **13.** A pole, so the train travels its own length: `180 ÷ 9 = 20 m/s`, then
  `× 18/5 = 72 km/h`.

  **14.** `1/12 + 1/18 + 1/36 = 3/36 + 2/36 + 1/36 = 6/36 = 1/6` → **6 hours**.

  **15.** Falls to 0.75. To get back to 1: `1 ÷ 0.75 = 1.333`, so a rise of
  **33.33%**. Not 25%, and this asymmetry is the whole point of the direction
  trap.

  **16.** Same direction, so subtract: `15 km/h × 4 = 60 km`.

  **17.** `30 + 45 − 15 = 60` take at least one, so **40% take neither**. This is
  the inclusion-exclusion shape and it appears in every paper. Subtract the
  overlap once, because it was counted twice.

  **18.** `200 + 300 = 500m` in 25 seconds = `20 m/s` = **72 km/h**.

  **19.** Ravi does `3/8` in 3 days, leaving `5/8`. Meera does `5/8` in 5 days,
  so `1/8` per day, so **8 days** alone.

  **20.** The difference in the ratio is `9 − 7 = 2` parts, and 2 parts = 96, so
  one part is 48. Total is `16 × 48 = 768`. Working with the **difference** as a
  ratio part is the trick, and it converts a hard-looking question into one
  division.
  :::
::

## Scoring yourself honestly

| Score | What it means | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 17 to 20 | These two shapes are solid | Move on. Come back weekly to stay fast |
| 12 to 16 | Method is there, speed is not | Redo the ones you missed, then resit in three days |
| 8 to 11 | One shape is weak | Find which. It is almost always rates or the direction trap |
| Under 8 | Reread both lessons | This is normal for a first attempt and says nothing about you |

::note
My own first honest attempt at a set like this, in 2018, was 9 out of 20, and I
had a degree. The gap was never intelligence. It was that nobody had ever told
me these were a small number of repeating shapes, so I had been treating every
question as a fresh problem.
::

## The mistakes worth naming

Go back through your wrong answers and put each in one of these three boxes.
The box tells you what to fix, and it is almost never "do more questions".

::feature-list{columns="3"}
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-shapes" title="Did not see the shape"}
  You did not recognise it as a rates question or a ratio question. Fix by
  rereading the lesson, not by practising more.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-calculator" title="Right method, slipped digit"}
  The commonest by far. Fix by writing every step down, including the ones that
  feel too easy to write.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-eye-off" title="Answered a different question"}
  Percentage points for percentage, increase for decrease, one part for the
  total. Fix by reading the final sentence twice before choosing.
  :::
::
