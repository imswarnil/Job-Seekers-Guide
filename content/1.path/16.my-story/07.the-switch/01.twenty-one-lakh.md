He was on **21 LPA**. I was on about **5**. We had started in the same place.

**2022. Bangalore.**

## The conversation

He had been a job seeker with me. Same city, same kind of queues, same kind of
nothing at the start. We had both got in, and both done our years.

Then we met, and talked about work, and at some point he told me his number.

::pull-quote
Twenty-one lakh. I was on five. Same start, same walk-ins, and he had moved and I
had not.
::

He had switched companies. That was the whole difference. Not a better degree,
not a better college, not some talent I did not have. He had interviewed outside
while I had stayed comfortable inside.

::story-media{kind="image" label="2022: the place where we met, or a photo of the two of us"}
An ordinary evening that changed the next four years.
::

## What I felt

I remember the feeling exactly, and it was not envy. I was happy for him. It was
a question, pointed at myself: **what am I doing?**

I had been telling myself a story for three years. Every year the appraisal came,
the salary went up a little, and I was grateful, because I remembered ₹13,000. I
had measured my progress against where I started instead of against what I was
worth now.

::compare{caption="The same salary, measured two ways."}
  :::compare-side{label="How I was measuring" verdict="wrong"}
  Against ₹13,000 a month in 2019. By that measure, 5 LPA was a success.
  :::
  :::compare-side{label="How the market measures" verdict="right"}
  Against what a Salesforce developer with three years of experience is paid
  elsewhere. By that measure, I was far behind.
  :::
::

## Why staying costs so much

Here is the arithmetic nobody explained to me, and I want to explain it to you.

Inside one company, a raise is usually a percentage of what you already earn. A
good year might mean ten or fifteen percent. If you start low, ten percent of low
is still low, and it compounds slowly.

When you switch, the new company does not care much what you earned. It cares
what your skills cost in the market today. That is why switches in Indian IT
often come with large jumps, and why the people who switch early tend to pull
away from the people who stay.

::note
**Appraisal** is the yearly review where your company decides your rating and
your raise. **Hike** is Bangalore's word for the raise, usually quoted as a
percentage: "a 40% hike on switching".
::

::warning
This is not an argument for switching every year. Too many short stints make a
CV look unreliable, and some roles are worth staying in for what you learn. It is
an argument for knowing your market value every year, by interviewing, even if
you stay.
::

## The decision

I decided that night, or close to it, that I would switch.

Not because I was unhappy at Accenture. I was not. I had friends, I knew the work,
and it had given me Salesforce. I decided because I finally saw that
comfortable and fairly paid are different things, and I had been confusing them
for three years.

The hard part was what came next. I would have to prepare for interviews again,
properly, as I had in the PG. But this time with a full-time job, and without the
fear that had pushed me in 2018.

## What this taught me

- Measure your salary against the market, not against where you started. Interview
  at least once a year, even if you plan to stay, to learn what you are worth.
- Switching is often where the large jumps are. [The interview](/interview) track
  covers negotiation and reading an offer.
