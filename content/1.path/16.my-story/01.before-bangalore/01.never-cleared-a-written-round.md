The interview track ended with you employable, and that is where I stop giving
you instructions for a while. Here is how I got there myself, from the start,
because it was not a straight line and I think you should see the bends.

**2013–2018. Mahroni, then Kota, then Bhopal.**

## Always seventy percent

I grew up in Mahroni, a small town in Uttar Pradesh. No engineer in the family,
and no plan for one.

I was an average student. Not the kind who fails and has a dramatic story about
it. The kind who gets seventy percent in everything, every year, and is therefore
invisible. Seventy in class ten. Seventy in class twelve. PCM (physics,
chemistry and maths), because that is what you take.

Then Kota, because clearing IIT or an NIT was the thing you were supposed to want.
I went. I tried. I did not clear it.

::story-media{kind="image" label="Kota, 2013: the coaching centre and the hostel corridor"}
The streets where two lakh students a year try for a few thousand seats.
::

Nobody told me that Kota is a filter, not a school. It is built so that most
people do not get through. I did not know that at seventeen. I thought I had
found out something about myself.

## The branch I picked

In 2014 I took admission at LNCT Bhopal, in computer science and engineering
(CSE).

I want to be exact about why, because it matters more than anything else in this
chapter. It was not passion. I had never written a line of code. My brother and
my guardians told me that in CSE getting a job is easy and the market is good.
That one sentence was the whole basis of a decision that shaped the next ten
years of my life.

::note
I am not saying that was wrong. It turned out fine. I am saying that this is how
most people actually choose a branch, and every syllabus is written as if they
chose it out of love. The gap between those two is where four years can go.
::

## Four years of pretending

I did not study well. I will not dress that up. I attended, I passed, and I did
not enjoy a single subject.

Operating systems. DBMS (database management systems). Java. Data structures.
Computer networks. The exact subjects I now know are the whole game in a
Bangalore interview. I sat through all of them, understood very little, and
nobody in that room ever connected one of them to a job I might one day do.

Passing and understanding are different things, and the marksheet does not have
a column for the difference.

## Rejected in round one

Placements came in the final year. I was not really interested in a job by then
(the next chapter is about what I was interested in), but I tried anyway,
because everybody tries.

Every company that came started with a **written round**: an online or paper test
before any human talks to you. Quantitative aptitude, which is arithmetic under a
clock. Logical reasoning. Verbal English. Sometimes a few Java or C questions.

I did not clear it. Not once in four years. Not "close, but not selected". I
could not get past the first filter.

The one exception was Tech Mahindra. I got through the test, then through the
next round, and reached HR. And I was rejected there.

::pull-quote
I remember exactly how that felt, and the word for it was loser. Not
disappointed. Not unlucky. Loser.
::

I want to tell you now what I only understood years later, because it would have
saved me a lot of nights. The written round was never measuring whether I could
be a software engineer. It was measuring whether I had practised reading a
passage and solving a percentage problem against a clock. I had not. Nobody had
told me that was a thing you practise, the way you practise anything else.

::real-life{title="What the written round actually tests" source="Every campus drive I sat, 2017–2018"}
Around 60 questions in around 60 minutes, often with negative marking.
Most of the questions are not hard. You fail them because you have never
done 60 of them in a row with a timer running, so you spend four minutes on
question seven and never see question forty.
::

College ended in 2018. I had a degree in computer science, no job, no offer, and
a marksheet that said I had passed subjects I could not explain.

## What this taught me

- The written round is a gate, not a verdict on you. Gates are passed by
  practising the gate: start with [Quantitative aptitude](/quantitative-aptitude)
  and [Verbal ability](/verbal-ability), timed, every day.
- If you passed CS subjects without understanding them, you are not behind
  forever. You are exactly where I was. [Java](/java) starts from zero on purpose.
