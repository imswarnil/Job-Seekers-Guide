The neighbourhood everybody pointed me at was **BTM Layout**. I stayed there for
the whole search, and I would send you there too.

**2018. BTM Layout, Bangalore.**

## Why BTM

BTM Layout is in south Bangalore, and in 2018 it was where job seekers lived. Not
by accident. The training institutes were there or a short bus ride away, the
walk-ins in the tech parks of south and east Bangalore were reachable, and the
rooms were priced for people with no salary yet.

::story-media{kind="image" label="BTM Layout, 2018: the street outside the PG"}
The main road, and an institute's board on what felt like every building.
::

When you walk through BTM you can tell who lives there by what is on the walls:
posters for Java classes, testing courses, "100% placement assistance" (ignore
that line wherever you see it), and signs for PGs on every other gate.

## The PG

A **PG** (paying guest) is a building of shared rooms rented by the bed, usually
with food included. You pay one monthly amount, you get a bed, a cupboard, two or
three meals a day and a shared bathroom. No lease, no deposit worth worrying
about, and you can leave with a month's notice.

::story-media{kind="image" label="The PG room: the bed, the shared cupboard, the books on the floor"}
A shared room, and a floor that was also my desk.
::

I lived in a shared room. It was not comfortable and it was not meant to be. It
was a bed near the institutes, with food I did not have to cook, at a price that
fitted inside my sister's ₹8,000 with something left for bus fare and printing
CVs.

::note
Prices change every year, and I will not pretend to remember what I paid to the
rupee. The [Moving to Bangalore](/bangalore) track has today's ranges for PGs in
BTM and nearby areas, with what to check before you pay anybody anything.
::

## Living on ₹8,000

Money shaped every day in that building. Here is roughly where it went:

::feature-list{columns="2"}
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-bed" title="The PG"}
  The biggest share by far, and it included most of my food.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-bus" title="Buses"}
  To the institute, and to walk-ins across the city. Walk-ins in Whitefield or
  Electronic City were a real cost in both money and hours.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-printer" title="Printing"}
  CVs, a dozen at a time, redone every time I changed a line.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-coffee" title="Everything else"}
  Tea, a phone recharge, and very little else.
  :::
::

I did not go out. I did not buy anything I did not need. Every rupee was my
sister's, and I knew it every time I spent one.

## The people in the building

Here is the part nobody tells you about a PG, and it was worth more than the
room.

Everybody in that building was doing what I was doing. Freshers from small
towns all over the country, all with degrees they
were learning to use. At night the conversation was always the same: which
company has a walk-in this week, what did they ask, who got through, which
institute is actually good.

::pull-quote
I learned more about how Bangalore hires from the people in that PG than from any
website.
::

When somebody got selected, the whole floor knew by evening. It was the best
news and the worst news at once: proof that it could be done, and proof that it
had not been done by you. I felt both, many times, before it was ever my turn.

::real-life{title="The PG network" source="BTM Layout, 2018"}
Many of the walk-ins I attended, I heard about from somebody in the building
before I saw them anywhere online, and often somebody from the building came with
me. Going to a walk-in with a friend is easier; you are not the only person in
the queue who is terrified.
::

## What this taught me

- Live where the job seekers live. The room matters less than the people in the
  building, and what they know about this week's walk-ins.
- Keep the money simple and small. Every rupee of runway is a week longer you can
  keep looking. The [Moving to Bangalore](/bangalore) track has the budget.
