The company was **Education First (EF)**, and it was in Europe.

**After three years and more at Twilio. Bangalore, then Europe.**

## The call

Education First, which most people know as **EF**, is an international education
company: language courses, study abroad, educational travel. It was looking for someone who understood Salesforce, analytics and the
go-to-market side of a company.

That was, almost word for word, the list of things I had spent the last several
years learning. Salesforce from Accenture. Analytics and GTM engineering from
Twilio. SQL from a classroom in BTM Layout.

::story-media{kind="image" label="The offer from Education First, or the first day at EF in Europe"}
The job that took a boy from Mahroni to Europe, and every skill it asked for learned in Bangalore.
::

I said yes. I went through the interviews, and I cracked them, using the same
method I had used since 2019: one system I knew end to end, one decision I could
defend, and ninety seconds about myself said out loud until it was mine.

## The visa

EF **sponsored my visa**.

::note
A **sponsored visa** means the employer applies for your right to live and work in
their country, and takes responsibility for it. For somebody from India, it is
usually the only realistic legal route to a job in Europe, and a company only
does it when it wants a specific person enough to go through the paperwork. It is
a strong signal that your skills are worth something to them.
::

I want to say this to anybody who thinks working abroad is only for people from
the famous colleges. I went to LNCT Bhopal. I did not clear a single written round
there. I started at ₹13,000 a month with a bond. What EF sponsored was not a
degree or a college name. It was years of skills built one small step at a time.

::pull-quote
Nobody sponsored my college. They sponsored what I had learned after it.
::

## The move

Then I moved. Packing, paperwork, goodbyes. Another long journey with a bag, very
different from the train from Mahroni, and in one way exactly the same: going
somewhere I knew nobody, to start again.

::story-media{kind="video" label="Leaving for Europe: the airport, or the first days in the new city"}
The second big journey of this story, filmed for the same reason as the first.
::

I thought about the train a great deal on that journey. The train window, my
sister's ₹8,000, the PG in BTM. Everything I was carrying this time had been built
in that city.

## What changed, and what did not

Almost everything around me changed: the country, the language on the street, the
weather, the food, the way meetings run.

What did not change was the work underneath. Salesforce, data, queries, being
able to explain what I had built and why. And the habits: a notebook, learning one
thing at a time, saying my answers out loud before I needed them.

## What this taught me

- Skills travel further than college names. The route abroad starts with the same
  foundations: [Java](/java), [SQL](/sql), and being able to explain your work in
  [the interview](/interview).
- Every stage is built on the one before it. Nothing here would have been possible
  without the ₹13,000 job.
