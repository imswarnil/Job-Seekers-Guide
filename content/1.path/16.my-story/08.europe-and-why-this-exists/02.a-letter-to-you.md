This is the last chapter, and it is the reason for all the others. The promise
was simple: if I got through, the record would become a path.

**Now. Europe, writing back to BTM Layout.**

## Why this exists

In 2018, a few weeks after arriving in Bangalore, I sat in a PG in BTM Layout
knowing that I knew nothing. I had a degree I could not use, a sister sending me
more than half her salary, and no one to tell me what to learn or in what order.

That was when I decided to document everything. Not for me. For whoever came next.

::story-media{kind="image" label="The notebooks from 2018–2019, stacked, the record this guide came from"}
Every question, every rejection and every fix, written down in a PG because I promised someone I had not met yet.
::

This guide is that record, rebuilt so that you do not have to lose the year I
lost. It is the order I learned things in, corrected by hindsight: where to live,
how to choose an institute, what to study first, how to clear the written round,
what they ask in the interview, and how to read the offer.

I was not talented. I was not passionate about software. I did not clear a single
written round in college. What I eventually got was a **sequence**: somebody
telling me what to learn, in what order, and how to talk about it. I paid for it
with money we did not have, and with a year of trial and error.

::pull-quote
That is what this guide is. The sequence, given away.
::

## A letter to you

If you are reading this because you have lost faith, this part is for you.

Maybe you have failed every written round, like I did. Maybe you are on walk-in
number twelve, or twenty, or thirty, and you have started to believe the problem
is you. Maybe there is a safe plan waiting for you at home, and it looks better
every week. Maybe someone at your kitchen table has already decided what you
should be.

::callout{icon="i-lucide-heart" color="neutral"}
I have been there. Not near it. There. Rejected in round one for four years. A
masked channel I could not talk about. A father who wanted me to be a teacher.
Thirty-three walk-ins and thirty-three bus rides home. ₹13,000 a month and a bond.
Three years of standing still. Nine months in a job I had to leave.
::

Here is what I know now that I did not know then.

::feature-list{columns="1"}
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-check" title="Rejection is information, not a verdict"}
  Every one of my thirty-three walk-ins told me exactly what to fix next. I did not
  hear it until I started writing it down.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-check" title="Unskilled is not the same as not good enough"}
  One is permanent. The other is a list, and lists get shorter.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-check" title="The first job is a door, not the room"}
  Take the small offer if it gets you in. Then keep studying on your day off.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-check" title="Nobody sees the middle"}
  People post the offer letter. Nobody posts month five. If you are in the middle,
  you are exactly where everybody who got through once was.
  :::
::

None of this is hard. It is a lot of small things done in the right order, for
longer than feels fair. That is all it was for me, and I started further back than
you probably have.

## What I am asking of you

Three things, and they are small.

::steps
### Start today, not on Monday
Open the first chapter of the guide. Read one lesson. That is the whole of today.

### Keep a notebook
Every interview, every question, every answer you should have given. It will be
your best study material within a month.

### Tell one person
Find the one person who backs you, the way my sister backed me, and tell them what
you are doing. Then make their faith in you worth it.
::

And when you get through, and you will if you keep going, write it down for the
next person. That is how this was always meant to work.

Swarnil

## What this taught me
