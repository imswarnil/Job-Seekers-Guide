I had come to Bangalore to be a web developer. My first job had been web
development. Accenture put me in **Salesforce**.

**2019–2022. Accenture, Bangalore.**

## The allocation

In a large service company, freshers are not asked what they would like to do.
The company looks at what its clients need in the next few months, and it
allocates the batch into **streams** to match: this many to Java, this many to
testing, this many to Salesforce, this many to whatever else is in demand.

I was allocated to Salesforce.

::note
**Salesforce** is a company and a platform: software that businesses use to
manage their customers, sales and service, which companies then customise heavily
for themselves. A Salesforce developer builds and changes those customisations,
using the platform's own tools and its programming language, Apex, which looks a
lot like Java.
::

I had never heard of it in any real sense. I had spent a year learning Java and
web technologies, and doing web development at the startup. I wanted to keep
going with that.

::story-media{kind="image" label="The Salesforce training, 2019: the first screen I saw of the platform"}
The moment the plan I had come to Bangalore with took a turn I had not chosen.
::

## How corporate works

This is the lesson of this chapter, and nobody told it to me in advance.

::pull-quote
In a large company you might get what you want, if you fight for it. If you do
not ask, you get what the company needs.
::

I did not fight hard for web development. I was new, I was grateful to be there,
and I did not know that asking was allowed. So I got what the company needed,
which was Salesforce.

Looking back, I think it is worth telling you what fighting for it would have
looked like, because the choice exists whether or not anybody mentions it:

::steps
### Say it early, and say it clearly
Tell your trainer and your manager in the first weeks what you want to work on,
and why. Not once in passing. On the record.

### Show it
Have something that proves it: a project, a certification, your own work in that
area. "I want to do web" is a wish. "Here is what I have built" is a case.

### Ask again at every change
New project, new manager, new appraisal: each is a chance to ask again. Stream
decisions are not permanent, but they do not reverse themselves.
::

## It turned out well

I want to be honest about how this ended, because it matters for you.

Salesforce turned out to be one of the best things that happened to my career.
Every later step in this story (the switch, Twilio, Europe) was built on it.
Companies all over the world run on Salesforce, and people who understand it
properly are in demand.

And the Java I had learned at JSpiders did not go to waste. Apex is close enough
to Java that everything Shishir Bhatt sir taught me carried straight over. The
SQL did too: Salesforce has its own query language, and it is how S. N. Dixit sir
taught me to think about a query that made it easy.

::tip
The foundations transfer. Whatever stream you are placed in, Java, SQL and the CS
subjects underneath are what every one of them is built on. That is why this
guide teaches them first and properly.
::

## Three years on projects

I spent about **three years** at Accenture, trained in Salesforce and put on
client projects. I learned how real projects run: requirements, estimates,
deployments, clients who change their minds, production issues at bad hours.

::story-media{kind="image" label="Accenture, 2020–2022: the desk, the project screens, or the work-from-home setup"}
Three years of real projects, and the place most of my working habits were formed.
::

And the salary moved slowly. By the end of three years I was on about **5 LPA**.
From ₹13,000 a month that felt like progress, and for a long time I did not
question it. I was comfortable. That was the problem, and I did not see it until
somebody showed me.

## What this taught me

- In a large company, say what you want early, on the record, and show evidence
  for it. If you do not ask, you get what the company needs.
- Whatever stream you land in, it sits on the same foundations. Learn
  [Java](/java) and [SQL](/sql) properly and they carry across.
