Sunday was the one day that was mine. I spent it the same way I had spent every
day in the PG: studying, and looking for the next interview.

**2019. Bangalore, Sundays.**

## Two feelings at once

I was happy. I had a job. I was paying my own rent. My sister could keep her
salary. After thirty-three walk-ins, I was finally on the other side of the
queue.

And I was not satisfied. Both of those were true at the same time, and I think
that is the most useful state a fresher can be in.

::compare{caption="Two feelings that sound like opposites, and are not."}
  :::compare-side{label="Happy" verdict="right"}
  I have a job, a salary and a line on my CV that says "experience". I am
  grateful for it.
  :::
  :::compare-side{label="Not satisfied" verdict="right"}
  1.8 LPA, a bond, six days, no training. This is a start, not where I stop.
  :::
::

The danger in a first job is letting "happy" win completely. You relax, the
months go by, and one year of experience becomes three years of the same year.

## The Sunday routine

So Sundays were not rest days. I kept the habits from the PG:

::steps
### Study
The CS subjects and Java topics I still felt weak on, and anything the week's
work had shown me I did not understand.

### Interviews
Walk-ins and interviews wherever I could get one without losing a working day.
Now I was going in as somebody with a job, which changes how you sit in the
chair.

### The notebook
Every question, every answer I should have given, same as before. The notebook
did not stop because I had an offer.
::

::story-media{kind="image" label="A Sunday in 2019: notebook open, laptop, the week's list of walk-ins"}
Six days for the company, and the seventh for the next step.
::

::pull-quote
The hardest interviews I ever gave were the ones after a six-day week. They were
also the ones that got me out.
::

## Interviewing with a job

Something changed in the interviews once I was employed, and it was not my Java.

I was no longer desperate. If a company rejected me, I still had my ₹13,000 on
Monday. That calm showed up in the room. I stopped rushing answers. I asked
questions back. I could talk about real work I was doing, with real problems in
it, rather than only the programs I had written for practice.

::tip
The best time to look for a better job is while you have one. Your answers are
calmer, your examples are real, and you can say no to a bad offer. If you are
in a first job you are not satisfied with, do not wait until you are unhappy
enough to quit. Start interviewing now.
::

## Two months

It took **two months**. Two months of six-day weeks and Sundays of study and
interviews, and then I was selected at **Accenture**.

A word about the bond, honestly. I had signed it, so leaving early meant dealing
with it. What mattered was that I had read it before signing. I knew what leaving
would cost, and that made the decision mine to make rather than a trap I
discovered.

::caution
Before you leave a job with a bond, reread the exact clause and work out what you
owe and what the company is holding, originals especially. Settle it properly and
get it in writing. Walking away from a bond without settling it follows you into
your next background check.
::

## What this taught me

- Happy with the job and not satisfied with it is the right state. Keep studying
  on your day off, and keep the notebook.
- Interview while you are employed. You are calmer, your examples are real, and
  you can walk away. [The interview](/interview) track covers how to talk about
  work you are doing now.
