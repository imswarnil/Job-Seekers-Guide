The thirty-fourth walk-in looked exactly like the other thirty-three. That is
worth knowing: the one that works does not announce itself.

**2019. A startup walk-in, Bangalore.**

## The morning

A startup was hiring a web developer. The walk-in was on the usual kind of
morning, and I did the usual things: printed CVs, took the bus, joined the queue.

The queue was enormous. Somewhere between **700 and 1000** people turned up,
depending on who you asked and when they counted. For a small number of seats.

::story-media{kind="image" label="The office building of the thirty-fourth walk-in, 2019"}
An ordinary building on an ordinary morning, and I did not know yet that it would be the one.
::

If you had told me then that I would be selected from that crowd, I would not
have believed you. Thirty-three walk-ins had taught me to expect the list to end
before my name.

## The rounds

This time, the rounds went the way my notebook had been preparing them to go.

::timeline{label="The thirty-fourth, round by round"}
  :::timeline-item{title="Written round" state="done"}
  Aptitude and a few technical questions against the clock. I recognised the
  shapes, skipped what I could not see in thirty seconds, and finished the paper.
  The list was read out, and my name was on it.
  :::
  :::timeline-item{title="Technical round" state="done"}
  Java, SQL and web questions. I answered with the example programs I had written
  myself, and when the interviewer asked "why?", I had an answer, because I had
  asked myself the same thing in my own notes.
  :::
  :::timeline-item{title="HR round" state="done"}
  "Tell me about yourself." The answer I had said out loud to the wall of the PG
  room so many times it finally sounded like me.
  :::
  :::timeline-item{title="Selected" state="current"}
  One of 700 to 1000.
  :::
::

Nothing in those rounds was new. That is the point. Every question was one I had
failed somewhere in the previous thirty-three, written down, studied and fixed.
The thirty-fourth was not a lucky day. It was the day the notebook was finally
complete enough.

::pull-quote
The thirty-fourth walk-in was not where I got lucky. It was where the other
thirty-three paid out.
::

## Hearing it

I was told I was the one selected. After thirty-three lists that ended before
my name, I did not quite trust it.

Then I told my sister.

::story-media{kind="video" label="The evening I was selected, 2019"}
Whatever I recorded that evening: the phone, the PG, and a face that had not quite believed it yet.
::

I will not dress up that moment, because it belongs to her more than to me.
She had sent me ₹8,000 of her ₹15,000 every month for this. It was her result
too.

## What I tell people about this day

People ask me what the secret was, and they are disappointed by the answer
because it is so ordinary.

::feature-list{columns="2"}
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-notebook-pen" title="The notebook"}
  Every question from thirty-three walk-ins, and a fixed answer to each.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-timer" title="The daily aptitude"}
  So the written round, the gate I had never passed, became a routine.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-code" title="My own examples"}
  So the technical round was about my programs, which I could defend.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-mic" title="Out loud, to a wall"}
  So "tell me about yourself" came out as me, not as a script.
  :::
::

There was no single breakthrough. There was a pile of small fixes, and at the
thirty-fourth walk-in the pile was finally tall enough.

## What this taught me

- The walk-in that works looks like every other one. Keep going to them; you
  cannot tell in advance which one it will be.
- Your preparation for walk-in number thirty-four is written in the rejections of
  the first thirty-three. Keep the notebook, and use [the interview](/interview)
  track to practise the rounds out loud.
