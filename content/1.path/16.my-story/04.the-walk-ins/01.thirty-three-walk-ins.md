Thirty-three. I kept count, because counting was the only way to make it feel
like progress rather than a loop.

**2018–2019. Walk-ins across Bangalore.**

## What a walk-in is

A **walk-in** is a hiring drive with no appointment. A company, or a consultancy
hiring on its behalf, posts a date, a time and an address. Anybody who fits the
criteria turns up with a CV. Everyone sits the same rounds on the same day, and
most people go home before lunch.

The numbers are brutal and nobody hides them. Hundreds of people turn up for a
handful of seats. The written round removes most of them in an hour. The
technical round removes most of the rest. That is not cruelty; it is the only way
a company can see that many people in one day.

## What each one felt like

The mornings were the same every time. Iron the shirt. Print the CVs. Take the bus
across the city, sometimes more than an hour each way. Stand in a queue that
went round the building. Look at everybody else in it and wonder what they knew
that I did not.

Then the written round, and then the wait while they read out the list of people
who had cleared it.

Early on, my name was not on the list. Later it was, and I would go into the
technical round and fail on something specific: a question on a topic I had
skipped, a query I wrote wrongly, an explanation of polymorphism that fell apart
when the interviewer asked "why?".

::pull-quote
Every rejection had a reason. For a long time I heard only the rejection.
::

The bus ride home was the worst part. Same bus, same city, same PG waiting, and
the whole floor asking "how did it go?". I learned to say "not this one" without
explaining, and to go and sit with my notebook.

## What changed between them

Here is the thing I most want you to take from this chapter. The walk-ins did
not get easier because I got lucky. They got further because after every one, I
wrote down exactly where it had ended and why, and fixed that thing before the
next one.

::compare{caption="The same walk-ins, read two different ways."}
  :::compare-side{label="What I did at first" verdict="wrong"}
  Go home, feel terrible, and go to the next one having changed nothing.
  :::
  :::compare-side{label="What I learned to do" verdict="right"}
  Go home, write down every question and where I failed, study that one gap, and
  go to the next one with it closed.
  :::
::

Looking back, the thirty-three fell into stages:

::timeline{label="How the walk-ins moved"}
  :::timeline-item{title="Out at the written round" state="done"}
  The same gate as college. Fixed with the daily timed aptitude and skipping.
  :::
  :::timeline-item{title="Out at the technical round" state="done"}
  Clearing the test, then failing on a specific question. Fixed one topic at a
  time from the notebook.
  :::
  :::timeline-item{title="Out at HR" state="done"}
  Through the technical round, and then losing the room on "tell me about
  yourself". Fixed by saying my answer out loud until it was mine.
  :::
  :::timeline-item{title="Selected" state="current"}
  The thirty-fourth.
  :::
::

I do not remember exactly which walk-in moved me from one stage to the next, and
I will not invent it. What I remember is that each stage lasted a while, and each
one felt permanent while I was in it.

## The money and the doubt

Every walk-in cost money: the bus, the printing, sometimes lunch. Every month the
₹8,000 came from my sister. Every month I had not got a job was another month of
hers.

There were weeks when I seriously thought about going home to Mahroni and doing
the BTC after all. My father's plan was still there. It would have been
respectable. Nobody would have blamed me.

::callout{icon="i-lucide-heart" color="neutral"}
If you are in this part right now, the part where it has been months and the
safe plan at home looks better every week, I want you to know that I was there,
in a PG in BTM, with that exact thought. It is not a sign you should stop. It is
what the long middle of a job search feels like for almost everybody.
::

## What this taught me

- After every rejection, write down where it ended and why, and fix that one thing
  before the next walk-in. That is the whole method.
- Know which stage you are failing at. Out at the written round needs
  [Quantitative aptitude](/quantitative-aptitude); out at technical needs the
  subject; out at HR needs [the interview](/interview).
