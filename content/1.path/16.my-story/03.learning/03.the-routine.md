The institute taught Java, SQL and web. The written round tested aptitude and
English, and interviews asked about CS subjects I had passed in college without
understanding. So the rest of the day belonged to those.

**2018. BTM Layout, a PG room and a classroom.**

## Three problems, not one

By now I could see that there were three separate reasons I was not getting
selected, and the institute fixed only one of them.

::feature-list{columns="3"}
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-code" title="Coding"}
  Java, SQL, web. The institute's job. Covered in class.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-cpu" title="CS subjects"}
  OOPs, DBMS, operating systems, networks. Asked in every technical round. Mine
  to fix.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-timer" title="The written round"}
  Aptitude, reasoning, English. The gate I had never passed. Mine to fix.
  :::
::

If I had fixed only the first, I would have been a candidate who could code and
who still never got past the test that decides whether anybody sees the code.
That had been my whole college life.

## What a day looked like

I will not pretend I kept this perfectly every day. This is the shape I aimed at,
and most days I hit most of it.

::timeline{label="A weekday, 2018"}
  :::timeline-item{title="Morning" date="Before class" state="done"}
  Aptitude. A set of questions against a timer, every day, no exceptions. Then
  checking every wrong answer until I knew why it was wrong.
  :::
  :::timeline-item{title="Class" date="Most of the day" state="done"}
  JSpiders. Java, then SQL, then web, in the order the course ran.
  :::
  :::timeline-item{title="Evening" date="Back at the PG" state="done"}
  Rewrite the day's class in my own notes. Write one example program of my own.
  :::
  :::timeline-item{title="Night" date="Last hour" state="done"}
  One CS subject topic (OOPs, DBMS, OS or networks), and English: read something,
  then say out loud what it said.
  :::
::

::story-media{kind="image" label="My study corner in the PG: notebooks, the aptitude book, the phone timer"}
Where the second half of every day happened, after the classroom emptied.
::

## Aptitude, finally

For four years I had treated aptitude as something you either had or did not.
In Bangalore I treated it as a skill with a small number of question shapes:
percentages, ratios, time and work, speed and distance, averages, series,
arrangements. Learn each shape once, then do dozens of them until the method is
automatic, then do them against a clock.

The clock was the part I had never practised. In college I would spend four
minutes on one question to prove I could solve it, and never reach the end of
the paper. Now I practised skipping: if I could not see the method in about
thirty seconds, move on, and come back if there was time.

::pull-quote
I did not get smarter at aptitude. I got faster at recognising which of ten
shapes the question was, and braver about skipping.
::

## English

My English was the other gate. Reading comprehension against a clock, grammar
questions, and then in interviews, speaking in full sentences to a stranger who
was judging me.

I did three things: read something every day and summarised it out loud; kept a
list of every word or phrase I did not know; and practised my interview answers
out loud, to the wall of the PG room, until they came out without stopping. It
felt foolish. It worked better than anything silent ever did.

## The CS subjects, again

OOPs, DBMS, operating systems, computer networks. I had passed all of them in
college. This time I studied them as interview topics: for each one, what are
the ten questions a fresher is actually asked, and can I answer each in two
minutes, with an example? That is a much smaller target than a university
syllabus, and it is the one that matters in Bangalore.

::tip
If you can only add one thing to your routine, add the timed aptitude set every
morning. It is the round that removes most people before a human reads their CV,
and it is the one most people practise least.
::

## What this taught me

- Coding, CS subjects and the written round are three separate gates. Work on all
  three every day, not one after another.
- Practise aptitude against a clock and practise skipping. Start with
  [Quantitative aptitude](/quantitative-aptitude), then [Verbal ability](/verbal-ability),
  and take the CS subjects from [DBMS](/dbms) and [Operating systems](/operating-systems).
