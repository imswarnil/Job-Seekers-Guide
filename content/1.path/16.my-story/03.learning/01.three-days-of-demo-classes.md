The way to tell the institutes apart was not to read their posters. It was to
sit in their classrooms before paying them anything.

**2018. BTM Layout and around it.**

## Every institute says it is the best

If you walk down the main roads around BTM, Marathahalli or Rajajinagar, you
will pass more training institutes than you can count. Every one has a board.
Every board says "placement assistance". Some say "100% placement", which is a
lie wherever you see it; no institute can promise that a company will hire you.

I had one chance at this. My sister's money was going to pay for one course, not
two. If I picked the wrong institute I would lose three months and a fee we could
not afford to lose.

::story-media{kind="image" label="The institute boards on the main road, BTM Layout, 2018"}
A wall of promises, and no way to tell from the outside which ones were true.
::

## The demo class

Most institutes in Bangalore offer a **demo class**: free sessions you can attend
before enrolling, to see how the trainer teaches. Many will let you sit for a few
days. I used that properly. I sat **three days of demo classes** at each
institute I was considering, before paying anybody a rupee.

Three days is enough to learn things no counsellor will tell you:

::steps
### Day one: can this trainer explain it?
Not "does the trainer know Java". They all know Java. Can they explain it to
somebody who does not? Do they draw it, or read it off slides?

### Day two: does it stay with me?
The test I cared about most. Could I remember and explain yesterday's lesson the
next morning, without looking? With self-study I never could.

### Day three: what are the other students like?
Are people asking questions, or sitting silent? Are the seniors who got placed
coming back to talk? Is anybody from my PG studying here, and what do they say
when the trainer is not listening?
::

## Choosing JSpiders

I chose **JSpiders**. Not because of its board or its brochure. Because in the
demo classes, the teaching went in. I could follow the Java trainer from one
idea to the next, and I could explain the previous day's class the next morning,
which I had not been able to do with a single video in all my weeks of
self-study.

::pull-quote
The first time a lesson stayed with me overnight, I knew I had found the right
room.
::

The other things that mattered:

- **The order was decided for me.** Core Java first, then SQL, then web
  technologies. I did not have to guess what came next, which was the exact
  thing that had broken my self-study.
- **Other job seekers were there.** Hundreds of us, many from my own area, all
  preparing for the same walk-ins.
- **It was in reach.** I could get there from the PG every day without losing
  half the morning on a bus.

::warning
I am telling you where I went and why, not recommending that you go there.
Trainers move between institutes, and the institute I chose in 2018 is not the
institute you would join today. Do the three days yourself, at more than one
place, and choose on what you see in the room.
::

## What I paid for

The fee, as I remember it, was about ₹40,000, and it was money we did not have.
Check today's fee rather than trusting my number.

I want to be exact about what that money actually bought, because "go to an
institute" is not the lesson. Every subject they taught was free on the internet
the whole time. What I paid for was somebody having decided the **sequence**:
this first, then this, and nobody letting me skip the boring part to get to the
part that felt like progress.

That sequence is what this guide gives you for nothing. It is still worth sitting
in a real classroom if you can, for the trainer and the people. But you do not
have to pay for the order any more.

## What this taught me

- Never pay an institute before sitting its demo classes. Three days each, and
  choose the room where the lesson is still in your head the next morning.
- What you are buying is a sequence and a teacher. The sequence is free here:
  it starts with [Java](/java).
