Direction sense put people on a map. Ranking puts them in a line: a queue at a
walk-in, a row of seats, a class ranked by marks. The questions ask where
somebody is from the other end, how many are between two people, or who came
third.

## Why it matters

Ranking and order appears in TCS NQT, Wipro, Capgemini, Infosys and
AMCAT-style papers, usually one or two questions, and comparison chains show up
inside puzzles too. Check the current pattern. With the formula below, the
counting questions take 20 seconds.

## What to study

| # | Type | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Total from two positions | "12th from the top and 28th from the bottom" |
| 2 | Position from the other end | "15th from the left in a row of 40" |
| 3 | People between two positions | "10th from left and 15th from right, row of 40" |
| 4 | Interchange of positions | "A and B swap, A is now 15th" |
| 5 | Comparison chains | "A > B, C < D, B > D" |

## The formulas

```text
Total  = position from one end + position from the other end − 1
From the other end = total − position + 1

A is a-th from the left, B is b-th from the right, row of N:
  B's position from the left = N − b + 1
  If a + b ≤ N:  people between = N − a − b
  If a + b > N + 1:  (they have crossed) people between = a + b − N − 2
```

::tip
When in doubt, draw a line of dashes and mark the two people. For rows under
50 this takes 10 seconds and cannot go wrong the way a half-remembered formula
can.
::

## A worked example

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="Total from two positions" icon="i-lucide-list-ordered"}
  **Ravi is 12th from the top and 28th from the bottom of his class. How many
  students are in the class?**

  ```text
  12 + 28 − 1 = 39
  ```

  Ravi is counted in both numbers, so subtract him once. **Answer: 39.**
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="People between" icon="i-lucide-arrow-left-right"}
  **In a row of 40, A is 10th from the left and B is 15th from the right. How
  many people sit between them?**

  ```text
  B from the left = 40 − 15 + 1 = 26
  Between positions 10 and 26: 26 − 10 − 1 = 15
  ```

  **Answer: 15.** Formula check: `40 − 10 − 15 = 15`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Interchange" icon="i-lucide-repeat"}
  **A is 8th from the left and B is 12th from the right. They swap places, and
  A is now 15th from the left. How many are in the row?**

  After the swap, A is where B was. So B's old place is 15th from the left and
  12th from the right.

  ```text
  15 + 12 − 1 = 26
  ```

  **Answer: 26.**
  :::
::

## Comparison chains

"Five students, A to E. A scored more than B. C scored less than D. B scored
more than D. E scored less than C."

Write every clue as a `>` and chain them:

```text
A > B,  B > D,  D > C,  C > E
A > B > D > C > E
```

Now any question (highest, lowest, third) is read off the line. If a clue
cannot be placed yet, write it under the chain and come back to it.

## Common mistakes

- **Forgetting the − 1.** The person is counted from both ends.
- **Counting the two people themselves** as "between".
- **Crossed positions.** If `a + b` is more than the row, the two have passed
  each other. Draw it.
- **Assuming a chain is complete** when one person could sit in two places.
  If the clues allow two orders, "cannot be determined" is the answer.

## How to practise

- **Days one and two:** 12 questions a day from IndiaBix "Ranking and Order"
  (sometimes listed as "Order and Ranking"), plus the comparison questions in
  R.S. Aggarwal's reasoning book.
- **Target:** 25 seconds for counting questions, 45 for chains.

## Practice questions

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. A candidate is 7th from the front and 12th from the back of a queue. How long is the queue?"}
  **18.** `7 + 12 − 1`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. In a row of 50, Meera is 20th from the left. Her position from the right?"}
  **31st.** `50 − 20 + 1`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. In a class of 35, Arjun ranks 9th from the top. His rank from the bottom?"}
  **27th.** `35 − 9 + 1`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. In a row of 30, P is 8th from the left and Q is 10th from the right. How many between them?"}
  **12.** Q is 21st from the left; `21 − 8 − 1 = 12`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="5. In a row of 20, X is 15th from the left and Y is 10th from the right. How many between them?"}
  **3.** Y is 11th from the left, so positions 12, 13 and 14 are between.
  Formula: `15 + 10 − 20 − 2 = 3`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="6. A > B, B > D, D > C, C > E (by marks). Who is third?"}
  **D.** The chain is A, B, D, C, E.
  :::
::
