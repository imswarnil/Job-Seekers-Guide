Analogies were about the relationship between two things. Blood relations are
about the relationships inside one family, stated in the most tangled sentence
the setter can write. The fix is the habit from the overview: draw it.

## Why it matters

Blood relations appear in TCS NQT, Wipro, Infosys, Capgemini and AMCAT-style
papers, usually one or two questions, sometimes a small set of coded relations.
Check the current pattern. With a tree they take 30–40 seconds. Without one,
people read the sentence four times and still guess.

## What to study

| # | Type | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Pointing / introducing | "Pointing to a photo, Ravi said, 'She is the daughter of my grandfather's only son.'" |
| 2 | Chain statements | "P is the sister of Q. R is the mother of Q..." |
| 3 | Coded relations | "A + B means A is the father of B" |
| 4 | Family puzzles | Eight people, three generations, several clues |

## The symbols

Use the same symbols every time so you never have to think about the drawing.

```text
Male:              square, or write a +     (Ravi+)
Female:            circle, or write a −     (Meera−)
Married:           a double line  =         Ravi+ = Meera−
Siblings:          a horizontal line  ---
Parent and child:  a vertical line  |       (parents above, children below)
Gender unknown:    no sign at all
```

One generation per row. Grandparents on top, you in the middle, children
below.

## The words to be sure of

| Word | Means |
| --- | --- |
| Paternal / maternal | On the father's side / on the mother's side |
| Uncle | Father's or mother's brother, or an aunt's husband |
| Aunt | Father's or mother's sister, or an uncle's wife |
| Nephew / niece | Your brother's or sister's son / daughter |
| Cousin | Your uncle's or aunt's child |
| Son-in-law / daughter-in-law | Your daughter's husband / your son's wife |
| Brother-in-law | Your spouse's brother, or your sister's husband |
| Grandson / granddaughter | Your child's son / daughter |

::tip
Work from the end of the sentence backwards. In "the daughter of my
grandfather's only son", start at "my grandfather", step to "his only son"
(my father), then "his daughter".
::

## A worked example

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="Pointing" icon="i-lucide-pointer"}
  **Pointing to a woman, Ravi said, "She is the daughter of my grandfather's
  only son." How is she related to Ravi?**

  ```text
  Ravi's grandfather
        |
  his only son = Ravi's father
        |
  his daughter = the woman, in Ravi's generation
  ```

  **Answer: his sister.**
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Coded relations" icon="i-lucide-code"}
  **A + B means A is the father of B. A − B means A is the wife of B. A × B
  means A is the brother of B. A ÷ B means A is the daughter of B. What does
  P ÷ Q + R mean?**

  ```text
  P ÷ Q  → P is the daughter of Q
  Q + R  → Q is the father of R
  So P and R are both children of Q, and P is female.
  ```

  **Answer: P is R's sister.**
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="A chain, and the direction of the answer" icon="i-lucide-git-branch"}
  **P is the sister of Q. R is the mother of Q. S is the father of R. T is the
  mother of S. How is P related to T?**

  ```text
  T−  (top)
  |
  S+
  |
  R−
  |
  P− --- Q
  ```

  T is three generations above P. **Answer: P is T's great-granddaughter.**
  Read the question's direction carefully: "how is P related to T" asks what
  P is to T, not what T is to P (great-grandmother).
  :::
::

## Common mistakes

- **Assuming gender from a name or from nothing.** "A is the child of B" does
  not say son or daughter. If the gender is not given, the answer may be
  "cannot be determined", and that option is often correct.
- **Answering the relation backwards.** "How is P related to T" wants P's
  relationship, described from T.
- **Mixing generations on one row.** Keep the rows strict.
- **Drawing only in your head** for three or more people.

## How to practise

- **Days one to three:** 12 questions a day from IndiaBix "Blood Relation Test"
  and R.S. Aggarwal's chapter of the same name. Draw every one.
- **Target:** 40 seconds each, 60 seconds for coded relations.
- **Then** do three coded-relation sets from company papers on PrepInsta; this
  is where TCS and Infosys like to raise the difficulty.

## Practice questions

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. A man said, 'That boy's mother is the only daughter of my mother.' How is the boy related to the man?"}
  **Nephew.** The only daughter of the man's mother is his sister. Her son is
  his nephew.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. Pointing to a woman in a photograph, Kiran (a man) said, 'Her father is the only son of my father.' How is she related to Kiran?"}
  **His daughter.** The only son of Kiran's father is Kiran himself.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. X is the husband of Y. Y is the sister of Z. Z is the son of W. How is X related to W?"}
  **Son-in-law.** Y is W's daughter, and X is her husband.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. A is the brother of B. C is the mother of A. D is the father of C. How is B related to D?"}
  **Grandchild; the gender cannot be determined.** Nothing says whether B is
  male or female, so "grandson" is not safe.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="5. Using the codes above, what does M × N + O mean?"}
  **M is O's uncle.** M is N's brother, and N is O's father.
  :::
::
