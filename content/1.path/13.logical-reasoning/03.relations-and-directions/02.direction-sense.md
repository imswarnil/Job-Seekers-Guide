Blood relations needed a family tree. Direction questions need a compass and a
pencil line: somebody walks, turns, walks again, and you are asked where they
ended up.

## Why it matters

Direction sense appears in Wipro, TCS NQT, Capgemini and AMCAT-style papers,
usually one question, sometimes two. Check the current pattern. It is one of
the easiest reasoning topics to make reliable, because every question is the
same drawing.

## The rules

```text
Draw N at the top of every diagram. East is right, west is left.

Turning right (clockwise):      N → E → S → W → N
Turning left (anticlockwise):   N → W → S → E → N

The "right" and "left" of a person depend on the way they face:
  facing north: right is east
  facing south: right is west
  facing east:  right is south
  facing west:  right is north

Shortest distance:  √(horizontal² + vertical²)

Morning: the sun is in the east, so shadows fall to the west.
Evening: the sun is in the west, so shadows fall to the east.
At noon there is (almost) no shadow in these questions.
```

::tip
Keep track of which way the walker is facing after every turn, not only where
they are. Write the facing direction as a small arrow at each corner of your
drawing. That one arrow prevents most left/right mistakes.
::

## A worked example

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="Three legs" icon="i-lucide-footprints"}
  **Meera walks 6 km south, turns left and walks 4 km, then turns left again
  and walks 3 km. How far is she from the start, and in which direction?**

  ```text
  Start at (0, 0).
  6 km south              → (0, −6), facing south
  Left of south is east   → 4 km to (4, −6), facing east
  Left of east is north   → 3 km to (4, −3)
  Distance = √(4² + 3²) = √25 = 5 km
  ```

  **Answer: 5 km, towards the south-east** (she is east and south of where she
  began).
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Shadows" icon="i-lucide-sun"}
  **One morning after sunrise, Ravi and Meera stand facing each other. Ravi's
  shadow falls exactly to his right. Which direction is Ravi facing?**

  Morning, so shadows fall west. Ravi's right is west. A person whose right is
  west is facing **south**.

  **Answer: south** (and Meera faces north).
  :::
::

## Common mistakes

- **Turning relative to the page instead of the walker.** "Turn left" after
  walking south means east, not west.
- **Direction from the wrong point.** "Where is A from B" means stand at B and
  look at A.
- **Forgetting that 45° turns give north-east and so on.** A 90° clockwise turn
  from south-east faces south-west.
- **Missing a leg** in long walks. Number the legs as you draw them.

## How to practise

- **Days one and two:** 12 questions a day from IndiaBix "Direction Sense
  Test". Draw a compass for every one.
- **Target:** 30 seconds each.
- **Learn the Pythagorean triples** (3-4-5, 5-12-13, 6-8-10, 8-15-17): most
  distances are built on them.

## Practice questions

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. Facing north, I turn right, then right again, then left. Which way am I facing?"}
  **East.** North → east → south → east.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. I walk 3 km east and then 4 km north. Distance and direction from the start?"}
  **5 km, north-east.**
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. I walk 5 km west, turn right and walk 5 km, turn right and walk 5 km. Where am I from the start?"}
  **5 km north.** West, then north, then east back over the start line.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. Facing south-east, I turn 90° anticlockwise. Which way am I facing?"}
  **North-east.**
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="5. In the evening a man's shadow falls to his left. Which way is he facing?"}
  **South.** Evening shadows fall east, so his left is east.
  :::
::
