Circular seating put people round a table. Puzzles put them against a scale
that has an order (floors, days, positions in a stack) and usually give each
person a second attribute as well. The method is the same: a frame first, the
fixed clue first, branch when forced.

## Why it matters

Puzzles are the longest sets in most reasoning sections. Infosys, Accenture,
Cognizant, Capgemini and bank-style papers use floor and scheduling puzzles
heavily, often as a set of three to five questions. TCS NQT and AMCAT-style
tests use shorter ones. Check the current pattern.

They are also where a reasoning section is won or lost on time. A puzzle you can
solve is worth several marks. A puzzle you cannot resolve in about four minutes
is worth nothing, and the discipline to leave it is as important as the method.

## What to study

| # | Type | The scale | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Floor puzzle | Floors 1 (bottom) to 5, 7 or 8 | High |
| 2 | Scheduling | Days of the week, months, time slots | High |
| 3 | Box / stack | Positions from bottom to top | Medium |
| 4 | Two attributes on one scale | Person + subject, person + city | High |
| 5 | Comparison puzzles (heights, marks, salaries) | Tallest to shortest | Medium |

## The grid method

::steps
### Draw the scale as rows
Floors top to bottom on the page, highest at the top, so the drawing looks like
the building. Days in order, Monday first.

### One column per attribute
`Floor | Person | Subject`. Leave blanks, not guesses.

### Place the fixed clues first
"Chetan lives on the top floor." "The test is on Wednesday."

### Then the tight links
"Immediately above", "the day after". These attach two things so they move
together as one block.

### Negative clues last
"Divya does not study SQL" removes one option. Use negatives once most of the
grid is filled, when removing one option leaves only one.
::

```text
Words that decide the answer, and what they mean:
  "above"              any floor higher
  "immediately above"  exactly one floor higher
  "between"            somewhere in between, not necessarily adjacent
  "odd-numbered floor" 1, 3, 5, 7 (floor 1 is odd too)
  "after Tuesday"      Wednesday or later, not only Wednesday
```

## A worked example

**Five students (Anil, Bina, Chetan, Divya, Esha) live on floors 1 to 5 of a PG,
one per floor, with floor 1 at the bottom. Each studies a different subject:
Java, SQL, HTML, Python or Testing.**

1. Anil lives on an odd-numbered floor, but not floor 1.
2. The SQL student lives immediately above Anil.
3. Chetan lives on the top floor.
4. Bina lives immediately below Anil.
5. Divya does not study SQL.
6. The Testing student lives on the lowest floor.
7. The Python student lives on a higher floor than the SQL student.
8. Bina does not study Java.

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="The solve" icon="i-lucide-building"}
  ```text
  Clue 1: Anil on 3 or 5
  Clue 2: someone must live above Anil → Anil on 3, SQL on 4
  Clue 3: Chetan on 5
  Clue 4: Bina on 2
  Floors 1 and 4 left for Divya and Esha.
  Clue 5: Divya not SQL, and floor 4 is SQL → Divya on 1, Esha on 4
  Clue 6: floor 1 is Testing → Divya studies Testing
  Clue 7: Python above floor 4 → floor 5, Chetan studies Python
  Java and HTML left for Anil and Bina.
  Clue 8: Bina not Java → Bina HTML, Anil Java
  ```

  | Floor | Student | Subject |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | 5 | Chetan | Python |
  | 4 | Esha | SQL |
  | 3 | Anil | Java |
  | 2 | Bina | HTML |
  | 1 | Divya | Testing |

  Every clue checks against the table. One solution, and only one.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="The move that made it fast" icon="i-lucide-zap"}
  Clue 1 alone allows two floors. Clue 2 kills floor 5, because nobody can
  live above the top floor. Reading clues 1 and 2 **together** fixed Anil
  before anything else was placed, and after that the puzzle fell in order.

  That pairing is the habit to build: when a clue leaves two options, look for
  the clue that mentions the same person or thing, and read them as one.
  :::
::

::tip
Scheduling puzzles are floor puzzles turned on their side. "Lecture on the day
after SQL" is "immediately above". Draw days top to bottom and use exactly the
same method.
::

## Common mistakes

- **Treating "above" as "immediately above".** The most common misreading.
- **Drawing floor 1 at the top.** Draw it like a building, floor 1 at the
  bottom.
- **Filling a guess into the grid in ink.** Put branch options in pencil, or
  draw two small grids side by side.
- **Using the negative clue first.** "Divya does not study SQL" leaves four
  options at the start; at the end it leaves one.
- **Staying too long.** Four minutes with nothing fixed means move on.

## How to practise

::steps
### Week one
One floor puzzle a day, untimed, from R.S. Aggarwal's *A Modern Approach to
Verbal and Non-Verbal Reasoning* or IndiaBix "Puzzles". Write the step list
exactly like the solve above.

### Week two
One floor and one scheduling puzzle a day, target **4 minutes each** including
the questions.

### Before a drive
Two puzzles from that company's previous papers on PrepInsta, timed, and one
honest decision about which to leave.
::

## Practice questions

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. Four lectures (Java, SQL, DBMS, OS) are held Monday to Thursday, one a day. DBMS is the day after SQL. OS is on Thursday. Java is before SQL. Which lecture is on Monday?"}
  **Java.** OS takes Thursday, so SQL and DBMS need two consecutive days from
  Monday to Wednesday, with Java before SQL: Java Monday, SQL Tuesday, DBMS
  Wednesday.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. Five boxes P, Q, R, S, T are stacked, position 1 at the bottom. R is at the bottom. Q is immediately above P. There are exactly two boxes between S and T, and T is above S. Which box is in the middle?"}
  **P.** S and T must be at 2 and 5 (1 is taken by R). That leaves 3 and 4 for
  P and Q, so P is 3, Q is 4. Stack from the bottom: R, S, P, Q, T.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. In the worked floor puzzle, who lives immediately below the Python student?"}
  **Esha**, on floor 4.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. In the worked floor puzzle, how many students live between the Testing student and the SQL student?"}
  **Two.** Bina and Anil, on floors 2 and 3.
  :::
::
