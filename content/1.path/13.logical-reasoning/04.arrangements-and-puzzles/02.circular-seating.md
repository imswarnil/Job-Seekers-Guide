A row has two ends to anchor on. A circle has none, and left and right depend on
which way each person faces. Two rules fix both problems, and after that a
circle is easier than a row, because there are fewer places to put anyone.

## Why it matters

Circular arrangements come in the same sets as linear ones: one table, three to
five questions. Infosys, Accenture, Cognizant, Capgemini and bank-style papers
use them often; TCS NQT and AMCAT-style tests occasionally. Check the current
pattern. When a circular set appears, it is usually worth four or five marks.

## What to study

| # | Sub-type | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Everyone facing the centre | High |

## The rules

```text
Facing the centre:   left = clockwise,       right = anticlockwise
Facing outward:      left = anticlockwise,   right = clockwise

In a circle of n people:
  "opposite" exists only when n is even: n/2 seats away
  "second to the left of X" = two seats clockwise from X (facing centre)
  Number of people between A and B is counted both ways round;
  questions mean the shorter way unless they say otherwise
```

::note
Why left is clockwise when facing the centre: sit at the bottom of the circle
looking up at the centre. Your left hand points to the left of the page, and
going clockwise from the bottom of a clock face also goes left. Try it once with
a real clock and you will never need to think about it again.
::

::steps
### Draw the circle and the seats
Six people, six dots. Write "facing centre" and a small clockwise arrow beside
it.

### Fix one person anywhere
A circle has no start, so the first person you place can go at the top. Choose
whoever appears in the most clues.

### Place relative to that person
"Opposite", "immediately left", "second to the right". Each one is now a fixed
seat.

### Verify every clue
Read each clue against the drawing, out loud in your head.
::

## A worked example

**Six friends from a PG sit round a table facing the centre: Kiran, Latha,
Manoj, Nisha, Omar and Pooja.**

1. Latha sits opposite Kiran.
2. Manoj sits immediately to the left of Kiran.
3. Nisha is not an immediate neighbour of Latha.
4. Pooja sits immediately to the right of Latha.

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="The solve" icon="i-lucide-pencil"}
  Number the seats 0 to 5 clockwise and put Kiran at 0.

  ```text
  Kiran at 0 (fixed, appears in two clues)
  Clue 1: opposite is 3 seats away       → Latha at 3
  Clue 2: left = clockwise, next seat    → Manoj at 1
  Clue 4: right = anticlockwise from 3   → Pooja at 2
  Free seats: 4 and 5
  Clue 3: seat 4 is next to Latha         → Nisha at 5, Omar at 4
  ```

  **Clockwise from Kiran: Kiran, Manoj, Pooja, Latha, Omar, Nisha.**
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Questions that hang off it" icon="i-lucide-list"}
  - Who sits between Omar and Kiran? **Nisha.**
  - Who is opposite Manoj? **Omar** (seat 1 and seat 4).
  - Who is second to the left of Latha? **Nisha** (two seats clockwise from 3).
  :::
::

::tip
If clue 4 had been placed with "right = clockwise", Pooja would land on seat 4,
and the set would still look solvable until a later question contradicted it.
Write the rule next to the circle before placing anyone.
::

## Common mistakes

- **Using your own left.** Every "left" in these questions belongs to the person
  named.
- **Forgetting outward-facing reverses the rule.** In a mixed set, draw a small
  arrow at every seat showing which way that person faces.
- **"Opposite" in an odd-sized circle.** With seven people there is no opposite
  seat. If a question asks for one, recheck the count.
- **Fixing the first person from clue 1 by habit** instead of the person in the
  most clues.

## How to practise

- **Week one:** one circular set a day from IndiaBix "Seating Arrangement" or
  R.S. Aggarwal's *A Modern Approach to Verbal and Non-Verbal Reasoning*,
  untimed, with the seat numbers written out.
- **Week two:** alternate linear and circular sets, two a day, **3 minutes per
  set**.
- **Then:** add square-table sets (four at the corners facing in, four at the
  middles facing out) once plain circles feel routine. PrepInsta's company pages
  show which companies use them.

## Practice questions

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. Eight people sit round a table facing the centre. How many seats apart are two people sitting opposite each other?"}
  **Four.** `8 ÷ 2`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. Facing the centre, A is immediately to the left of B. Is B to A's left or right?"}
  **Right.** A is one seat clockwise from B, so B is one seat anticlockwise from
  A, which is A's right.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. In the worked example, if everyone turns to face outward, who is immediately to the left of Kiran?"}
  **Nisha.** Facing out, left is anticlockwise: seat 5.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. Six people sit round a table. Can two people have exactly three people between them?"}
  **No.** Going the short way the most is two between (opposite seats have two
  on each side).
  :::
::
