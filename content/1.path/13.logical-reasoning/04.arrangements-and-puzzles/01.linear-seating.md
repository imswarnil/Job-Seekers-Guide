Ranking and order put people in a line by one number each. A seating
arrangement puts them in a line by a set of clues, and one correct drawing
answers four or five questions at once. This page is how to get that drawing
right the first time.

## Why it matters

Seating arrangement is one of the heaviest topics in the reasoning section. In
Infosys, Accenture, Cognizant, Capgemini and bank-style papers it often comes as
a **set**: one arrangement, three to five questions. In TCS NQT and AMCAT-style
tests it may be a single question. Check the current pattern, but plan for
three to five marks riding on one drawing.

That is why it rewards method so heavily. A set that resolves in three minutes
gives five marks in the next thirty seconds. A set attempted in your head gives
nothing, however long you spend.

## What to study

| # | Sub-type | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Single row, everyone facing north | High |
| 2 | Single row, some facing north and some facing south | High |
| 3 | Two parallel rows facing each other | Medium |
| 4 | A row with an extra attribute (subject, city, colour) | Medium |
| 5 | Rows with unknown number of people | Low, skip at first |

## The rules

::steps
### Draw the frame before reading any clue
Six people, six slots: `_ _ _ _ _ _`. Write "facing north" above it, and
number the slots 1 to 6 from the left.

### Place the absolute clue first
"Chitra is third from the left" fixes a person to a slot. "Anita is right of
Deepak" does not. Start with whatever is fixed, whatever order the clues are
printed in.

### Then the tight relative clues
"Immediately right of", "next to", "at an end". These leave only two or three
options each.

### Branch when you have to
If a clue allows two positions, draw both rows one under the other. Usually one
dies within two clues.

### Verify every clue against the final row
Ten seconds. It is the difference between a right answer and a confident wrong
one.
::

## The direction table

| Facing | Their left is | Their right is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| North (up the page, same way as you) | Your left | Your right |
| South (down the page, towards you) | Your right | Your left |

```text
Facing north:   "A is immediately right of B"  →  B A
Facing south:   "A is immediately right of B"  →  A B   (as drawn on paper)

"Third from the left" and "fourth from the right" are the same seat
in a row of six:  from the right = total − from the left + 1
```

::warning{icon="i-lucide-alert-triangle"}
When some people face north and some face south, write an arrow above each
person as you place them. "Left of X" is always X's own left, and in a mixed
row that changes from seat to seat.
::

## A worked example

**Six students sit in a row facing north: Anita, Bhavesh, Chitra, Deepak, Esha
and Farhan.**

1. Chitra sits third from the left.
2. Esha sits at one of the ends.
3. Bhavesh sits immediately to the right of Chitra.
4. Anita sits immediately to the right of Deepak.
5. Farhan is not at either end.
6. Deepak is not at either end.

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="The solve, step by step" icon="i-lucide-pencil"}
  ```text
  Frame            1  2  3  4  5  6
  Clue 1           _  _  C  _  _  _      absolute, so first
  Clue 3           _  _  C  B  _  _      tight, right after it
  Clue 2, branch   E  _  C  B  _  _      (a) Esha on the left
                   _  _  C  B  _  E      (b) Esha on the right
  ```

  Clue 4 needs two adjacent free seats for Deepak then Anita.

  - Branch (b): the only adjacent free pair is 1–2, so Deepak 1, Anita 2,
    Farhan 5. Clue 6 says Deepak is not at an end. **Branch (b) dies.**
  - Branch (a): the only adjacent free pair is 5–6, so Deepak 5, Anita 6,
    Farhan 2. Clue 5: Farhan not at an end. Clue 6: Deepak not at an end.

  **Final: Esha, Farhan, Chitra, Bhavesh, Deepak, Anita.**

  Check all six clues against that row before answering anything. They hold.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Questions that hang off it" icon="i-lucide-list"}
  - Who sits second from the right? **Deepak.**
  - Who sits between Farhan and Bhavesh? **Chitra.**
  - How many people sit to the right of Chitra? **Three.**

  One drawing, three marks, each in under ten seconds.
  :::
::

::tip
Without clue 6 both branches survive, and the question would have two answers.
When you reach the end with two valid rows, you have almost always skipped a
clue. Reread the list, do not guess between them.
::

## Common mistakes

- **Holding it in your head.** Draw the frame, every time, even for five people.
- **Taking the clues in printed order.** Setters often put the absolute clue
  last on purpose.
- **"Immediately right" versus "right".** "Right of" means anywhere to the
  right. "Immediately" means the next seat.
- **Facing south, drawn as north.** Mark the arrow.
- **Abandoning a branch too early** or never branching at all. Two short rows
  are faster than one long guess.

## How to practise

::steps
### Week one
One arrangement set a day (4–5 questions) from IndiaBix "Seating Arrangement"
or R.S. Aggarwal's *A Modern Approach to Verbal and Non-Verbal Reasoning*.
Untimed. Write the step table like the one above.

### Week two
Two sets a day, target **3 minutes per set** including all its questions.

### Before a drive
Two sets from that company's previous papers on PrepInsta, timed.
::

::note
If a set has not placed a single person absolutely after two minutes, mark it
and move on. The marks are gone whether you spend two more minutes or not, and
there are easier ones later in the paper.
::

## Practice questions

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. Five staff sit facing north: Priya, Ravi, Sunil, Tara, Uma. Sunil is at the extreme right, Priya second from the left, Tara immediately left of Sunil, and Uma is not at an end. Who is at the extreme left?"}
  **Ravi.** `_ P _ T S`. Uma cannot take seat 1 (an end), so Uma is 3 and Ravi
  is 1.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. Six people sit in a row facing south. Anil is third from the left end. What is his position from the right end?"}
  **Fourth.** `6 − 3 + 1 = 4`. Facing direction does not change this, as long
  as both ends are counted from the same point of view.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. Seven people sit in a row. How many sit between the second from the left and the second from the right?"}
  **Three.** Seats 3, 4 and 5.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. In the worked example, if Farhan and Deepak swap seats, who sits immediately to the right of Farhan?"}
  **Anita.** The row becomes Esha, Deepak, Chitra, Bhavesh, Farhan, Anita.
  :::
::
