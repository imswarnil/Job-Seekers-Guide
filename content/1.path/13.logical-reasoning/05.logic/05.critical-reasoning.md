Data sufficiency asked whether the information was enough. Critical reasoning
asks whether an argument holds up: what it depends on, what would help it, and
what would break it. It is the last logic topic, and the one that looks most
like real work.

## Why it matters

Critical reasoning appears in Infosys, Accenture, Cognizant, Capgemini and
AMCAT-style papers, sometimes inside the verbal section rather than
reasoning. Expect one to three questions when present, and check the current
pattern. The passages are four or five lines long, so each question is about
a minute, most of it reading.

It is also the reasoning topic interviewers use without calling it one: "we
added a feature and sign-ups went up, so the feature worked, yes?" is a
critical-reasoning question.

## What to study

| # | Question type | What it asks | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Weaken | Which option most damages the argument? | High |
| 2 | Strengthen | Which option most supports it? | High |
| 3 | Assumption | What must be true for the argument to work? | High |
| 4 | Inference | What must be true, given the passage? | Medium |
| 5 | Paradox | Which option explains two facts that seem to clash? | Medium |
| 6 | Main conclusion | What is the argument trying to prove? | Medium |

## The method

::steps
### Find the conclusion
The sentence the rest is trying to prove. Words like "so", "therefore",
"this shows" point to it.

### Find the evidence
The facts offered in support.

### Find the gap
What has to be true for the evidence to lead to the conclusion? That gap is
the assumption, and it is what strengthen and weaken options attack.

### Read the question stem last, the options after
Know which of the six types it is before you read a single option.
::

```text
Argument  = evidence + assumption → conclusion

Weaken:     attack the assumption (often: another cause explains the evidence)
Strengthen: support the assumption (rule out the other cause)
Assumption: the gap itself (negate it and the argument collapses)
Inference:  must be true from the passage, nothing added
```

## A worked example

**Passage:** "Students who attended the institute's weekend aptitude classes
scored higher in the placement test than students who did not. So the weekend
classes improve test scores."

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="Split it" icon="i-lucide-scissors"}
  - **Evidence:** attendees scored higher.
  - **Conclusion:** the classes caused the higher scores.
  - **Gap:** the two groups were similar in every other way that matters.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Weaken" icon="i-lucide-trending-down"}
  **Best weakener:** "The students who chose weekend classes were already the
  most motivated, and most of them practised daily on their own."

  This gives another cause for the higher scores. The classes might have done
  nothing.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Strengthen" icon="i-lucide-trending-up"}
  **Best strengthener:** "When places in the class were given by lottery, the
  students who got a place still scored higher than those who did not."

  A lottery rules out the "motivated students chose it" explanation.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Assumption" icon="i-lucide-lightbulb"}
  **Assumption:** "Students who attended were not already stronger at aptitude
  before the classes began."

  Negate it ("they were already stronger") and the argument falls apart.
  :::
::

::tip
For weaken questions, look first for an option that gives **another reason**
for the evidence. It is the right answer more often than any other pattern.
::

## Common mistakes

- **Choosing an option that is true but irrelevant.** It must touch the gap.
- **Out-of-scope options.** An option about a different institute, year or
  group of students rarely affects this argument.
- **Extreme inferences.** "All students should attend" goes far beyond the
  passage.
- **Reading the options before finding the conclusion.** The options are
  written to pull you into agreeing with them.

## How to practise

- **Week one:** 5 passages a day from IndiaBix "Verbal Reasoning" or
  PrepInsta's critical reasoning pages, writing conclusion, evidence and gap
  before reading the options.
- **Week two:** 8 a day, target **60 seconds** each.
- **Reference:** R.S. Aggarwal's *A Modern Approach to Verbal and Non-Verbal
  Reasoning* for volume; company previous papers for the current style.

## Practice questions

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. 'Since the PG added Wi-Fi, residents study more hours. So Wi-Fi improves study time.' Which weakens it? (a) Residents like Wi-Fi. (b) The PG added Wi-Fi in the month before placement season, when everyone studies more."}
  **(b).** Another cause, the season, explains the extra hours.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. In the worked example, what kind of option is: 'The classes cover exactly the topics tested in the placement exam'?"}
  **A strengthener.** It gives a reason the classes could raise scores, though
  it does not rule out the motivation explanation.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. 'Every candidate who cleared the written round had practised mock tests.' What must be true? (a) Mock tests guarantee clearing. (b) No one who cleared skipped mock tests."}
  **(b).** It is the passage restated. (a) goes beyond it: others may have
  practised and still not cleared.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. Paradox: 'Applications to the college rose this year, but total fee collection fell.' Which explains it? (a) More students paid in full. (b) The college gave large scholarships to most new students."}
  **(b).** More students, less money each.
  :::
::
