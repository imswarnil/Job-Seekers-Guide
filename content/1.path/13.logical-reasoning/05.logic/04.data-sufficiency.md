Cause and effect asked how two events relate. Data sufficiency asks about
information itself: given this question and these two facts, **could** you
answer it? You rarely need to finish the calculation, and that is where the
speed comes from.

## Why it matters

Data sufficiency (DS) appears in Infosys, Capgemini, AMCAT-style and bank-style
papers, and in TCS NQT in some years, in both the reasoning and the quant
sections. Expect one to three questions when present, and check the current
pattern. The topics underneath are ones you already know (ranks, blood
relations, directions, ages, percentages), so the only new thing is the format.

## What to study

The standard options, which nearly every paper uses:

| Option | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| (a) | Statement I alone is sufficient, II alone is not |
| (b) | Statement II alone is sufficient, I alone is not |
| (c) | Either statement alone is sufficient |
| (d) | Both statements together are needed |
| (e) | Both together are still not sufficient |

Sub-types in priority order: ranking and order, blood relations, direction
sense, ages and numbers, then coding and day-of-the-week questions.

## The method

::steps
### Read the question and decide what "an answer" is
A single value, or a definite yes or no. "Yes" and "no" both count as
sufficient; "it depends" does not.

### Test statement I alone
Cover statement II with your hand. Can you answer? Write yes or no.

### Test statement II alone
Cover I. Can you answer? Write yes or no.

### Only if both were no, combine them
Now read both together.
::

```text
I yes, II no   → (a)
I no,  II yes  → (b)
I yes, II yes  → (c)
both no, together yes → (d)
both no, together no  → (e)
```

::tip
Stop as soon as you know. If statement I pins down the rank, you do not need
the rank itself, you need to know that one number is possible. That is the
whole speed of this topic.
::

## A worked example

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="Either alone" icon="i-lucide-list-ordered"}
  **What is Ravi's rank from the top in a class of 40?**

  I. Ravi is 12th from the bottom.
  II. Meera is 24th from the top, and Ravi is 5 ranks below Meera.

  ```text
  I alone:  40 − 12 + 1 = 29           → sufficient
  II alone: 24 + 5 = 29                → sufficient
  ```

  **Answer: (c), either statement alone.** Both give 29, which is a good sign
  you read them correctly.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Both needed" icon="i-lucide-users"}
  **How is K related to M?**

  I. K is the son of N.
  II. N is M's sister.

  I alone says nothing about M. II alone says nothing about K. Together: K is
  the son of M's sister, so K is M's **nephew**.

  **Answer: (d), both together.**
  :::
::

## Common mistakes

- **Carrying statement I into the test of statement II.** Test each alone,
  truly alone.
- **Treating a "no" answer as insufficient.** "Is Ravi taller than Meera? No."
  is a sufficient answer.
- **Assuming a gender.** "N is M's sibling" does not tell you sister or
  brother, and relation questions often turn on that.
- **Finishing the arithmetic.** You need to know one answer exists, not what it
  is.

## Practice questions

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. What is the day today? I. Yesterday was Tuesday. II. Tomorrow is not Friday."}
  **(a).** I alone gives Wednesday. II alone rules out only one day.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. Is P to the north of Q? I. Q is 5 km south of R. II. P is 2 km north of R."}
  **(d).** Neither alone links P and Q. Together, P is 7 km north of Q: yes.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. What is Asha's age? I. Asha is 4 years older than Neha. II. Neha is 22."}
  **(d).** Together, 26.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. Who is the tallest of A, B and C? I. A is taller than B. II. B is taller than C."}
  **(d).** Neither alone compares all three. Together, A > B > C, so A. Picking
  (e) here is the mistake of stopping after testing each statement alone.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="5. How many students are in the class? I. Priya is 15th from both ends. II. There are 14 students above Priya."}
  **(a).** I alone: `15 + 15 − 1 = 29`. II alone says nothing about those below.
  :::
::
