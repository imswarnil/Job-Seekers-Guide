The thirty-day quant plan ends with you sitting full maths sections against a
clock. The same paper has a second section in it, reasoning, and this page
answers what it contains, how it is timed, and how to approach it before you
learn a single topic.

## Why it matters

Every mass-recruitment written round I sat in 2018, and every one I have seen
since, has a reasoning section. It is usually the same size as the quant
section, and in some papers it is larger. A company that cuts on a combined
score will happily take a candidate who is average at maths and strong at
reasoning. A company that cuts section by section needs you to clear both.

Reasoning is also the section where I improved fastest once I started practising
properly in Bangalore. Maths needed formulas. Reasoning mostly needed me to stop
holding things in my head.

## How the section is built

The table below is a rough picture, not an official one. Companies rename
sections, merge them and change question counts almost every year, so treat the
numbers as the size of the job, and check the current pattern on PrepInsta or
the company's own hiring page before a drive.

| Test | What the section is called | Roughly how many questions | Topics it leans on |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| TCS NQT | Reasoning ability / logical reasoning | 15–30 | Series, coding, seating, blood relations, data sufficiency, statements |
| Infosys | Logical reasoning | 12–15, some long | Puzzles, arrangements, data sufficiency, syllogisms |
| Wipro | Logical reasoning | 14–16 | Series, coding, directions, blood relations, syllogisms |
| Accenture | Critical reasoning and problem solving | 12–16 | Critical reasoning, statements, arrangements, input-output |
| Cognizant | Logical / analytical reasoning | 12–16 | Puzzles, input-output, data sufficiency, visual reasoning |
| Capgemini | Logical / game-based reasoning | 15–25 | Series, coding, arrangements, visual patterns |
| AMCAT / eLitmus style | Logical ability | 12–20 | Deductive and inductive reasoning, puzzles, figure series |

::note
Some companies now use short game-based rounds (moving shapes, switch puzzles)
instead of, or on top of, classic reasoning questions. The skill underneath is
the same: spot a rule, test it, apply it. The topics in this track prepare you
for both.
::

## Time per question

Most reasoning sections give you **about a minute a question on average**, like
quant. That average hides two very different kinds of question:

| Kind | Examples | Target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One-shot questions | Series, coding, analogy, odd one out, direction, ranking | 20–40 seconds |
| Set questions | Seating, puzzles, input-output (one setup, 3–5 questions) | 3–5 minutes for the whole set |

A set looks expensive, but once the diagram is drawn, each of its questions
takes 10 seconds. A five-question set in four minutes is faster per mark than
five separate series questions.

## What to study

The topics of this track, in the order I would learn them if I had a month:

::steps
### The fast marks (week one)
Number series, letter series, coding-decoding, analogies and odd one out.
Short, frequent, and they train your eye for patterns.

### The diagram topics (week two)
Blood relations, direction sense, ranking and order. Each falls to a small
drawing.

### The sets (week three)
Linear and circular seating, and the floor, scheduling and box puzzles.
Highest value per minute once you are fluent, highest risk before.

### The logic topics (week four)
Syllogisms, statements and assumptions, cause and effect, data sufficiency,
critical reasoning, input-output, cubes and dice, and visual reasoning.
::

## The one habit: draw before you think

Almost every reasoning question gives you more facts than a person can hold in
their head at once. The people who do well here are not cleverer. They put the
facts on paper in a shape (a family tree, a row of seats, a compass, a grid)
and then read the answer off the shape.

::compare
  :::compare-side{label="Holding it in your head" verdict="wrong"}
  "A is to the left of B, C is next to A but not at the end, D is..." By the
  fourth clue the first one has gone, and you start again. Two minutes gone,
  one question unanswered.
  :::
  :::compare-side{label="Drawing it" verdict="right"}
  Five blanks on the page. Write each clue in as you read it. Clues you cannot
  place yet go in a list under the diagram. Ninety seconds, and the next four
  questions are free.
  :::
::

::warning{icon="i-lucide-alert-triangle"}
In a reasoning question, your own knowledge of the world is a liability. If the
statements say "all fees are refundable", you accept that for the question.
Answer from what is written, never from what is usually true.
::

## What to skip, and when

- **Skip a set** if you have read it twice and cannot draw the first line of the
  diagram. Come back at the end.
- **Skip a series** after 30 seconds without a pattern. It is worth the same
  mark as an easy question.
- **Never skip data sufficiency or syllogism questions** on time alone. They
  are short and methodical, and they are where prepared candidates pull ahead.
- If the paper has **negative marking**, leave pure guesses blank. If it does
  not, answer everything before time runs out. Check the instructions page each
  time; it changes between companies and years.

## Common mistakes

- **Starting a big puzzle first** because it is at the top of the section.
- **Re-reading a long setup** instead of drawing it once.
- **Treating "left" and "right" loosely** in seating questions.
- **Believing the section is about intelligence.** It is about method, and
  method is learnable in a month.

## How to practise

- **Daily:** 30–40 minutes of reasoning, after your quant, on the topic of the
  day in the plan at the end of this track.
- **Where:** IndiaBix's logical and verbal reasoning sections for volume,
  PrepInsta for company-wise previous papers, and R.S. Aggarwal's *A Modern
  Approach to Verbal and Non-Verbal Reasoning* for depth.
- **Weekly:** one timed mixed section of 20 questions in 25 minutes.

## Practice questions

A taste of the section, one question from each week of the plan.

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. Next in the series: 3, 7, 15, 31, ?"}
  **63.** Each term is `× 2 + 1`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. If COLLEGE is written DPMMFHF, how is STUDENT written?"}
  **TUVEFOU.** Every letter moves one place forward.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. I walk 4 km north, turn right and walk 3 km. How far am I from the start?"}
  **5 km, towards the north-east.** A 3-4-5 right-angled triangle.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. All interns are graduates. Some graduates are engineers. Does 'some interns are engineers' follow?"}
  **No.** The engineers could all be graduates who are not interns.
  :::
::
