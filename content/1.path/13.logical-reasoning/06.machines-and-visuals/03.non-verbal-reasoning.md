Cubes and dice were the first questions in this track that needed you to see a
shape. Non-verbal reasoning is all shapes: a figure in a mirror, a figure upside
down in water, a figure that changes by one rule from box to box. It is the last
topic in the track.

## Why it matters

Non-verbal (or "visual") reasoning appears in TCS NQT, Wipro, Capgemini,
Cognizant, AMCAT-style and eLitmus-style papers, anything from one to five
questions depending on the company and year. Check the current pattern. These
questions are fast once you know what to look at, and slow if you stare at the
whole picture hoping it will make sense.

## The rules

| Image | What flips | What stays |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Mirror** (placed at the side) | Left and right | Top and bottom |
| **Water** (reflection below) | Top and bottom | Left and right |

```text
Capital letters that look the same in a side mirror:
  A H I M O T U V W X Y

Capital letters that look the same in a water image:
  B C D E H I K O X

Clock in a mirror:  time shown = 11:60 − actual time
  (3:40 → 11:60 − 3:40 = 8:20)

Clock in water:     the rule papers use is 18:30 − actual time
  (3:00 → 18:30 − 3:00 = 15:30, read as 3:30)
```

::tip
For a figure series, check these five things one at a time, in this order:
**rotation**, **number of elements**, **position** (moving round the box),
**shading**, and **size**. Most series change two of them at once. Naming them
turns a picture you stare at into a checklist you tick.
::

## A worked example

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="Clock in a mirror" icon="i-lucide-clock"}
  **A clock shows 3:40. What time does its mirror image show?**

  ```text
  11:60 − 3:40 = 8:20
  ```

  **Answer: 8:20.** Check with a picture you can hold: 3:00 in a mirror looks
  like 9:00, and `11:60 − 3:00 = 9:00`. The rule works.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Figure series" icon="i-lucide-arrow-up-right"}
  **What comes next: ↑ ↗ → ↘ ?**

  One element, one change: the arrow rotates 45° clockwise each step.

  **Answer: ↓.**

  A second one: **△ □ ⬠ ?** The number of sides goes 3, 4, 5. **Answer: a
  hexagon, ⬡.**
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="A word in a mirror" icon="i-lucide-flip-horizontal"}
  **Which of these words looks exactly the same in a mirror placed beside it:
  MOM, DAD, NUN, SIS?**

  A side mirror reverses the order of the letters and flips each one. The word
  must read the same backwards and use only letters from the mirror list.

  **Answer: MOM.** DAD reads the same backwards, but D is not symmetric left to
  right.
  :::
::

## Common mistakes

- **Mixing up mirror and water.** Mirror swaps left and right; water swaps top
  and bottom.
- **Reading a mirrored word in the original order.** A side mirror also
  reverses the order of the letters.
- **Looking at the whole figure.** Take one element at a time and one property
  at a time.
- **Ignoring the odd-one-out's simplest difference.** Count sides, lines and
  dots first. Clever differences are rarely the intended one.

## How to practise

- **Days one and two:** mirror and water images, 15 questions a day from R.S.
  Aggarwal's *A Modern Approach to Verbal and Non-Verbal Reasoning* (the
  non-verbal half is the standard source) or IndiaBix "Non Verbal Reasoning".
- **Days three and four:** figure series and odd figure out, 15 a day, target
  **30 seconds** each, saying the five-point checklist as you go.
- **Then:** paper folding and embedded figures, 10 a day for two days.
  PrepInsta's TCS and Wipro pages show the current style, which is often
  simpler than the book.
