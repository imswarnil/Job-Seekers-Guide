The machine was a rule applied in steps. Cubes and dice are a rule applied in
space, and the trick is the same one this whole track has used: do not picture
it, use a method you can write down.

## Why it matters

Cube and dice questions appear in TCS NQT, Wipro, Capgemini, AMCAT-style and
eLitmus-style papers, usually one or two questions, sometimes a set of three on
one painted cube. Check the current pattern. With the formulas below, a
painted-cube question takes 20 seconds. Without them, people count on their
fingers and get it wrong.

## The formulas

A large cube, painted on all six faces, is cut into `n × n × n` equal small
cubes.

```text
3 faces painted (the corners):        8
2 faces painted (edges, no corners):  12 × (n − 2)
1 face painted (face centres):        6 × (n − 2)²
0 faces painted (the hidden core):    (n − 2)³

Check: the four add up to n³
```

::tip
Always add your four answers and compare with `n³`. For `n = 4`: `8 + 24 + 24 +
8 = 64`. If they do not add up, one of them is wrong, and you find out before
the examiner does.
::

## The dice rules

```text
Rule 1: faces that appear together in any view are adjacent,
        so they can never be opposite each other.

Rule 2: if two views share one face in the same position,
        read the other faces clockwise from the shared face;
        faces in matching places are opposite.

Standard die: 1–6, 2–5, 3–4 are opposite (each pair adds to 7).
Do not assume a die is standard unless the question says so.
```

In a **net** (an unfolded cube): faces separated by exactly one face in a
straight line are opposite.

## A worked example

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="The painted cube" icon="i-lucide-box"}
  **A cube painted on all faces is cut into 64 equal small cubes. How many have
  exactly two faces painted? How many have none?**

  ```text
  n³ = 64  →  n = 4
  2 faces: 12 × (4 − 2)  = 24
  0 faces: (4 − 2)³      = 8
  (3 faces: 8, 1 face: 6 × 4 = 24; total 64)
  ```

  **Answer: 24 and 8.**
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Two views of a die" icon="i-lucide-dice-5"}
  **Two positions of the same die show these faces, read clockwise starting
  from the face showing 1:**

  - View 1: 1, 2, 3
  - View 2: 1, 4, 5

  **Which face is opposite 2?**

  1 is common and in the same position, so read clockwise after it:

  ```text
  View 1:  2   3
  View 2:  4   5
  2 is opposite 4,  3 is opposite 5
  The only faces left are 1 and 6, so 1 is opposite 6
  ```

  **Answer: 4.** Notice this die is not standard: 2 and 4 do not add to 7.
  That is why you never assume.
  :::
::

## Common mistakes

- **Counting corners as edges.** A corner cube has three painted faces, never
  two.
- **Using `n` where the formula needs `n − 2`.** The outer layer is removed
  from both sides.
- **Assuming a standard die.** Only if the question says so.
- **Rotating the die in your head.** Use rule 1 to eliminate, then rule 2.

## How to practise

- **Day one:** learn the formulas and the two rules. 10 painted-cube questions
  from IndiaBix "Cube and Cuboid" or R.S. Aggarwal's *A Modern Approach to
  Verbal and Non-Verbal Reasoning*.
- **Day two:** 10 dice questions from the same book, target **30 seconds** each.
- **Then:** two in each non-verbal practice set. A real die from a board game
  helps for the first week; after that, trust the rules.

  **27.** `3³`. Check: `8 + 36 + 54 + 27 = 125`.

  **6.** Everything else has been seen beside it.
