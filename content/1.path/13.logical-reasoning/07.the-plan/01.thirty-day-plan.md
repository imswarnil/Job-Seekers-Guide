Non-verbal reasoning was the last topic. This page puts all of them into thirty
days, in the order this track teaches them, with tests built in so you find out
what has not stuck while there is still time to fix it.

## Why it matters

Reading a guide page once does not clear a written round. Timed repetition does.
Reasoning improves faster than quant for most people, because there is less
arithmetic to slow you down, but only if the practice is daily. Thirty days at
about an hour a day is enough to go from skipping every seating set to
attempting it first.

When I prepared in Bangalore in 2018, aptitude and English were the things I
studied on my own alongside the Java classes. This plan is built to fit beside a
day like that, and beside the [30-day quant plan](/quantitative-aptitude/revision/thirty-day-plan).

## What to do every day

::steps
### 10 minutes: yesterday's mistakes
Open the back of your notebook. Redo every question you got wrong yesterday,
without looking at the solution.

### 35–40 minutes: today's topic
Read the guide page, then its practice questions, then 10–15 questions from
IndiaBix or R.S. Aggarwal's *A Modern Approach to Verbal and Non-Verbal
Reasoning*.

### 10 minutes: timed
Five questions from an earlier topic, against a clock. This is what keeps week
one alive in week four.

### 5 minutes: the notebook
Every wrong answer goes in the back, with one line on **why**: misread, wrong
rule, ran out of time.
::

## The order you attempt a real section in

Practise this on days 27 and 29, because it is a decision, not a skill, and
decisions need rehearsing.

::steps
### First pass: the quick singles
Series, coding, analogies, directions, blood relations, syllogisms. 30–45
seconds each. Skip anything that stalls.

### Second pass: the sets you trust
Seating or puzzles you can draw in under a minute. Each one pays several marks.

### Last pass: what is left
The long puzzle, the awkward machine, the figure you could not see. Only now.
::

::warning{icon="i-lucide-alert-triangle"}
If a paper has negative marking, do not guess blindly in the last pass. Guess
only when you have eliminated at least two options. Check the paper's
instructions; they change from company to company.
::

## Common mistakes

- **Skipping the test days** because they feel less productive than new topics.
  They are where you find out what has not stuck.
- **Practising only the topics you like.** The notebook will tell you which ones
  you avoid.
- **Never timing yourself** until the real paper.
- **Doing thirty days in ten.** Three topics a day for ten days leaves nothing
  in your head by the drive.

## How to practise after day 30

Two reasoning sets a day (one quick-singles set, one arrangement or puzzle)
until your drive, and a full timed section every weekend. The mistake notebook
is what you read in the queue on the morning of a walk-in.

## Practice questions

A small mixed check, one from each chapter, to see where you stand on day 1
and again on day 30.

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. Next in the series 2, 6, 12, 20, 30, ?"}
  **42.** Differences 4, 6, 8, 10, 12.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. A walks 3 km north, turns right and walks 4 km. How far is A from the start?"}
  **5 km.** A 3-4-5 right triangle.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. Six people sit round a table facing the centre. Who is immediately to the left of the person in seat 0, if seats are numbered clockwise?"}
  **The person in seat 1.** Facing the centre, left is clockwise.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. All interns are trainees. Some trainees are graduates. Does 'Some interns are graduates' follow?"}
  **No.** All + Some gives no definite conclusion.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="5. A painted cube is cut into 64 small cubes. How many have exactly three painted faces?"}
  **8.** The corners, whatever the size.
  :::
::
