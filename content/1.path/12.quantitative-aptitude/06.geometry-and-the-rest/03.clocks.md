The solids were about space. Clocks are back to motion: two hands moving round
the same track at different speeds, which is relative speed from the time
chapter measured in degrees instead of kilometres.

## Why it matters

Clocks appear in TCS NQT, Wipro, Capgemini and AMCAT-style papers, sometimes
in the quant section and sometimes in reasoning. Expect one question at most,
and some papers have none; check the current pattern. It is a good topic to
own because one formula answers most questions in 20 seconds.

## What to study

| # | Sub-type | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Angle between the hands at a given time | High |
| 2 | When the hands coincide between two hours | High |
| 3 | When they are opposite, or at right angles | Medium |
| 4 | How many times a day they coincide / are opposite / at 90° | Medium |
| 5 | A clock that gains or loses time | Low |

## The rules

```text
Minute hand: 360° in 60 min  = 6° per minute
Hour hand:   360° in 12 h    = 0.5° per minute  (30° per hour)
Minute hand gains 5.5° per minute on the hour hand

Angle at H hours M minutes:
  θ = | 30H − 5.5M |
  If θ > 180, the smaller angle is 360 − θ

Hands coincide every 65 5/11 minutes (not every 65)
```

| In 12 hours | In 24 hours |
| --- | --- |
| Coincide 11 times | 22 times |
| Opposite (180°) 11 times | 22 times |
| At right angles 22 times | 44 times |
| In a straight line (0° or 180°) 22 times | 44 times |

::note
Why 11 and not 12: between 11 and 1 the hands meet only once, at 12. So one of
the twelve hour-gaps has no meeting of its own.
::

## A worked example

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="Angle at a time" icon="i-lucide-clock"}
  **What is the angle between the hands at 3:40?**

  ```text
  θ = | 30 × 3 − 5.5 × 40 | = | 90 − 220 | = 130°
  ```

  **Answer: 130°.** Under 180, so that is the smaller angle.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="When do they meet?" icon="i-lucide-timer"}
  **At what time between 4 and 5 do the hands coincide?**

  At 4:00 the hour hand is `4 × 30 = 120°` ahead. The minute hand closes the gap
  at 5.5° a minute.

  ```text
  120 ÷ 5.5 = 240/11 = 21 9/11 minutes
  ```

  **Answer: 21 9/11 minutes past 4.** For "opposite", the minute hand needs to
  close the gap to 180° behind or get 180° ahead; for "right angle", 90°.
  :::
::

## Common mistakes

- **Forgetting the hour hand moves.** At 3:40 the hour hand is not on the 3.
  The formula already includes this; mental pictures do not.
- **Giving the reflex angle.** If the formula gives over 180, subtract from 360.
- **Saying 12 or 24 coincidences.** It is 11 in 12 hours and 22 in a day.
- **Using 65 minutes** instead of 65 5/11 for the gap between meetings.

## How to practise

- **One evening:** learn the formula and the table. 10 angle questions.
- **Next day:** 10 mixed questions from IndiaBix "Clock", target **30
  seconds**.
- **Then:** one clock question every few days so the formula stays.

::tip
Test the formula on times you can picture: 6:00 gives 180, 3:00 gives 90,
12:00 gives 0. If the formula gives you the obvious answer for those, you have
it the right way round.
::

## Practice questions

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. Angle between the hands at 2:30?"}
  **105°.** `| 60 − 165 |`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. Angle between the hands at 9:15?"}
  **172.5°.** `| 270 − 82.5 | = 187.5`, so the smaller angle is `360 − 187.5`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. How many times do the hands coincide in a day?"}
  **22.**
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. How many times are the hands at right angles in a day?"}
  **44.**
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="5. Between 7 and 8, when are the hands exactly opposite?"}
  **5 5/11 minutes past 7.** At 7:00 the gap is 210°. The minute hand must gain
  30° to make it 180°: `30 ÷ 5.5 = 60/11`.
  :::
::
