2D shapes gave you area and perimeter. Solids add a third measurement, so you
get **volume** (how much fits inside) and **surface area** (how much material
covers the outside).

## Why it matters

3D mensuration is less common than 2D but still appears in TCS NQT, Capgemini
and AMCAT-style papers, roughly one question when it shows up. It is almost all
recall, so a formula you know is a question you finish in 40 seconds, and one
you do not know is a question you skip. Check the current pattern.

Two words. **Curved surface area** (CSA, also "lateral") is the side only.
**Total surface area** (TSA) is the side plus the flat ends.

## The formulas

| Solid | Volume | Curved / lateral surface | Total surface |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cube, edge `a` | `a³` | `4a²` | `6a²` |
| Cuboid `l, b, h` | `lbh` | `2h(l + b)` | `2(lb + bh + hl)` |
| Cylinder `r, h` | `πr²h` | `2πrh` | `2πr(r + h)` |
| Cone `r, h`, slant `l` | `⅓πr²h` | `πrl` | `πr(r + l)` |
| Sphere `r` | `(4/3)πr³` | `4πr²` | `4πr²` |
| Hemisphere `r` | `(2/3)πr³` | `2πr²` | `3πr²` |

```text
Cone slant height:   l = √(r² + h²)
Cube diagonal:       a√3
Cuboid diagonal:     √(l² + b² + h²)

Melt and recast:     volume stays the same
  number of small pieces = big volume ÷ one small volume

Scaling: multiply every length by k
  surface area × k²
  volume       × k³
```

::tip
Cones are built on Pythagorean triples: (3, 4, 5), (5, 12, 13), (7, 24, 25),
(8, 15, 17). If a cone has radius 7 and height 24, the slant is 25 before you
calculate anything.
::

## A worked example

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="Cylinder" icon="i-lucide-cylinder"}
  **A cylindrical water tank on a college hostel roof has radius 7 m and height
  10 m. Volume and curved surface area?**

  ```text
  V   = 22/7 × 7 × 7 × 10 = 1540 m³
  CSA = 2 × 22/7 × 7 × 10 = 440 m²
  ```

  1 m³ is 1,000 litres, so that tank holds 15,40,000 litres.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Melt and recast" icon="i-lucide-flame"}
  **A metal sphere of radius 6 cm is melted and recast into small spheres of
  radius 2 cm. How many?**

  ```text
  Number = (4/3 π 6³) ÷ (4/3 π 2³) = 216 ÷ 8 = 27
  ```

  Shortcut: same shape, so it is `(6 ÷ 2)³ = 27`. The `4/3 π` cancels, so
  never multiply it out.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Cone" icon="i-lucide-cone"}
  **A cone has radius 3 cm and height 4 cm. Curved surface and volume?**

  ```text
  l   = √(9 + 16) = 5
  CSA = π × 3 × 5 = 15π cm²
  V   = ⅓ × π × 9 × 4 = 12π cm³
  ```
  :::
::

## Common mistakes

- **Height for slant height** in the cone's surface formula.
- **Curved for total surface.** "Cost of painting the outside of a closed
  tank" is total. "An open tank" drops one end.
- **Multiplying out `π`** when it cancels.
- **Litres.** 1 m³ = 1,000 litres, 1 litre = 1,000 cm³.

## Practice questions

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. A cube has volume 64 cm³. Its total surface area?"}
  **96 cm².** Edge 4, `6 × 16`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. A cuboid 10 × 8 × 5 cm. Volume and total surface?"}
  **400 cm³ and 340 cm².** `2(80 + 40 + 50) = 340`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. A cylinder with radius 14 cm and height 5 cm. Volume?"}
  **3080 cm³.** `22/7 × 196 × 5`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. A cone with radius 7 cm and height 24 cm. Curved surface?"}
  **550 cm².** Slant 25, `22/7 × 7 × 25`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="5. How many 2 cm cubes can be cut from a 10 cm cube?"}
  **125.** `(10 ÷ 2)³`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="6. Every edge of a cube is doubled. Its volume becomes?"}
  **8 times.** `2³`. Its surface area becomes 4 times.
  :::
::
