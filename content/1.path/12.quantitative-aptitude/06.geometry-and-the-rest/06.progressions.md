Logarithms dealt with repeated multiplication. Progressions deal with sequences
that grow by a fixed rule: add the same amount each time, or multiply by the
same amount each time. The last formula topic before data interpretation.

## Why it matters

Progressions appear as direct questions in TCS NQT, Infosys, Capgemini and
AMCAT-style papers, roughly one question when present, and indirectly in the
number series questions of the reasoning section, where half the series are an
AP or a GP in disguise. Check the current pattern.

Two words. An **arithmetic progression** (AP) adds the same number each step:
`3, 7, 11, 15`. A **geometric progression** (GP) multiplies by the same number
each step: `3, 6, 12, 24`.

## The formulas

```text
AP: first term a, common difference d, last term l
  nth term       Tn = a + (n − 1)d
  Number of terms n = (l − a) / d + 1
  Sum            Sn = n/2 × (2a + (n − 1)d)  =  n/2 × (a + l)

GP: first term a, common ratio r
  nth term       Tn = a × r^(n − 1)
  Sum            Sn = a(r^n − 1) / (r − 1)     (r ≠ 1)
  Infinite sum   S  = a / (1 − r)             (only if −1 < r < 1)

Means of two numbers a and b:
  Arithmetic mean = (a + b) / 2
  Geometric mean  = √(a × b)

Natural numbers 1 to n:
  Sum        n(n + 1) / 2
  Squares    n(n + 1)(2n + 1) / 6
  Cubes      [n(n + 1) / 2]²
  First n odd numbers: n²
```

::tip
`n/2 × (first + last)` is the only AP sum you need in most questions. It is
also how you add 1 to 100 in your head: `100/2 × 101 = 5050`.
::

## A worked example

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="nth term and sum of an AP" icon="i-lucide-trending-up"}
  **Find the 20th term and the sum of the first 20 terms of 3, 7, 11, ...**

  ```text
  a = 3, d = 4
  T20 = 3 + 19 × 4 = 79
  S20 = 20/2 × (3 + 79) = 10 × 82 = 820
  ```

  **Answer: 79 and 820.**
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Counting terms" icon="i-lucide-list-ordered"}
  **How many terms are there in 5, 9, 13, ..., 201?**

  ```text
  n = (201 − 5) / 4 + 1 = 49 + 1 = 50
  ```

  **Answer: 50.** Forgetting the `+ 1` gives 49, and 49 will be an option.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="A GP" icon="i-lucide-trending-up"}
  **In the GP 3, 6, 12, ..., find the 8th term and the sum of 8 terms.**

  ```text
  a = 3, r = 2
  T8 = 3 × 2⁷ = 384
  S8 = 3 × (2⁸ − 1) / (2 − 1) = 3 × 255 = 765
  ```
  :::
::

## Common mistakes

- **The missing `+ 1`** when counting terms.
- **`r^n` instead of `r^(n − 1)`** for the nth term of a GP.
- **Using the infinite-sum formula when r is 1 or more.** It only works for
  ratios between −1 and 1.
- **Treating a GP as an AP** because the first two gaps look similar. Check
  three terms.
