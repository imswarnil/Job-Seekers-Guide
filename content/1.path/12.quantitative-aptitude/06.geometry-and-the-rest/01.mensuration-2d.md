Probability was the last of the counting topics. From here to the end of the
chapter the topics are formula-led: learn the table properly, then practise
until you stop looking at it. **Mensuration** means measuring shapes, and this
page is the flat ones.

## Why it matters

2D mensuration turns up in TCS NQT, Wipro, Capgemini, Cognizant and AMCAT-style
papers, usually as one question, and it hides inside data interpretation and
percentage questions too ("the length is increased by 20%..."). Check the
current pattern for your company.

It is almost entirely recall. If you know the formula, the question takes 30
seconds. If you do not, no amount of cleverness saves it.

## The formulas

| Shape | Area | Perimeter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rectangle `l × b` | `l × b` | `2(l + b)` |
| Square side `a` | `a²` or `d²/2` | `4a` |
| Triangle | `½ × base × height` | sum of sides |
| Equilateral triangle side `a` | `(√3/4) × a²` | `3a` |
| Circle radius `r` | `πr²` | `2πr` |
| Semicircle | `πr²/2` | `πr + 2r` |
| Sector, angle `θ` | `(θ/360) × πr²` | arc `(θ/360) × 2πr` |
| Parallelogram | `base × height` | `2(a + b)` |
| Rhombus, diagonals `d1`, `d2` | `½ × d1 × d2` | `4a` |
| Trapezium, parallel sides `a`, `b` | `½ × (a + b) × h` | sum of sides |

```text
Heron's formula (three sides a, b, c):
  s = (a + b + c) / 2
  area = √( s(s − a)(s − b)(s − c) )

Diagonal of a rectangle  = √(l² + b²)
Diagonal of a square     = a√2
Rhombus side             = √( (d1/2)² + (d2/2)² )
Wheel: distance in one revolution = circumference = 2πr
```

::tip
Use `π = 22/7` whenever the radius is a multiple of 7. Question setters pick
radii like 7, 14, 21 and 35 exactly so that it cancels.
::

## A worked example

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="Circle from its circumference" icon="i-lucide-circle"}
  **The circumference of a circle is 44 cm. Find its area.**

  ```text
  2πr = 44  →  2 × 22/7 × r = 44  →  r = 7
  Area = 22/7 × 7 × 7 = 154 cm²
  ```

  **Answer: 154 cm².**
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Path round a lawn" icon="i-lucide-square"}
  **A college lawn is 20 m by 15 m. A path 2 m wide runs round the outside.
  Area of the path?**

  The path adds 2 m on every side, so 4 m to each dimension.

  ```text
  Outer: 24 × 19 = 456
  Inner: 20 × 15 = 300
  Path:  456 − 300 = 156 m²
  ```

  **Answer: 156 m².** Adding 2 m instead of 4 m to each side is the classic
  slip.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Heron's formula" icon="i-lucide-triangle"}
  **A triangle has sides 13, 14 and 15 cm. Its area?**

  ```text
  s = 42 / 2 = 21
  area = √(21 × 8 × 7 × 6) = √7056 = 84 cm²
  ```

  **Answer: 84 cm².** (13, 14, 15) is a favourite; remember the answer.
  :::
::

## Common mistakes

- **Diameter for radius.** Read which one is given.
- **Path inside versus outside.** A path inside the lawn subtracts from the
  dimensions.
- **Adding percentages for area.** Length up 20% and breadth down 20% is not
  "no change": `1.2 × 0.8 = 0.96`, a 4% decrease.
- **Mixed units.** Metres and centimetres in one question is a deliberate trap.

## How to practise

- **Day one:** write the table from memory three times. Check it each time.
- **Days two and three:** 10 questions a day from IndiaBix "Area", target **40
  seconds**.
- **Then:** R.S. Aggarwal's mensuration chapter for volume, and one question in
  every mixed set.

## Practice questions

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. A square has a diagonal of 10 cm. Its area?"}
  **50 cm².** `d²/2 = 100/2`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. Length up 20%, breadth down 20%. Change in area?"}
  **4% decrease.** `1.2 × 0.8 = 0.96`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. The radius of a circle is doubled. The area becomes?"}
  **4 times.** Area goes with `r²`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. A rhombus has diagonals 16 and 12 cm. Area and side?"}
  **96 cm², side 10 cm.** `½ × 16 × 12 = 96`; side `√(8² + 6²) = 10`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="5. A wheel of radius 35 cm. Revolutions to cover 440 m?"}
  **200.** Circumference `2 × 22/7 × 35 = 220` cm = 2.2 m. `440 ÷ 2.2 = 200`.
  :::
::
