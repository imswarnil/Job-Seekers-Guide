Clocks tracked two hands through a day. Calendars track weekdays through years,
and the whole topic rests on one idea: **odd days**, the days left over once you
take out complete weeks.

## Why it matters

Calendar questions appear in TCS NQT, Wipro, AMCAT-style papers and in
reasoning sections, roughly one question when present. They are quick once the
odd-day tables are in your head, and slow and error-prone if you try to count
on your fingers. Check the current pattern.

## The rules

```text
Odd days = number of days mod 7

Ordinary year: 365 days = 52 weeks + 1  → 1 odd day
Leap year:     366 days = 52 weeks + 2  → 2 odd days

Leap year: divisible by 4,
  but a century year only if divisible by 400
  (2000 was a leap year, 1900 was not, 2100 will not be)

Odd days in centuries:
  100 years → 5     200 years → 3
  300 years → 1     400 years → 0

Month odd days:  31 days → 3,  30 days → 2,  Feb → 0 (or 1 in a leap year)

Day codes: 0 Sunday, 1 Monday, 2 Tuesday, 3 Wednesday,
           4 Thursday, 5 Friday, 6 Saturday
```

::tip
Most questions are type 1: you are given a day and asked about a later date.
You never need the century table for those. Count the days between, take mod
7, move forward that many days.
::

## A worked example

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="Next year, same date" icon="i-lucide-calendar"}
  **1 January 2025 was a Wednesday. What day was 1 January 2026?**

  2025 is an ordinary year: 1 odd day. Wednesday + 1 = **Thursday**.

  For 1 January 2024 to 1 January 2025: 2024 is a leap year, 2 odd days,
  Monday + 2 = Wednesday. Both are correct against a real calendar.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="A historical date" icon="i-lucide-flag"}
  **What day of the week was 15 August 1947?**

  Count the odd days up to that date.

  ```text
  1600 years                         → 0
  300 years (1601–1900)              → 1
  1901–1946: 46 years, 11 leap       → 11×2 + 35×1 = 57 → 57 mod 7 = 1
  1947, Jan–Jul: 3+0+3+2+3+2+3 = 16  → 2
  August: 15 days                    → 1
  Total: 0 + 1 + 1 + 2 + 1 = 5       → Friday
  ```

  **Answer: Friday.** That is correct.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="When does a calendar repeat?" icon="i-lucide-repeat"}
  **Which year has the same calendar as 2021?**

  Add odd days year by year until the total is a multiple of 7, and the target
  year is also an ordinary year like 2021.

  ```text
  2021: 1, 2022: 1, 2023: 1, 2024: 2, 2025: 1, 2026: 1  → total 7
  ```

  **Answer: 2027.** Both years begin on a Friday and neither is a leap year.
  :::
::

## Common mistakes

- **Calling 1900 a leap year.** Centuries need 400.
- **Counting the start date.** "61 days after Monday" moves 61 days forward;
  you do not count Monday itself.
- **Forgetting February's leap day** when the date range crosses it.
- **Matching the first day but not the leap status** in repeat-calendar
  questions.

## Practice questions

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. Odd days in 2000 years?"}
  **0.** 2000 is five lots of 400.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. Was 1900 a leap year?"}
  **No.** A century year must be divisible by 400.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. Today is Monday. What day is it 61 days from now?"}
  **Saturday.** `61 mod 7 = 5`. Monday + 5.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. What day was 26 January 1950?"}
  **Thursday.** Up to 1949: `0 + 1 + (12 × 2 + 37) mod 7 = 0 + 1 + 5 = 6`.
  Add 26 days: `26 mod 7 = 5`. `6 + 5 = 11`, and `11 mod 7 = 4`: Thursday.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="5. 1 January 2018 was a Monday. What day was 1 January 2019?"}
  **Tuesday.** 2018 is ordinary: 1 odd day.
  :::
::
