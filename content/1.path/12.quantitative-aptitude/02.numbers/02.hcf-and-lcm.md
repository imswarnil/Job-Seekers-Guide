Divisibility told you whether one number divides another. HCF and LCM ask the
same thing about a whole set of numbers at once, and this page covers how to
tell which one a question wants, because that decision is most of the work.

## Why it matters

One or two questions in most papers, a little more in TCS NQT and AMCAT-style
tests. They are rarely written as "find the HCF". They are written as bells
ringing together, tiles covering a floor, tape measuring several lengths, or
runners meeting at the start line. Once you see through the story, they take
under thirty seconds.

## What to study

- [ ] Finding HCF and LCM by **prime factorisation**
- [ ] **HCF x LCM = product** of two numbers
- [ ] **Which one the story wants**: "largest" or "greatest" usually means HCF, "smallest", "least" or "together again" usually means LCM
- [ ] Remainder variants: the same remainder, a fixed remainder, different remainders
- [ ] HCF and LCM of **fractions**

## The rules

```text [Definitions and the link between them]
HCF  (highest common factor): the largest number that divides all of them
LCM  (lowest common multiple): the smallest number all of them divide

By prime factors:
  HCF = each common prime, to its LOWEST power
  LCM = every prime that appears, to its HIGHEST power

For two numbers only:
  HCF x LCM = first number x second number

Fractions:
  HCF = HCF of numerators / LCM of denominators
  LCM = LCM of numerators / HCF of denominators
```

| The story says | It wants | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Largest tile, longest tape, biggest box that fits exactly | HCF | A size that divides every length |
| Bells, lights, runners "together again" | LCM | A time every interval divides |
| Smallest number divisible by all of them | LCM | |
| Largest number that divides them leaving the same remainder | HCF of the differences | The remainder cancels when you subtract |
| Smallest number that leaves remainder r when divided by each | LCM + r | |

## A worked example

::steps
### Find the HCF and LCM of 36 and 84
36 = 2^2 x 3^2. 84 = 2^2 x 3 x 7.
HCF: common primes at lowest power = 2^2 x 3 = **12**.
LCM: every prime at highest power = 2^2 x 3^2 x 7 = **252**.
Check with the link: 12 x 252 = 3,024, and 36 x 84 = 3,024.

### The HCF of two numbers is 12, the LCM is 360, one number is 72. Find the other.
Other = 12 x 360 / 72 = 4,320 / 72 = **60**. No factorising needed.

### Three bells ring every 6, 8 and 12 minutes. They ring together at 9:00. When next?
LCM of 6, 8, 12: 6 = 2 x 3, 8 = 2^3, 12 = 2^2 x 3, so LCM = 2^3 x 3 = 24.
They ring together again at **9:24**.

### Find the largest number that divides 43, 91 and 183 leaving the same remainder.
The remainder cancels in the differences: 91 - 43 = 48, 183 - 91 = 92,
183 - 43 = 140. HCF of 48, 92 and 140 is **4**. Check: all three leave
remainder 3 when divided by 4.
::

## Common mistakes

- **Picking the wrong one.** Read the question's last line before anything
  else. "Least number of tiles" is still an HCF question: the largest tile gives
  the fewest tiles.
- **Using HCF x LCM = product for three numbers.** It only holds for two.
- **Forgetting the remainder in "leaves remainder 4"**. The answer is LCM + 4,
  and LCM alone is always one of the options.
- **Lowest and highest powers the wrong way round.** HCF takes the lowest
  power because it must fit inside every number.

## How to practise

- **Eight questions a day** for two days, then fold into mixed practice.
- **Target: 30 seconds** for straight HCF or LCM, 45 seconds for story and
  remainder questions.
- **Where:** IndiaBix "Problems on H.C.F and L.C.M", PrepInsta's HCF and LCM
  page, and R.S. Aggarwal's chapter of the same name.
- For each story question, write "HCF" or "LCM" next to it before calculating.
  Getting that label right is the skill.

## Practice questions

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. Two numbers are in the ratio 3:4 and their HCF is 5. What is their LCM?"}
  **60.** The numbers are 3 x 5 = 15 and 4 x 5 = 20. LCM of 15 and 20 is 60.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. What is the smallest number exactly divisible by 8, 12 and 18?"}
  **72.** 8 = 2^3, 12 = 2^2 x 3, 18 = 2 x 3^2. LCM = 2^3 x 3^2 = 72.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. What is the longest tape that can measure 495 cm, 900 cm and 1,665 cm exactly?"}
  **45 cm.** 495 = 3^2 x 5 x 11, 900 = 2^2 x 3^2 x 5^2, 1,665 = 3^2 x 5 x 37.
  HCF = 3^2 x 5 = 45.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. What is the largest four-digit number divisible by 12, 15 and 18?"}
  **9,900.** LCM = 180. 9,999 / 180 = 55 remainder 99, so 55 x 180 = 9,900.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="5. What is the smallest number that leaves remainder 4 when divided by 12, 15 or 20?"}
  **64.** LCM of 12, 15, 20 is 60. Add the remainder: 64.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="6. Three runners take 30, 45 and 60 seconds a lap. After how long are they together at the start again?"}
  **3 minutes.** LCM of 30, 45, 60 is 180 seconds.
  :::
::
