Surds and indices were the rules for powers. This page is the speed: the
values you should know without working them out, and the tricks that make two-
digit multiplication a mental step instead of a written one.

## Why it matters

There is almost never a question titled "fast multiplication". There are sixty
questions that each end in a multiplication, and the paper does not allow a
calculator. Somebody who knows 17 squared is 289 saves ten seconds on every
question that needs it, and ten seconds across forty questions is six and a
half minutes: five or six more questions attempted.

When I practised in 2018, the people who finished a paper early were not
better at maths than me. They had squares up to 30 and the fraction table in
their heads, and I was still multiplying on paper.

## The five tricks

```text [Fast multiplication]
Ending in 5:     (n5)^2 = n x (n+1), then write 25
                 65^2 -> 6 x 7 = 42 -> 4225

Near 100:        97 x 96: deficits are 3 and 4
                 left = 97 - 4 = 93, right = 3 x 4 = 12 -> 9312
                 103 x 107: excesses 3 and 7
                 left = 103 + 7 = 110, right = 3 x 7 = 21 -> 11021
                 (right part always two digits: 2 becomes 02)

Times 11:        write the two digits apart, put their sum between
                 45 x 11 -> 4 (4+5) 5 -> 495
                 78 x 11 -> 7 (15) 8 -> carry the 1 -> 858

Either side:     (a + b)(a - b) = a^2 - b^2
                 48 x 52 = 50^2 - 2^2 = 2500 - 4 = 2496

Near a base:     (a - b)^2 = a^2 - 2ab + b^2
                 47^2 = 2500 - 300 + 9 = 2209
```

## Roots of perfect powers

```text [Square root of a perfect square]
Last digit of the square -> possible last digits of the root
  1 -> 1 or 9     4 -> 2 or 8     9 -> 3 or 7
  6 -> 4 or 6     5 -> 5          0 -> 0

Cube roots: the last digit fixes the last digit of the root exactly
  cube ends in 2 -> root ends in 8, 8 -> 2, 3 -> 7, 7 -> 3, others the same
```

## A worked example

::steps
### Square root of 784
Ends in 4, so the root ends in 2 or 8. Drop the last two digits: 7. The
largest square not above 7 is 4 = 2^2, so the root is 2-something. To choose
between 22 and 28, multiply the tens digit by the next number: 2 x 3 = 6.
7 is more than 6, so take the larger: **28**. Check: 28^2 = 784.

### Cube root of 1,75,616
Ends in 6, so the root ends in **6**. Drop the last three digits: 175. The
largest cube not above 175 is 125 = 5^3. So the root is **56**.
Check: 56^3 = 175,616.

### 97 x 96
Base 100, deficits 3 and 4. 97 - 4 = 93, and 3 x 4 = 12. **9,312**.
::

## Common mistakes

- **Using the base trick with a one-digit right part.** 98 x 99: deficits 2
  and 1, right part is 02, not 2. Answer 9,702, not 972.
- **Carrying in the times-11 trick.** 78 x 11 is 858, not 7158.
- **Cube roots of numbers that are not perfect cubes.** The two-step method
  only works on perfect cubes. If the question does not say so, check with an
  option.
- **Memorising without testing.** Knowing that you once learned 23^2 is not
  the same as producing 529 in one second.

## How to practise

- **Five minutes every morning** for the whole thirty days: write squares 1–30
  and cubes 1–15 from memory, and check them.
- **Twenty mental multiplications a day** for the first week, using the tricks.
  Target: under 10 seconds each.
- **Where:** any list of two-digit numbers works. IndiaBix "Square Root and
  Cube Root" for root questions, and the "Square Root and Cube Root" chapter of
  R.S. Aggarwal.

::tip
Say the square aloud when you check it. "Twenty-three squared, five hundred
and twenty-nine." It sticks faster than silent reading.
::

  **2,491.** 50^2 - 3^2 = 2,500 - 9.

  :::accordion-item{label="5. What is the square root of 1,156?"}
  **34.** Ends in 6, so 4 or 6. 11 is between 9 and 16, so 3-something.
  3 x 4 = 12, and 11 is less than 12, so the smaller: 34.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="6. What is the cube root of 39,304?"}
  **34.** Ends in 4, so the root ends in 4. 39 is between 27 and 64, so 3.
