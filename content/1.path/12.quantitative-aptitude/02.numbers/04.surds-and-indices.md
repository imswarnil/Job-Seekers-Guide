Simplification put powers and roots second in the order of operations. This
page is those two on their own: the laws, and the handful of question shapes
built from them.

## Why it matters

One or two questions in most service-company papers, more in AMCAT and eLitmus
style tests. They look frightening, with their fractional powers and nested
roots, and that is why people skip them. They are among the most reliable marks
in the paper, because every one of them falls to seven laws.

The same laws come back in logarithms and in compound interest, so learn them
once, properly.

## What to study

## The laws

A power (index, exponent) says how many times a number is multiplied by itself.
A surd is a root that does not come out whole, like the square root of 2.

```text [Laws of indices]
a^m x a^n   = a^(m+n)
a^m ÷ a^n   = a^(m-n)
(a^m)^n     = a^(mn)
(ab)^n      = a^n x b^n
a^0         = 1          (a not 0)
a^(-n)      = 1 / a^n
a^(m/n)     = (nth root of a)^m

Roots as powers:  square root of a = a^(1/2),  cube root of a = a^(1/3)
```

```text [Surds]
root(a) x root(b)     = root(ab)
root(a) ÷ root(b)     = root(a/b)
Simplify: pull out the largest square factor.  root(50) = root(25 x 2) = 5 root(2)
Add only like surds:  3 root(2) + 5 root(2) = 8 root(2)
Rationalise:          1 / (root(a) - root(b)) = (root(a) + root(b)) / (a - b)
```

## A worked example

::steps
### Solve 2^(x+3) = 32^(x-1)
Put both sides on the same base. 32 = 2^5, so the right side is 2^(5x-5).
Same base, so the powers are equal: x + 3 = 5x - 5, which gives 8 = 4x, so
**x = 2**. Check: 2^5 = 32 and 32^1 = 32.

### Find 27^(2/3) and 16^(-3/4)
27^(2/3): cube root of 27 is 3, squared is **9**.
16^(-3/4): fourth root of 16 is 2, cubed is 8, and the minus sign flips it:
**1/8**.

### Which is larger, root(2) or cube root of 3?
Different orders, so raise both to a common power. LCM of 2 and 3 is 6.
root(2)^6 = 2^3 = 8. (cube root of 3)^6 = 3^2 = 9. So the **cube root of 3**
is larger.
::

::tip
**Same base first.** Nearly every equation-in-the-power question is solved by
rewriting both sides as powers of 2, 3 or 5. Know the powers: 2 up to 2^10 =
1,024, 3 up to 3^6 = 729, 5 up to 5^5 = 3,125.
::

## Common mistakes

- **Adding powers when the bases differ.** 2^3 x 3^2 is 8 x 9 = 72, not 6^5.
- **a^m + a^n = a^(m+n).** Wrong. The laws are for multiplication, never
  addition. 2^3 + 2^3 = 16, not 2^6.
- **Adding unlike surds.** root(2) + root(3) is not root(5).
- **Forgetting the minus sign flips.** 2^(-3) is 1/8, not -8.

## How to practise

- **Eight questions a day** for three days.
- **Target: 40 seconds.** If you are writing more than three lines, you have
  not put things on the same base yet.
- **Where:** IndiaBix "Surds and Indices", PrepInsta, and R.S. Aggarwal's
  chapter of the same name.
- Write the powers of 2, 3 and 5 from memory until they are automatic.

## Practice questions

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. Solve 5^(x-2) = 125"}
  **x = 5.** 125 = 5^3, so x - 2 = 3.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. Find 64^(1/3) x 81^(1/4)"}
  **12.** Cube root of 64 is 4, fourth root of 81 is 3.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. Simplify root(50) + root(18)"}
  **8 root(2).** 5 root(2) + 3 root(2).
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. Find 9^(3/2)"}
  **27.** Square root of 9 is 3, cubed is 27.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="5. Simplify (2^3)^2 ÷ 2^4"}
  **4.** 2^6 ÷ 2^4 = 2^2.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="6. Which is larger, cube root of 4 or root(2)?"}
  **Cube root of 4.** Raise both to the 6th power: 4^2 = 16 against 2^3 = 8.
  :::
::
