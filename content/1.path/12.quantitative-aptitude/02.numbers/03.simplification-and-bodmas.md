HCF and LCM gave you common denominators for free. Simplification is where you
use them: a long expression, several operations, one answer. This page answers
what order to do things in, and how to avoid doing most of the arithmetic at
all.

## Why it matters

Simplification and approximation are a small share of service-company papers
(two or three questions) and a large share of some others: bank-style tests and
several AMCAT-style modules open with five or more. The real reason it matters
is time. Every other quant topic ends in a calculation like these, and a
fresher who simplifies slowly loses ten seconds on every question in the paper.

## What to study

- [ ] **BODMAS**: the order of operations
- [ ] **Fraction to percentage to decimal** equivalents, from memory
- [ ] **Approximation**: rounding to friendly numbers when the options are far apart
- [ ] Operations on fractions and decimals
- [ ] "Find the missing number" questions (the `?` in the middle of an equation)

## The order: BODMAS

```text [Brackets, Orders, Division, Multiplication, Addition, Subtraction]
1. Brackets              ( ) then { } then [ ]
2. Orders                powers and roots
3. Division and Multiplication, left to right, whichever comes first
4. Addition and Subtraction, left to right, whichever comes first
```

::warning
Division does **not** always come before multiplication. They share a level,
and you go left to right. 24 ÷ 6 x 2 is 4 x 2 = 8, not 24 ÷ 12 = 2. The
second answer will be among the options, because it is the expected mistake.
::

## The equivalents to know by heart

| Fraction | Percentage | Decimal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1/2 | 50% | 0.5 |
| 1/3 | 33.33% | 0.333 |
| 1/4 | 25% | 0.25 |
| 1/5 | 20% | 0.2 |
| 1/6 | 16.67% | 0.1667 |
| 1/7 | 14.28% | 0.1428 |
| 1/8 | 12.5% | 0.125 |
| 1/9 | 11.11% | 0.111 |
| 1/10 | 10% | 0.1 |
| 1/11 | 9.09% | 0.0909 |
| 1/12 | 8.33% | 0.0833 |
| 1/16 | 6.25% | 0.0625 |

These convert "12.5% of 640" into "640 ÷ 8 = 80", which you do in your head
without risk. The same table carries you through percentages, profit and loss
and data interpretation, so it is worth an evening.

## A worked example

::steps
### Simplify 36 + 24 ÷ 6 x 2 - 5
Division and multiplication left to right: 24 ÷ 6 = 4, then 4 x 2 = 8.
Then 36 + 8 - 5 = **39**.

### Simplify 3/4 + 5/6 - 1/3
LCM of 4, 6 and 3 is 12. 9/12 + 10/12 - 4/12 = 15/12 = **5/4**.

### Approximate 29.98 x 4.02 + 199.9 ÷ 10.05
Options are 120, 140, 160, 180. Round every number to its nearest friendly
value: 30 x 4 + 200 ÷ 10 = 120 + 20 = **140**. The exact value is about
140.4, and the options are twenty apart, so rounding costs nothing.
::

::tip
Look at the options **before** you calculate. If they are far apart, round
aggressively. If they are close together (say 138, 140, 142, 144), you need
more precision, and it may be a pass-two question.
::

## Common mistakes

- **Division before multiplication, always.** They are equal; go left to right.
- **Squaring a negative.** (-3)^2 = 9, but -3^2 = -9. Brackets decide.
- **Rounding in the same direction every time.** Round one number up and the
  next down when you can, so the errors cancel.
- **Calculating exactly when approximation would do.** Two minutes on long
  multiplication for an answer whose options differ by 50.

## How to practise

- **Fifteen questions a day for the first week.** Simplification is the
  warm-up that makes every other topic faster.
- **Target: 20 seconds** for BODMAS and approximation, 40 seconds for
  missing-number questions.
- **Where:** IndiaBix "Simplification" and "Decimal Fraction", PrepInsta, and
  R.S. Aggarwal's "Simplification" chapter.
- Write the fraction table from memory every morning for a week.

## Practice questions

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. Simplify 18 ÷ 3 x 2 + 4 - 6 ÷ 2"}
  **13.** 18 ÷ 3 = 6, x 2 = 12. 6 ÷ 2 = 3. 12 + 4 - 3 = 13.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. Simplify 4 + 8 ÷ 2 x 3 - (6 - 2)^2 ÷ 4"}
  **12.** Bracket: 4, squared: 16. 8 ÷ 2 x 3 = 12. 16 ÷ 4 = 4.
  4 + 12 - 4 = 12.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. Find 0.25 x 48 + 0.5 x 36"}
  **30.** A quarter of 48 is 12, half of 36 is 18.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. Find 36 ÷ (0.6 x 0.5)"}
  **120.** 0.6 x 0.5 = 0.3, and 36 ÷ 0.3 = 120.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="5. Approximately, 399.8 ÷ 19.9 x 5.1 is: (a) 80 (b) 100 (c) 120 (d) 140"}
  **(b) 100.** 400 ÷ 20 x 5 = 100. (Exact: about 102.5.)
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="6. 25% of 480 + 12.5% of 640 = ?"}
  **200.** 480 ÷ 4 = 120, 640 ÷ 8 = 80.
  :::
::
