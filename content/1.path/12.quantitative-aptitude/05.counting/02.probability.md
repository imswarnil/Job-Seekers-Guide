Permutations and combinations counted the ways a thing can happen. Probability
is two of those counts in a fraction: the ways you want, over all the ways
there are.

## Why it matters

Probability appears in most quant sections: TCS NQT, Infosys, Accenture,
Cognizant, Capgemini and AMCAT-style tests all include it often enough to
plan for one question, sometimes two. Check the current pattern.

Almost every written-round probability question uses one of four props: coins,
dice, a deck of cards, or a bag of coloured balls. Know those four props
cold, and the topic becomes counting.

## The props, as facts

| Prop | What you need to know |
| --- | --- |
| One coin | 2 outcomes. `n` coins: `2^n` outcomes (3 coins = 8) |
| One die | 6 outcomes. Two dice: 36 outcomes |
| A deck | 52 cards, 4 suits of 13. Hearts and diamonds red, spades and clubs black |
| Face cards | Jack, Queen, King: 3 per suit, 12 in all. Aces are not face cards |
| A bag | Draw 2 at once: use combinations, `nC2`, for both top and bottom |

## The formulas

```text
P(event)          = favourable outcomes / total outcomes
0 ≤ P ≤ 1
P(not A)          = 1 − P(A)
P(at least one)   = 1 − P(none)
P(A or B)         = P(A) + P(B) − P(A and B)
P(A and B)        = P(A) × P(B)            (independent events)
```

::tip
For two dice, draw the 6 × 6 grid once in your notebook and look at it until
you can see it. The sum 7 has 6 ways, the most of any. Sums 2 and 12 have one
way each.
::

## A worked example

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="Two dice" icon="i-lucide-dice-5"}
  **Two dice are thrown. What is the probability that the sum is 8?**

  ```text
  Favourable: (2,6) (3,5) (4,4) (5,3) (6,2)  → 5
  Total:      36
  P = 5/36
  ```

  **Answer: 5/36.** Count (3,5) and (5,3) separately: they are different
  outcomes on two different dice.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="A bag, two drawn" icon="i-lucide-shopping-bag"}
  **A bag has 5 red and 3 blue balls. Two are drawn at random. Probability both
  are red?**

  ```text
  Favourable: 5C2 = 10
  Total:      8C2 = 28
  P = 10/28 = 5/14
  ```

  **Answer: 5/14.**
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Or, with an overlap" icon="i-lucide-spade"}
  **One card is drawn from a deck. Probability it is a king or a heart?**

  ```text
  Kings 4, hearts 13, king of hearts counted twice → subtract 1
  (4 + 13 − 1) / 52 = 16/52 = 4/13
  ```

  **Answer: 4/13.**
  :::
::

## Common mistakes

- **Counting (2,6) and (6,2) once.** With two dice they are two outcomes.
- **Forgetting the overlap** in "or" questions.
- **Counting "at least one" case by case.** Use `1 − P(none)`: three coins,
  at least one head is `1 − 1/8 = 7/8`.
- **Calling aces face cards.** There are 12 face cards, not 16.
- **Not simplifying.** The options are in lowest terms; `10/28` will not be
  there, `5/14` will.
