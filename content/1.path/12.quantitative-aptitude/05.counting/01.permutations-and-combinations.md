The time chapter was about rates. This chapter is about counting: in how many
ways can a thing happen? It is the ground the next page, probability, stands
on, so it comes first.

## Why it matters

Permutations and combinations (P&C) shows up in TCS NQT, Infosys, Capgemini
and AMCAT-style papers, and it is heavier in the tougher papers (Infosys and the
"digital" or higher-package tracks of service companies). Expect roughly one
question, sometimes two, and check the current pattern.

It has a reputation for being hard, and for the tricky ones the reputation is
earned. But most written-round P&C questions come from six patterns, and those
six are learnable in two evenings. I skipped this topic entirely in college and
it cost me a question in every paper.

## What to study

| # | Pattern | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Arranging letters of a word, with repeated letters | High |
| 2 | Selecting a committee or team, with conditions ("at least one") | High |
| 3 | Some items always together, or never together | High |
| 4 | Forming numbers from digits, with or without repetition | Medium |
| 5 | Circular arrangements (people round a table) | Medium |
| 6 | Distributing identical things (rare in service-company papers) | Low, skip at first |

## The one question

Before any formula, ask: **does order matter?**

::compare
  :::compare-side{label="Order matters: permutation" verdict="neutral"}
  Arranging, ranking, seating, forming a number, a password.

  Choosing a class monitor and a deputy from 10 students: Ravi-then-Meera is
  different from Meera-then-Ravi. `10P2 = 90`.
  :::
  :::compare-side{label="Order does not matter: combination" verdict="neutral"}
  Choosing, selecting, a committee, a team, a group.

  Choosing 2 students for a committee from 10: Ravi-and-Meera is the same pair
  as Meera-and-Ravi. `10C2 = 45`.
  :::
::

## The formulas

```text
n! = n × (n−1) × ... × 1          0! = 1
nPr = n! / (n − r)!               (arrangements of r from n)
nCr = n! / (r! × (n − r)!)        (selections of r from n)
nCr = nC(n−r)                     (so 10C8 = 10C2 = 45)

Letters with repeats:   n! / (p! × q! × ...)
Circular, n people:     (n − 1)!
Necklace / garland:     (n − 1)! / 2
Items always together:  treat them as one block, then multiply
                        by the ways to arrange inside the block
"At least one":         total − none
```

::tip
Learn `1! to 7!` by heart: 1, 2, 6, 24, 120, 720, 5040. Most answers are built
out of these.
::

## A worked example

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="Letters with a repeat" icon="i-lucide-type"}
  **In how many ways can the letters of LEADER be arranged?**

  Six letters, E appears twice.

  ```text
  6! / 2! = 720 / 2 = 360
  ```

  **Answer: 360.** Forgetting the repeat gives 720, and it will be an option.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="A committee with a condition" icon="i-lucide-users"}
  **A committee of 5 is chosen from 6 men and 4 women. In how many ways, if it
  has exactly 2 women?**

  ```text
  Women: 4C2 = 6
  Men:   6C3 = 20
  Ways:  6 × 20 = 120
  ```

  **Answer: 120.** "And" multiplies. "Or" adds.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Two always together" icon="i-lucide-link"}
  **Five students sit in a row. Two of them must sit together. How many
  arrangements?**

  Glue the two into one block. Now there are 4 things to arrange, and the two
  inside the block can swap.

  ```text
  4! × 2! = 24 × 2 = 48
  ```

  **Answer: 48.** "Never together" is then `5! − 48 = 72`.
  :::
::

## Common mistakes

- **Using P when order does not matter**, or C when it does. Ask the question
  every time.
- **Forgetting to divide by repeated letters.**
- **Forgetting the arrangement inside a block** in "always together" questions.
- **Counting "at least one" case by case** instead of `total − none`.
- **Circular arrangements with `n!`.** Round a table, fix one person and
  arrange the rest: `(n − 1)!`.

## How to practise

::steps
### Evenings one and two
Patterns 1–3 only. IndiaBix "Permutation and Combination", 10 questions a
night, writing "order matters?" at the top of each.

### Days three to five
8 questions a day, all six patterns mixed. Target **60 seconds**.

### Then
Two in every mixed set. If a P&C question has not given you a pattern in 20
seconds, skip it and come back at the end.
::

## Practice questions

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. Arrangements of the letters of APPLE?"}
  **60.** `5! / 2! = 120 / 2`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. Ways to choose 3 students from 8?"}
  **56.** `8 × 7 × 6 / 6`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. Seven people round a table?"}
  **720.** `(7 − 1)! = 6!`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. Three-digit numbers from 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, no digit repeated?"}
  **60.** `5 × 4 × 3`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="5. A team of 4 from 5 boys and 4 girls, with at least one girl?"}
  **121.** Total `9C4 = 126`, all boys `5C4 = 5`. `126 − 5`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="6. Arrangements of BANANA?"}
  **60.** `6! / (3! × 2!) = 720 / 12`.
  :::
::
