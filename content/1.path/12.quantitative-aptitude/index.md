You know the subjects now. Java, SQL, the core four, the web, and the bits in
between that an interviewer asks about ten minutes in. None of it gets read if
you do not clear the test that comes first.

## The round that stopped me

In college I sat every written round that came to LNCT, and I did not clear
one. I told myself the tests were random. They were not. I had never practised
a single topic against a clock, and the people who cleared them had.

In Bangalore in 2018 I learned this properly, on my own, alongside the Java
classes at JSpiders. By the time I was going to walk-ins, the written round had
stopped being the part that ended my day. That change took about a month of
daily practice, and this track is that month, written down.

::note
Quantitative aptitude is the most learnable part of the whole process. The
question types have barely changed in fifteen years, they are public, and
every one of them falls to a method you can learn in an evening and speed up
in a week.
::

## What is in this track

::steps
### The written round
How the test is built, what each kind of company weights, the sixty-second rule,
and what to skip.

### Numbers
Number system, divisibility, HCF and LCM, simplification, surds and indices,
and fast arithmetic. The foundation every other chapter leans on.

### Arithmetic
Percentages, profit and loss, interest, ratio, partnership, averages, ages and
mixtures. The largest block of marks in almost every paper.

### Time
Time and work, pipes and cisterns, speed and distance, trains, and boats and
streams.

### Counting
Permutations, combinations and probability.

### Geometry and the rest
Mensuration, clocks, calendars, logarithms and progressions.

### Data interpretation
Tables, bar charts, line graphs, pie charts and caselets.

### Revision
Every formula on one page, and the thirty-day plan.
::

## How each page works

Every topic is a guide page, not a lecture. It tells you what the topic is,
which tests ask it and how often, what to study in priority order, the formulas,
a worked example with the shortcut, the mistakes people make, and how to
practise. A few questions with answers sit at the end of each page. Full
question banks come later.

::tip
Keep one notebook for this track and nothing else. Every formula goes in the
front, every mistake goes in the back with the reason you made it. The back
half is what you revise from the night before a drive.
::
