The cheat-sheet is the what. This page is the when: thirty days, every topic
in the order this track teaches them, and the routine that turns a formula you
understand into a question you finish in under a minute.

## Why it matters

Without a plan, aptitude practice drifts to the topics you already like, and
the weak ones never get touched. In Bangalore in 2018 I studied aptitude on my
own next to the Java classes at JSpiders, and what made the difference was not
talent. It was that every topic got its day, and the weak ones got a second
day.

Thirty days at about ninety minutes a day is enough to clear the quant section
of a service-company paper comfortably. It is not enough for eLitmus-level
difficulty; for that, repeat the plan with harder question sets.

## What to do

- [ ] Fix a **time of day** and protect it. Mine was early morning, before
      the institute.
- [ ] Keep **one notebook**: formulas in the front, mistakes in the back.
- [ ] Use a **timer** for every question from day one.
- [ ] Do the **mixed tests** on the days marked. They are where the progress
      shows.
- [ ] Take the **three mocks** in the last week under real conditions: one
      sitting, no phone, one rough sheet.

## The daily routine

::steps
### 10 minutes: warm-up
Squares 1–30 and cubes 1–15 from memory, the fraction table, and five mental
multiplications.

### 35 minutes: the day's topic
Read the page. Copy its formulas into the notebook. Work the worked examples
yourself without looking.

### 35 minutes: timed practice
Twenty to twenty-five questions on the day's topic from IndiaBix or
PrepInsta. Note the time per question.

### 10 minutes: the mistake log
Every wrong or slow question goes in the back of the notebook, with one line on
why. "Took profit on SP." "Forgot to convert km/h."
::

whether you can recognise it, which is what the real paper asks.

## Doing this with reasoning and verbal

The three written-round tracks each have a thirty-day plan. You have two
honest options.

| Option | How | When it fits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| In sequence | Quant for 30 days, then reasoning, then verbal | You have no drive for three months |
| In parallel | About 60 minutes of quant, 45 of reasoning, 30 of verbal, every day | You are already going to walk-ins, as I was |

I did it in parallel, because walk-ins do not wait for you to finish a plan.
If you do the same, stretch this plan to about six weeks.

## A worked example: reading your mock

::steps
### The result
Mock 1: 20 quant questions, 25 minutes. Attempted 15, right 11, and the log
shows 4 minutes on one probability question.

### What it means
Accuracy 11 of 15 is about 73%. The four wrong answers are the problem only
partly. The bigger loss is the 4 minutes, which is four easy questions never
reached.

### What changes
Day 25: probability relearned, and a rule written at the top of the next mock:
"no question past 90 seconds on pass one".
::

## Common mistakes

- **Skipping the mixed tests** because the topic days feel more productive.
- **Practising without a timer.** Untimed practice builds understanding, not
  speed, and the paper tests speed.
- **Starting mocks too late.** A mock on day 29 shows you a problem you have
  no time to fix.
- **Treating a bad mock as a verdict.** It is a list of what to study next. My
  first full mocks in 2018 were bad, and they were the most useful days of the
  month.

## How to practise

- **Where:** IndiaBix for topic practice, PrepInsta for company-wise papers
  (TCS NQT, Infosys, Wipro, Accenture, Cognizant, Capgemini), the company's own
  sample tests where they publish them, and R.S. Aggarwal's *Quantitative
  Aptitude* for depth on any topic that stays weak.
- **Volume:** about 25 questions a day on topic days, 30 on mixed-test days.
  About 650 questions across the month, mocks included.
- **Target:** by day 24, an average under 60 seconds a question with at least
  70% accuracy on a mock.

## Practice questions

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. A mock has 20 questions in 25 minutes. What is your time per question?"}
  **75 seconds.** 25 x 60 = 1,500 seconds, divided by 20.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. You attempted 15 and got 11 right. What is your accuracy?"}
  **About 73%.** 11 / 15 x 100 = 73.3.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. At 25 questions a day for 20 topic days and 30 for 3 mixed-test days, how many questions is that?"}
  **590.** 20 x 25 = 500, plus 3 x 30 = 90. With the three mocks, about 650.
  :::
::
