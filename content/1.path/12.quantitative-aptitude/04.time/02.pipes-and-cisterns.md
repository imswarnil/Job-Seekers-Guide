Time and work gave you rates that add. Here some of the rates are negative: a
pipe that fills is a worker, a leak is a worker undoing the job. That is the
only new idea on this page.

## Why it matters

Pipes and cisterns is usually counted inside time and work, and a paper that
has two time-and-work questions often makes one of them a tank. You will see it
in TCS NQT, Wipro, Capgemini, AMCAT-style tests and the R.S. Aggarwal chapter
that most question setters copy from. Treat it as one question per paper,
roughly, and check the current pattern.

A **cistern** is a water tank. An **inlet** fills it, an **outlet** or a **leak**
empties it.

## What to study

| # | Sub-type |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Two or three pipes open together, some filling, some emptying |
| 2 | A leak that makes filling slower: find how long the leak alone takes |
| 3 | A pipe closed or opened after some minutes |
| 4 | Tank partly full to begin with |
| 5 | Pipes opened alternately, one minute each (rare, slow, often worth skipping) |

## The rules

```text
Filling pipe:  rate = +1 / (time to fill)
Emptying pipe: rate = −1 / (time to empty)
Net rate       = sum of all open pipes
Time           = work to be done ÷ net rate

Fill in a hours, with a leak it takes b hours:
  leak alone empties the full tank in (a × b) / (b − a) hours
```

::note
If the net rate comes out negative, the tank never fills. It empties. Some
questions want exactly that answer, and "the tank will never be filled" is a
real option.
::

## The shortcut

Use the same total-work trick as the last page. Call the tank LCM units.

::steps
### LCM of all the times
Pipes of 4, 6 and 12 hours: the tank is 12 units.

### Units per hour, with signs
A fills `12 ÷ 4 = +3`. B fills `12 ÷ 6 = +2`. C empties `12 ÷ 12 = −1`.

### Net, then divide
`3 + 2 − 1 = 4` units an hour. `12 ÷ 4 = 3` hours.
::

## A worked example

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="The leak question" icon="i-lucide-droplet"}
  **A pipe fills a tank in 5 hours. Because of a leak it takes 6 hours. How
  long would the leak take to empty a full tank?**

  ```text
  Tank = LCM(5, 6) = 30 units
  Pipe alone: 30 ÷ 5 = 6 units/hour
  Pipe with leak: 30 ÷ 6 = 5 units/hour
  So the leak removes 6 − 5 = 1 unit/hour
  Leak alone: 30 ÷ 1 = 30 hours
  ```

  **Answer: 30 hours.** Formula check: `5 × 6 ÷ (6 − 5) = 30`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="A pipe closed partway" icon="i-lucide-timer"}
  **Pipe A fills a tank in 10 minutes, pipe B in 15. Both are opened, and B is
  closed after 3 minutes. How long does the tank take to fill in total?**

  ```text
  Tank = LCM(10, 15) = 30 units
  A = 3/min, B = 2/min
  First 3 minutes together: 3 × 5 = 15 units
  Remaining: 15 units, A alone at 3/min = 5 minutes
  Total: 3 + 5 = 8 minutes
  ```

  **Answer: 8 minutes.**
  :::
::

## Common mistakes

- **Forgetting the minus sign** on the outlet. Write the sign next to every
  rate before you add.
- **Starting from an empty tank when it is half full.** Read the first line
  again: "a tank is one-third full" changes the work to be done.
- **Mixing minutes and hours.** Convert everything to one unit first.
- **Spending three minutes on alternate-pipes questions.** They can be solved
  by cycles, but they are slow. If you are behind on time, skip them.

## How to practise

- **Daily:** 8 questions, after your time-and-work set, so you feel that it is
  the same method.
- **Target:** 40 seconds each.
- **Where:** IndiaBix "Pipes and Cistern", R.S. Aggarwal's chapter of the same
  name, and PrepInsta's company-wise pages for TCS and Wipro.

::tip
Two days of this topic is enough if time and work is already solid. Do not
spend a week here.
::

## Practice questions

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. A fills a tank in 20 minutes, B in 30. Both open?"}
  **12 minutes.** Tank 60: `3 + 2 = 5` a minute, `60 ÷ 5 = 12`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. One pipe fills in 8 hours, another empties in 12. Both open on an empty tank?"}
  **24 hours.** Tank 24: `+3 − 2 = 1` an hour.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. A pipe fills in 12 hours; with a leak, 15 hours. The leak alone empties a full tank in?"}
  **60 hours.** `12 × 15 ÷ (15 − 12) = 180 ÷ 3 = 60`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. Three pipes fill in 10, 12 and 15 hours. All open?"}
  **4 hours.** Tank 60: `6 + 5 + 4 = 15` an hour, `60 ÷ 15 = 4`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="5. A fills in 6 hours, B empties in 8. The tank is half full and both are opened. Time to fill?"}
  **12 hours.** Tank 24: net `4 − 3 = 1` an hour. Half the tank is 12 units.
  :::
::
