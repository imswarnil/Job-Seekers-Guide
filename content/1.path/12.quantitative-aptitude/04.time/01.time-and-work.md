Mixtures closed the arithmetic chapter. This chapter is about things happening
over time, and it looks like five topics until you see that it is one idea:
**work with rates, never with times.** This page is the first and most common
form of it.

## Why it matters

Time and work turns up in almost every quant section I have seen: usually one
or two questions in TCS NQT, Infosys, Wipro, Capgemini and AMCAT-style papers,
sometimes three if you count pipes and cisterns, which is the same topic in a
different costume. Check the current pattern for the company you are sitting,
but it is safe to assume at least one question.

It is also one of the fastest topics to master. Once the method is in your
hand, most questions take 30–45 seconds.

## What to study

In priority order:

| # | Sub-type | How often |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Two or three people working together | Almost every paper |
| 2 | Someone leaves or joins partway | Very common |
| 3 | Efficiency ("A is twice as good as B") | Common |
| 4 | Pairs given (A+B, B+C, C+A) | Occasional |
| 5 | Men, days and hours (`M1 D1 H1 = M2 D2 H2`) | Common in TCS and Wipro |
| 6 | Wages shared by work done | Occasional |

## The one idea

If you take 6 days to do a job, you do `1/6` of it per day. That fraction is
your **rate**. Rates add when people work together. Times never add.

::compare
  :::compare-side{label="Averaging the times" verdict="wrong"}
  Ravi takes 6 days, Meera takes 3. "Together, `(6 + 3) ÷ 2 = 4.5` days."

  Impossible: two people together must be faster than the faster one alone,
  and Meera alone takes 3.
  :::
  :::compare-side{label="Adding the rates" verdict="right"}
  `1/6 + 1/3 = 1/6 + 2/6 = 1/2` of the job per day.

  Half a job per day means **2 days**. Faster than Meera alone, as it must be.
  :::
::

::tip
Always check a combined answer against the fastest worker. It must be smaller.
That two-second check catches most arithmetic slips in this topic.
::

## The formulas

```text
Rate           = 1 / (days to finish alone)
Together       = 1 / (sum of rates)
Remaining work = 1 − (work already done)

If A and B together take T days, and A alone takes a days:
  B alone      = (a × T) / (a − T)

Two people:    together = (a × b) / (a + b)

Men and days:  M1 × D1 × H1 / W1 = M2 × D2 × H2 / W2
               (M men, D days, H hours a day, W amount of work)

Efficiency:    if A is k times as efficient as B, A takes 1/k of B's time
```

## The shortcut: total work as the LCM

Fractions are slow and easy to slip on. Instead, pretend the job has a number
of **units** equal to the LCM of the days given. Then every rate is a whole
number.

::steps
### Take the LCM of the times
A takes 12 days, B takes 18. LCM of 12 and 18 is 36. Call the job 36 units.

### Turn each person into units per day
A does `36 ÷ 12 = 3` units a day. B does `36 ÷ 18 = 2` units a day.

### Add, divide
Together: `3 + 2 = 5` units a day. `36 ÷ 5 = 7.2` days.
::

## A worked example

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="Someone leaves partway" icon="i-lucide-user-minus"}
  **A can finish a job in 20 days and B in 30. They work together for 6 days,
  then A leaves. How many more days does B take?**

  ```text
  Total work = LCM(20, 30) = 60 units
  A = 3 units/day, B = 2 units/day
  Together for 6 days: 6 × 5 = 30 units
  Remaining: 60 − 30 = 30 units
  B alone: 30 ÷ 2 = 15 days
  ```

  **Answer: 15 more days.** The question asks for the extra days, not the total
  (21). The total will be one of the options.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Efficiency" icon="i-lucide-gauge"}
  **A is twice as efficient as B. Together they finish in 14 days. How long
  does A take alone?**

  ```text
  Rates in the ratio 2 : 1, so say A = 2 units/day, B = 1 unit/day
  Total work = (2 + 1) × 14 = 42 units
  A alone: 42 ÷ 2 = 21 days
  ```

  **Answer: 21 days.**
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Men and days" icon="i-lucide-users"}
  **15 men finish a job in 24 days. How many days will 20 men take?**

  ```text
  M1 × D1 = M2 × D2
  15 × 24 = 20 × D2
  D2 = 360 ÷ 20 = 18 days
  ```

  **Answer: 18 days.** More men, fewer days. If your answer went up, you
  multiplied the wrong way round.
  :::
::

## Common mistakes

- **Averaging or adding times.** Always rates.
- **Answering the total when the question asks for "more days"**, or the other
  way round. Underline what is asked before you start.
- **Forgetting hours a day** in men-days questions. If the hours change, they go
  into the formula.
- **Using fractions when the LCM method is faster.** With three people and odd
  numbers of days, fractions cost you a minute.
- **Wages by time, not by work.** Wages are shared in the ratio of work done,
  which for people working the same days is the ratio of their rates.

## Practice questions

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. A takes 10 days, B takes 15. Together?"}
  **6 days.** LCM 30: A = 3, B = 2, together 5 a day. `30 ÷ 5 = 6`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. A and B take 12 days, B and C take 15, C and A take 20. All three?"}
  **10 days.** LCM 60: `(A+B) = 5`, `(B+C) = 4`, `(C+A) = 3`. Adding gives
  `2(A+B+C) = 12`, so `A+B+C = 6` units a day. `60 ÷ 6 = 10`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. A and B together take 8 days. A alone takes 12. B alone?"}
  **24 days.** `1/8 − 1/12 = 3/24 − 2/24 = 1/24`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. 12 men working 8 hours a day finish in 10 days. How many days for 16 men working 6 hours a day?"}
  **10 days.** `12 × 8 × 10 = 16 × 6 × D`, so `960 = 96D`, `D = 10`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="5. A can do a job in 18 days, B in 9. They earn ₹5,400 together for it. A's share?"}
  **₹1,800.** Rates are `1/18 : 1/9 = 1 : 2`, so A gets one third of ₹5,400.
  :::
::
