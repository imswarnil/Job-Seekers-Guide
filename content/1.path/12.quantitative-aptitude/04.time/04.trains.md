The last page gave you `D = S × T` and relative speed. A train question is
that formula with one extra thought: **the distance includes the train's own
length.**

## Why it matters

Train questions are a favourite of TCS NQT, Wipro, Capgemini and every
R.S. Aggarwal-derived paper, usually one question when they appear. They
are short, and with the rule below they take 30–40 seconds. They are also a
place where setters love to put the wrong answer you get from forgetting the
length as option (a).

## What to study

| # | The train passes... | Distance covered |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | A pole, a person standing, a signal | Its own length |
| 2 | A platform, a bridge, a tunnel | Its length + the platform's length |
| 3 | A person walking or a car moving | Its length, at relative speed |
| 4 | Another train, opposite directions | Both lengths, speeds added |
| 5 | Another train, same direction | Both lengths, speeds subtracted |

## The formulas

```text
Pass a pole or a standing person:  time = L ÷ S
Pass a platform of length P:       time = (L + P) ÷ S

Two trains, lengths L1 and L2, speeds S1 and S2:
  opposite directions:  time = (L1 + L2) ÷ (S1 + S2)
  same direction:       time = (L1 + L2) ÷ (S1 − S2)

Lengths are in metres, so convert km/h to m/s (× 5/18) first.
```

::note
Why the length counts: the train has "passed" the platform only when its last
coach leaves the far end. From the moment the engine reaches the platform to
that moment, the engine has travelled the platform plus the whole train.
::

## A worked example

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="A platform" icon="i-lucide-train-front"}
  **A 150 m train passes a 250 m platform in 20 seconds. What is its speed in
  km/h?**

  ```text
  Distance = 150 + 250 = 400 m
  Speed    = 400 ÷ 20 = 20 m/s
  In km/h: 20 × 18/5 = 72 km/h
  ```

  **Answer: 72 km/h.** Using only 250 m gives 45 km/h, and 45 will be in the
  options.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Two trains, both directions" icon="i-lucide-arrow-left-right"}
  **Trains of 120 m and 180 m run at 50 km/h and 40 km/h. How long to pass each
  other (a) in opposite directions, (b) in the same direction?**

  ```text
  Total length = 300 m
  (a) 50 + 40 = 90 km/h = 25 m/s → 300 ÷ 25 = 12 seconds
  (b) 50 − 40 = 10 km/h = 25/9 m/s → 300 × 9/25 = 108 seconds
  ```

  **Answers: 12 s and 108 s.**
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Pole and platform together" icon="i-lucide-ruler"}
  **A train passes a pole in 15 seconds and a 100 m platform in 25 seconds.
  How long is the train?**

  Same speed both times, so:

  ```text
  L ÷ 15 = (L + 100) ÷ 25
  25L = 15L + 1500
  L = 150 m
  ```

  Faster: the extra 10 seconds were spent on the extra 100 m, so the speed is
  10 m/s, and 15 seconds at 10 m/s is 150 m.
  :::
::

## Common mistakes

- **Forgetting the train's length.** The single most common error.
- **Not converting km/h to m/s** when lengths are in metres.
- **Adding speeds for the same direction.** Opposite adds, same subtracts.
- **Counting a person's length.** A person or a pole has no length in these
  questions. A platform, a bridge and another train do.

## How to practise

- **Daily for three days:** 10 questions from IndiaBix "Problems on Trains".
- **Target:** 35 seconds each. Say the distance out loud before you divide:
  "train plus platform".
- **Then:** two train questions in every mixed set until the mocks.

::tip
Memorise the `18 km/h = 5 m/s` family (36 = 10, 54 = 15, 72 = 20, 90 = 25).
Most train questions are built on these numbers so the arithmetic comes out
clean.
::

## Practice questions

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. A 180 m train at 54 km/h passes a pole. How long?"}
  **12 seconds.** 54 km/h = 15 m/s. `180 ÷ 15 = 12`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. A 240 m train at 72 km/h crosses a 360 m bridge. How long?"}
  **30 seconds.** `600 ÷ 20 = 30`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. A train at 60 km/h passes a man walking at 6 km/h in the same direction in 10 seconds. Its length?"}
  **150 m.** Relative speed 54 km/h = 15 m/s. `15 × 10 = 150`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. Two 100 m trains at 36 and 54 km/h in opposite directions. Time to pass?"}
  **8 seconds.** 90 km/h = 25 m/s. `200 ÷ 25 = 8`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="5. A train passes a pole in 12 s and a 180 m platform in 24 s. Its speed in km/h?"}
  **54 km/h.** The extra 12 s covered 180 m, so 15 m/s = 54 km/h.
  :::
::
