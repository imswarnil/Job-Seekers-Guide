## Why it matters

Speed and distance, with trains and boats counted in, is one of the most asked
topics in the whole written round. In TCS NQT, Infosys, Accenture, Cognizant,
Capgemini and AMCAT-style papers I would expect one to three questions across
the three pages of this chapter. Check the current pattern, but do not sit a
paper without this topic.

It also carries straight into the next two pages. Trains are speed and
distance with a length added. Boats are speed and distance with a current added.
Get this page solid and those two take a day each.

## The formulas

```text
Distance = Speed × Time
Speed    = Distance ÷ Time
Time     = Distance ÷ Speed

km/h → m/s : multiply by 5/18
m/s → km/h : multiply by 18/5

Average speed = total distance ÷ total time      (always)
Equal distances at a and b:  average = 2ab / (a + b)
Three equal distances at a, b, c: average = 3 / (1/a + 1/b + 1/c)

Relative speed, opposite directions: a + b
Relative speed, same direction:      a − b

Distance fixed: speed and time are inversely proportional
  new speed = (p/q) × usual  →  new time = (q/p) × usual
```

::tip
Learn four conversions by heart and you will rarely need to calculate one:
`18 km/h = 5 m/s`, `36 = 10`, `54 = 15`, `72 = 20`. Every multiple of 18 km/h
is a whole number of metres per second.
::

## The average speed trap

You go from your PG to a walk-in venue at 30 km/h and come back the same way at
60 km/h. What is your average speed?

::compare
  :::compare-side{label="Averaging the speeds" verdict="wrong"}
  `(30 + 60) ÷ 2 = 45 km/h`

  It is always one of the options, because the setter knows you will reach for
  it.
  :::
  :::compare-side{label="Total distance over total time" verdict="right"}
  Say the trip is 60 km each way. Out: 2 hours. Back: 1 hour.

  `120 km ÷ 3 hours = 40 km/h`. Shortcut: `2 × 30 × 60 ÷ 90 = 40`.
  :::
::

You spend twice as long at the slow speed, so the slow speed pulls the average
down. The average of two speeds is only right when the **times** are equal.

## A worked example

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="Late and early" icon="i-lucide-clock"}
  **Walking at 4 km/h I reach the office 10 minutes late. At 5 km/h I reach 5
  minutes early. How far is it?**

  The two times differ by 10 + 5 = 15 minutes = 1/4 hour.

  ```text
  d/4 − d/5 = 1/4
  (5d − 4d) / 20 = 1/4
  d / 20 = 1/4
  d = 5 km
  ```

  Shortcut: `distance = (product of speeds ÷ difference of speeds) × time
  difference = (20 ÷ 1) × 1/4 = 5 km`.

  **Answer: 5 km.** Check: 5/4 hours = 75 min, 5/5 = 60 min. Difference 15. Good.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Fraction of usual speed" icon="i-lucide-gauge"}
  **Walking at 3/4 of his usual speed, a student reaches college 20 minutes
  late. What is his usual time?**

  Speed becomes 3/4, so time becomes 4/3 of usual. The extra is 1/3 of usual.

  ```text
  (1/3) × usual = 20 minutes
  usual = 60 minutes
  ```

  **Answer: 60 minutes.** No distance needed at all.
  :::
::

## Common mistakes

- **Mixed units.** Speed in km/h, time in minutes. Convert before you divide.
- **Averaging speeds.** Total distance over total time, every time.
- **Adding speeds when both move the same way.** Same direction subtracts.
- **Rearranging the formula wrongly under pressure.** Draw the triangle: D on
  top, S and T at the bottom. Cover the one you want.

## Practice questions

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. 54 km/h in metres per second?"}
  **15 m/s.** `54 × 5/18 = 15`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. A bus covers 240 km in 4 hours and returns in 6. Average speed?"}
  **48 km/h.** `480 ÷ 10`. Not 50.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. At 60 km/h a journey takes 6 hours. What speed makes it 4 hours?"}
  **90 km/h.** Distance is 360 km. `360 ÷ 4 = 90`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. At 5/6 of her usual speed, she is 10 minutes late. Usual time?"}
  **50 minutes.** Time becomes 6/5 of usual; the extra 1/5 is 10 minutes.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="5. Two cyclists leave the same point in opposite directions at 12 and 18 km/h. Distance apart after 2.5 hours?"}
  **75 km.** Relative speed 30 km/h, `30 × 2.5 = 75`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="6. Three equal stretches at 10, 20 and 60 km/h. Average speed?"}
  **18 km/h.** `3 ÷ (6/60 + 3/60 + 1/60) = 3 ÷ (10/60) = 18`.
  :::
::
