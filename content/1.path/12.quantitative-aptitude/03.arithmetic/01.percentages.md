Fast multiplication pays for itself from this page on, because a percentage is
multiplication wearing a different name. This page answers: what do percentage
questions look like, which formulas do they use, and how do you get each one
done in under a minute?

## Why it matters

Percentages are the most common single topic in the written round, and they are
also hiding inside profit and loss, interest, data interpretation and mixtures.
In the papers I sat in 2018, and in the TCS NQT, Infosys, Wipro, Accenture,
Cognizant and AMCAT-style papers I have seen since, there were usually **2–4
direct percentage questions**, and percentages showed up again in another five
or six. Check the current pattern for the company you are sitting, but do not
expect this topic ever to be missing.

::note
If you are short of time, learn this page first. Every other page in the
Arithmetic chapter assumes it.
::

## The formulas

Everything on this page is one of these.

```text [Percentages]
Part                 = Whole × Rate ÷ 100
Percentage change    = (New − Old) ÷ Old × 100        divide by the OLD value
Successive a% and b% = a + b + (a × b ÷ 100)          use −a for a fall
Increase by r%       = multiply by (1 + r/100)        20% up   → × 1.2
Decrease by r%       = multiply by (1 − r/100)        20% down → × 0.8
A is r% more than B  → B is  r ÷ (100 + r) × 100  % less than A
A is r% less than B  → B is  r ÷ (100 − r) × 100  % more than A
Price up r%, spend the same → cut consumption by r ÷ (100 + r) × 100 %
Population after n years    = P × (1 + r/100)^n
Value after n years of depreciation = P × (1 − r/100)^n
```

### The fraction table

Learn this until you do not have to think. It turns "16.67% of 360" into
"a sixth of 360", which is 60.

| Fraction | Percentage | Fraction | Percentage |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1/2 | 50% | 1/9 | 11.11% |
| 1/3 | 33.33% | 1/10 | 10% |
| 1/4 | 25% | 1/11 | 9.09% |
| 1/5 | 20% | 1/12 | 8.33% |
| 1/6 | 16.67% | 1/15 | 6.67% |
| 1/7 | 14.29% | 1/16 | 6.25% |
| 1/8 | 12.5% | 1/20 | 5% |

::tip
Once you know 1/8 is 12.5%, you also know 3/8 is 37.5%, 5/8 is 62.5% and 7/8
is 87.5%. The table is about twenty facts, and they unlock about sixty.
::

## A worked example

### 1. Successive change

> A college's annual fee rises 10% one year and 20% the next. What is the total
> percentage rise?

```text [The long way, and the shortcut]
Long:      assume a fee of 45,000
           45,000 × 1.10 = 49,500
           49,500 × 1.20 = 59,400
           (59,400 − 45,000) ÷ 45,000 = 32%

Shortcut:  10 + 20 + (10 × 20 ÷ 100) = 32%
           or: 1.10 × 1.20 = 1.32  →  32%
```

The shortcut needs no starting number, which matters because the question
usually does not give you one.

### 2. The denominator swap

> 60% of applicants to a college are from the state and 40% from outside. 30% of
> state applicants and 45% of outside applicants are shortlisted. What
> percentage of the **shortlisted** applicants are from outside the state?

Almost everybody answers 45%. That is the share of outside applicants who were
shortlisted, not the share of shortlisted people who are from outside.

```text [Assume 100 applicants]
State:    60 × 30% = 18 shortlisted
Outside:  40 × 45% = 18 shortlisted
Total shortlisted  = 36

From outside: 18 ÷ 36 = 50%
```

**Assume 100** whenever the answer is a percentage and no total is given. The
total cannot change a percentage answer, so pick the one that is easiest.

### 3. Price and consumption

> The price of rice rises 25%. By what percentage must a hostel cut its rice
> consumption so that it spends the same amount?

```text [Shortcut]
r ÷ (100 + r) × 100 = 25 ÷ 125 × 100 = 20%
```

Check it: price × 1.25, quantity × 0.80, spend × 1.00. It holds.

## Common mistakes

::compare
  :::compare-side{label="Wrong" verdict="wrong"}
  Applications went from 400 to 500 and back to 400, so it rose 25% and fell
  25%.
  :::
  :::compare-side{label="Right" verdict="right"}
  It rose 25% (100 on a base of 400) and fell **20%** (100 on a base of 500). The
  base changed, so the percentage changed.
  :::
::

- **Dividing by the new value.** Percentage change is always on the old value.
- **Thinking a rise and an equal fall cancel.** A 25% rise then a 25% fall
  leaves you at 93.75, down 6.25%.
- **Confusing percentage points with percent.** Attendance going from 68% to 76%
  is a rise of 8 percentage points, which is a rise of about 11.8%.
- **Doing it in your head to look fast.** Most wrong answers I made in practice
  were a correct method with a slipped digit. Write the two lines.

::warning
Options in these questions are designed around the mistakes above. If 25% and
20% are both in the options, that is not a coincidence. Slow down for five
seconds and check which base the question uses.
::

## Practice questions

Sixty seconds each, on paper.

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. A course had 120 seats and now has 96. What is the percentage decrease?"}
  **20%.** `(120 − 96) ÷ 120 × 100 = 24 ÷ 120 × 100 = 20%`. Dividing by 96
  gives 25%, the classic wrong option.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. A fee falls 15% and then rises 15%. What is the net change?"}
  **Down 2.25%.** `−15 + 15 + (−15 × 15 ÷ 100) = −2.25`. Or `0.85 × 1.15 = 0.9775`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. A's salary is 25% more than B's. By what percentage is B's salary less than A's?"}
  **20%.** `25 ÷ 125 × 100 = 20%`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. A town of 20,000 people grows 10% a year. What is the population after 2 years?"}
  **24,200.** `20,000 × 1.1 × 1.1 = 20,000 × 1.21 = 24,200`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="5. A candidate needs 35% to pass. He scores 210 and fails by 35 marks. What are the maximum marks?"}
  **700.** The pass mark is `210 + 35 = 245`, and 245 is 35% of the maximum:
  `245 ÷ 0.35 = 700`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="6. Attendance rose from 68% to 76%. By how many percent did it rise?"}
  **About 11.8%**, which is 8 percentage points. `8 ÷ 68 × 100 = 11.76%`.
  "8%" confuses points with percent.
  :::
::
