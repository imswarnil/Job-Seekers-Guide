## Why it matters

Mixtures and alligation is asked in roughly **0–2 questions** a paper, more in
AMCAT-style, Capgemini and Cognizant papers than in TCS NQT. It also solves
questions that do not look like mixtures at all: boys and girls with different
average marks, two loans at different rates, a train and a car covering one
journey. If you know alligation, those take 20 seconds.

## The rules

**Alligation** is a rule for the ratio in which two ingredients must be mixed to
produce a mixture at a given average price or strength (the **mean**).

```text [The alligation cross]
     cheaper (c)            dearer (d)
               \           /
                 mean (m)
               /           \
       (d − m)               (m − c)

cheaper : dearer  =  (d − m) : (m − c)
```

```text [Other mixture rules]
Pure quantity after n replacements:
  final = initial × (1 − y ÷ x)^n
  x = total volume,  y = volume removed and replaced each time

Changing a ratio by adding one ingredient:
  the OTHER ingredient's quantity stays fixed; work from that.

Water costs ₹0. Treat it as the cheaper item at price 0.
```

::tip
The cross is diagonal subtraction. Put the two prices at the top, the mean in
the middle, and subtract across. The number under the cheaper item comes from
the dearer one, which is the part people get backwards.
::

## A worked example

### 1. Two prices

> Rice at ₹40 a kg is mixed with rice at ₹55 a kg to get a mixture worth ₹45 a
> kg. In what ratio are they mixed?

```text [Alligation]
      40          55
         \      /
           45
         /      \
   55 − 45      45 − 40
     = 10         = 5

Cheaper : dearer = 10 : 5 = 2 : 1
```

Check with a weighted average: `(2 × 40 + 1 × 55) ÷ 3 = 135 ÷ 3 = 45`. Correct.

### 2. Repeated replacement

> A container holds 40 litres of milk. 4 litres are taken out and replaced with
> water. This is done three times. How much milk is left?

```text [Working]
Each time, 4 of 40 litres is removed, so 36/40 = 0.9 of the milk stays.
Milk = 40 × 0.9 × 0.9 × 0.9 = 40 × 0.729 = 29.16 litres
```

### 3. Changing the ratio

> 60 litres of a mixture has milk and water in the ratio 2 : 1. How much water
> must be added to make the ratio 1 : 2?

```text [Working]
Milk  = 40,  water = 20
Milk stays at 40. For 1 : 2, water must be 80.
Add 80 − 20 = 60 litres of water
```

## Common mistakes

::compare
  :::compare-side{label="Wrong" verdict="wrong"}
  Cheaper : dearer `= (m − c) : (d − m) = 5 : 10 = 1 : 2`
  :::
  :::compare-side{label="Right" verdict="right"}
  Cheaper : dearer `= (d − m) : (m − c) = 10 : 5 = 2 : 1`. The mean is closer to
  the cheaper price, so there must be more of the cheaper item.
  :::
::

- **Swapping the ratio.** Sanity-check: the mean sits closer to whichever item
  there is more of.
- **Subtracting instead of multiplying** in replacement questions. Three
  replacements of 10% leave `0.9³ = 72.9%`, not 70%.
- **Letting both quantities change** when only water is added. The milk is
  fixed. Start from it.

## Practice questions

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. Tea at ₹120 a kg and ₹180 a kg is mixed to give ₹160 a kg. In what ratio?"}
  **1 : 2.** `(180 − 160) : (160 − 120) = 20 : 40`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. In a class, boys average 70 marks and girls average 85. The class average is 75. What is the ratio of boys to girls?"}
  **2 : 1.** Boys are the cheaper item: `(85 − 75) : (75 − 70) = 10 : 5`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. A vessel has 80 litres of milk. 8 litres are replaced with water, twice. How much milk is left?"}
  **64.8 litres.** `80 × 0.9 × 0.9 = 80 × 0.81`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. 20 litres of a solution is 20% alcohol. 5 litres of water are added. What percentage is alcohol now?"}
  **16%.** Alcohol is 4 litres out of 25: `4 ÷ 25 × 100`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="5. In what ratio must water be mixed with milk costing ₹60 a litre to get a mixture worth ₹50 a litre?"}
  **1 : 5 (water to milk).** Water costs 0. `(60 − 50) : (50 − 0) = 10 : 50`.
  :::
::
