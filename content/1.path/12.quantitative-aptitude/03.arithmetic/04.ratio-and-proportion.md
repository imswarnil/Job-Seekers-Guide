Interest compared a sum with itself over time. A ratio compares two things with
each other, and once you see it as a single multiplier you will find it inside
the next four pages as well.

## Why it matters

Ratio and proportion is asked directly in most papers, usually **1–2
questions**, and it is the engine inside partnership, ages, mixtures and a good
share of data interpretation. TCS NQT, Infosys, Wipro, Cognizant and
AMCAT-style tests all include it. If you are weak here, you lose marks on five
topics, not one.

## The rules

A **ratio** `a : b` says "for every a of one, there are b of the other". It
says nothing about size. Write the real quantities as `ax` and `bx`, where `x`
is the **multiplier**, and every question becomes finding `x`.

```text [Ratio and proportion]
Quantities in ratio a : b   →   ax and bx
Multiplier x = real quantity ÷ its part of the ratio
Dividing N in a : b : c     →   each part = N ÷ (a + b + c)

Proportion  a : b = c : d   →   a × d = b × c
Mean proportional of a and b      = √(a × b)
Third proportional to a and b     = b² ÷ a
Fourth proportional to a, b, c    = (b × c) ÷ a

Combining A:B = p:q and B:C = r:s
  → A:B:C = p×r : q×r : q×s        (make B the same in both)
```

::tip
To combine ratios, write them one above the other and make the shared term
equal by multiplying each ratio through. `2 : 3` and `4 : 5` share B: make B
equal to 12, and you get `8 : 12 : 15`.
::

## A worked example

### 1. The multiplier

> The ratio of students to faculty in a college is 22 : 3. There are 66 faculty.
> How many students?

```text [Two steps, every time]
Multiplier = 66 ÷ 3 = 22
Students   = 22 × 22 = 484
```

### 2. Combining ratios and dividing

> A : B = 2 : 3 and B : C = 4 : 5. Divide ₹1,050 among A, B and C.

```text [Working]
Make B equal:  A:B = 8 : 12,  B:C = 12 : 15
A : B : C      = 8 : 12 : 15    (35 parts)
One part       = 1,050 ÷ 35 = 30
A = 240,  B = 360,  C = 450     (check: 240 + 360 + 450 = 1,050)
```

### 3. Adding to both

> Two numbers are in the ratio 3 : 5. If 10 is added to each, the ratio becomes
> 5 : 7. Find the numbers.

```text [Working]
(3x + 10) ÷ (5x + 10) = 5 ÷ 7
7(3x + 10) = 5(5x + 10)
21x + 70   = 25x + 50
4x = 20  →  x = 5
Numbers: 15 and 25     (check: 25 : 35 = 5 : 7)
```

::note
When the options are numbers, checking them is often faster than the algebra.
Only 15 and 25 are in ratio 3 : 5 and become 5 : 7 after adding 10.
::

## Common mistakes

- **Treating the ratio as the quantity.** 3 : 5 does not mean there are 3 and 5.
- **Combining ratios without making the shared term equal.** `2:3` and `4:5` do
  not make `2:3:5`.
- **Adding to one side only** when the question says "each".
- **Coins by count versus value.** A ratio of 5 : 6 : 8 in *number* of coins is
  not the ratio of their *values*. Convert to rupees before dividing.

## Practice questions

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. Divide ₹1,200 in the ratio 5 : 3 : 2."}
  **₹600, ₹360, ₹240.** 10 parts, one part is 120.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. Find the mean proportional of 9 and 16."}
  **12.** `√(9 × 16) = √144 = 12`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. Find the fourth proportional to 4, 9 and 12."}
  **27.** `9 × 12 ÷ 4 = 27`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. A bag has ₹1, 50 p and 25 p coins in the ratio 5 : 6 : 8 by number. The total is ₹210. How many of each?"}
  **105, 126, 168.** One set of 5, 6 and 8 coins is worth
  `5 + 3 + 2 = ₹10`. `210 ÷ 10 = 21` sets, so `5 × 21`, `6 × 21`, `8 × 21`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="5. Two salaries are in the ratio 3 : 4. Each rises by ₹5,000 and the ratio becomes 4 : 5. Find the salaries."}
  **₹15,000 and ₹20,000.** `5(3x + 5,000) = 4(4x + 5,000)` gives
  `15x + 25,000 = 16x + 20,000`, so `x = 5,000`.
  :::
::
