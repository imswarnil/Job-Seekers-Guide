Percentages told you how to measure a change against a base. Profit and loss is
the same idea with two different bases, and the whole topic comes down to
knowing which base each question is using.

## Why it matters

Profit and loss is a favourite of every service-company paper: TCS NQT,
Infosys, Wipro, Accenture, Cognizant, Capgemini and AMCAT-style tests usually
have **1–3 questions** on it, and it turns up again inside data interpretation
sets. It is also one of the fastest topics to score on once the formulas are
automatic, often under 45 seconds a question.

## The formulas

```text [Profit and loss]
Profit          = SP − CP              (SP = selling price, CP = cost price)
Profit %        = Profit ÷ CP × 100    always on CP
Loss %          = Loss ÷ CP × 100      always on CP
SP              = CP × (100 + P%) ÷ 100
CP              = SP × 100 ÷ (100 + P%)
                  for a loss, use (100 − L%) instead

Discount        = MP − SP              (MP = marked price, the label price)
Discount %      = Discount ÷ MP × 100  always on MP
SP after discount = MP × (100 − D%) ÷ 100

Successive discounts d1% and d2%  = d1 + d2 − (d1 × d2 ÷ 100)
Same SP, one at x% gain and one at x% loss → always a LOSS of x² ÷ 100 %
False weight: gain % = Error ÷ (True value − Error) × 100
```

::tip
Think in multipliers again. Marked up 40% is × 1.4. A 20% discount is × 0.8.
Marked up 40% then discounted 20% is `1.4 × 0.8 = 1.12`, a 12% profit. One
multiplication, no cost price needed.
::

## A worked example

### 1. Mark-up and discount

> A shopkeeper marks a laptop bag 40% above cost and then gives a 20% discount.
> What is his profit percentage?

```text [Shortcut]
1.40 × 0.80 = 1.12  →  profit of 12%
```

Assume a cost of ₹100 if you prefer: marked at ₹140, 20% off ₹140 is ₹28, sold
at ₹112, profit ₹12 on ₹100.

### 2. Same selling price, gain and loss

> Two phones are sold for ₹12,000 each. One is sold at a 20% gain and the other
> at a 20% loss. What is the overall result?

```text [The long way]
Phone 1 CP = 12,000 ÷ 1.2 = 10,000
Phone 2 CP = 12,000 ÷ 0.8 = 15,000
Total CP   = 25,000     Total SP = 24,000
Loss       = 1,000 on 25,000 = 4%
```

```text [Shortcut]
Same SP, same x% gain and loss → loss of x² ÷ 100 = 20 × 20 ÷ 100 = 4%
```

It is always a loss, because the losing item had the larger cost price.

### 3. False weights

> A dealer claims to sell at cost price but uses a 900 g weight instead of 1 kg.
> What is his gain percentage?

```text [Shortcut]
Error = 100 g,  True value − Error = 900 g
Gain % = 100 ÷ 900 × 100 = 11.11%   (11 1/9 %)
```

He charges for 1,000 g and gives 900 g, so his gain is on what he actually gave.

## Common mistakes

::compare
  :::compare-side{label="Wrong" verdict="wrong"}
  Sold for ₹1,200 at a 20% profit, so the profit is 20% of 1,200, which is ₹240.
  :::
  :::compare-side{label="Right" verdict="right"}
  Profit is on cost price. `CP = 1,200 ÷ 1.2 = 1,000`, so the profit is ₹200.
  :::
::

- **Taking profit percentage on the selling price.** The single most common
  error. Profit and loss are on CP, discount is on MP.
- **Adding successive discounts.** 20% and 10% off is 28%, not 30%.
- **Expecting "same gain and loss" to break even.** It is always a loss.
- **Dividing by 900 or 1,000 at random in false-weight questions.** The
  denominator is what the customer actually received.

## Practice questions

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. A book costs ₹800 and is sold for ₹920. What is the profit percentage?"}
  **15%.** `120 ÷ 800 × 100 = 15%`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. Selling a watch for ₹1,140 gives a 5% loss. At what price should it be sold for a 5% gain?"}
  **₹1,260.** `CP = 1,140 ÷ 0.95 = 1,200`, then `1,200 × 1.05 = 1,260`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. What single discount equals successive discounts of 20% and 10%?"}
  **28%.** `20 + 10 − (20 × 10 ÷ 100) = 28`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. A trader buys pens at 6 for ₹5 and sells them at 5 for ₹6. What is his gain percentage?"}
  **44%.** CP of one pen is `5/6`, SP is `6/5`. `(6/5) ÷ (5/6) = 36/25 = 1.44`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="5. The marked price is ₹2,500. After a 12% discount there is still a 10% profit. Find the cost price."}
  **₹2,000.** `SP = 2,500 × 0.88 = 2,200`, `CP = 2,200 ÷ 1.1 = 2,000`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="6. The profit on selling at ₹480 equals the loss on selling at ₹360. What is the cost price?"}
  **₹420.** CP sits exactly halfway: `(480 + 360) ÷ 2 = 420`.
  :::
::
