Partnership split one total unevenly between people. An average does the
opposite: it asks what single value, given to everybody, would make the same
total. Hold on to the word *total*, because it solves most of this page.

## Why it matters

Averages appear in nearly every paper, usually **1–2 questions**, in TCS NQT,
Infosys, Wipro, Accenture, Cognizant and AMCAT-style tests. They also sit
inside data interpretation ("what is the average production over five
years?") and inside ages. Most average questions can be done in 30 seconds with
the deviation method, and most people take two minutes because they do not
know it.

## The rules

```text [Averages]
Average = Sum ÷ Count          →    Sum = Average × Count

A new value joins n values with average A:
  new average = A + (new value − A) ÷ (n + 1)

Replacing one person changes the average of n people by k:
  new person = old person + n × k

Combined average of two groups:
  (n1 × A1 + n2 × A2) ÷ (n1 + n2)

A value misread by d:  corrected average = old average ± d ÷ n

Average of consecutive numbers   = (first + last) ÷ 2   = the middle one
Average of first n natural numbers = (n + 1) ÷ 2
```

::tip
**The deviation method.** When a new value joins, ask how far it is from the old
average, and share that difference out among everybody now in the group. A
teacher scoring 31 above a class average of 30 students lifts it by
`31 ÷ 31 = 1`.
::

## A worked example

### 1. A new value joins

> The average marks of 30 students is 62. When the teacher's marks are included
> the average of 31 becomes one more. What did the teacher score?

```text [The long way]
Old sum = 30 × 62 = 1,860
New sum = 31 × 63 = 1,953
Teacher = 1,953 − 1,860 = 93
```

```text [Deviation shortcut]
The average rose by 1, and there are now 31 people.
The teacher must bring 1 extra mark for each of the 31:
Teacher = old average + 31 × 1 = 62 + 31 = 93
```

### 2. Replacement

> The average weight of 8 people increases by 2.5 kg when a person weighing
> 65 kg is replaced by someone new. What does the new person weigh?

```text [Shortcut]
New = old + n × k = 65 + 8 × 2.5 = 65 + 20 = 85 kg
```

The total went up by 20 kg, and all of that came from the swap.

### 3. The overlap question

> The average of 11 results is 50. The average of the first six is 49 and of the
> last six is 52. Find the sixth result.

```text [Working]
First six + last six = 6 × 49 + 6 × 52 = 294 + 312 = 606
All eleven           = 11 × 50 = 550
Sixth (counted twice) = 606 − 550 = 56
```

## Common mistakes

- **Averaging averages.** Class A (30 students, 60) and Class B (20 students,
  70) do not average to 65. Weight by size: `(1,800 + 1,400) ÷ 50 = 64`.
- **Dividing by the old count** after someone joins.
- **Forgetting the overlap** when two groups share a value.
- **Average speed as the mean of two speeds.** Covered properly in Time, speed
  and distance, but know now that 30 km/h out and 60 km/h back is not 45.

::warning
In the batsman question (average rises by k after an innings), people solve for
the old average and give that as the answer. Read which average the question
asks for, old or new, before you pick the option.
::

## Practice questions

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. What is the average of the first 50 natural numbers?"}
  **25.5.** `(50 + 1) ÷ 2`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. A batsman scores 87 in his 17th innings and raises his average by 3. What is his average after 17 innings?"}
  **39.** Old average `x`: `16x + 87 = 17(x + 3)`, so `x = 87 − 51 = 36`, and
  the new average is `39`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. Class A has 30 students averaging 60 and class B has 20 averaging 70. What is the combined average?"}
  **64.** `(30 × 60 + 20 × 70) ÷ 50 = 3,200 ÷ 50`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. The average of 10 numbers is 40. A 55 was copied as 25. What is the correct average?"}
  **43.** The sum was short by 30, so `40 + 30 ÷ 10 = 43`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="5. The average of 5 consecutive odd numbers is 27. What is the largest?"}
  **31.** The average is the middle one: `23, 25, 27, 29, 31`.
  :::
::
