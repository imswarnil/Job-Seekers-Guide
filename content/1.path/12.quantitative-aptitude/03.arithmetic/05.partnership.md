Ratio turned every comparison into a multiplier. Partnership adds one idea to
it: money that stays in a business longer earns a bigger share, so you multiply
each person's money by the months it was there.

## Why it matters

Partnership is a small topic, usually **0–1 question** in TCS NQT, Infosys,
Wipro and AMCAT-style papers, and more common in Cognizant and Capgemini ones.
It is worth learning because it is almost free: one rule, and questions that
take 40 seconds once you have seen five of them.

## What to study

| # | Sub-type | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Same time, different capital: share in ratio of capital | Must |
| 2 | Different times: share in ratio of capital × months | Must |
| 3 | A partner joins late or leaves early | High |
| 4 | A partner adds or withdraws money partway through | High |
| 5 | Working partner paid a salary or a percentage first | Medium |
| 6 | Finding capital or time from a given profit ratio | Medium |

## The rule

**Capital** is the money each partner puts in.

```text [Partnership]
Same time for everyone:   profit ratio = capital ratio
Different times:          profit ratio = (capital × months) for each partner
Money changes partway:    add each period separately
                          e.g. 50,000 for 6 months + 30,000 for 6 months
Working partner salary:   take it out of the profit FIRST,
                          then share the rest in the capital ratio
```

::tip
Divide every product by a common factor before comparing. `360,000 : 360,000`
is `1 : 1`, and `480,000 : 420,000` is `8 : 7`. Big numbers are where digits
slip.
::

## A worked example

### 1. Joining late

> A starts a business with ₹30,000. After 4 months B joins with ₹45,000. At the
> end of the year the profit is ₹28,000. How is it shared?

```text [Working]
A: 30,000 × 12 = 360,000
B: 45,000 ×  8 = 360,000
Ratio 1 : 1   →   ₹14,000 each
```

B put in more money, but for less time, and here it balances exactly.

### 2. Withdrawing partway

> A invests ₹50,000 and B invests ₹35,000. After 6 months A withdraws ₹20,000.
> The year's profit is ₹30,000. Find each share.

```text [Working]
A: 50,000 × 6 + 30,000 × 6 = 300,000 + 180,000 = 480,000
B: 35,000 × 12             = 420,000
Ratio 480 : 420 = 8 : 7   (15 parts)
One part = 30,000 ÷ 15 = 2,000
A = 16,000,  B = 14,000
```

## Common mistakes

::compare
  :::compare-side{label="Wrong" verdict="wrong"}
  B joined after 4 months, so multiply B's capital by 4.
  :::
  :::compare-side{label="Right" verdict="right"}
  B was in the business for `12 − 4 = 8` months. Multiply by the months the
  money was **in**, not the month it arrived.
  :::
::

- **Ignoring time** and sharing in the capital ratio when people joined at
  different points.
- **Sharing the salary** along with the profit. The working partner's salary
  comes off the top.
- **Forgetting the months after a withdrawal.** The reduced amount still earns
  for the rest of the year.

## Practice questions

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. A, B and C invest ₹20,000, ₹30,000 and ₹50,000 for a year. The profit is ₹15,000. Find each share."}
  **₹3,000, ₹4,500, ₹7,500.** Ratio `2 : 3 : 5`, 10 parts of ₹1,500.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. A invests ₹12,000 for 12 months and B invests ₹16,000 for 9 months. What is the ratio of their profits?"}
  **1 : 1.** `144,000 : 144,000`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. A and B invest in the ratio 3 : 2. A is the working partner and takes 10% of the profit as salary. The profit is ₹50,000. What does A get in total?"}
  **₹32,000.** Salary `5,000`. Remaining `45,000` in `3 : 2` is `27,000` and
  `18,000`. A gets `5,000 + 27,000`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. A invests for 8 months and B for 10 months. Their profits are in the ratio 5 : 4. What is the ratio of their capitals?"}
  **25 : 16.** Capital `= profit ÷ time`: `5/8 : 4/10`. Multiply both by 40:
  `25 : 16`. Check: `25 × 8 = 200`, `16 × 10 = 160`, and `200 : 160 = 5 : 4`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="5. A starts with ₹20,000. After 6 months C joins with ₹20,000. The year's profit is ₹18,000. What is C's share?"}
  **₹6,000.** `240,000 : 120,000 = 2 : 1`, so C gets one third.
  :::
::
