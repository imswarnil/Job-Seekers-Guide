Averages taught you to think about a group's total. Ages questions use that,
and ratios, and add one more idea: when time moves, it moves by the same number
of years for everybody.

## Why it matters

Problems on ages appear in most service-company papers, usually **1 question**,
sometimes 2, in TCS NQT, Infosys, Wipro, Accenture and AMCAT-style tests. They
are among the most predictable questions in the whole paper. The wording
changes; the shape almost never does. That makes them some of the cheapest
marks available.

## What to study

| # | Sub-type | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | "A is k times B now; after n years, A is m times B" | Must |
| 2 | Present ratio, and a ratio n years ago or hence | Must |
| 3 | Sum of ages with a condition in the past | High |
| 4 | Average age of a family, then and now | High |
| 5 | Three people, chained conditions | Medium |

## The method

::steps
### Choose one unknown
Usually the youngest person's present age, or the ratio multiplier `x`.

### Draw a small timeline
Three columns: *years ago*, *now*, *years hence*. Fill in each person's age as
an expression in `x`.

### Write one equation from the condition
Ratio conditions cross-multiply. "Twice" or "three times" become a
multiplication.

### Check the answer against every condition
It takes five seconds and catches the most common mistake on this page.
::

```text [Ages]
n years ago:    age − n     for EVERY person
n years hence:  age + n     for EVERY person
Present ratio a : b         →   ax and bx
Average age of a group of k, n years later  →  sum rises by k × n
The difference between two people's ages never changes
```

::tip
When the options are pairs of ages, test them. Four options, two conditions,
maybe 20 seconds. It is often faster than solving, and it is never wrong.
::

## A worked example

### 1. Times, now and later

> A father is three times as old as his son. In 15 years he will be twice as old
> as his son. Find their present ages.

```text [Working]
          now      in 15 years
Son       s        s + 15
Father    3s       3s + 15

3s + 15 = 2(s + 15)
3s + 15 = 2s + 30
s = 15   →   father 45
Check: in 15 years, 60 and 30. Twice. Correct.
```

### 2. Ratio now and then

> The ratio of A's and B's ages is 4 : 5. Five years ago it was 7 : 9. Find
> their present ages.

```text [Working]
(4x − 5) ÷ (5x − 5) = 7 ÷ 9
9(4x − 5) = 7(5x − 5)
36x − 45  = 35x − 35
x = 10   →   A = 40, B = 50
Check: five years ago, 35 and 45, and 35 : 45 = 7 : 9. Correct.
```

### 3. Family averages

> Three years ago the average age of a husband, wife and child was 27. Five
> years ago the average age of the wife and child was 20. How old is the husband
> now?

```text [Working]
Husband + wife + child now = 3 × 27 + 3 × 3 = 81 + 9 = 90
Wife + child now           = 2 × 20 + 2 × 5 = 40 + 10 = 50
Husband now                = 90 − 50 = 40
```

## Common mistakes

::compare
  :::compare-side{label="Wrong" verdict="wrong"}
  Five years ago the father was `3s − 5` and the son was `s`.
  :::
  :::compare-side{label="Right" verdict="right"}
  Five years ago the father was `3s − 5` and the son was `s − 5`. Time moves for
  everybody.
  :::
::

- **Moving one person's age and not the other's.** The most common error, shown
  above.
- **Adding years to a group total once.** Three people who each age 3 years add
  9 years to the total, not 3.
- **Answering with the age at the wrong time.** The question asks "present age"
  and you found the age five years ago. Read the last line again.

## Practice questions

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. Two present ages are in the ratio 3 : 4 and add up to 56. What will the ratio be in 4 years?"}
  **7 : 9.** Ages are 24 and 32. In 4 years, 28 and 36, and `28 : 36 = 7 : 9`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. A is 6 years older than B. In 4 years their ages will be in the ratio 5 : 4. Find their present ages."}
  **26 and 20.** `4(B + 10) = 5(B + 4)` gives `B = 20`. Check: in 4 years, 30
  and 24.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. A mother and daughter's ages add up to 50. Five years ago the mother was 7 times as old as the daughter. Find their ages."}
  **40 and 10.** `M − 5 = 7(D − 5)` and `M + D = 50` give `8D − 30 = 50`, so
  `D = 10`. Check: five years ago, 35 and 5.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. Ten years ago A was half B's age. Their present ages are in the ratio 3 : 4. What is the total of their present ages?"}
  **35.** `2(3x − 10) = 4x − 10` gives `x = 5`, so 15 and 20. Ten years ago,
  5 and 10.
  :::
::
