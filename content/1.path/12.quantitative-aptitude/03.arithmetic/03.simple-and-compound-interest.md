Profit applied a percentage once. Interest applies a percentage every year, and
the only question is whether each year's percentage is taken on the original sum
or on the growing one. That single difference is this whole page.

## Why it matters

Interest questions appear in most service-company papers, usually **1–2
questions**, and they are more common in bank-style and AMCAT-style tests.
TCS NQT, Infosys, Accenture and Capgemini papers I have seen ask about the
difference between compound and simple interest more than anything else on
the topic, because it has a shortcut that separates people who practised from
people who did not.

## The formulas

Words first: **principal** (P) is the sum borrowed or invested, **rate** (R) is
the percentage per year, **time** (T) is in years, **amount** (A) is principal
plus interest.

```text [Simple interest: the same interest every year]
SI = P × R × T ÷ 100
A  = P + SI
Sum doubles in T years  →  R = 100 ÷ T
```

```text [Compound interest: interest on interest]
A  = P × (1 + R/100)^T
CI = A − P
Half-yearly:  rate R/2,  time 2T
Quarterly:    rate R/4,  time 4T

CI − SI for 2 years = P × (R/100)²
CI − SI for 3 years = P × (R/100)² × (3 + R/100)
Two years at R% compound = one year at (2R + R²/100)%
```

::tip
Learn these multipliers: `1.1² = 1.21`, `1.1³ = 1.331`, `1.05² = 1.1025`,
`1.05³ = 1.157625`, `1.2² = 1.44`, `1.2³ = 1.728`. Most CI questions use 5%, 10%
or 20%, and the cube is the slow part if you have to work it out.
::

## A worked example

### 1. Both kinds on the same sum

> Find the simple and compound interest on ₹10,000 at 10% a year for 2 years.

```text [Working]
SI = 10,000 × 10 × 2 ÷ 100 = 2,000
A  = 10,000 × 1.21 = 12,100   →  CI = 2,100
Difference = 100
Shortcut check: 10,000 × (10/100)² = 10,000 × 0.01 = 100
```

The extra ₹100 is the second year's interest on the first year's interest:
10% of ₹1,000.

### 2. The difference question, backwards

> The difference between compound and simple interest on a sum at 5% for 2
> years is ₹25. Find the sum.

```text [Shortcut]
P × (5/100)² = 25
P × 0.0025   = 25
P            = 25 ÷ 0.0025 = 10,000
```

Without the shortcut this takes two minutes. With it, fifteen seconds.

### 3. Doubling and tripling

> A sum doubles in 8 years at simple interest. What is the rate, and how long
> will it take to triple?

```text [Working]
Doubles: interest = P in 8 years  →  R = 100 ÷ 8 = 12.5%
Triples: interest = 2P            →  2P = P × 12.5 × T ÷ 100
                                     T = 200 ÷ 12.5 = 16 years
```

At simple interest, tripling takes twice as long as doubling, because the
interest needed is twice as much and it grows at a fixed pace.

## Common mistakes

::compare
  :::compare-side{label="Wrong" verdict="wrong"}
  20% a year compounded half-yearly for 1 year: `P × 1.2`.
  :::
  :::compare-side{label="Right" verdict="right"}
  Half-yearly means 10% per half-year for 2 half-years: `P × 1.1² = P × 1.21`.
  :::
::

- **Forgetting to adjust rate and time** for half-yearly or quarterly
  compounding. Halve the rate, double the periods.
- **Giving the amount when the question asks for interest**, or the other way
  round. The options always contain both.
- **Using the 2-year difference formula for 3 years.** The 3-year one has the
  extra `(3 + R/100)` factor.
- **Months as years.** 9 months is 0.75 years in the SI formula.

## Practice questions

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. Find the simple interest on ₹6,000 at 8% a year for 3 years."}
  **₹1,440.** `6,000 × 8 × 3 ÷ 100 = 1,440`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. Find the compound interest on ₹8,000 at 10% a year for 3 years."}
  **₹2,648.** `8,000 × 1.331 = 10,648`, so `CI = 10,648 − 8,000 = 2,648`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. At simple interest a sum becomes ₹7,200 in 2 years and ₹8,400 in 4 years. Find the rate."}
  **10%.** Two extra years gave ₹1,200, so ₹600 a year. Principal is
  `7,200 − 1,200 = 6,000`. Rate is `600 ÷ 6,000 × 100 = 10%`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. Find the compound interest on ₹5,000 at 20% a year, compounded half-yearly, for 1 year."}
  **₹1,050.** 10% for 2 periods: `5,000 × 1.21 = 6,050`, CI `= 1,050`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="5. At compound interest a sum becomes ₹8,820 in 2 years and ₹9,261 in 3 years. Find the rate and the sum."}
  **5% and ₹8,000.** `9,261 ÷ 8,820 = 1.05`, so 5%. Then
  `8,820 ÷ 1.1025 = 8,000`.
  :::
::
