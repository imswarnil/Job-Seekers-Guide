Bar charts made you read heights. A line graph joins the same points with
lines, and the lines give you something free: the **slope**, how steep a
segment is, shows the size of a change before you do any arithmetic.

## Why it matters

Line graphs appear in DI sets in TCS NQT, Infosys, Cognizant, Capgemini and
AMCAT-style papers, usually three to five questions on one graph. Check the
current pattern. They almost always show a quantity over time, and often two or
three lines at once, which is where the mistakes happen.

## What to study

| # | Question type | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Steepest rise or fall between two years | High |
| 2 | Percentage change for one line | High |
| 3 | Gap between two lines in a year | High |
| 4 | Year when the lines are closest or cross | Medium |
| 5 | Totals and averages for one line | Medium |

## How to read one

::steps
### Legend first
Which line is which? Mark them on the paper: "C" and "E" beside each line.

### Scale and units
Hundreds? Lakhs? Percent?

### Write the values in a small table
Two rows, one per line. From here on you are answering from a table, which you
already know how to do.
::

::tip
For "steepest rise" in absolute terms, compare the slopes by eye and only
calculate the two candidates that look closest. For "highest percentage rise",
a small base with a moderate rise can beat a big base with a big rise, so check
the low starting points too.
::

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. In which year did CSE applications rise the most over the previous year?"}
  The steepest CSE segment is 2024 to 2025: +6 hundred. In percentage terms it
  is also the largest: `6 ÷ 18 = 33.3%`, against `3 ÷ 12 = 25%` in 2022.

  **2025.**
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. In which year were the two departments closest?"}
  Gaps: 2, 4, 1, 6, 12. **2023**, a gap of 100 applications. On the graph, it
  is where the lines nearly touch.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. By what percentage did CSE exceed ECE in 2025?"}
  `(24 − 12) ÷ 12 = 100%`. The base is ECE, because the question says "exceed
  ECE". Using CSE as the base gives 50%, which will be an option.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. Total CSE applications over the five years?"}
  `12 + 15 + 14 + 18 + 24 = 83` hundred = **8,300**. Forgetting the unit gives
  83, which will also be an option.
  :::

## Common mistakes

- **Reading the wrong line.** Label them before you start.
- **Forgetting the unit.** "In hundreds" means multiply by 100 at the end.
- **The wrong base in "by what percentage is A more than B".** The base is the
  thing after "than".
- **Steepest in absolute terms versus percentage terms.** Read which one is
  asked.

## Practice questions

Using the same graph:

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. Percentage increase in ECE from 2021 to 2023?"}
  **30%.** `3 ÷ 10`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. Average yearly CSE applications?"}
  **1,660.** `83 ÷ 5 = 16.6` hundred.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. In how many years did ECE applications fall compared with the previous year?"}
  **1.** Only 2024 (13 to 12). 2025 stayed level.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. Ratio of total CSE to total ECE applications over five years?"}
  **83 : 58.** ECE total is `10 + 11 + 13 + 12 + 12 = 58`.
  :::
::
