Tables gave you the numbers directly. A bar chart makes you read them off a
scale first, which adds one new way to lose marks: misreading the height. The
questions after that are the same as the last page.

## Why it matters

Bar charts are the most common chart type in written-round DI. Expect them in
TCS NQT, Infosys, Accenture, Cognizant, Capgemini and AMCAT-style papers, as a
set of three to five questions. Check the current pattern. Grouped bars
(two bars per year, for example boys and girls) are especially common because
they double the data without adding space.

## What to study

| # | Type | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Simple bar chart: one bar per category | High |
| 2 | Grouped bars: two or three bars per category | High |
| 3 | Stacked bars: parts of one bar adding to a total | Medium |
| 4 | Horizontal bars (same thing, rotated) | Low |

## How to read one

::steps
### Title, axis labels, units
"Students placed" versus "placement percentage" changes every answer. Check
whether the axis is in hundreds or thousands.

### The scale step
Find the gap between gridlines. If gridlines are every 50, a bar halfway
between 150 and 200 is 175.

### Write the values down
Once. Above each bar, in pencil. Every question after that reads your numbers,
not the chart.
::

::tip
In most exam charts the values are printed on the bars or sit exactly on a
gridline. If you find yourself estimating to the nearest 3, re-check the scale:
you have probably misread the step.
::

## Grouped and stacked bars

- **Grouped bars:** two bars per year, for example CSE and ECE placements side
  by side. Questions compare within a year ("ratio of CSE to ECE in 2024") or
  across years for one series. Put a mark on the series the question names
  before reading.
- **Stacked bars:** one bar split into parts. The top of the bar is the total;
  a segment's value is its top minus its bottom, not the gridline it ends on.

::warning{icon="i-lucide-alert-triangle"}
Reading a stacked segment off the axis is the most common bar-chart error. A
segment from 100 to 160 is worth 60, not 160.
::

## Common mistakes

- **Misreading the scale step.**
- **Tallest bar as "fastest growth".** Growth is relative to the year before.
- **Reading the wrong series** in grouped bars.
- **Stacked segments read from zero.**

## How to practise

- **Daily for a week:** one bar-chart set (4–5 questions) from IndiaBix "Bar
  Charts". Target **5 minutes** for the set, including writing the values down.
- **Then:** alternate bar, line and pie sets daily until the mocks.

## Practice questions

Using the same chart:

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. In how many years were placements above the five-year average?"}
  **2.** 2024 (180) and 2025 (210) are above 159.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. What share of all five years' placements came in 2025, roughly?"}
  **About 26%.** `210 ÷ 795 ≈ 0.264`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. Percentage fall from 2022 to 2023?"}
  **10%.** `15 ÷ 150`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. If 2026 grows by the same percentage as 2025 did, how many placements in 2026?"}
  **245.** 2025 grew `30 ÷ 180 = 1/6`. `210 × 7/6 = 245`.
  :::
::
