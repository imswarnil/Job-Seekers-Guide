Line graphs showed change over time. A pie chart shows something else: how one
whole is divided into parts. Every question on it is a percentage question, and
half of them need no rupee amount at all.

## Why it matters

Pie charts are a regular DI type in TCS NQT, Infosys, Accenture, Cognizant and
AMCAT-style papers, usually three to five questions on one pie or on two pies
side by side. Check the current pattern. They are often the fastest DI set in
the paper, which makes them a good one to attempt first.

## What to study

| # | Question type | Priority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Convert a share to an amount | High |
| 2 | Convert between degrees and percentages | High |
| 3 | Difference or ratio between two sectors | High |
| 4 | Two pies with different totals | Medium |
| 5 | A sector changes; what happens to the rest | Low |

## The conversions

```text
The whole circle = 360° = 100%
1% = 3.6°
Degrees → percent:  divide by 3.6
Percent → degrees:  multiply by 3.6

Amount of a sector = total × (percent ÷ 100)
                   = total × (degrees ÷ 360)

Ratio of two sectors = ratio of their percentages (or degrees)
```

::tip
Ratios and "what percent of" questions between sectors never need the total.
If rent is 40% and food is 20%, rent is twice food whatever the budget is.
Answer those from the percentages directly.
::

## A worked example

A job seeker's monthly budget in Bangalore, total ₹15,000:

| Item | Share | Degrees |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| PG rent | 40% | 144° |
| Food | 20% | 72° |
| Course fee instalment | 15% | 54° |
| Travel | 10% | 36° |
| Other | 10% | 36° |
| Phone and internet | 5% | 18° |
| **Total** | **100%** | **360°** |

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. How much goes on rent?"}
  `15,000 × 40% = ₹6,000`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. How much more is spent on rent than on food?"}
  The difference in share is 20 percentage points: `15,000 × 20% = ₹3,000`.
  One multiplication, not two.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. A sector is 54°. What percentage is it?"}
  `54 ÷ 3.6 = 15%`. The course fee.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. Food is what percentage of rent?"}
  `20 ÷ 40 = 50%`. No rupees needed.
  :::
::

## Two pies

When there are two pies (for example, one year's budget and the next year's),
check whether the **totals** are the same.

::warning{icon="i-lucide-alert-triangle"}
A bigger slice in the second pie does not mean a bigger amount if the second
total is smaller. 30% of ₹10,000 is less than 25% of ₹15,000. Convert both to
amounts before comparing across pies.
::

## Common mistakes

- **Treating degrees as percentages.** A 72° sector is 20%, not 72%.
- **Comparing slices across two pies** without their totals.
- **Calculating both amounts** for a difference question when the difference in
  share gives it in one step.
- **"Percentage of" with the wrong base.** "Food as a percentage of rent" has
  rent on the bottom.

## How to practise

- **Daily for five days:** one pie set from IndiaBix "Pie Charts". Target **4
  minutes** a set.
- **Learn** the degree values of 5%, 10%, 12.5%, 20% and 25% (18°, 36°, 45°,
  72°, 90°) until you read them without dividing.

## Practice questions

Using the same budget:

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="1. Travel plus phone and internet, in rupees?"}
  **₹2,250.** 15% of 15,000.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="2. Ratio of rent to the course fee instalment?"}
  **8 : 3.** `40 : 15`.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="3. If the total rises to ₹18,000 with the same shares, how much is travel?"}
  **₹1,800.** 10% of 18,000.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="4. What is the central angle of rent and food together?"}
  **216°.** 60% × 3.6.
  :::
