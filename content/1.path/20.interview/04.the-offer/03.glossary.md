Four rounds and an offer letter, and about thirty-five terms nobody defined
while you were signing things. Here they are, because several of them are
expensive to misunderstand.

## The process

::field-group
  :::field{name="Screening round" type="process"}
  The first filter, usually a fifteen-minute call with a recruiter who is not an
  engineer. Checks that you are real and that you want this specific work.
  :::
  :::field{name="Written round" type="process"}
  Aptitude, reasoning and English, often with a coding section. The largest
  filter in the pipeline and the one candidates prepare for least.
  :::
  :::field{name="Sectional cutoff" type="process"}
  A minimum score required **in each section**, not overall. Where it exists,
  skipping a section is fatal however well you did elsewhere. Find out before
  the day.
  :::
  :::field{name="Coding round" type="process"}
  Writing code while somebody watches, or on a timed platform. Thinking out loud
  is scored, whether or not anybody says so.
  :::
  :::field{name="Technical round" type="process"}
  A conversation about what you have built and what you know. Where the offer is
  usually decided.
  :::
  :::field{name="HR round" type="process"}
  Not a formality. Deciding whether the team can work beside you and whether you
  will still be here in a year.
  :::
  :::field{name="Loop" type="process"}
  Several rounds on one day, often at product companies. Exhausting by design,
  and worth eating properly before.
  :::
  :::field{name="Bar raiser" type="process"}
  An interviewer from outside the hiring team whose job is to stop standards
  drifting. They can veto, and they are usually the hardest round.
  :::
::

## Being found

::field-group
  :::field{name="ATS" type="applying"}
  Applicant Tracking System. Software that stores and filters applications. Why
  a CV should be plain text in a normal layout: a two-column design with the
  dates in a sidebar often parses into nonsense.
  :::
  :::field{name="Referral" type="applying"}
  Somebody inside the company submitting your application. Moves you past the
  first filter, and it is the single highest-return thing you can arrange.
  :::
  :::field{name="Ghosting" type="applying"}
  No reply, ever. Common, impersonal, and not information about you. Assume no
  news is no and keep applying.
  :::
  :::field{name="Notice period" type="applying"}
  How long you must work after resigning. Three months is common in India, and
  it materially affects whether companies will wait for you.
  :::
::

## The offer letter

::field-group
  :::field{name="CTC" type="money"}
  Cost to company. The headline number and **not** what arrives in your account.
  :::
  :::field{name="Fixed pay" type="money"}
  The part that is guaranteed. This is the number to compare between offers.
  :::
  :::field{name="Variable pay" type="money"}
  Depends on performance, yours or the company's. Ask what percentage was
  actually paid out last year, and treat a vague answer as an answer.
  :::
  :::field{name="Gratuity" type="money"}
  A statutory payment on leaving, usually after five years. Frequently included
  in CTC and almost never received, because most people leave before five years.
  :::
  :::field{name="Employer PF" type="money"}
  The company's provident fund contribution. Real money and yours, but not cash
  this month.
  :::
  :::field{name="Joining bonus" type="money"}
  Paid once, usually with a clawback if you leave inside a year. Not salary, and
  should not be compared against it.
  :::
  :::field{name="Retention bonus" type="money"}
  Paid at a future date if you are still there. Same warning.
  :::
  :::field{name="ESOP" type="money"}
  Employee stock options: the right to buy shares at a fixed price later. Worth
  nothing unless the company is sold or listed, and worth understanding the
  **strike price**, the **vesting schedule** and the **cliff** before counting
  any of it.
  :::
  :::field{name="Cliff" type="money"}
  The date before which no options vest at all, usually one year. Leave at
  eleven months and you get nothing.
  :::
  :::field{name="Bond" type="money"}
  An agreement to stay for a period or pay a penalty. Legally contested,
  still common, and worth reading closely before signing.
  :::
::

## Reading a CTC honestly

::compare{caption="Two offers, and the larger CTC is the smaller one."}
  :::compare-side{label="₹9,00,000 CTC" verdict="neutral"}
  Fixed ₹6,50,000 · variable ₹1,50,000 · gratuity ₹43,000 · employer PF
  ₹57,000

  In hand, monthly, before tax: about ₹54,000. The variable paid out at 60% last
  year.
  :::
  :::compare-side{label="₹8,00,000 CTC" verdict="neutral"}
  Fixed ₹7,20,000 · no variable · employer PF ₹80,000

  In hand, monthly, before tax: about ₹60,000, guaranteed.

  Lower headline, more money, less risk.
  :::
::

::warning{icon="i-lucide-alert-triangle"}
Compare **fixed pay**, not CTC. A company can add gratuity, employer
contributions and an optimistic variable to make a number look larger without
anybody receiving more. This is not deception and it is not accidental either.
::

## The words about you

::field-group
  :::field{name="Fresher" type="level"}
  No professional experience. A description, not a judgement, and it stops
  applying after about eighteen months.
  :::
  :::field{name="Notice-period buyout" type="level"}
  A new employer paying your old one so you can leave sooner. Negotiable, and
  worth asking about.
  :::
  :::field{name="Counter-offer" type="level"}
  Your current employer matching a new offer to keep you. Usually solves the
  money and never the reason you were leaving.
  :::
::
