You are paid for judgement. Specifically: for being the person who can look at a
half-described problem and a pile of somebody else's code and decide what should
happen. The typing is how the decision gets recorded.

That sounds like a platitude until you look at what it rules out.

## Why this matters in a job

Because it decides what "getting good" means, and therefore how you should
spend the next year. If the job were typing, the way to get good would be to
memorise syntax. It is not, so it is not.

I got this wrong for about eighteen months. I collected languages. I could write
a for-loop in four of them and could not tell you why the fee report was showing
duplicate rows.

::side-note{kind="go-deeper"}
This is also the honest answer to "has AI taken this job". A machine is now
genuinely better than a junior at producing syntax. It has no opinion about
whether a duplicate fee row is a display bug or a data-integrity disaster, and
the money was always attached to the second question.
::

## The four things somebody pays for

::feature-list{columns="2"}
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-search" title="Working out what is actually wanted"}
  "Let the registrar filter applicants" is not a specification. What happens to
  an applicant with no score yet? Is a rejected applicant still in the list? You
  find those out before you build, or you build the wrong thing politely.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-book-open" title="Reading what already exists"}
  You will almost never start from nothing. You will start from forty thousand
  lines somebody left, and being able to read them is worth more than being able
  to write them.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-shield-check" title="Knowing whether it is right"}
  Not whether it runs. Whether it is correct, at the boundaries, on bad input,
  with two people doing it at once. This is the whole of §12 of this course and
  the single most valuable habit in it.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-message-square" title="Making it legible to other people"}
  A fix nobody can find, review or understand is worth a fraction of the same fix
  explained. This is why the next track in this course is English.
  :::
::

## Watch it happen

Here is a real one, in the smallest form I can put it. The registrar asks for the
average score of this intake's applicants.

```sql [averages.sql]
SELECT AVG(score) AS average_score
FROM Application
WHERE intake = '2026';
```

It runs. It returns `72.4`. Everyone is happy.

Now the judgement. `Application`, not `Applicant`. One person applying to three
programmes is counted three times, so the average is weighted toward people who
applied a lot. And `AVG` silently ignores rows where `score` is `NULL`, so the
two hundred applicants who have not been marked yet are not in the number at all,
and nothing on the screen says so.

::compare{caption="Both queries run. One of them is a number the dean can use."}
  :::compare-side{label="Runs" verdict="wrong"}
  `72.4`, presented as "the average score this intake".
  :::
  :::compare-side{label="Correct" verdict="right"}
  `72.4`, presented as "the average of 1,840 marked applications out of 2,040,
  counting each application separately". Same number. Completely different
  claim.
  :::
::

Nobody pays for the `SELECT`. They pay for the second label.

## What confuses people here

That this makes the syntax optional. It does not. You cannot have judgement about
a language you cannot read, and you cannot spot the `NULL` problem if you do not
know what `AVG` does with one. Foundations are not in tension with judgement.
They are the thing judgement is made of.

::warning
The opposite error is more common in 2026 and more expensive: skipping the
foundation because a model will write the query. You then cannot tell a right
answer from a confident wrong one, which is the only job left.
::

## What you can do now

You can state what you are being trained for in one sentence, which is more than
most graduates can do. It will change what you build: a project with one hard
decision in it, that you can defend, beats five tutorials finished.

## Your turn

Take the query above and write the honest one-line label for its result yourself,
before reading mine again. Then write down one more thing that could make `72.4`
misleading that I did not mention.

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="One I left out" icon="i-lucide-lightbulb"}
  Withdrawn applications. If an applicant withdrew in March, their score is still
  sitting in the table and still in the average, and whether it should be is a
  question for the registrar, not for you. Noticing that you have to ask is the
  skill.
  :::
::

## The interview question this answers

**"What do you think makes a good engineer?"**

Most candidates answer with a technology list. A good answer is about judgement,
with a small concrete example: a time you found out that the thing you were asked
for was not the thing that was needed. If you have not had a job yet, use a
college project. The example does not have to be grand, it has to be real.
