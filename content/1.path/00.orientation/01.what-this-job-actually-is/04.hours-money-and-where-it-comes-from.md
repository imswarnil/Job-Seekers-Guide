Your salary is decided less by how good you are than by which of three business
models you are standing inside. I did not know this and it cost me three years on
5.5 lakh while a friend with the identical start was on 21.

## Why this matters in a job

Because "how do I earn more" has a structural answer before it has a skill
answer. Getting better inside a company that bills your hours to a client raises
your salary slowly, because the client's rate card is the ceiling. Moving is what
raises it.

::real-life{title="The number that made me change everything" source="Bangalore, 2022"}
Durgesh and I started the same way: same walk-ins, same rejections, same nothing.
Three years in he moved companies and went to 21 LPA. I had stayed and was on 5.5.
Same start, same skill, roughly. He had understood the structure and I had not.
::

## The three models

::pros-cons
---
title: Service company (TCS, Infosys, Accenture, Cognizant, and hundreds smaller)
pros:
  - Hires in thousands, trains you, takes freshers without a strong CV
  - Genuine chance for somebody with no network at all. This is how I got in
  - You see many industries, which is a real education
cons:
  - You are billed to a client at a rate, and your salary is a fraction of it
  - Raises are banded and small; the fast route out is usually out
  - You may be put on whatever project needs a body, not what you are good at
---
::

::pros-cons
---
title: Product company (a company whose product is software)
pros:
  - Revenue is not your hours, so good work compounds instead of being billed
  - Pays substantially more, and often in stock as well as salary
  - You own something long enough to see the consequences of your decisions
cons:
  - Hires far fewer people and filters hard on fundamentals
  - Expects you to decide things, which is uncomfortable at first
  - A layoff round is a real risk and is not about your performance
---
::

::pros-cons
---
title: Startup
pros:
  - You will do six jobs and learn faster than anywhere else
  - Real responsibility within months, not years
  - Equity, occasionally worth something
cons:
  - Salary is often below market and the company may not exist in two years
  - Almost no mentoring, which is dangerous as a first job
  - "Wear many hats" can mean nobody teaches you to do any of them properly
---
::

That single picture is why the advice "just keep learning where you are" fails
people. Learning raises what you are worth. It does not raise what you are
billed at.

## What confuses people here

Two things, both expensive.

**"Package" is not salary.** A 12 LPA offer in India typically contains base pay,
a provident-fund contribution, gratuity, a variable bonus you may not receive in
full, and sometimes a joining bonus counted once. In-hand monthly can be 25–35%
below `package ÷ 12`. Ask for the breakdown in writing. My first job was 1.8 LPA,
and ₹13,000 a month is what actually arrived.

**Stock is not money until it is.** RSUs at a listed company are close to cash on
a schedule. Options at a private startup are a lottery ticket with tax
complications. They are not the same instrument and are quoted as though they
are.

::warning{icon="i-lucide-alert-triangle"}
Never accept a verbal number. Get the offer letter with the components listed.
I have seen a "9 LPA" offer where 1.5 of it was a performance bonus nobody in the
team had ever received in full.
::

## The hours, honestly

A normal week is 40 to 45 hours. There are bad weeks: a release, an incident, a
deadline somebody promised without asking you. There should not be bad months,
and if there are, that is information about the company rather than about the
profession.

The sustained 70-hour week is real in some startups and in some badly run service
projects. It is not the industry norm and you should not plan a life around
enduring it.

## What you can do now

You can read a job advert and infer the model, and you can ask the one question
that gets you the real number: "could you share the component-wise breakup of the
CTC?"

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="What gives it away" icon="i-lucide-search"}
  Service: "client", "resource", "billable", a named technology stack per
  project, mass hiring drives, "immediate joiners". Product: the company's own
  product is the subject of the advert, and the requirements are about depth

## Check yourself

1. Why does getting significantly better at your job often not raise your salary
   much inside a service company?
2. **An interviewer asks: "what are your salary expectations?"** What do you need
   to know before answering, and what is the one thing you should ask for?

## The interview question this answers

**"What are your salary expectations?"**

Give a range, anchored to the market for the role and city rather than to your
current pay, and ask for the component breakup. As a fresher with no leverage,
"I am flexible, and I would like to understand the component-wise breakup" is an
honest and non-damaging answer. Naming a number far below market is the mistake
that follows people for years, because the next offer is often calculated from
this one.
