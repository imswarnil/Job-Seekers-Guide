Four routes, described honestly. None of them involves being discovered. Three of
the four are available to you this year.

## Why this matters in a job

Because the single most common reason people never start is that the only stories
they have heard are of people whose circumstances they cannot reproduce. The
routes below are boring, repeatable, and the one I took is the least glamorous of
the four.

## The four

::persona
---
name: The campus route
role: Placed through a college drive, joins a service company
background: Tier 2 or 3 engineering college, placement cell arranges the drive
goal: An offer before graduating
blocker: One shot, on a date, filtered on aptitude before anybody reads any code
---
The largest route by volume. It is real and it works, and it has one property
nobody warns you about: it happens once, at a fixed time, and if you are not
ready in your seventh semester it is simply over. Prepare for the aptitude gate
in your third year, not your fourth.
::

::persona
---
name: The off-campus route
role: Applies continuously to small and mid-size companies, walk-ins and job boards
background: Graduated without an offer, or unhappy with the one they got
goal: A first job, any first job, then move
blocker: No deadline, so nothing forces the work; and nobody to tell you what to learn
---
This is mine. Thirteen thousand rupees a month, one selection out of roughly a
thousand applicants, after months of walk-ins. It has no ceiling and no floor,
it takes longer than anyone tells you, and it is completely available to somebody
with no college brand and no network.
::

::persona
---
name: The support-to-engineering route
role: Joins in technical support or QA, moves internally within two years
background: Often a non-CSE degree, or a degree from somewhere nobody has heard of
goal: Get inside a company, then move sideways
blocker: You have to be deliberate, or you are still in support in year five
---
Underrated and genuinely common. The bar to enter is lower, you learn the product
and the domain faster than the engineers do, and internal moves are far easier
than external ones. The condition is that you build and show real work while you
are there, on your own time.
::

::persona
---
name: The no-degree route
role: Self-taught or bootcamp, hired on evidence
background: Any degree or none. Sometimes a diploma, sometimes a completely
  different field
goal: Be judged on what they can show
blocker: The first interview. After the first job, nobody asks again
---
Harder to start and not impossible, and it is the one most affected by the
current market: the bar for "can you show me something real" has risen, because
generating something that looks real is now trivial. A system you can be
questioned about for forty minutes is the evidence. A repository of tutorials is
not.
::

## What they have in common

::feature-list{columns="2"}
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-hammer" title="Evidence, not certificates"}
  Every one of these routes converts at the moment somebody believes you can do
  the work. A thing you built and can explain does that; a certificate does not.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-repeat" title="Volume of attempts"}
  I cleared one interview in roughly forty. That ratio is normal and nobody
  publishes it, so everybody assumes their own rejections are unusual.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-message-circle" title="Being able to talk"}
  All four are decided in a conversation. This is why English is the next track
  in this course and not an afterthought at the end.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-arrow-up-right" title="The first job is not the job"}
  Every route above is about getting in. What you earn in year five is decided by
  what you do after you are in, and it is a different question entirely.
  :::
::

## What confuses people here

That these are ranked. They are not. The campus route is not better than the
off-campus route; it is earlier. The no-degree route is not braver; it is harder
to start and identical afterwards.

::real-life{title="What actually happened to the four of us" source="People I know, ten years on"}
Two of the four routes above are people I know personally and the other two are
patterns I watched repeatedly in Bangalore. Ten years in, the route somebody took
is invisible: what is visible is who kept learning after the first job and who
stopped. The person on the highest salary in my batch started in support.
::

## What you can do now

You can name which route you are actually on, which is different from the one you
imagined. That changes what you should do this month.

Not "learn Java". "Finish the Java track and have the console version of the
admissions tool running, by the 30th."

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="Why the date matters more than the plan" icon="i-lucide-calendar"}
  Because the off-campus and no-degree routes have no external deadline, and a
  plan with no date is a wish. The campus route has a deadline supplied for you,
  which is the only genuine advantage it has.

## Check yourself

1. Which of the four routes has no deadline, and why is that both its advantage
   and its danger?
2. **An interviewer asks: "walk me through your background."** Answer in ninety
   seconds, ending on why you are in this room.

## The interview question this answers

**"Tell me about yourself."**

The most-asked and most-wasted question in hiring, and the one a friend told me
decides most of the outcome. It is not a biography. It is: where you started,
what you decided to do about it, what you built, and why this role. Ninety
seconds, rehearsed out loud until it is not rehearsed. The interview track at the
end of this course takes it apart line by line, and it is worth knowing now that
it is the question that matters most.
