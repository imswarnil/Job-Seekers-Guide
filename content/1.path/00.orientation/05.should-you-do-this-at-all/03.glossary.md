No code in this track, and about fifty words anyway. Here they are, because
every one will appear in a job advert you read this year and nobody defines them
anywhere.

## The work

::field-group
  :::field{name="Front end" type="role"}
  What runs in the browser. Structure, appearance, behaviour.
  :::
  :::field{name="Back end" type="role"}
  What runs on a server. Data, rules, authorisation.
  :::
  :::field{name="Full stack" type="role"}
  Both, to some depth. In a job advert it frequently means "one person, both
  jobs, one salary", which is worth knowing before you apply.
  :::
  :::field{name="DevOps" type="role"}
  Not a person, originally. A practice: the people who write software also run
  it. As a job title it usually means deployment, infrastructure and monitoring.
  :::
  :::field{name="SRE" type="role"}
  Site Reliability Engineering. Keeping things running, with engineering rather
  than rotas, and an explicit budget for how much downtime is acceptable.
  :::
  :::field{name="QA" type="role"}
  Quality assurance. Increasingly automated, and the automation is programming,
  which makes it one of the more accessible ways in.
  :::
  :::field{name="Product manager" type="role"}
  Decides what gets built and in what order. Not your manager, and the
  distinction matters when you are given conflicting priorities.
  :::
::

## How work arrives

::field-group
  :::field{name="Ticket" type="process"}
  One unit of work with a description and a number. Your day is mostly tickets.
  :::
  :::field{name="Sprint" type="process"}
  A fixed period, usually two weeks, with an agreed set of tickets in it.
  :::
  :::field{name="Standup" type="process"}
  A short daily meeting. Done well it is fifteen minutes of unblocking; done
  badly it is a status report to a manager.
  :::
  :::field{name="Retrospective" type="process"}
  A meeting after a sprint about how the work went rather than what got done.
  The one meeting worth protecting.
  :::
  :::field{name="Code review" type="process"}
  Somebody reads your change before it merges. Not an insult, and the fastest
  way to learn in your first year.
  :::
  :::field{name="On call" type="process"}
  Carrying responsibility for production outside working hours, usually on a
  rota. **Ask about this in an interview.** It is a real cost and it is rarely
  advertised.
  :::
::

## The industry's own history

::field-group
  :::field{name="Mainframe" type="history"}
  One enormous shared machine, time-shared between users. Still running banks
  and airlines today, which surprises people.
  :::
  :::field{name="Open source" type="history"}
  Software whose source anybody can read, use and modify, under a licence that
  says so. Most of what you will build on.
  :::
  :::field{name="Services company" type="history"}
  Builds software for other companies, for a fee. The bulk of Indian
  entry-level hiring, and the route most people take.
  :::
  :::field{name="Product company" type="history"}
  Builds and sells its own software. Fewer roles, usually higher pay, usually
  harder interviews.
  :::
  :::field{name="Bench" type="history"}
  Employed by a services company and not currently assigned to a client project.
  Paid, and usually not learning much, which is why it matters.
  :::
  :::field{name="Billing rate" type="history"}
  What the services company charges a client per hour for your time. It is
  several times your salary, and knowing that this gap exists is useful context
  rather than a grievance.
  :::
::

## Money

::field-group
  :::field{name="CTC" type="money"}
  Cost to company. The headline number, and **not** what arrives in your
  account. Includes employer contributions, insurance and sometimes a notional
  bonus.
  :::
  :::field{name="In hand" type="money"}
  What actually reaches your bank account monthly, after deductions. This is the
  number to compare between offers.
  :::
  :::field{name="Variable pay" type="money"}
  A portion of CTC that depends on performance, the company's or yours. Ask what
  percentage was actually paid last year.
  :::
  :::field{name="Joining bonus" type="money"}
  Paid once, often with a clawback if you leave within a year. Not salary, and
  it should not be compared against salary.
  :::
  :::field{name="Notice period" type="money"}
  How long you must work after resigning. Three months is common in India, and
  it materially affects your ability to move.
  :::
  :::field{name="Bond" type="money"}
  An agreement to stay for a period or pay a penalty. Legally contested and
  still common. Read it before signing, and prefer not to.
  :::
::

## Words that mean less than they sound like

::compare{caption="Job advert language, translated."}
  :::compare-side{label="What it says" verdict="neutral"}
  "Rockstar developer"

  "Fast-paced environment"

  "Wear many hats"

  "Like a family"

  "Competitive salary"
  :::
  :::compare-side{label="What it often means" verdict="neutral"}
  One person doing three jobs

  Deadlines set without consulting engineering

  No specialisation, and no time to develop any

  Boundaries about hours are unwelcome

  They will not state a number, so you must
  :::
::

::note
None of these is proof of anything, and plenty of good companies write bad job
adverts. Treat them as questions to ask rather than reasons to skip, and ask
them in the interview: "what does fast-paced mean here in practice?" gets an
honest answer more often than you would expect.
::

## Words that do tell you something

::feature-list{columns="2"}
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-git-pull-request" title="Named tools and versions"}
  "PostgreSQL 16, Java 21, deployed on Kubernetes" is a team that knows what it
  runs. Vagueness about the stack usually means vagueness about the work.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-book-open" title="A described process"}
  Mentioning code review, tests, or on-call rotation means these exist. An
  advert that mentions none of them is not evidence they are absent, but one
  that does is evidence they are present.
  :::
::
