Three clauses decide what leaving will cost you later: the **bond**, the
**notice period** and the **background verification**. Read all three before you
sign, because each one is much harder to change afterwards.

## Why it matters

Freshers sign offer letters fast, because they are relieved. That is the moment
the bond amount, the notice period and the conditions of the offer are agreed,
and none of them feel important until the day you want to leave.

::real-life{title="My first job had a bond" source="Bangalore, 2018–2019"}
The startup that selected me at my 34th walk-in paid 1.8 LPA, about ₹13,000 a
month, six days a week, and came with a bond. I signed it, because I needed the
start. Two months later Accenture selected me, and the bond was suddenly the
most important page I owned. Read it the day you sign it, not the day you need
it.
::

## Bonds and service agreements

A **bond** (or **service agreement**) is a clause saying you will stay for a
period, usually one to three years, or pay the company an amount if you leave
before it ends. Companies say it recovers the cost of training you.

::steps
### Find the duration and the amount
"24 months, ₹1,00,000" or "equivalent to three months' salary". Both must be
written down.

### Check whether it reduces over time
A fair bond reduces as you serve it: leave after 18 months of a 24-month bond
and you pay a quarter, not the whole amount. Ask.

### Check what it is for
Training costs, a relocation, a certification. A bond with no stated reason and
a large number is a warning sign.

### Check what they hold
Some companies ask for original certificates, or blank signed cheques, as
security. Do not give either. A legitimate employer looks at originals and hands
them back.
::

::warning{icon="i-lucide-alert-triangle"}
**I am not a lawyer, and this is not legal advice.** In general, Indian courts
have tended to enforce a bond when the amount is a reasonable recovery of real
training costs, and to be more sceptical of a large penalty meant only to stop
you leaving. Whether a particular bond can be enforced depends on its wording
and the facts. If you are thinking of breaking one, read it carefully and take
proper advice. The safest plan is to know the terms before you sign.
::

::compare{caption="Two bonds for the same kind of fresher job."}
  :::compare-side{label="Be careful" verdict="wrong"}
  - 3 years, ₹3,00,000 flat, no reduction
  - "Original degree certificate to be submitted at joining"
  - No mention of what training it covers
  - Signed on the day of the walk-in, before an offer letter
  :::
  :::compare-side{label="Usually reasonable" verdict="right"}
  - 1–2 years, an amount close to the training cost
  - Reduces month by month as you serve it
  - Tied to a stated training programme
  - Part of the offer letter, which you can read at home first
  :::
::

## Notice period

The **notice period** is how long you must keep working after you resign,
before you can leave. It is in your offer letter and your employment contract.

| Where | Typical notice period |
| --- | --- |
| During probation (often first 3–6 months) | Shorter, often 15–30 days |
| Startups and small companies | 30–60 days |
| Large service companies, after probation | 60–90 days |

It matters in two directions. When you join, a company hiring a fresher prefers
"immediate joiner" to anybody with a notice period, which is an advantage you
have only once. When you switch, a 90-day notice period means the new company
must wait three months for you, and some will not.

::tip
A **notice buyout** is when you (or the new company) pay the old company for the
days you do not serve. Some companies allow it, some do not, and some let you use
unused leave to shorten the period. Find out what your contract says before you
start interviewing for a switch, not after.
::

## Background verification

**Background verification (BGV)** is a check, usually done by an outside agency
after you accept, that what you told the company is true.

::feature-list{columns="2"}
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-graduation-cap" title="Education"}
  Your degree and marks, checked with the university. Photocopies you submit are
  compared with the originals.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-briefcase" title="Previous employment"}
  For switchers: dates, designation, sometimes salary, checked with the old
  company's HR. Also your PF history, which lists every employer who paid into
  your account.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-home" title="Address and identity"}
  Aadhaar, PAN, current and permanent address. Sometimes a physical visit.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-shield" title="Criminal record, sometimes"}
  More common at banks and large companies with overseas clients.
  :::
::

::warning{icon="i-lucide-alert-triangle"}
**Any mismatch can end the offer**, even after you have joined. The classic
failure is fake experience: a letter from a company that does not exist, or dates
that do not match PF records. The second classic failure is small and honest: a
wrong joining date or a rounded-up salary on a form. Fill every form exactly, and
keep your documents (offer letters, relieving letters, payslips) from every job.
::

## Between accepting and joining

::steps
### Accept in writing
Reply to the email or sign the portal. Keep a copy of the signed letter.

### Submit documents promptly
Photocopies, scans, forms. BGV starts from these, and delays here delay your
joining date.

### Stay in touch
If there is a gap of weeks before joining, reply to every email. Silent
candidates are sometimes assumed to have gone elsewhere.

### If you are switching: do not resign until the offer letter is signed
A verbal offer or an email saying "you are selected" is not an offer letter.
Resign after you have the letter, and ideally after BGV has started.

### If you decline, decline properly
A short, polite email. You may want to apply there again one day.
::

## Common mistakes

- **Signing a bond without reading the amount.**
- **Handing over original certificates or blank cheques.**
- **Resigning on a verbal offer.** People have been left with no job.
- **Rounding numbers on BGV forms.** Write exact dates and exact figures.
- **Not keeping a relieving letter.** The next company's BGV will ask for it.

## How to practise

Take any offer letter, yours or a friend's, and write down five things from it:
the bond duration and amount, whether it reduces, the notice period during and
after probation, the documents BGV will check, and the joining date. If any of
the five is missing from the letter, that is your list of questions for HR.
