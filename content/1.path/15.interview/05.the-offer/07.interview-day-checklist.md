This is the page to open on the morning of an interview. Everything from this
track, in the order you need it on the day, plus the one exercise that changes
results more than any reading: a full mock interview the week before.

## Why it matters

On the day, nerves take away whatever you have not practised until it is
automatic. A checklist removes the small things (a missing photocopy, a dead
phone, a late bus) so that all your attention is left for the questions.

## The week before: one full mock interview

Everything in this track is knowledge. An interview is a performance, and
nobody gets better at a performance by reading about it.

::steps
### Block ninety minutes and ask a friend to be the interviewer
Alone is far better than not at all. "I could not find anybody" is the reason
most people never do this.

### Run it as a real interview, with no pausing
Tell me about yourself, your project, five technical questions from the fifty,
one coding problem out loud, then HR: why this company, a weakness, a failure
story, salary, and "any questions for us?"

### Record it
Audio is enough. You will not want to listen. Listening is where the value is.

### Listen back for three things
Where you went quiet, where you got vague, and which filler word you said most.
The vague places are your study list for the week.

### Do it again three days later
The second run is always better, and not because you learned anything new. The
gap between knowing and saying closes with repetition, not with study.
::

## The night before

```text [night-before.txt]
[ ] Read the job description again. Note 3 things it asks for
[ ] Read the company's website: what they do, one recent news item
[ ] Read your own resume, line by line. One likely question per line
[ ] Say "tell me about yourself" aloud once, timed (60–90 seconds)
[ ] Say your project walkthrough aloud once (2 minutes)
[ ] Revise 5 of the fifty questions you are weakest on
[ ] Write 3 questions to ask them
[ ] Check the address, the route, and the travel time at that hour
[ ] Iron the clothes. Charge the phone and power bank
[ ] Pack the bag (list below). Sleep by 11
```

## What to carry

```text [the-bag.txt]
[ ] 5 printed copies of your resume
[ ] Photo ID (Aadhaar / PAN) + 1 photocopy
[ ] Mark sheets and degree certificate: photocopies (originals only if
    asked in writing, and they come back to you the same day)
[ ] 2 passport photos
[ ] 2 pens and a small notebook
[ ] The interview email / invite, with the contact person's name and number
[ ] Phone, charged. Power bank. Water. Something to eat
[ ] Laptop with your project running, if they said to bring one
```

## For an online interview

```text [online-interview.txt]
[ ] Test the link, camera and microphone 30 minutes early
[ ] Laptop charging, phone hotspot ready as a backup
[ ] Quiet room, plain wall behind you, light in front of you, not behind
[ ] Close every other tab and notification
[ ] Your project open and running, on the file you want to show
[ ] Water within reach. Notes out of the camera's view (glance, don't read)
```

## The morning

::steps
### Leave with an hour to spare
Bangalore traffic will take whatever margin you give it. Being early costs you a
coffee. Being late can cost you the interview.

### Say your introduction once on the way
Quietly, on the bus or while walking. Not to memorise it; to warm up.

### Arrive, register, and wait calmly
Switch the phone to silent. Be polite to everyone, including security and
reception. Sometimes they are asked.
::

## In the rooms

::feature-list{columns="2"}
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-mic" title="Tell me about yourself"}
  Present, past, skills, why this role. 60 to 90 seconds, then stop.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-list-ordered" title="Every technical question"}
  Definition, how it works, example, trade-off, stop.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-code" title="The coding problem"}
  Clarify, example by hand, brute force then better, write while talking, dry
  run and complexity.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-circle-help" title="When you do not know"}
  "I don't know. What I do know is… and I'd find out by…" Never bluff.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-users" title="HR"}
  Answer the question underneath. Evidence, not adjectives. Honest about
  conditions.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-message-circle-question" title="Any questions for us?"}
  Always two or three. Never "no, you covered everything".
  :::
::

## After, whatever happens

::steps
### On the way home, write everything down
Every question you were asked, the ones you answered badly, and the one thing
you would do differently. Fifteen minutes, while you still remember.

### Send a short thank-you if you have an email address
Two lines. It rarely changes a decision; it never hurts one.

### If it is an offer
Ask for the breakup, read the bond and the notice period, and reply in writing.

### If it is a rejection
Add the questions that beat you to your study list and go to the next one. I was
rejected 33 times. The 34th was the one that counted, and every one of the 33
put something on my list.
::

## Common mistakes

- **Revising new topics the night before.** Revise what you know. New material
  the night before only adds panic.
- **Arriving with no time to spare.** You walk in flustered and it shows in the first
  answer.
- **Treating a rejection as a verdict.** At a drive with 700 people and 20 seats,
  most rejections are arithmetic.
- **Not writing down the questions.** The same ones come back at the next
  company.

## How to practise

Print this page, or copy the three lists into your phone. Do the mock interview
this week, not the week you get a call. Then use the lists every time until you
no longer need them.
