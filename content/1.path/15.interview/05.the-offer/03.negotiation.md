As a fresher at a mass-hiring company, the salary is usually fixed and you
should not push on it. Almost everything else is worth one polite question. As a
switcher, everything is negotiable, and the strongest position you will ever have
is more than one real offer.

## Why it matters

Your next salary is often calculated from this one, so a small difference now
compounds. And the difference between a candidate who negotiates and one who
does not is rarely skill. It is usually one email.

::timeline{label="My salary, and what moved it"}
  :::timeline-item{title="First job, a startup" date="1.8 LPA" state="done"}
  About ₹13,000 a month, with a bond. No negotiation: one offer, no alternative,
  and I was right to take it.
  :::
  :::timeline-item{title="Accenture, two months later" date="A service company fresher band" state="done"}
  A fixed band for freshers. Nothing to negotiate, and the job was the step up.
  :::
  :::timeline-item{title="Three years at Accenture" date="About 5 LPA" state="done"}
  Appraisals, a percentage at a time. This is the part I would change.
  :::
  :::timeline-item{title="The first switch" date="15.5 LPA, five offers" state="done"}
  I cracked five companies and joined Cognizant on the best offer. About three
  times what I was earning.
  :::
  :::timeline-item{title="Nine months later" date="30+ LPA" state="current"}
  I cracked PwC and Twilio and joined Twilio as a Salesforce analytics engineer
  in the GTM team.
  :::
::

The two largest jumps both came from switching with more than one offer in hand.
None of them came from an appraisal.

## As a fresher: what is and is not negotiable

| Usually fixed, do not push | Often worth one polite question |
| --- | --- |
| The salary at a mass-hiring service company (a fixed band for every fresher) | The joining date, by one or two weeks |
| The training programme and its length | The location preference, stated early |
| The bond at a large company (same for everybody) | The bond amount or duration at a small company or startup |
| The notice period in the standard contract | Moving some variable pay into fixed, at the same CTC |
| | The salary itself, at a startup or small company, with a reason |
| | When the first appraisal happens (six months instead of twelve) |

```text [fresher-salary-ask.txt]
"Thank you, I'm glad to have the offer and I'd like to accept.

Before I do: for fresher Java developer roles in Bangalore I've seen
offers closer to [X] LPA. Is there any flexibility on the fixed
component?"
```

It works because you say you want to accept (no threat), you anchor on the
market and the role (not on your needs), and you ask about **fixed** pay (not
the variable, where a negotiation is often quietly settled with money you may
never receive).

```text [if-the-number-cannot-move.txt]
"Understood, thank you. Two other things then: could the joining date
be [date], and would you be open to a salary review at six months rather
than twelve?"
```

::tip
Never negotiate on need. "My rent is high" invites sympathy, not money, and
tells them your floor. Negotiate on the market and the role: "for this role, at
this level, in this city".
::

## As a switcher: how it works

::steps
### Know your market number before you apply
Look at what people with your years and skills are paid: AmbitionBox, Glassdoor,
LinkedIn salary data, and above all people you know who switched recently. My
friend on 21 LPA told me more about my market than any website.

### Interview with several companies in the same few weeks
Offers that arrive together can be compared and used. Offers that arrive two
months apart cannot, because the first one expires.

### Delay the number as long as you can
When a recruiter asks for your expectation on the first call, ask for their
band first. If they insist, give a range anchored to the market, not to your
current salary.

### Negotiate after the offer, in writing, once
With the offer letter in hand, you have the most leverage you will have. Ask
once, clearly, with a reason.

### Use competing offers honestly
Name only offers you really have, and be ready to show the letter. Recruiters
check, and they talk to each other more than you think.
::

## Anchoring

The first number said out loud tends to become the centre of the negotiation.
That is **anchoring**. If you say it and it is low, you are negotiating up from
your own mistake.

::compare{caption="A recruiter asks 'what is your current CTC and expectation?'"}
  :::compare-side{label="Anchoring low" verdict="wrong"}
  "My current CTC is 5 LPA. I'm expecting a 30 to 40% hike."

  They were budgeted up to 16 LPA for the role. They offer 7 and everybody is
  pleased, except you, a year later, when you find out.
  :::
  :::compare-side{label="Anchoring on the role" verdict="right"}
  "I'd rather understand the role first. What is the range you have for it?"

  If pressed: "For this role with three years of Salesforce experience, I'm
  looking at 14 to 18 LPA, depending on the rest of the package."

  The anchor is the market for the role, not the salary you are trying to leave.
  :::
::

::note
In India many companies still insist on your current CTC and ask for payslips.
You cannot always avoid giving it. What you can avoid is letting it be the
anchor: give it if you must, and in the same breath say what the role is worth
in the market.
::

## Using competing offers

```text [competing-offer.txt]
"Thank you for the offer. I'd genuinely like to join [Company], because
[one specific reason: the team / the domain / the role].

I have another offer at [amount] fixed. I'd prefer [Company] if we can
get closer to that. Is there any flexibility on the fixed component?"
```

::warning{icon="i-lucide-alert-triangle"}
**Never invent an offer.** It is checked more often than people think, a
recruiter may ask for the letter, and an invented offer found out ends the
conversation and your name with that recruiter. Real offers are the only
leverage worth having, which is why interviewing with several companies at once
matters more than any script.
::

## A worked example: 5 LPA to 15.5 LPA

What the switch from Accenture looked like, as a method you can copy.

| Step | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| The trigger | A friend who had been a job seeker with me had switched and was on 21 LPA. I was on about 5 |
| Preparation | I revised the Salesforce platform and the interview basics properly, on evenings and weekends, while still working |
| Applying | Several companies in the same window, not one at a time |
| Result | Five offers. The best was 15.5 LPA, about three times my salary |
| The choice | I joined Cognizant on that offer |

The lesson is not a script. It is that five offers arriving close together made
the number move in a way no email from a single offer could have. The same was
true nine months later, with PwC and Twilio both on the table.

## Common mistakes

- **Naming your number first when you could have asked theirs.**
- **Anchoring on your current salary.** It is the number you are leaving.
- **Negotiating the total CTC and accepting it as extra variable.** Ask about
  fixed pay.
- **Bluffing a competing offer.**
- **Accepting on the call.** "Thank you, could I have the letter and reply by
  Thursday?" is normal and costs nothing.
- **Negotiating at a mass-hiring fresher drive.** The band is the band. Ask
  about the joining date instead.

## How to practise

Write your market number today, with three sources for it. Then write the
competing-offer email with your own details and say it aloud as if on a call.
The first time you say a salary number aloud to a stranger should not be the
real call.
