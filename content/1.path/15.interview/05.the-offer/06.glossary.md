Every word from this track that nobody explains to a candidate, in one place.
Several of them are on documents you sign, and a few are expensive to misread.

## The process

::field-group
  :::field{name="Walk-in (drive)" type="process"}
  An interview day with no appointment. Anybody who meets the eligibility turns
  up with a resume. Often a written test, a technical round and HR on the same
  day.
  :::
  :::field{name="Off-campus / pool campus" type="process"}
  Hiring freshers outside their own college's placement cell. A pool campus
  gathers students from several colleges at one venue.
  :::
  :::field{name="Written round" type="process"}
  Aptitude, reasoning and English, often with a coding section. The largest
  filter in fresher hiring.
  :::
  :::field{name="Sectional cut-off" type="process"}
  A minimum score in each section, not only overall. Skipping a section can fail
  you however well you did elsewhere.
  :::
  :::field{name="Technical round" type="process"}
  Questions on your resume, the core subjects and a small coding problem. Where
  most fresher offers are decided.
  :::
  :::field{name="HR round" type="process"}
  Checks whether you will join, stay and fit, and whether you accept the
  conditions. Not a formality.
  :::
  :::field{name="TMAY" type="process"}
  "Tell me about yourself". The first question of almost every interview.
  :::
  :::field{name="STAR" type="process"}
  Situation, Task, Action, Result. The structure for answering "tell me about a
  time when…".
  :::
  :::field{name="Shortlisted" type="process"}
  Selected for the next round. Not an offer.
  :::
::

## Being found

::field-group
  :::field{name="ATS" type="applying"}
  Applicant tracking system. Software that stores and filters resumes on
  keywords. Why a resume should be one plain column.
  :::
  :::field{name="Referral" type="applying"}
  An employee submitting your resume through the company's internal system.
  Usually skips the first filter.
  :::
  :::field{name="Immediate joiner" type="applying"}
  Somebody with no notice period, who can start within days. A fresher's one
  built-in advantage.
  :::
  :::field{name="Ghosting" type="applying"}
  No reply, ever. Common, impersonal, and not a verdict on you.
  :::
::

## The offer letter

::field-group
  :::field{name="LPA" type="money"}
  Lakhs per annum. 6 LPA means ₹6,00,000 a year of CTC.
  :::
  :::field{name="CTC" type="money"}
  Cost to company. Everything the company spends on you in a year, including
  money you never see as salary. Not what arrives in your account.
  :::
  :::field{name="Gross salary (gross fixed)" type="money"}
  Basic plus allowances, before deductions. The number to compare between
  offers.
  :::
  :::field{name="In-hand (take-home, net)" type="money"}
  What arrives in your bank account each month, after PF, professional tax and
  income tax.
  :::
  :::field{name="Variable pay" type="money"}
  A bonus that depends on ratings or company results, "up to" an amount. Ask
  what percentage was actually paid last year.
  :::
  :::field{name="Joining bonus" type="money"}
  Paid once, often clawed back if you leave within a year. Not salary.
  :::
  :::field{name="Bond (service agreement)" type="money"}
  A promise to stay for a period or pay an amount if you leave early. Read the
  duration, the amount and whether it reduces.
  :::
  :::field{name="Probation" type="money"}
  The first months (often 3–6) when either side can end the job with shorter
  notice.
  :::
  :::field{name="ESOP" type="money"}
  Employee stock options: the right to buy company shares later at a fixed
  price. Worth nothing unless the company is sold or listed.
  :::
  :::field{name="RSU" type="money"}
  Restricted stock units: actual shares given on a schedule. At a listed company,
  close to cash once they vest.
  :::
  :::field{name="Vesting and cliff" type="money"}
  Vesting is when stock becomes yours, on a schedule. The cliff is the date
  before which nothing vests, usually one year.
  :::
::

## The salary slip

::field-group
  :::field{name="Basic" type="salary"}
  The base salary, usually 40–50% of CTC. PF and gratuity are calculated from it.
  :::
  :::field{name="HRA" type="salary"}
  House rent allowance. Partly tax-free if you pay rent and choose the old tax
  regime.
  :::
  :::field{name="Special allowance" type="salary"}
  The balancing figure that makes the components add up to the CTC.
  :::
  :::field{name="PF (EPF)" type="salary"}
  Provident fund. 12% of basic from you and 12% from the employer, saved for
  retirement. Yours, but locked.
  :::
  :::field{name="UAN" type="salary"}
  Universal account number. Your PF account number for life. Keep the same one
  across every job.
  :::
  :::field{name="Gratuity" type="salary"}
  A payment on leaving after five years of service. Often in CTC, rarely
  received.
  :::
  :::field{name="Professional tax" type="salary"}
  A small state tax on salaries. ₹200 a month for most salaries in Karnataka.
  :::
  :::field{name="TDS" type="salary"}
  Tax deducted at source. The income tax your employer deducts each month.
  :::
::

## After you join, and when you leave

::field-group
  :::field{name="BGV" type="later"}
  Background verification. An agency checks your education, previous
  employment, identity and sometimes criminal record.
  :::
  :::field{name="Appraisal" type="later"}
  The yearly review that decides your rating and raise.
  :::
  :::field{name="Hike" type="later"}
  A salary increase, usually said as a percentage: "a 30% hike".
  :::
  :::field{name="Notice period" type="later"}
  How long you must keep working after you resign. Often 60–90 days at large
  companies.
  :::
  :::field{name="Notice buyout" type="later"}
  Paying the old company (you or the new employer) for the notice days you do
  not serve.
  :::
  :::field{name="Relieving letter" type="later"}
  The letter confirming you left properly. The next employer's BGV will ask for
  it.
  :::
  :::field{name="Counter-offer" type="later"}
  Your current company offering more to keep you. It usually fixes the money and
  never the reason you were leaving.
  :::
  :::field{name="Lateral hire" type="later"}
  Somebody hired with experience, as opposed to a fresher.
  :::
  :::field{name="Anchoring" type="later"}
  The first number said in a negotiation pulling the final number towards it.
  Why you ask for their range first.
  :::
::

## The pairs people confuse

::compare{caption="Two offers, and the larger CTC is the smaller one."}
  :::compare-side{label="₹9,00,000 CTC" verdict="neutral"}
  Fixed ₹6,50,000 · variable ₹1,50,000 · gratuity ₹43,000 · employer PF
  ₹57,000

  About ₹54,000 a month before deductions. Last year the variable paid out at
  60%.
  :::
  :::compare-side{label="₹8,00,000 CTC" verdict="neutral"}
  Fixed ₹7,20,000 · no variable · employer PF ₹80,000

  About ₹60,000 a month before deductions, guaranteed.

  Lower headline, more money, less risk.
  :::
::

- **CTC and in-hand.** CTC is what you cost. In-hand is what you get.
- **Bond and notice period.** A bond is money owed for leaving early. A notice
  period is time owed after resigning. You can have both.
- **ESOP and RSU.** An option you might buy one day, against a share you are
  given.
