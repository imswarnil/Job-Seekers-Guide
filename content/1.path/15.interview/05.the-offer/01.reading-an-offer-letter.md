CTC (cost to company) is what you cost the company in a year, not what you are
paid. Your monthly in-hand salary is usually 20 to 30% lower than CTC divided by
twelve, and this page shows exactly where the difference goes.

## Why it matters

Two reasons. First, you budget your rent and your life on the in-hand number,
not the CTC. A fresher who expects ₹50,000 a month from a 6 LPA offer and gets
₹39,000 has already signed a PG agreement they cannot afford.

Second, and this one cost me years: your next salary is often calculated from
this one. When you switch, the new company asks your current CTC and offers a
percentage on top. A low first number follows you.

::real-life{title="The number that followed me" source="2018 to 2022"}
My first offer was 1.8 LPA, about ₹13,000 a month in hand, and I was delighted,
and right to take it. At Accenture my salary grew by appraisals, and after three
years I was on about 5 LPA. Each raise was a percentage of the last number. It
took a switch, not an appraisal, to break out of it: five offers and 15.5 LPA.
::

## What is in a CTC

| Component | What it is | Money in your account? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basic salary | The base. Usually 40–50% of CTC. PF and gratuity are calculated from it | Yes, monthly |
| HRA (house rent allowance) | Usually 40–50% of basic. Partly tax-free if you pay rent (old tax regime) | Yes, monthly |
| Special allowance | The balancing figure that makes the total add up | Yes, monthly |
| Employer PF (provident fund) | 12% of basic, paid by the company into your PF account | No. Yours, but locked until you withdraw |
| Gratuity | About 4.81% of basic, paid only if you stay 5 years | No. Most freshers never receive it |
| Variable / performance bonus | Paid yearly or quarterly, "up to" this amount, depending on ratings | Maybe, once a year |
| Joining bonus | Paid once, often clawed back if you leave within a year | Once, in year one only |
| Insurance | Medical cover premium | No. A real benefit, not cash |

And out of the monthly money, three things are deducted:

- **Employee PF**: another 12% of basic, from your side, into the same PF account.
- **Professional tax**: a state tax. In Karnataka, ₹200 a month for most
  salaries.
- **Income tax (TDS)**: tax deducted at source, depending on your total income
  and the regime you choose.

## A worked example: a 6 LPA fresher offer

::warning{icon="i-lucide-alert-triangle"}
**Illustrative only.** Salary structures differ by company, and tax rules change
every year. The income tax line uses the new tax regime as it stood for 2025–26,
under which income up to ₹12 lakh after the ₹75,000 standard deduction paid no
income tax. Check the current year's slabs and your own offer letter.
::

| Component | Per year (₹) | Per month (₹) | How it is calculated |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Basic | 2,40,000 | 20,000 | 40% of CTC |
| HRA | 1,20,000 | 10,000 | 50% of basic |
| Special allowance | 1,39,656 | 11,638 | Whatever is left |
| **Gross fixed salary** | **4,99,656** | **41,638** | Basic + HRA + special |
| Employer PF | 28,800 | 2,400 | 12% of basic. Not paid to you |
| Gratuity | 11,544 | 962 | 4.81% of basic. Only after 5 years |
| Variable pay | 60,000 | (yearly) | 10% of CTC, if paid in full |
| **CTC** | **6,00,000** | 50,000 | What the letter says |

Now the monthly deductions from the gross fixed salary:

| Monthly | ₹ |
| --- | ---: |
| Gross fixed salary | 41,638 |
| Employee PF (12% of basic) | − 2,400 |
| Professional tax (Karnataka) | − 200 |
| Income tax (new regime, 2025–26, illustrative) | − 0 |
| **In hand, per month** | **≈ 39,038** |

So a **6 LPA** offer is about **₹39,000 a month** in your account, not ₹50,000.
The rest is PF (yours, but locked away), gratuity (probably never), and a
variable that arrives once a year if your rating and the company's year allow.

::note
Some companies cap PF at ₹1,800 a month (12% of a ₹15,000 wage ceiling) instead
of 12% of the full basic. That puts a little more in hand each month and a little
less in PF. It is worth asking which one your offer uses.
::

::tip
The number to compare between two offers is the **gross fixed salary**, not the
CTC. A company can add gratuity, insurance and an optimistic variable to make
the CTC larger without anybody being paid more.
::

## What to check in the letter

::steps
### The component-wise breakup
If the letter only has a CTC, ask for the breakup before you accept. It is a
normal request.

### How much is variable, and how much was actually paid last year
"Up to ₹60,000" can mean ₹60,000 or ₹20,000. Ask: "what percentage of the
variable was paid out last year, on average?"

### A joining bonus and its clawback
Paid once, and usually returned if you leave within 12 months. It makes year
one look bigger than year two.

### The designation, location and joining date
They should match what you were told. "Trainee" and "Software Engineer" can be
different pay bands later.

### The bond, notice period and probation
Next page. Read these before you sign, not after.
::

## The one question to ask, always

```text [ask-before-accepting.txt]
"Thank you for the offer. Before I accept, could you share the
component-wise breakup of the CTC, and the actual payout percentage on the
variable component last year?"
```

Normal, professional, and it turns a headline into facts. I have never heard of
an offer being withdrawn over it.

## Common mistakes

- **Budgeting on CTC ÷ 12.** Budget on the in-hand number.
- **Comparing two offers by CTC.** Compare gross fixed.
- **Accepting a verbal number.** Ask for the letter with the breakup.
- **Counting gratuity and the full variable as salary.** Treat gratuity as zero
  and the variable as a bonus if it comes.

## How to practise

Take this offer apart before reading the answer: what arrives each month, what
would you ask, and what would you try to change?

```text [offer-to-practise.txt]
Software Engineer, CTC ₹9,00,000 per annum
  Basic                        3,00,000
  HRA                          1,50,000
  Special allowance            1,80,000
  Employer PF                    36,000
  Gratuity                       14,400
  Performance bonus            1,50,000
  Joining bonus                  50,000
  Insurance                      19,600
```

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="What this offer actually is" icon="i-lucide-calculator"}
  **Gross fixed is ₹6,30,000** (basic + HRA + special), or ₹52,500 a month.
  Everything else is conditional, one-off, deferred or not cash.

  **In hand, roughly ₹49,300 a month**: 52,500 minus ₹3,000 employee PF (12% of
  the ₹25,000 monthly basic) minus ₹200 professional tax, with no income tax
  under the 2025–26 new-regime rules used above. Not ₹75,000.

  **Year two is smaller on paper.** The ₹50,000 joining bonus does not come
  again.

  **The performance bonus is about 17% of CTC**, which is high. If last year's
  payout was 60%, you receive ₹90,000 of it, and this is really an 8.4 LPA offer
  in year one and about 7.9 LPA in year two.

  **What to ask:** the actual bonus payout last year; whether the joining bonus
  is clawed back within twelve months; when the first appraisal is.

  **What to try to change:** move some of the variable into fixed, even at the
  same CTC. Companies agree to that more often than to a higher total, and
  almost nobody asks.
  :::
::
