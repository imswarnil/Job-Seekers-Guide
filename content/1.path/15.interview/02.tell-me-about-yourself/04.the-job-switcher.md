Once you have worked somewhere, the question is the same and the answer has a
different job to do. It still runs present, past, skills, why this role. But the
interviewer is now also listening for why you are leaving, whether you actually
did the work on your resume, and whether you will leave them in a year too.

## Why it matters

A switch is where salaries jump. The same work that earns a 7 to 10% appraisal
where you are can earn a much larger raise somewhere else, and this answer is
the first thing the new company hears.

In 2022, after three years at Accenture on about 5 LPA, I switched. I cracked
five companies, took the best offer at 15.5 LPA and joined Cognizant. This was
the first answer in every one of those interviews.

## What changes when you have experience

::feature-list{columns="2"}
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-briefcase" title="Present is your job, not your degree"}
  Role, years, company, domain. The degree drops to a clause, or disappears.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-hammer" title="Skills become work"}
  Not "I studied SQL". What you built, fixed or ran, for whom, and what changed
  because of it.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-arrow-right-left" title="Past is the path, not the story"}
  How you got into this work, in one line. At Accenture I was assigned the
  Salesforce stream. I wanted web development. That is one sentence, not five.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-target" title="Why this role carries the leaving"}
  Say what you are moving towards. Never what you are running from.
  :::
::

## A worked example: my switch from Accenture

**The situation.** Three years at Accenture, assigned to Salesforce after
training, working on a client project. Wanted more depth, more ownership and a
salary that matched the market. Interviewing for Salesforce developer roles.

::compare{caption="Leaving Accenture, 2022."}
  :::compare-side{label="The weak version" verdict="wrong"}
  "I have 3 years of experience in Accenture. I joined as a fresher and I was put
  in Salesforce. I have worked on Apex, triggers, flows, Lightning components,
  reports, dashboards, integration, data loader and deployment. I am looking for
  a change because there is no growth in my current company and the salary is
  very low. I am a quick learner and I can adapt to any technology."

  A list of eleven technology words and no piece of work. The reason for leaving
  is a complaint, and "salary is very low" hands them the number they will
  anchor their offer to.
  :::
  :::compare-side{label="The strong version" verdict="right"}
  "I'm a Salesforce developer with three years at Accenture.

  I joined as a fresher, was trained on the Salesforce platform, and I've spent
  the last three years on one client project, which means I've seen the same
  system through build, go-live and support.

  Most of my work has been Apex and flows on the service side, and more and more
  of it is reports and dashboards for the business teams, which is the part I
  enjoy most, because people make decisions from it the same day.

  I'm looking at this role because it's closer to the data and the business
  side, with more ownership than I can get on a large services project, and
  that's the direction I want the next three years to go."
  :::
::

::note
That is roughly what I said. I have trimmed the details of the client because
they were not mine to share, and in an interview you should do the same: name
the domain ("a US insurance client", "a retail client"), not the company, unless
it is public.
::

What makes the second version work:

::steps
### It says three years on one project, as a strength
Seeing a system through build, go-live and support is something a new team
values. The weak version never says it.

### It picks two areas instead of listing eleven
Apex and flows, then reports and dashboards. The interviewer will ask about
those, and those are what I knew best.

### It says what I enjoyed, which points at the new role
"People make decisions from it the same day" is a reason, not an adjective.

### It leaves for something, not from something
"Closer to the data, more ownership" says where I want to go. It never mentions
salary, and never says a bad word about Accenture.
::

::warning{icon="i-lucide-alert-triangle"}
**Never criticise your current company in this answer**, even when it is true.
When I later left Cognizant after nine months, the real reasons were
micromanagement and how people were treated. In interviews I said I was looking
for a team with more ownership. Both were true. Only one of them was useful to
say to a stranger deciding whether to hire me.
::

## If you are switching field, not company

Support to development, testing to development, non-IT to IT. The template holds.
The past part carries the switch, and the skills part has to prove it with
something you built outside your current job.

```text [field-switch.txt]
PRESENT   I'm a [current role] at [company], [years] years, and for the last
          [months] I've been moving into [new field].
PAST      In my current role I [the part of your job closest to the new field].
          That's what made me want to do it full-time.
SKILLS    I've learned [skill 1] and [skill 2], and built [project] which
          [what it does, one number].
WHY       I'm applying for this role because it's [new field] full-time, and
          I bring [one thing from the old job the new team will value].
```

## Common mistakes

- **A technology list instead of work.** Eleven words nobody can follow up on.
- **Complaining about the current company.** It tells them how you will talk
  about them.
- **Mentioning salary as the reason.** It is a reason, and it is fine to have.
  It is not a reason to say in the first minute.
- **Claiming work the team did as your own.** "We migrated the system" is
  honest. "I migrated the system" will be tested with a question only the person
  who did it can answer.

## How to practise

Write your version tonight. Then write the answer to "why are you leaving?" in
one sentence that points forward, because it comes straight after this one in
most interviews. Say both aloud, one after the other.
