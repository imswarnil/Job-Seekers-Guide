Four parts, in a fixed order: **present, past, skills, why this role**. It takes
60 to 90 seconds, and because the order never changes, you cannot lose your
place under pressure.

## Why it matters

The order is what stops the answer turning into a biography. Starting with the
present means the first thing the interviewer hears is who you are now, not
where you were born. Ending on why this role means the last thing they hear is
the reason you are in the room.

## The four parts

::flow{numbered direction="vertical" caption="Present, past, skills, why this role. Roughly 15, 25, 25 and 15 seconds."}
  :::flow-step{label="Present: who you are now (about 15 seconds)" icon="i-lucide-map-pin"}
  One or two sentences. Your role or status today and your degree. "I'm a Java
  fresher, B.E. CSE, 2018." Or "I'm a Salesforce developer with three years at
  Accenture."
  :::
  :::flow-step{label="Past: the decision that got you here (about 25 seconds)" icon="i-lucide-git-fork"}
  Not your whole history. The one or two things you did that explain the present:
  the training, the switch, the gap year and what you did with it. This is where
  you become a person rather than a resume.
  :::
  :::flow-step{label="Skills: the evidence (about 25 seconds)" icon="i-lucide-hammer" highlight}
  Two or three skills by name, and one project or piece of work that proves them.
  The interview will come from this part, so choose it deliberately.
  :::
  :::flow-step{label="Why this role: the connection (about 15 seconds)" icon="i-lucide-target"}
  One sentence joining what you can do to what the job needs. Almost nobody says
  it, and it is the difference between "a candidate" and "a candidate for this
  job".
  :::
::

## The fill-in-the-blanks version

Copy it, fill every bracket, and read it aloud. Then cut anything that does not
earn its place.

```text [tell-me-about-yourself.txt]
PRESENT (1–2 sentences)
I'm a [role or "fresher" + main skill], [degree] from [college], [year].

PAST (2–3 sentences)
After [college / my last job / the gap], I [the decision you made:
trained in X / switched to Y / spent Z months doing W] because [the honest
reason, in one line].

SKILLS (2–3 sentences)
I'm strongest in [skill 1] and [skill 2], and I've [studied / worked with]
[skill 3]. The thing I'd point to is [project or work]: it [what it does, with
one number], and the part I did was [your part].

WHY THIS ROLE (1 sentence)
I'm applying for [this role] because [what the role needs] is [what you
have been doing / want to do more of].
```

::tip
Write it in the words you actually speak, not in resume language. "I have been
responsible for the development of" is a sentence nobody says out loud. "I built"
is. If a line is hard to say, it is the wrong line.
::

## A worked example

The template, filled in by a fresher who trained at an institute in Bangalore.

```text [filled-in.txt]
PRESENT
I'm a Java fresher, B.E. computer science from a college in Bhopal, 2018.

PAST
After college I realised I could pass exams but couldn't build anything, so
I moved to Bangalore and spent three months training full-time in Core Java,
SQL and web basics, and studied DBMS and operating systems alongside.

SKILLS
I'm strongest in Core Java and SQL. The thing I'd point to is a student result
system in Java and MySQL: it stores marks for 120 students and calculates
grades in SQL, and I wrote the JDBC layer myself.

WHY THIS ROLE
I'm applying for this Java developer role because backend work with a database
is exactly what I've been practising every day for three months.
```

Said at a normal pace, that is about 55 seconds. It names Java, SQL, DBMS, OS,
JDBC and the project. Every one of those is a question the candidate would be
happy to get.

::compare{caption="The same four parts, two lengths."}
  :::compare-side{label="Over three minutes" verdict="wrong"}
  All four parts, but each one grows: every subject at the institute, both
  college projects, the internship certificate, the hobbies, and "I'm a quick
  learner and a team player" at the end. The interviewer interrupts at two
  minutes and the "why this role" never arrives.
  :::
  :::compare-side{label="60 to 90 seconds" verdict="right"}
  One sentence of present, one decision in the past, two skills and one
  project, one sentence of why this role. Then stop, and let them ask.
  :::
::

## Changing it for each interview

The first three parts barely change. The fourth changes every time.

| The job | Change the skills part to lead with | Change "why this role" to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Java developer | Core Java, OOPs, the project's Java side | Backend work in Java |
| SQL / database / data role | SQL, joins, the project's queries | Working with data every day |
| Testing / QA | How you tested the project, the bugs you found | Quality and finding what breaks |
| Support / service company trainee | Breadth of subjects, willingness to learn | The training and the range of work |

## Common mistakes

- **Memorising it word for word.** It sounds recited, and one interruption
  makes you lose the rest. Memorise the four parts and the nouns, not the
  sentences.
- **Past longer than present.** If you spend 50 seconds on college, the
  interviewer hears somebody who is still there.
- **No "why this role".** The most skipped part, and the easiest to say.
- **Naming a skill you cannot answer questions on.** You will be asked about it
  within the next minute.

## How to practise

::steps
### Fill in the template today
Ten minutes. Brackets and all.

### Say it aloud five times
Different words each time. Keep the four parts and the order.

### Time the fifth one
Over 90 seconds: cut the past. Under 45: add one sentence to the skills part.
::

## Next

::callout{icon="i-lucide-arrow-right"}
A template is easy to fill in when your story is a straight line. But what if
you are from a college nobody in Bangalore has heard of, or you have a year you
did not work and cannot explain? Those are the answers people dread, and they
are the ones that need the template most.
