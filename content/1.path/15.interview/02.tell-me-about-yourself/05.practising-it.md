An answer you have only written is not an answer yet. The gap between knowing
what to say and being able to say it, out loud, to a stranger, is large, and
nobody can see it until they try.

## Why it matters

The first time most people say their answer out loud, it runs to twice the
length, loses the order, and fills with "basically" and "actually". That is
normal and it is fixable in about a week. What is not fixable is finding it out
in the room.

## What to do: four ways to practise

::tabs
  :::tabs-item{label="Record yourself" icon="i-lucide-mic"}
  Phone voice recorder or front camera. Say the answer once, no restarting, and
  listen back. It is uncomfortable and it is the most useful thing on this page.
  You will hear the filler words, the pauses and the place you lost the order,
  none of which you noticed while speaking.
  :::
  :::tabs-item{label="Time it" icon="i-lucide-timer"}
  A stopwatch. Target 60 to 90 seconds. Over 90: cut the past. Under 45: add a
  sentence to the skills part. Most first attempts are two to three minutes.
  :::
  :::tabs-item{label="The mirror" icon="i-lucide-scan-face"}
  Say it to your own face. It feels silly. It shows you where you look away,
  where you fidget, and whether you smile at all. In a video interview this is
  exactly what the interviewer sees.
  :::
  :::tabs-item{label="A friend" icon="i-lucide-users"}
  Somebody else in the PG or at the institute. Ask them to interrupt you halfway
  with "sorry, what was that project?", because a real interviewer will. Then
  swap and do theirs. You learn as much from hearing somebody else's.
  :::
::

## A one-week plan

| Day | Do this | Time |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Fill in the template. Say it three times. Record the third | 20 min |
| 2 | Listen to day 1. Write down every filler word and where you went over time. Record again | 15 min |
| 3 | Say it to a mirror five times, different words each time, same four parts | 10 min |
| 4 | Say it to a friend. Ask them to interrupt once. Swap | 20 min |
| 5 | Record it. Compare with day 1 | 10 min |
| 6 | Write the one-line follow-ups: "tell me more about the project", "why this role", "why the gap" | 20 min |
| 7 | Say the answer and all three follow-ups in a row, recorded | 15 min |

About two hours across the week. I have not found a better two hours anywhere in
interview preparation.

## What to listen for

When you play back the recording, check these five, in this order.

::steps
### Did the first sentence say who you are now?
Not your name, not your town. Your role or status.

### Was it under 90 seconds?
Timed, not guessed. Everybody guesses short.

### How many filler words?
"Basically", "actually", "like", "you know", "so yeah". Two or three is normal.
Ten is a habit, and it clusters exactly where you are least sure of the content.

### Did you name a skill and a project?
If the recording has adjectives and no nouns, the interviewer has nothing to
ask about.

### Did you stop cleanly?
The most common ending is trailing off: "…so yeah, that's it, basically." End on
the "why this role" sentence, then stop talking.
::

## Common mistakes

::compare{caption="The five mistakes I hear on almost every first recording."}
  :::compare-side{label="What happens" verdict="wrong"}
  - Starts with name and hometown
  - Runs over two minutes
  - Lists adjectives: hardworking, quick learner, team player
  - Apologises: "I don't have much experience, but…"
  - Trails off at the end
  :::
  :::compare-side{label="The fix" verdict="right"}
  - Start with the present: "I'm a Java fresher…"
  - Time it, and cut the past first
  - Replace each adjective with one fact that proves it
  - Delete the apology. They know you are a fresher
  - End on "why this role" and stop
  :::
::

::tip
The interviewer may interrupt with "I've seen your resume, tell me something
that isn't on it." Have one extra sentence ready: the reason you chose this
field, the thing you are learning right now, or the one decision you are proudest
of in your project. It is the same answer with the resume parts removed.
::

## How to practise, from today

Two minutes every morning until your first interview: say the answer once, out
loud, timed. It becomes automatic by the end of the first week, and an automatic
answer leaves your nerves free for the questions you cannot prepare for.
