"So, tell me about yourself." It is the first question in almost every
interview, technical or HR, fresher or experienced. It sounds like small talk.
It is not, and I did not understand that for months.

## Why it matters

Three reasons, and the third is the one nobody uses.

::feature-list{columns="3"}
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-clock" title="It is the first impression"}
  The first one or two minutes set how the interviewer hears everything after.
  A clear, confident start buys you patience on the hard questions later.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-repeat" title="It is always asked"}
  Almost every interview, every round. It is the one question you can be
  certain of, which makes it the best-prepared answer you should have.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-signpost" title="It chooses the next questions"}
  Interviewers pick their next question from what you have said. Whatever you
  mention, they ask about. You are being handed 60 to 90 seconds to decide what
  the interview is about.
  :::
::

::pull-quote
Mention Java and your project and you will be asked about Java and your project.
Mention your hobbies and you will be asked about cricket while the interviewer
decides you had nothing else to say.
::

## What the question really asks

They have your resume in front of them. They are not asking for it read aloud.
The question underneath is:

> *Who are you now, what can you do, and why are you in this room for this job?*

A good answer tells them those three things in about a minute, and leaves two or
three doors open that you would like them to walk through.

## How I fumbled it

::real-life{title="What I said at my early walk-ins" source="Bangalore, 2018"}
"My name is Swarnil. I am from Mahroni, a small town in Uttar Pradesh. I did my
schooling there and then B.E. in computer science from LNCT Bhopal. My father's
name is… My hobbies are watching movies and playing cricket. I am hardworking, a
quick learner and a team player."

Ninety seconds and not one sentence that any of the other 700 people in the
building could not have said. It told them where I was born and nothing about
what I could do. The interviewer's next question was whatever came into their
head, which was usually something I could not answer.
::

::compare{caption="The same person, the same year, the same resume."}
  :::compare-side{label="What I said for months" verdict="wrong"}
  Name, town, schooling, father's name, hobbies, three adjectives.

  No skill mentioned, no project mentioned, no reason for being there. The
  interviewer has nothing to pick up, so they pick something at random.
  :::
  :::compare-side{label="What I learned to say" verdict="right"}
  "I'm a Java fresher, B.E. computer science from LNCT Bhopal, 2018. For the last
  three months I've been training full-time in Core Java, SQL and web basics in
  Bangalore, and studying DBMS and OS alongside. I built a student result system
  in Java and MySQL, and the part I understand best is the database side. I'm
  applying for this developer role because that's the work I've been preparing
  for every day since I moved here."

  About 45 seconds. Four doors: Java, SQL, DBMS, the project. All of them
  things I had studied that week.
  :::
::

Count what the second version does. It says what I am now (a Java fresher). It
says what I did about it (three months of training). It gives evidence (the
project). It connects it to the job (why this role). And every noun in it is
something I wanted to be asked about.

## What to do

::steps
### Decide what you want to be asked
Pick the two or three topics you are strongest in. Those go into the answer by
name.

### Leave out what you do not want to be asked
If you are weak on networks, do not say "I studied all the CS subjects". Name the
ones you did study.

### Use the template on the next page
Present, past, skills, why this role. It keeps you out of your biography.

### Keep it under 90 seconds
Long answers lose the room. The interviewer will ask for more if they want it.
::

## Common mistakes

- **Reciting the resume.** They can read. Tell them what the resume does not.
- **Starting with your birthplace, parents or schooling.** Unless it is part of
  why you are here, it is not the answer.
- **Opening with your weakest point.** "I don't have much experience, but…"
  They know you are a fresher. You do not need to apologise for it.
- **Adjectives instead of evidence.** "Hardworking, quick learner, team player"
  carries no information, because everybody says it.
- **Stopping at 20 seconds.** "I'm Swarnil, I did CSE, I'm looking for a job."
  Too short leaves the interviewer to choose for you.

## How to practise

Write your current answer down, exactly as you would say it today, before you
read the next page. Keep it. You will compare it with the version you have at
the end of this chapter.
