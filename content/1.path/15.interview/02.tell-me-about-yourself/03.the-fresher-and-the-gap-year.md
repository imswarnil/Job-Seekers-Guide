The template works for any story. These are the two stories freshers are most
nervous about telling, worked through from the version most people say to the
version that works.

## Why it matters

Both situations feel like weaknesses to the person in them, so both get either
hidden or apologised for. Hiding fails, because the college and the dates are on
the resume. Apologising fails, because it makes the weakness the first thing the
interviewer remembers. The answer is to state it in one line, as a fact, and
move straight to what you did.

I was the first case. LNCT Bhopal is a fine college, but not one a Bangalore
recruiter had any feeling about, and I had been an average student who never
cleared a written round there.

## A worked example: the tier-3 fresher

**The situation.** B.Tech in CSE from a private college in a small city, 62%, no
campus placement, trained for three months at an institute in Bangalore, one
project.

::compare{caption="Same candidate, same resume."}
  :::compare-side{label="The weak version" verdict="wrong"}
  "Good morning sir. My name is Rahul. I am from Gorakhpur. I completed my B.Tech
  from a private engineering college in 2018 with 62 percent. Unfortunately our
  college did not have good placements, so I could not get a job. My father is a
  government employee and my mother is a homemaker. My strengths are
  hardworking and quick learner. My hobbies are listening to music and playing
  cricket."

  It opens on the town, apologises for the college, blames the placements,
  names no skill and no project, and ends on hobbies. The interviewer's next
  question will be "so why no placement?", which is the one Rahul least wants.
  :::
  :::compare-side{label="The strong version" verdict="right"}
  "I'm a Java fresher, B.Tech in computer science, 2018.

  My college didn't have placements for software roles, so after graduating I
  moved to Bangalore and trained full-time for three months in Core Java, SQL
  and web basics, and I've been studying DBMS and operating systems on my own
  alongside.

  I'm strongest in Core Java and SQL. I built a student result system in Java
  and MySQL that stores marks for 120 students and calculates grades in a single
  SQL query per class.

  I'm applying for this role because it's Java backend work, which is what I've
  spent every day of the last three months on."
  :::
::

What changed:

::feature-list{columns="2"}
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-map-pin" title="Present first"}
  "Java fresher" is the first thing heard, not Gorakhpur.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-scale" title="The college in one line, as a fact"}
  "My college didn't have placements for software roles." No apology, and the
  very next words are what he did about it.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-hammer" title="Evidence instead of adjectives"}
  "Hardworking" disappeared. "Every day of the last three months" says it
  better, with a number.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-target" title="Doors he wants opened"}
  Java, SQL, DBMS, OS, the project. The follow-up is now on his ground.
  :::
::

::note
Your percentage does not need to be in the answer. It is on the resume. If they
ask, say it plainly and without comment. A cut-off is either met or it is not;
explaining it does not change it.
::

## A worked example: the gap year

**The situation.** Graduated in 2017. Spent most of a year preparing for a
government exam, did not clear it. Started learning Java in mid-2018. Now
interviewing.

A gap (a period with no job and no study on the resume) is common in India:
government exam preparation, family responsibilities, illness, a failed
business, a second attempt at an entrance exam. Interviewers see it constantly.
What they are checking is whether you can explain it in one honest line and
whether you are now fully committed to this job.

::compare{caption="The same year, told two ways."}
  :::compare-side{label="The weak version" verdict="wrong"}
  "I completed my B.E. in 2017. After that there was a gap because of some
  personal reasons and I was preparing for some exams, and then I thought I
  should do something in IT, so I joined a course. Actually I always had an
  interest in coding…"

  "Personal reasons" and "some exams" sound like hiding something, which invites
  the question. "Actually I always had an interest" contradicts the year spent
  on something else, and the interviewer notices.
  :::
  :::compare-side{label="The strong version" verdict="right"}
  "I'm a Java fresher, B.E. electronics, 2017.

  After college I spent a year preparing for the SSC exams because my family
  wanted a government job for me. I didn't clear it, and by the end of that year
  I knew I wanted to build software instead. So since June I've been training in
  Core Java and SQL in Bangalore, full-time.

  I'm strongest in Java and SQL, and I've built a small attendance tracker in
  Java and MySQL that flags students below 75%.

  I'm applying for this role because I've made the decision, I've put the months
  in, and I want to start building for real."
  :::
::

The strong version names the gap, gives the real reason in one sentence, says
what ended it, and moves on to evidence. The interviewer's follow-up, if there is
one, is usually "why software?", which the candidate is ready for.

::tip
**Say what the gap was, not why it was a mistake.** "I prepared for the SSC
exams and didn't clear it" is complete. You do not owe anybody a defence of a
choice your family and you made. Interviewers who have seen hundreds of gap
years care about the last sentence, not the first.
::

::warning{icon="i-lucide-alert-triangle"}
**Never invent a job to fill a gap.** A made-up year at a small company is
exactly what background verification is built to find, and it turns an ordinary
gap into a withdrawn offer. A true gap, explained in one line, costs you far
less.
::

## Common mistakes

- **Blaming the college, the placements or the economy.** True or not, it sounds
  like somebody who waits for things to happen to them.
- **"Personal reasons" with no detail.** You do not need to share anything
  private. "A family responsibility" or "a health issue, now resolved" is enough
  and does not sound like hiding.
- **Claiming a lifelong passion for coding** after a year of something else. The
  honest version is stronger: you found out, and you acted.
- **Leaving the gap out of the answer entirely** when it is visible on the
  resume. They will ask, and it will sound like you hoped they would not.

## How to practise

Write your own version of whichever situation is yours, using the template. Then
say the one sentence about the college or the gap out loud ten times until it
comes out flat and factual, with no apology in your voice. That sentence is the
one nerves make people rush.
