The written round gets you into the room, and "tell me about yourself" is the
first thing said in it. But before any room, somebody reads a single page about
you for about ten seconds and decides whether to call you at all. This page
answers what goes on that page, and in what order.

## Why it matters

Every walk-in I went to in Bangalore started with somebody at a desk taking my
resume, looking at it for a few seconds, and putting it on one of two piles. At
the big drives, with 700 or more people in the queue, that first look is the
whole of your first round. At an online application, it is a recruiter with a
few hundred resumes for one opening, or a piece of software doing the same job
before any human sees it.

::real-life{title="What I carried to my first walk-ins" source="Bangalore, 2018"}
Two pages. An objective statement at the top: "seeking a challenging position in
a reputed organisation where I can utilise my skills". My class X and XII
percentages. A list of every technology I had heard of. A college project with
one line under it, and hobbies. Not one sentence that described something I
could actually do. I rewrote it in the months at the institute, and the new one
was shorter, plainer, and got read.
::

## What the recruiter reads first

Two passes, and the first one is not reading.

::flow{numbered direction="vertical" caption="The first pass is a scan. You only get the second if the first finds something."}
  :::flow-step{label="Five to ten seconds: the scan" icon="i-lucide-scan" highlight}
  Name and contact, then the skills line, then the project titles, then the
  degree and year. They are checking one thing: does this person match the
  opening? If they cannot find Java on a Java opening in that time, it goes on
  the other pile.
  :::
  :::flow-step{label="About a minute: the read" icon="i-lucide-book-open"}
  Only if the scan passed. They read the project bullets properly, and pick the
  thing they will ask you about first. What they read here decides the
  interview.
  :::
  :::flow-step{label="Sometimes a parser before either" icon="i-lucide-file-search"}
  Large companies and job portals run an ATS (applicant tracking system:
  software that reads resumes and filters them on keywords). A two-column
  layout, a table or contact details in the page header can come out as
  scrambled text, and you are rejected without anybody seeing you.
  :::
::

## What to do

In priority order. The first three matter more than everything after them.

::steps
### Keep it to one page
A fresher has one page of evidence. A second page tells the reader you cannot
tell what matters, and nobody reads it.

### Put the basics in the body, at the top
Name, phone, email, city, LinkedIn, GitHub. In plain text in the first two
lines, not in the page header where a parser skips it. "Bangalore" as the city
matters: plenty of recruiters filter for candidates who are already here.

### Match the skills line to the opening
The skills line is the first thing scanned. If the opening says Java, SQL and
Spring, and you know them, those three words must be the first three on the
line. Change the order for each application. It takes two minutes.

### Projects above education
With no work experience, your projects are your experience. Put them second,
straight after the summary. The degree is one line.

### Write every bullet as what you did, how, and the result
A bullet with no result is a job description, not something you did. "Built a
result module in Java and JDBC that calculates grades for 120 students from a
MySQL table" is a bullet. "Worked on Java" is not.

### One column, one font, saved as PDF
Plain layout, 10 to 12 point, a readable font, no photos, no skill bars. Name
the file `Firstname-Lastname-Resume.pdf`, not `resume-final-2.pdf`.
::

## The template

Copy this and fill it in. The order is deliberate.

```text [fresher-resume.txt]
FIRSTNAME LASTNAME
Bangalore · +91 98XXX XXXXX · you@gmail.com
linkedin.com/in/you · github.com/you

SUMMARY
Java developer (fresher). Trained in Core Java, SQL and web basics.
Built a student result system in Java, JDBC and MySQL. Available to join
immediately.

SKILLS
Languages:  Java, SQL, JavaScript
Web:        HTML, CSS, basic Spring Boot
Database:   MySQL, JDBC
Tools:      Git, Eclipse / IntelliJ, Postman
CS basics:  OOPs, DBMS, Operating Systems, Computer Networks

PROJECTS
Student Result System | Java, JDBC, MySQL          github.com/you/results
- Stores marks for 120 students across 6 subjects in 3 MySQL tables
- Calculates percentage and grade with a stored rule, one query per class
- Rejects marks above the maximum at the database with a CHECK constraint

Attendance Tracker (final-year project) | HTML, CSS, JavaScript, PHP
- Team of 4. I built the login and the daily attendance screen
- Shows each student's attendance % and flags anyone below 75%

EDUCATION
B.E. Computer Science, <College>, <City>             2018 · 68%
Class XII, <Board>                                   2014 · 72%

TRAINING (optional)
Java Full Stack course, <Institute>, Bangalore       3 months, 2018

LANGUAGES
English, Hindi
```

::tip
The summary is two lines about what you can do, not an objective statement
about what you want. "Available to join immediately" is worth including as a
fresher. Companies hiring freshers often need somebody in two weeks, and you are
the only candidate who does not have a notice period.
::

## What to cut

::feature-list{columns="2"}
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-x" title="The objective statement"}
  "Seeking a challenging position…" Every resume in the pile has it. It says
  nothing and takes the most valuable space on the page.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-x" title="Class X marks, if space is short"}
  Keep the degree and class XII. Some service companies still ask for class X
  percentage on their own form. They will ask; it does not need the space.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-x" title="Skill bars and star ratings"}
  Four stars out of five in Java means nothing, and it invites the question of
  what the fifth star is.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-x" title="Photo, date of birth, father's name, full address"}
  Space with no signal. City and phone number are enough.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-x" title="Anything you cannot talk about for five minutes"}
  Every word on the page is a question you may be asked. Listing Python because
  you watched one video is a trap you set for yourself.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-x" title="Hobbies, unless they are evidence"}
  "Reading, music, cricket" is filler. "I write a weekly blog on Java" is
  evidence, and belongs under projects.
  :::
::

## A worked example

The same fresher, the same project, written twice.

::compare{caption="The bullets are where the interview gets decided."}
  :::compare-side{label="What most freshers write" verdict="wrong"}
  **Student Management System**
  - Developed a student management system using Java
  - Worked on database and backend
  - Implemented various features

  Three bullets and nothing an interviewer can ask a second question about.
  "Various features" is the sound of somebody with nothing specific to say.
  :::
  :::compare-side{label="The same work, written properly" verdict="right"}
  **Student Result System** | Java, JDBC, MySQL
  - Stores marks for 120 students across 6 subjects in 3 MySQL tables
  - Calculates percentage and grade in one SQL query per class, using GROUP BY
  - Rejects marks above 100 at the database with a CHECK constraint

  Every line has a number and a mechanism. Each one invites a question you
  would be happy to answer: "why a CHECK constraint and not a Java if?"
  :::
::

::pull-quote
An interviewer picks their first question from your resume. Every bullet is a
door you are inviting them to open. Write only the doors you want opened.
::

## Common mistakes

- **Two pages.** Cut it to one. If you cannot, the second page is the part to
  delete.
- **A fancy template from a website.** Two columns, icons, a coloured sidebar.
  It looks good to you and arrives at the parser as nonsense.
- **The same resume for every opening.** The skills line should match the job.
  Reorder it every time.
- **Spelling mistakes.** One typo in "Jave" or "responsiblity" is enough for the
  other pile, because the job is writing things a computer reads exactly.
- **An email address from school.** `rockstar_raju99@…` costs you nothing to
  replace today.
- **A long list of technologies.** Ten languages on a fresher resume reads as
  ten things you have not used. Four you can defend beat ten you cannot.

## How to practise

::steps
### Write it today, in one sitting
Use the template above. One hour. It does not need to be perfect; it needs to
exist.

### The ten-second test
Hand it to somebody for exactly ten seconds, take it back, and ask what they
remember. If they cannot say what job you want and one thing you built, the top
third needs rewriting.

### Read every line aloud and ask "what would they ask me?"
If the honest answer is "something I cannot answer", change or delete the line.

### Update it every week you are searching
Every new thing you learn or build goes on. The resume you carry to your tenth
walk-in should be better than the one you carried to your first.
::
