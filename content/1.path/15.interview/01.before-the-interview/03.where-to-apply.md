This page answers where a fresher's calls actually come from, and what to set up
on each channel so that recruiters find you instead of the other way round.

## Why it matters

A fresher search in Bangalore runs on four channels. Three are online and are on
this page. The fourth, walk-ins, has the next page to itself, because it is how
I got my first job.

| Channel | What it is good for | What it costs you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Naukri | Service companies, mid-size firms, consultancies. Recruiters search it every day | Free. The paid plans are optional |
| LinkedIn | Startups, product companies, referrals, recruiters who post openings | Free, and an hour to set up properly |
| Referrals | Skipping the first filter at almost any company | Asking people, which is uncomfortable |
| Walk-ins | Freshers with no referral and no campus placement | A day per walk-in, travel, and patience |

::note
None of these works alone. The people I knew who got jobs in 2018 and 2019 were
applying on all of them in the same week, and it was rarely obvious in advance
which channel would bring the call.
::

## What to do: Naukri

Naukri is where recruiters at service companies and consultancies search for
candidates. Most of your calls from it will come from being found, not from
applying.

::steps
### Fill in every field
Profile completeness affects how often you appear in recruiter searches. Photo
optional, everything else filled: education, skills, projects, preferred
location, expected salary.

### Write the headline for the search box
Recruiters search by keyword. "Java Developer | Fresher | Core Java, SQL, JDBC,
HTML, CSS | Bangalore | Immediate joiner" is found. "Seeking opportunities" is
not.

### Set the key skills to match the jobs you want
Up to the limit, starting with the most important. Java, SQL, JDBC, Spring
Boot, HTML, CSS, JavaScript, OOPs, DBMS.

### Update the profile often
Recruiters often sort by recently active or recently updated. Changing a line
every few days keeps you near the top. It is a known habit, and it costs a
minute.

### Upload the PDF resume
The same one-page resume. Recruiters download it before calling.
::

::warning{icon="i-lucide-alert-triangle"}
**No genuine company asks a candidate for money.** Not for registration, not for
a "training fee", not for a laptop deposit, not for "processing". Fake recruiters
use Naukri names and logos in calls and on WhatsApp. If money is mentioned
before you have an offer letter from a company you can find on its own website,
end the call.
::

## What to do: LinkedIn

LinkedIn matters more for startups and product companies, and it is where
referrals come from.

::steps
### A headline that says what you do
"Java Developer (Fresher) · Core Java, SQL, Spring Boot · Bangalore". Not
"Student at XYZ College" and not "Aspiring".

### An About section of four or five lines
What you learned, what you built, what role you want. Your resume summary,
slightly longer.

### Projects under Featured, with the GitHub link
Somebody who opens your profile should reach your code in one click.

### Turn on Open to Work, visible to recruiters
You can choose recruiters only if you do not want the green frame.

### Search jobs with "Past week" and "Entry level"
Apply to the new ones first. A post that is three weeks old already has
hundreds of applicants.
::

## Referrals

A referral is somebody inside the company submitting your resume through the
company's internal system. It usually skips the first filter, and at some
companies it is the only reliable way a fresher is seen at all.

You do not need to know somebody well. You need to ask well. Most engineers get
a referral bonus if you are hired, so a good request is doing them a favour too.

```text [referral-request.txt]
Hi <Name>,

I'm <Your Name>, a Java fresher in Bangalore (B.E. CSE, 2018). I saw the
opening for <Job Title> at <Company> (Job ID <ID>, link below).

I match it on Java, SQL and JDBC, and I built a student result system in
Java and MySQL: github.com/you/results

Would you be willing to refer me? I've attached my resume, and I'm happy to
send anything else you need.

Thank you either way.
<Your Name>
<Phone>
```

It works because it is short, it names the exact job, it gives them everything
they need to submit the referral in two minutes, and it does not ask them to
find you a job. It asks them to do one specific thing.

::tip
Ask people from your college who graduated one to three years before you. They
remember being you. Search LinkedIn for "<your college> <company>", and send the
message to five people at once, not one at a time over a month.
::

## A worked example: one Monday

::timeline{label="What an hour a day on these three channels looks like"}
  :::timeline-item{title="15 minutes" date="Naukri" state="done"}
  Update one line of the profile. Apply to five fresh openings that match.
  :::
  :::timeline-item{title="20 minutes" date="LinkedIn" state="done"}
  Search the past week's entry-level Java openings. Apply to five. For two of
  them, find an alumnus at the company and send the referral message.
  :::
  :::timeline-item{title="10 minutes" date="The tracking sheet" state="current"}
  Every application goes in a sheet: date, company, role, channel, status.
  :::
  :::timeline-item{title="15 minutes" date="Walk-in listings" state="todo"}
  Check for walk-ins this week and pick the ones worth a day. Next page.
  :::
::

## Common mistakes

- **Paying a stranger to "guarantee" interviews or a job.** Nobody can.
- **Applying to hundreds of openings in a day with one click.** Easy-apply to
  everything gets you calls for jobs you do not want, at companies you cannot
  remember when they phone.
- **An empty LinkedIn profile with 3 connections.** Recruiters open it before
  they call.
- **Asking for a referral with "please refer me for any suitable role".**
  Nobody can act on that. Give them one job ID.
- **Not answering unknown numbers.** Recruiter calls come from unknown numbers.
  Answer, and answer as if it is an interview, because sometimes it is.

An hour a day, every day of the search, split roughly as in the Monday above.
