A project is anything you built that runs and that you can explain line by line.
Your final-year project counts. The mini project at a training institute counts.
A small thing you built on a Sunday counts. What does not count is anything you
cannot explain.

## Why it matters

A fresher resume has three sections that carry weight: skills, projects and
education. Education is the same for thousands of people from the same
university. Skills is a list anybody can type. Projects is the only section
where two candidates from the same college look different.

It also decides the interview. In most fresher technical rounds I sat, the
first real question after the introduction was about the project on my resume.
The candidates who could explain their own project well were given the next
twenty minutes on ground they knew.

## What to do

::steps
### Pick one or two projects, not five
One you can talk about for ten minutes beats five you can talk about for one
minute each. Put the strongest first.

### Know every line of it
If it was a team project, know the whole thing, and say clearly which part was
yours. "My part was the login and the attendance screen" is a strong sentence.

### Give it a number and a mechanism
How many students, tables, screens, tests. How it works in one line. See the
worked example below.

### Put it on GitHub with a README
A link is checkable, and that is why it carries weight a claim does not.

### Be ready to open the code
Some interviewers ask you to share your screen and walk them through it. Have it
on your laptop, running, before every interview.
::

## Institute and college projects, described honestly

Most training institutes in Bangalore end a course with a mini project, and
thousands of students submit something very close to the same one. That is
fine. Interviewers know it. What separates you is whether you understood it and
whether you changed anything.

::compare{caption="The same institute project, two ways to describe it."}
  :::compare-side{label="Hiding where it came from" verdict="wrong"}
  **Hospital Management System** | Java, Spring, Hibernate, MySQL
  - Designed and developed a complete hospital management system
  - Implemented the full backend architecture

  An interviewer who has seen the same project from fifty candidates asks one
  question about Hibernate and the answer falls apart. "Designed" was not true.
  :::
  :::compare-side{label="Honest, and still strong" verdict="right"}
  **Student Result System** (course project, extended) | Java, JDBC, MySQL
  - Built during a 3-month Java course; extended it on my own afterwards
  - Added grade calculation in SQL and a CHECK constraint for marks over 100
  - Wrote the JDBC layer myself instead of the provided one, to understand it

  It says where it came from, and then says what you did beyond that. Every
  line survives a follow-up question.
  :::
::

::tip
The strongest thing you can do with an institute project is change one real
thing yourself. Add a feature, replace a part, fix something that was wrong.
Then you have a sentence nobody else in the queue has: "the part I added was…"
::

## Fake experience

In Bangalore you will meet people, sometimes at the institute, sometimes online,
who offer "experience letters" from companies for a fee, or tell you to add two
years at a small firm to your resume because "everyone does it".

::warning{icon="i-lucide-alert-triangle"}
Do not. Almost every company that hires in numbers runs a background
verification (BGV) after the offer: an agency contacts your previous employers,
checks the company exists, checks the PF (provident fund) records, which show
every employer who ever paid into your account. Fake experience is found there,
often after you have resigned from somewhere else or turned down other offers.
The result is a withdrawn offer, sometimes termination after joining, and at the large IT companies a
record that the next employer can see.
::

It also fails earlier than that. A "two years of experience" candidate is asked
two-years questions: how deployments worked, what the team size was, what the
client was like. Freshers who faked it cannot answer, and the interviewer knows
within five minutes.

I was a fresher with no experience at 33 walk-ins. I was selected at the 34th as
a fresher with no experience. It is slower than lying, and it is the only
version that does not fall apart later.

## What a GitHub profile needs

If you put a GitHub link on the resume, somebody may open it, and they will
judge it in about thirty seconds.

::compare{caption="Two profiles, same person, same amount of work."}
  :::compare-side{label="What most freshers have" verdict="wrong"}
  Fourteen repositories. Nine are tutorial copies named `java-practice` and
  `test123`. No READMEs. Commit messages "update", "fix", "asdf". Nothing
  touched in five months.
  :::
  :::compare-side{label="What gets you a call" verdict="right"}
  Two or three repositories, pinned. The main one has a README saying what it
  does, how to run it, the table structure, and one thing you decided and why.
  Commit messages are sentences.
  :::
::

A README that is enough:

```md [README.md]
# Student Result System

Stores marks for students and calculates percentage and grade per class.
Java 17, JDBC, MySQL 8.

## Run it
1. Create the database: `mysql -u root -p < schema.sql`
2. Set your password in `db.properties`
3. `javac -cp lib/* -d out src/**/*.java`
4. `java -cp out:lib/* ResultApp`

## Tables
student(id, name, department), subject(id, name, max_marks),
exam_result(student_id, subject_id, marks)

## One decision
Marks above max_marks are rejected by a CHECK constraint in the database,
not only in Java, so a bad row cannot get in from anywhere.
```

## Common mistakes

- **Listing a project you cannot run.** If the interviewer says "show me" and
  it does not start, the project becomes evidence against you.
- **"Designed and developed" for a team project.** Say which part was yours.
  Claiming all of it is caught the moment they ask about the part you did not
  write.
- **Copying a GitHub project and renaming it.** Interviewers search. It takes
  them a minute.
- **Five small projects of the same kind.** Two calculators and three to-do
  lists say less than one thing you understood deeply.
- **Adding experience you do not have, even "a small internship".** If it is on
  paper, it can be checked.

## How to practise

::steps
### Write the ten-minute version
For your main project: what it does, the tables, the one hardest part, the one
thing you would change. Write it on one page.

### Explain it to somebody who does not code
If they understand what it does after two minutes, you have the opening of your
answer.

### Explain it to somebody who does
Ask them to interrupt with "why?" after every sentence. The place you run out of
answers is the part to go and learn this week.
::
