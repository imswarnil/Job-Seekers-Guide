The HR round is deciding whether you will join, whether you will stay, and
whether the team can work beside you. It is not a formality, and treating it as
one is visible.

## Why it matters

At a fresher walk-in, HR is often the last round of the day, after the written
test and the technical round. By then most candidates are tired and relieved,
and they answer HR questions carelessly because "the hard part is over".

It is not over. I have seen people clear the technical round and be rejected in
HR. Nobody tells you which round lost it, so they conclude they were not good
enough at programming and prepare for the wrong thing.

::real-life{title="Why I underestimated it" source="Bangalore, 2018"}
In my first walk-ins I assumed the HR round was paperwork: date of birth,
expected salary, sign here. When an HR person asked "why should we hire you?" I
said "because I am hardworking and I need this job." Both were true. Neither was
a reason for them. It took me a while to understand that every HR question is
really asking about the company's risk, not my feelings.
::

## The five things HR is deciding

::feature-list{columns="2"}
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-log-in" title="Will you actually join?"}
  Companies make more offers than they have seats, because a share of people
  accept and never turn up. HR is estimating whether you are one of them.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-anchor" title="Will you stay?"}
  Training a fresher costs months. If you leave in six, that money is gone. The
  bond, relocation and five-years questions are all this question.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-users" title="Can the team work beside you?"}
  How you talk about other people, how you handle a disagreement, whether you
  blame. Behavioural questions test this.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-check-square" title="Do you accept the conditions?"}
  Shifts, location, bond, notice period, joining date. A no here can end the
  process even after a perfect technical round.
  :::
  :::feature{icon="i-lucide-message-circle" title="Can you communicate?"}
  Clear, calm, honest answers in plain English. In many service companies HR is
  also judging whether you can talk to a client.
  :::
::

## How it differs by company

| | Service company (mass hiring) | Startup | Product company |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Length | 10–20 minutes | Often merged with the founder or manager | 30–45 minutes, sometimes a full "culture" round |
| Focus | Conditions: bond, shifts, relocation, joining date | Will you stay when it gets hard? Can you do many things? | Behavioural: past situations, STAR answers |
| Salary | Usually fixed for freshers, not discussed much | Often negotiated here | Discussed with a recruiter, often separately |
| What loses it | Refusing a condition, looking like you will not join | Looking like you want a big-company job | Vague, rehearsed answers with no real examples |

## What to do

::steps
### Answer the question underneath
"Where do you see yourself in five years?" is "will you still be here?". Answer
that one.

### Be honest, and positive
Never lie about a condition you cannot accept. But say a true thing in its most
useful form: "I'd prefer day shifts, and I'm fine with rotational shifts when
the project needs it" rather than "I don't like night shifts".

### Use evidence, not adjectives
"I'm hardworking" carries no information. "I studied on Sundays for two months
while working six days a week" does.

### Know your own conditions before you walk in
Can you relocate? Can you do night shifts? Can you sign a two-year bond? Decide
at home, not in the room.

### Stay as alert as you were in the technical round
It is still an interview.
::

## Common mistakes

- **Treating it as paperwork.** Relaxed body language and one-word answers read
  as not caring.
- **Saying yes to everything and then declining the offer.** HR remembers, and
  in some companies it is recorded.
- **Talking about what you need** ("I need this job, my family…") instead of
  what you bring.
- **Criticising a previous company, college or colleague.** It tells them how
  you will talk about them.

## How to practise

Read the next two pages and write your answers down in your own words. Then ask
a friend to play HR and to push back on one answer: "but why would you stay two
years?" The follow-up is where rehearsed answers break.
