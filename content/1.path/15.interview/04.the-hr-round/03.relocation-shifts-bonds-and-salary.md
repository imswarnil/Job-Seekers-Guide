These questions are not about your personality. They are about conditions, and
a "no" to one of them can end the process even after a perfect technical round.
So decide your answers at home, honestly, before anybody asks.

## Why it matters

In mass fresher hiring, HR has a list of conditions and a pile of candidates. If
you cannot meet a condition, the kindest thing for both of you is to find out in
the room. If you can, say so clearly, because hesitation reads as "will not
join".

I signed a bond for my first job. It was a startup, 1.8 LPA, six days a week,
and the bond was part of the offer. I read it, understood what leaving early
would cost, and signed, because I needed the start. Two months later I got into
Accenture and had to deal with it. Decide what you can live with before the room
decides it for you.

## What to do

::steps
### Decide your real answer to each, at home
Relocation, shifts, bond, joining date. Write them down.

### Say yes clearly to what you can accept
Not "I think so", not "if required". "Yes, I can relocate."

### Say no honestly, with an alternative if there is one
"I'd prefer Bangalore, and I can relocate for the first year if the project
needs it" is honest and flexible.

### Never say yes to something you will refuse later
It costs you the offer later instead of now, and costs the company a seat.
::

## The questions

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="Are you willing to relocate?" icon="i-lucide-map"}
  Service companies hire for all their offices and assign locations after
  training. "Yes" is what they are hoping to hear. If you have a real reason to
  stay in one city, say it once, briefly.

  ```text [if yes]
  "Yes. I moved from my hometown to Bangalore for this career, so I'm
  comfortable moving again. I'd prefer Bangalore, but location won't stop me
  joining."
  ```

  ```text [if there is a real constraint]
  "I'd prefer Bangalore because [one honest reason: a parent's health,
  a family responsibility]. If the role needs another city, I'd like to
  discuss it, but I don't want to say yes now and change it later."
  ```
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Can you work night shifts / rotational shifts?" icon="i-lucide-moon"}
  Many support and service roles for clients in the US or UK work in their time
  zone, which means evening or night shifts in India. Development roles more
  often have occasional late calls than permanent night shifts. Ask which it is.

  ```text [template]
  "Yes, I can work rotational shifts. Could you tell me how often the
  night shift comes up in this role, so I can plan for it?"
  ```

  If you genuinely cannot, say so. A night-shift job you cannot sustain is a job
  you will leave in three months.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Are you ready to sign a bond / service agreement?" icon="i-lucide-file-signature"}
  A bond (also called a service agreement) is a promise to stay for a period,
  usually one to three years, or pay an amount if you leave early. It is common
  at companies that train freshers for months. The offer chapter explains how to
  read one.

  ```text [template]
  "Yes, I understand you invest in training. Could you tell me the
  duration and the amount, and whether it reduces over time, so I know
  exactly what I'm agreeing to?"
  ```

  Asking the terms is normal and does not count against you. Signing without
  knowing them is how people end up owing lakhs.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="Why is there a gap in your resume?" icon="i-lucide-calendar-x"}
  One honest line about what the gap was, one line about what you did, one line
  about why you are fully committed now.

  ```text [template]
  "After [college / my last job] I spent [time] [what: preparing for
  exams / looking after a family member / recovering from illness].
  [What ended it]. Since then I've [what you've done to prepare]."
  ```

  *"After college I prepared for the SSC exams for a year and didn't clear
  them. That's when I decided on software. Since June I've been training
  full-time in Java and SQL, and I've built a project I can show you."*

  Do not invent a job to fill it. Background verification exists to find that.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="When can you join?" icon="i-lucide-calendar-check"}
  As a fresher, "immediately" or a specific date within two weeks is a real
  advantage over experienced candidates with a notice period. Say it clearly.
  If you are waiting on another interview, you still give a date; you do not
  need to mention the other interview here.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="What salary do you expect? (fresher)" icon="i-lucide-indian-rupee"}
  At mass-hiring service companies the fresher salary is usually a fixed band,
  and HR asks mainly to check you will accept it. Find the band before the
  interview: the job listing, Glassdoor, AmbitionBox, and people who joined last
  year.

  ```text [if the band is fixed and you know it]
  "I understand the package for freshers is around [band]. That works
  for me. Could you share the component-wise breakup?"
  ```

  ```text [if you don't know the band]
  "As a fresher I'm flexible, and I'm more focused on the role and the
  training. Could you tell me the range you have for this position?"
  ```

  ```text [if they insist on a number]
  "Based on what I've seen for fresher Java developer roles in Bangalore,
  I'd expect something in the range of [X] to [Y] LPA, depending on the
  rest of the package."
  ```

  **Loses:** "Anything is fine" or "as per company norms". Both sound like you
  have no idea of your own value, and the first reliably gets the bottom of the
  band. **Also loses:** a number far above the market with nothing behind it.
  :::
::

## A worked example

**The situation.** A fresher at a walk-in, HR round, service company. The offer
has a two-year bond, rotational shifts, and a location to be decided after
training.

::compare{caption="The same candidate, the same conditions."}
  :::compare-side{label="Hesitating" verdict="wrong"}
  **HR:** Are you okay with a two-year bond?
  **Candidate:** Umm… how much is it? Actually I was thinking of doing an MBA
  after one year… but I can manage, I think.

  HR has heard "will leave in a year". The offer is now unlikely, and the
  candidate did not even find out the amount.
  :::
  :::compare-side{label="Clear" verdict="right"}
  **HR:** Are you okay with a two-year bond?
  **Candidate:** Yes, I understand you're training us for three months. Could
  you tell me the amount and whether it reduces after the first year?

  A clear yes, a sensible question, and the candidate now knows what they are
  agreeing to.
  :::
::

## Common mistakes

- **Saying yes to relocation or nights and then declining the offer** when you
  see the location. HR remembers, and it wastes a seat another fresher needed.
- **Signing a bond without asking the amount.**
- **A long story about the gap.** One line of what, one line of what now.
- **Naming a number first when you could have asked the band.** Ask once,
  politely. If they insist, give a researched range.

## How to practise

Write your honest answer to each of the six, today. For each "no", write the
most flexible true version. Then say them aloud to a friend playing HR, who
should push once on each: "and if the only opening is in Chennai?"
