The script for the family conversation needed a budget in your hand. This page
builds it, line by line, in rupees, so the number you say at home is one you
can defend.

## Why it matters

More job searches end because the money ran out than because the person could
not learn Java. Money pressure makes you take the first scam that promises a
job, skip the institute, or go home at month four when month six was going to
be the one.

I did not have family money to fall back on. My sister was earning and sending
me part of her salary every month, and I knew exactly what that cost her. That
is why I count every rupee on this page. If you are in the same position, you
will too.

::warning
Every figure here is **approximate**. The "2018 to 2019" column is what I and
the people around me paid. The "roughly now" column is a rough guide from
current listings, and it will be wrong for some areas and some months. **Check
today's prices yourself** before you plan around any of them.
::

## The six-month budget

This assumes a shared room in a PG in an area like BTM Layout, meals included,
an institute course, and walk-ins across the city.

| Item | 2018 to 2019, per month | Roughly now, per month | Six months, at today's rough figure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PG, shared room, with two or three meals | ₹5,500 to ₹7,000 | ₹9,000 to ₹14,000 | ₹54,000 to ₹84,000 |
| PG deposit (once, refundable) | one month's rent | one to two months' rent | ₹9,000 to ₹28,000 |
| Food outside the PG (tea, snacks, walk-in days) | ₹1,500 | ₹2,500 to ₹3,500 | ₹15,000 to ₹21,000 |
| Local travel (BMTC buses, metro) | ₹800 to ₹1,200 | ₹1,500 to ₹2,500 | ₹9,000 to ₹15,000 |
| Institute fees (once) | tens of thousands for a full Java course | check each institute | ask at the demo |
| Phone and data | ₹200 to ₹400 | ₹300 to ₹500 | ₹1,800 to ₹3,000 |
| Printing, photos, resumes, folders | ₹300 | ₹500 | ₹3,000 |
| Toiletries, laundry, small things | ₹700 | ₹1,000 to ₹1,500 | ₹6,000 to ₹9,000 |
| Train ticket, one way, sleeper | under ₹1,000 | check IRCTC for your route | once |
| Emergency money, untouched | | | ₹15,000 to ₹20,000 |

Add it up with your own numbers. Without the institute fee and the laptop, the
six months come to something in the region of **₹1.1 to ₹1.8 lakh** at today's
rough prices. Add the institute on top.

::note
Institute fees vary too much to give one number: by institute, course, branch,
and whether there is an offer running. Ask the fee at the demo class, ask what
is included, and ask whether it can be paid in instalments. The chapter *How to
learn* covers this.
::

## The laptop question

You do not need to buy a laptop before you go.

::pros-cons
---
title: Buying a laptop in month one
pros:
  - You can practise at the PG at night, which is when most of the practice happens
  - Online platforms and mock tests are easier on a proper screen
cons:
  - Institutes have labs, and the first month is mostly classes
  - It is the single biggest cost you can postpone
  - A new one can take a third of your whole budget
---
::

My advice: go without one. Use the institute lab and a cyber café for the first
month. If you find you are practising every night and the lab is not enough, buy
a second-hand one in month two. In 2018 a usable second-hand laptop was around
₹15,000 to ₹25,000. Check today's.

## What to do

- [ ] Write the table above in a notebook with **your** numbers.
- [ ] Decide where the money comes from: savings, family, a sibling, a loan from
      a relative. Write each source and the amount beside it.
- [ ] Keep the emergency money in a separate account, or with someone at home
      who will only send it if you ask.
- [ ] Decide now what happens at month six if there is no offer. More money from
      home? A part-time job? Going home? Decide it while you are calm.
- [ ] Track what you spend, daily, in one line. Mine lived in the back of my
      aptitude notebook.

## A worked example

Here is a monthly plan for somebody with ₹1.2 lakh for six months, after the
institute fee is paid.

| Month | Planned spend | Running balance | What is happening |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Before leaving | ₹1,000 (train) | ₹1,19,000 | |
| Month 1 | ₹24,000 (includes deposit) | ₹95,000 | PG, SIM, bank, demo classes, institute starts |
| Month 2 | ₹15,000 | ₹80,000 | Institute |
| Month 3 | ₹15,000 | ₹65,000 | Institute, first walk-ins |
| Month 4 | ₹16,000 | ₹49,000 | Walk-ins, more travel |
| Month 5 | ₹16,000 | ₹33,000 | Walk-ins |
| Month 6 | ₹16,000 | ₹17,000 | Walk-ins. Emergency money still intact |

The balance at the end of month six is the emergency money, untouched. If you
are dipping into it by month four, something in the table is wrong and you should
fix it then, not at month six.

## Where the money actually leaks

::feature-list{columns="2"}
:::feature{icon="i-lucide-coffee" title="Eating out with friends"}
Friends in the PG going out every weekend. ₹300 a time adds up to a month's
travel.
:::
:::feature{icon="i-lucide-car-taxi-front" title="Autos and cabs to walk-ins"}
You will be late and you will take an auto. Leave earlier and take the bus.
:::
:::feature{icon="i-lucide-badge-indian-rupee" title="Paid courses you never finish"}
A course bought at midnight during a sale, opened twice. Finish the free ones
first.
:::
:::feature{icon="i-lucide-shield-alert" title="Scams"}
"Registration fees" and "training fees" for jobs. The *Finding a job* chapter
lists them. Never pay for a job.
:::
::

## Common mistakes

- **Budgeting for three months.** Plan for six, have a plan for nine.
- **Spending the deposit in your head.** It is refundable only if the PG gives it
  back. Many do not, or deduct heavily. Do not count on it.
- **No emergency line.** Somebody will fall ill, a phone will break, a PG will
  close. It happened to people around me every month.
- **Being too proud to say money is short.** Say it early, at home, when there
  is time to fix it.

## How to practise

At the end of every week, spend five minutes adding up the week's spending and
writing the running balance. If one week is more than a quarter of the monthly
plan, find the line that leaked.
