You know which city. Now you have to say it at home. This page is about the
conversation that decided my life, and how to have yours so it ends in a plan
rather than an argument.

## Why it matters

In most families like mine, moving to a big city for a private job is not a
decision you make alone. Your parents may be paying for it. They may have a
plan for you already. And even if they are not paying, you will call home every
week for a year, and every call will be easier if they understand what you are
doing.

My father had a plan ready. He wanted me to become a **government teacher**. It
is secure, it is respected, and in Mahroni it is exactly the kind of job that
lets a family stop worrying. It was not a bad plan. It was the only plan he could
see.

I wanted to be a YouTuber, and I knew I needed a job first. I did not say much
at all.

::pull-quote{attribution="My sister, 2018"}
He is not going to stay here. He is going to Bangalore. He will study there and
attend walk-ins.
::

My sister said that, and she backed it with money: she was earning and she sent
me part of her salary every month. Between the two of us we made the decision,
and my father came round. I did not win the conversation. She did. What I learned
is that you need **one person in the room on your side**, and a plan specific
enough that they can defend it when you are not there.

## The honest comparison

Your family is not wrong to prefer a government job. Do not pretend it has no
advantages. They will know you are pretending.

::pros-cons
---
title: Preparing for a government exam
pros:
  - Job security that a private company will never match
  - Respect at home and in your town, which matters to your parents
  - Pension and benefits, and a steady, predictable life
  - You can prepare from home, which costs far less
cons:
  - Very large numbers of applicants for a small number of posts
  - Exam cycles can be delayed by months or years, and results with them
  - Years of preparation can end with nothing that transfers to another job
  - Salary growth follows a fixed scale, however good you are
---
::

::pros-cons
---
title: A private IT job
pros:
  - Hiring happens every week, not once a year
  - The skills transfer: Java, SQL and interviews are useful at every company
  - Salary can grow fast if you keep learning and switching
  - A degree in CS already points you here
cons:
  - Less security: companies do lay people off
  - The first salary can be low, and the first job hard
  - You have to move to a city and spend money before you earn any
  - The first year is lonely and uncertain
---
::

My own numbers, for what they are worth: my first salary was ₹13,000 a month.
Years later, after switching companies, I was earning many times that. Neither
of those numbers would have been possible for me in the government route, but a
government job would have given my parents a kind of peace that my first year
in Bangalore did not.

## What to do

::steps
### Find your one person
A sibling, an uncle, a cousin already working, a teacher your father respects.
Talk to them first. Ask them to be in the room.

### Prepare the numbers, not the dream
Parents do not argue with a plan. They argue with a feeling. Bring the six-month
budget from the next page, the timeline from the previous chapter, and the name
of the area you will live in.

### Say what happens if it fails
This is the line that changed my father's mind more than anything. Say it
plainly: "If I have no job in twelve months, I will come back and try it your
way." Mean it.

### Agree on check-ins
A call every Sunday. A message after every walk-in. Parents are scared of
silence more than of failure.

### Do not bring up the dream yet
I did not tell my father about YouTube. The job came first. Your passion is not
cancelled. It is queued until there is a salary.
::

## A worked example

Here is a script. Change the words to your own, but keep the shape.

::callout{icon="i-lucide-message-square-quote"}
"Papa, I know you want me to prepare for the teacher's exam, and I understand
why. I want to try for a software job first. I have looked at it properly. I will
live in BTM Layout in Bangalore, in a PG that costs about this much. I will join
an institute for three months and then go to walk-in interviews. I have a budget
for six months, here it is. I will call every Sunday. If I do not have a job in
twelve months, I will come home and prepare for the exam, and I will not argue."
::

Notice what the script does. It respects the other plan. It gives a place, a
cost and a date. It has an exit. None of that is emotional, and that is the
point.

## Common mistakes

- **Arguing about which job is better.** You will not win, and it is the wrong
  question. The question is whether this plan is good enough to try.
- **Asking for money without a budget.** It sounds like a gap year. Show the
  table.
- **Promising a job in three months.** You are setting up a disappointment for
  both of you. Promise effort and check-ins, not an outcome.
- **Going silent after a bad week.** Call anyway. "Three walk-ins, no luck, I
  know what to work on" is a good call.
- **Treating the government route as failure.** For many people it is the right
  choice. It was not mine.

::tip
If nobody in your family will back you, that is worth knowing about your family,
not about you. Talk to a senior from college who made the move. Sometimes the one
person on your side is outside the house.
::

## How to practise

Say the script out loud twice, alone, before you say it at home. It feels silly.
It works: the second time, your voice stops shaking on the part about coming
back if it fails.
