The route starts with one decision that shapes the rest: which city to move to.
This page compares the six cities where most fresher IT jobs in India are, and
tells you why I picked Bangalore.

## Why it matters

The city decides four things you cannot change later without moving again: how
many companies are hiring freshers near you, whether they hire through walk-ins
you can turn up to, how much a month of living costs, and whether there are
good, affordable institutes within a bus ride.

Get the city wrong and you pay for it every day, in bus fares, in rent, and in
walk-ins you could not reach.

::note
I lived in Bangalore, so I know it properly. For the other cities I am relying
on friends who job-hunted there and on what I saw in walk-in listings. Treat my
view of them as a starting point, and ask someone who has lived there.
::

## The cities, side by side

| City | Fresher openings | Walk-in culture | Cost of living for a job seeker | Training institutes | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Bangalore** | The most, by a distance | Very strong: drives most weeks | Highest of the six | The largest clusters in the country (BTM, Marathahalli, Rajajinagar) | Software development, testing, startups, product companies |
| **Pune** | Many | Strong | Medium | Good (Shivajinagar, Hinjewadi side) | Services companies, testing, automotive IT |
| **Hyderabad** | Many, and growing | Strong, especially at large services firms | Medium | Very good (Ameerpet is famous for it) | Services, large captives, Salesforce and cloud skills |
| **Chennai** | Many | Strong at services firms | Medium, cheaper food | Good | Services companies, support and testing roles |
| **NCR (Noida, Gurgaon, Delhi)** | Many, spread out | Medium: more through consultancies | Medium to high, long commutes | Good (Noida, Laxmi Nagar) | Services, startups, if you have family nearby |
| **Kolkata** | Fewer | Weaker | Lowest | Fewer | Staying near family in the east, services roles |

These are relative, not measured. Nobody publishes a clean count of fresher
openings by city, and the picture shifts year to year. Check current listings on
Naukri and LinkedIn for "fresher" plus the city before you decide.

## Why I chose Bangalore

::feature-list{columns="2"}
:::feature{icon="i-lucide-building-2" title="The most companies"}
More IT companies of every size than anywhere else, from services giants to
twenty-person startups. More companies means more walk-ins, and more walk-ins
means more chances to fail and learn.
:::
:::feature{icon="i-lucide-users" title="The job-seeker ecosystem"}
Whole neighbourhoods, like BTM Layout, exist around people like us: PGs, cheap
mess food, institutes, cyber cafés that print resumes. You are not alone there.
:::
:::feature{icon="i-lucide-door-open" title="Walk-ins you can reach"}
Most weeks there was a drive somewhere in the city that took freshers with my
degree. I could turn up with a printed resume and sit the written round.
:::
:::feature{icon="i-lucide-message-circle" title="Everybody said so"}
My seniors from college who got jobs mostly went there. When you know nothing,
following people who made it is not a bad rule.
:::

## What to do

Decide in this order, and stop at the first rule that settles it for you.

::steps
### Where do you already know someone?
A cousin, a senior, a college friend. One person who can meet you at the station
and tell you which PG to avoid is worth more than any comparison table.

### What do you want to do?
If you want software development or testing and you have no preference, choose
Bangalore. If you are drawn to a specific company's stream, look at where it
hires most.

### What can you afford for six months?
If Bangalore's rent would leave you with no emergency money, Pune, Hyderabad or
Chennai are sensible. Being able to stay for eight months in a cheaper city
beats running out of money at month four in an expensive one.

### Which language do you speak?
Offices work in English everywhere, but daily life is easier in Hindi-speaking
NCR, and people from the north manage fine in Bangalore, Pune and Hyderabad too.
::

## Common mistakes

- **Choosing by salary headlines.** The famous packages you read about are not
  fresher packages. Fresher salaries are close enough across these cities that
  cost of living matters more.
- **Moving without six months of money** because "I will get something
  quickly". This is the mistake I see most.
- **Splitting between two cities.** Pick one. Walk-ins reward the person who
  can turn up at nine the next morning.
- **Ignoring the commute.** Bangalore is large and slow. Where you live inside
  the city matters almost as much as the city itself. The *Where to live*
  chapter covers that.

## How to practise

Spend thirty minutes tonight on Naukri and LinkedIn. Search "fresher Java" or
"fresher software" and filter by each city. Count the results for the last
seven days and write them down. It is rough, but it is today's number, not mine
from 2018.
