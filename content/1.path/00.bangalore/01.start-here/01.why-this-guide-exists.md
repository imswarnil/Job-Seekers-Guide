This page answers two questions: why somebody wrote a guide to moving to
Bangalore and getting a first IT job, and how to use it without getting lost.

## Why it matters

In 2018 I finished Computer Science at LNCT Bhopal. Four years, a degree, and I
had never cleared a written round. Not one. I was an average student and I had
not studied well, and I knew it, but I did not yet know how much it would cost.

I went home to Mahroni, a small town in Uttar Pradesh. I wanted to be a
YouTuber. My father wanted me to become a government teacher. My sister backed
me, and between the two of us we decided I would go to Bangalore, study, and
attend walk-ins until somebody hired me.

So I took the train to Bangalore. I gave a few interviews in the first weeks,
and they went badly for a reason that was not mysterious at all.

::pull-quote
I had a degree in this, and I knew nothing. Not "a bit rusty". Nothing.
::

That was the moment this guide started. I sat in my PG in BTM Layout and made
myself a promise: I would write down everything about the job search, the good
decisions and the stupid ones, the prices, the places, the rejections. If I got
a job, that record would become a path for somebody like me, so they would not
have to work it all out alone.

I got the job. It took a year, 33 walk-ins, and a selection at the 34th. This is
the record.

::callout{icon="i-lucide-scroll-text"}
It is written by one person, from one route. I am not a placement agency and I
cannot promise you a job. What I can promise is that every step here is one I
actually took, with the numbers I actually paid, and that where I got something
wrong I say so.
::

## What to do

Read this track in order before you leave home. It is short. The rest of the
guide is the studying, and you will live in that part for months.

- [ ] Read **Start here** (this chapter) today.
- [ ] Read **The decision** before you talk to your family, not after.
- [ ] Read **Getting there** the week you book the ticket.
- [ ] Read **Where to live** on the train.
- [ ] Read **How to learn** before you pay any institute a rupee.
- [ ] Read **Finding a job** before your first walk-in, even if that is weeks away.

## How to use this guide

There is no menu and no marketing. There are three things on the screen, and
that is on purpose: fewer places to wander off to.

::feature-list{columns="3"}
:::feature{icon="i-lucide-panel-left" title="The sidebar"}
The whole guide, in order, grouped into parts: the move, learning to code,
computer science, the written round, the interview and my story. Your progress
shows beside each part.
:::
:::feature{icon="i-lucide-search" title="Search"}
Press `/` or `⌘K` (`Ctrl K` on Windows) anywhere. It searches every page and
every heading, so "tatkal", "bond" or "left join" all go straight to the right
place.
:::
:::feature{icon="i-lucide-circle-check" title="Mark as finished"}
At the bottom of every page. It ticks the page off in the sidebar so you can see
how far you have come on a bad day, and there will be bad days.
:::
::

::warning
Your progress is saved **in this browser, on this device**, and nowhere else.
There is no account and no login. If you clear your browser data, or switch from
your phone to a cyber café computer, the ticks will not follow you. Use one
device for reading the guide if you can.
::

Every page also has **previous** and **next** at the bottom. The guide is
written as a chain: each page ends on the problem the next page solves. If you
follow next, you will never wonder what to read after this.

## A worked example

Here is what a sensible first day with this guide looks like, for somebody who
is still at home:

::steps
### Morning
Read this chapter and the next page, *The whole route, and how long it takes*.
About twenty minutes.

### Afternoon
Read the three pages of *The decision*. Write down, on paper, which city you are
leaning towards and why, and fill in the budget table with your own numbers.

### Evening
Do not talk to your family yet. Sleep on it. Have the conversation tomorrow,
with the budget in your hand.
::

## Common mistakes

- **Skipping to the Java track.** The move decides whether you get to study at
  all. A bad PG or a money crisis in month three ends more job searches than bad
  code does.
- **Reading everything and doing nothing.** This guide is only useful if you act
  on it the same day.
- **Treating my route as the only route.** It is one route that worked. Where
  there is a real choice, I show you both sides.
