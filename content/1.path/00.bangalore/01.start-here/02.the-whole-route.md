You have seen why this guide exists. This page shows you the whole route at
once, from home to an offer letter, and answers the question everybody asks
first and nobody answers honestly: how long will this take?

## Why it matters

When I arrived in Bangalore I thought I would be working in three months. That
belief nearly broke me, because at month four I had no offer, my money was
running out, and I read the gap as proof that I was not good enough.

It was not proof of anything. My plan was wrong, not me. If somebody had shown
me a realistic timeline before I left, month four would have felt like month
four, not like failure.

::pull-quote
The most dangerous thing you can carry to Bangalore is a timeline somebody made
up to make you feel better.
::

## The honest timeline

Here is the same route as a plan you can put money against. The ranges are wide
because people are different. Plan for the long end.

| Phase | Short end | Long end | What decides it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Deciding and saving at home | 2 weeks | 2 months | Whether your family needs convincing, and whether you have savings |
| Settling in (PG, SIM, bank, routes) | 1 week | 2 weeks | How quickly you find a PG you trust |
| Choosing an institute (demo classes) | 3 days | 2 weeks | How many you try |
| Institute course | 3 months | 4 months | The course; mine was about 3 months |
| Walk-ins until the first offer | 1 month | 8 months | Your written round, mostly |
| **Total, home to offer** | **about 5 months** | **about 14 months** | |

Mine was about a year. I would plan for nine to twelve months and be pleased if
it is shorter.

::warning
The walk-in phase is the one people under-budget, in money and in patience. It
is not a sign that something is wrong if it takes six months. It is a sign that
something is wrong if, after ten walk-ins, you are still failing at the same
round and have not changed how you prepare for it.
::

## What to do

- [ ] Write down the date you plan to leave home.
- [ ] Add twelve months to it. That is your planning horizon, not your target.
- [ ] Budget money for **six months** in the city (the next chapter shows you
      how), and know where months seven onwards would come from.
- [ ] Start aptitude practice **at home, now**, before you board the train. It
      is the round that rejected me most, and it needs no institute.
- [ ] Tell one person you trust what the real timeline is, so somebody knows
      that month five without an offer is normal.

## Where the time actually goes

People imagine the year as "studying, then a job". It is not. It is two things
running at once, with the second one starting earlier than you expect.

::flow{direction="vertical" caption="The two tracks overlap. Walk-ins start while you are still learning."}
:::flow-step{label="Learning (months 1 to 3)"}
Institute classes in the day. CS subjects, aptitude and English in the evening.
:::
:::flow-step{label="Learning and walk-ins together (months 3 to 5)" highlight}
You start attending walk-ins before you feel ready. Every rejection tells you
exactly which round to prepare for next.
:::
:::flow-step{label="Walk-ins, with study around them (months 5 onwards)"}
Two or three walk-ins a week. Revision and mock interviews on the other days.
:::
::

The middle step is the one that matters. I waited too long to start going to
walk-ins after the institute, because I wanted to feel ready. You never feel
ready. The walk-in is the test that tells you what to study.

## A worked example

Say you leave home on 1 July.

::steps
### July
Settle into a PG in the first week. Sit demo classes in the second. Start the
institute course by the third week.

### August to September
Institute in the day, CS subjects and aptitude in the evening. Try one or two
walk-ins at the end of September, expecting to fail them. Write down which round
you failed at.

### October to January
Two walk-ins a week. Keep revising the round that is rejecting you.

### February
If you are still without an offer, you are inside the normal range. Check your
money, not your worth.
::

## Common mistakes

- **Planning for three months.** It sets you up to feel like a failure on a
  schedule that was never real.
- **Waiting to feel ready before the first walk-in.** You will learn more from
  one real written round than from a week of practice papers.
- **Leaving aptitude until after the institute.** It is the first round at
  almost every walk-in. Start it at home.
- **Comparing your month with someone else's.** A friend who got placed in six
  weeks may have been studying for a year before you met him.

## How to practise

There is nothing to practise on this page, but there is one habit to start
today: **write down every day what you did**, in one line. When I finally got
the offer, those lines were the proof that the year had not been wasted, and
they became this guide.
