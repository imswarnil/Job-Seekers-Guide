You have an institute for the day and platforms for the evening. This page puts
them into one week that you can repeat for three months without burning out.

## Why it matters

At the institute I studied Java, SQL and web technologies for about three
months. In parallel, on my own, I studied the important CS subjects, aptitude and
English. The institute was half the plan. The other half was what I did every
evening, and without a routine the evening disappears: a video, a chat with a
roommate, a bit of scrolling, and it is midnight.

A written round tests aptitude first. The technical round asks about DBMS and
OOPs as often as Java. The HR round is in English. If you only do what the
institute teaches, you will be well prepared for about one round in three.

::pull-quote
I never missed a single class at the institute. Not one. But the classes were the easy
half. The evenings were where the job was won or lost.
::

## What to study, in priority order

::steps
### 1. The institute course
Java, SQL, web. Attend every class. Make your own notes, not only the handouts.
Write your own small example programs for every topic, so that in an interview
you can explain something you built.

### 2. Aptitude, every day
Quantitative aptitude, logical reasoning and verbal ability. It is the first
round at most walk-ins and the one that rejected me most. Forty-five minutes a
day, timed.

### 3. One CS subject at a time
DBMS, then OOPs, then operating systems, then computer networks. One at a time,
in order, for two to three weeks each.

### 4. English, out loud
Ten to fifteen minutes a day of speaking, not reading. Tell me about yourself,
your projects, why you want the job.

### 5. Mock interviews, weekly
Once a week, with a friend or at the institute. Technical questions and HR
questions, spoken, with somebody watching.
::

## The weekly timetable

This is a timetable for somebody in a morning institute batch. Shift the blocks
if your batch is in the afternoon; keep the amounts.

| Time | Monday to Friday | Saturday | Sunday |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6:30 to 7:15 | Aptitude, 20 questions, timed | Aptitude, a full mock paper | Sleep in |
| 7:15 to 8:15 | Bath, breakfast, walk | Bath, breakfast, walk | |
| Morning to early afternoon | Institute classes | Institute classes, or a walk-in once you start | Revise the week's institute notes (2 hours) |
| Afternoon | Institute lab: type out and run the day's programs | Lab or walk-in | Free: go outside, call home |
| 17:00 to 18:00 | Rest, walk, tea | Rest | |
| 18:00 to 19:30 | CS subject: one lecture plus notes | Mock interview with a friend | Plan next week, one page |
| 19:30 to 20:00 | English: speak out loud, record yourself | English | |
| 20:00 to 21:00 | Dinner | Dinner | Dinner |
| 21:00 to 22:30 | Coding practice: 2 or 3 easy problems | Free | Early night |
| 22:30 | Write one line: what I did today. Sleep | Sleep | Sleep |

Totals for a week: roughly 5 hours of aptitude, 7 hours of CS subjects, 3 hours
of English, 7 hours of coding practice, and one mock interview, on top of the
institute.

::tip
Stick the timetable on the wall beside your bed. Tick each block when it is
done. Missing one block is fine. Missing the same block three days running means
the timetable is wrong, not you; move it to a time that works.
::

## Sunday

Sunday is not a study day off. It is a different kind of day.

::feature-list{columns="3"}
:::feature{icon="i-lucide-notebook-pen" title="Morning: revise"}
Two hours going back over the week's institute notes. This is when things
actually stick.
:::
:::feature{icon="i-lucide-sun" title="Afternoon: live"}
Go outside. A park, a walk, a cheap lunch with friends. Call home.
:::
:::feature{icon="i-lucide-calendar" title="Evening: plan"}
One page for next week. Which CS topic, which aptitude topics, which walk-ins.
Then sleep early.
:::
::

Sundays stayed like this after I got my first job, too. That job was six days a
week, so Sunday was the only day left, and I spent it studying and preparing for
the next interview. The habit I built here is what got me out of that job two
months later.

## A worked example

Here is how a single weekday of this looks when it goes right, and when it goes
wrong.

::compare{caption="Same day, same person, same distractions."}
:::compare-side{label="Without a timetable" verdict="wrong"}
Back from the institute at 3. Lie down for ten minutes, wake at 5. Watch a
DBMS video at 2x while chatting. Dinner. Scroll. Start aptitude at 11pm, do six
questions, sleep at 1am. Wake late, miss breakfast.
:::
:::compare-side{label="With the timetable" verdict="right"}
Aptitude done before breakfast. Institute and lab. A walk at 5. One DBMS
lecture with notes at 6. Fifteen minutes of spoken English. Two coding problems
after dinner. One line in the notebook. Asleep by 11.
:::
::

## Common mistakes

- **Doing only what the institute teaches.** Aptitude and English decide the
  first and last rounds.
- **Studying all CS subjects at once.** One at a time, in order.
- **No rest.** Seven days of eleven hours burns you out by week six.
- **Reading English instead of speaking it.** Interviews are spoken.
- **Planning the perfect timetable and never starting.** Start today with the
  aptitude block; add the rest over the week.

## How to practise

Tonight, write your own timetable using the table above, with your batch timing.
Tomorrow, follow only the first block (aptitude before breakfast). Add one block
each day. By the end of the week you will be running the whole thing.
