The institute covers Java, SQL and web basics. The rest (aptitude, English, the
CS subjects, and practice) happens in the evenings, on your own, on free and paid
websites. This page tells you which one is for what.

## Why it matters

There is more free material for a job seeker today than anyone could finish in
ten years. That is the problem. Without a rule for which platform does which
job, you spend the evening choosing a video instead of learning from one.

::pull-quote
Every subject I needed was free on the internet the whole time. What I lacked
was somebody saying: this one, for this, now.
::

## Which platform for what

| Need | Platform | Free or paid | What it is good for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Aptitude and reasoning practice | **IndiaBix** | Free | Topic-wise questions with solutions, in the style of written rounds |
| Aptitude, company-specific papers | **PrepInsta**, **Freshersworld** practice sections | Mostly free, some paid | Previous papers and patterns from specific companies |
| CS subjects (DBMS, OS, networks, OOPs) | **GeeksforGeeks** | Free | Short articles on every interview topic, plus interview experiences |
| CS subjects, properly | **NPTEL** | Free (certificate exam is paid) | University lectures from IIT and IISc professors, in full |
| Coding practice, interview-style | **LeetCode** | Free, with a paid tier | Data structures and algorithms problems; start with the easy ones |
| Coding practice, gentler | **HackerRank** | Free | Language basics and structured practice tracks; many companies test on it |
| Web reference | **MDN Web Docs** | Free | The most accurate reference for HTML, CSS and JavaScript |
| Web, first look | **W3Schools** | Free | Quick examples to try; check details on MDN |
| A full structured course | **Udemy** | Paid (often discounted) | One complete course on a topic; buy in a sale, and finish it |
| University-style courses | **Coursera** | Free to audit many, paid certificates | Courses from universities and companies |
| Explanations, any topic | **YouTube** | Free | See below |

::note
Platforms change their free tiers and prices. Everything above was free, or
mostly free, when I used it or checked it; confirm before you pay for anything.
::

## YouTube, by kind of channel

I am not going to list channel names, because good channels stop posting and new
ones appear every year. Look for these **kinds** instead:

::feature-list{columns="2"}
:::feature{icon="i-lucide-list-video" title="Full playlists for one subject"}
A complete DBMS or OS series, taught in order by one teacher, often in Hindi or
English. The best kind for CS subjects. Watch it in order.
:::
:::feature{icon="i-lucide-calculator" title="Aptitude shortcut channels"}
Teachers who solve written-round questions fast. Good for methods; practise the
methods on IndiaBix afterwards.
:::
:::feature{icon="i-lucide-mic" title="Mock interview channels"}
Recorded mock interviews for freshers. Watch how candidates answer, and how
interviewers follow up.
:::
:::feature{icon="i-lucide-languages" title="Spoken English channels"}
Daily-use English and interview phrases. Ten minutes a day, then speak it out
loud.
:::
::

::tip
Before you start a YouTube playlist, check three things: is it complete, is it
recent enough for the topic, and have people in the comments said it helped them
in interviews. Then commit to finishing it.
::

## What to do

::steps
### Pick one platform per need
From the table above, write down one platform for aptitude, one for CS subjects,
one for coding practice and one for English. One each.

### Free first
Finish a free resource before you buy anything. NPTEL and GeeksforGeeks cover
nearly every CS subject an interviewer asks about.

### Buy one paid course, at most, at a time
If you buy a Udemy course, finish it before buying the next. A sale will come
round again.

### Practise more than you watch
For every hour of video, spend at least an hour solving questions without the
video.
::

## A worked example

A month of evenings for somebody at the institute in the day:

| Week | Aptitude (IndiaBix) | CS subject | Coding practice | English |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Percentages, 20 a day | DBMS: first third of an NPTEL or YouTube playlist | HackerRank Java basics, 3 a day | 10 minutes a day, speak aloud |
| 2 | Ratios and averages | DBMS: second third | HackerRank Java, 3 a day | Tell me about yourself, drafted |
| 3 | Time and work | DBMS: finish; GeeksforGeeks DBMS interview questions | LeetCode easy, 1 a day | Tell me about yourself, recorded |
| 4 | Mixed papers, timed | OOPs: GeeksforGeeks articles | LeetCode easy, 1 a day | Mock with a friend |

The rest of this guide has tracks for each of these subjects, in the right
order. Use the platforms to practise what the guide teaches.

## Common mistakes

- **Collecting.** Bookmarks, playlists, courses in the cart. Collecting feels
  like progress and is not.
- **Watching at 2x without practising.** You will recognise everything and be
  able to do none of it.
- **Starting LeetCode on hard problems.** Start on easy, and stay there until
  easy is easy.
- **Relying on AI chat for answers you have not tried first.** It will often be
  right and sometimes confidently wrong, and you cannot tell which until you
  understand the topic yourself.

## How to practise

Tonight, make four bookmarks, one per need, and delete the rest. Then do twenty
aptitude questions on IndiaBix, with a timer, before you go to sleep.
