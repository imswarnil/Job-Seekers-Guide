The first line in the budget is a train ticket. This page covers booking it,
choosing the right station, packing, and the folder of documents that you will
carry to every walk-in for the next year.

## Why it matters

A train booked late means a waiting-list ticket, a night in a general coach, or
a flight you cannot afford. A missing marksheet means a walk-in you cannot
attend, because the HR desk checks documents before the written round. And
getting off at the wrong station can mean an hour and a half across Bangalore
traffic with your luggage.

None of this is hard. All of it is easy to get wrong the first time.

## Booking on IRCTC

IRCTC is the Indian Railways booking website and app. You need an account with
a verified mobile number and email, and it is worth making it days before you
book, because verification sometimes fails the first time.

::steps
### Make the account early
Register on the IRCTC app or website. Verify your mobile and email. Add yourself
to the saved passenger list, with your name exactly as it is on your ID.

### Book as early as you can
The general booking window opens a fixed number of days before the journey. It
was 120 days when I went; it has since been cut to 60. Check the current window
on IRCTC. The earlier you book, the more likely you get a confirmed berth.

### Choose the class
Sleeper is the cheapest class with a berth, and it is what I and most people I
knew travelled in. 3AC is more comfortable and costs roughly two and a half to
three times as much. Over a journey of a day and a half or more, a berth matters.

### Understand the ticket status
`CNF` is confirmed. `RAC` means you share a berth but you can travel. `WL` is
waiting list: you may or may not get a berth, and an e-ticket that stays on the
waiting list when the chart is prepared is cancelled automatically.
::

::tip
If you are travelling on a waiting-list ticket, look at the IRCTC "confirmation
chance" or a PNR prediction app. A high waiting-list number close to the
journey date rarely clears. Book another train as a backup rather than hoping.
::

### Tatkal

Tatkal is a quota of seats that opens **one day before the journey**, at a
fixed time, for a higher fare. When I went, AC tatkal opened at 10:00 and
sleeper tatkal at 11:00. Check the current timings on IRCTC.

::warning
Tatkal seats on popular trains are gone in a minute or two. If you are going to
try it, be logged in five minutes early, have your passenger details saved, and
keep your UPI app open. Do not rely on tatkal for the journey that starts your
whole plan. Book ahead.
::

## Which station

Most trains into Bangalore arrive at one of three big stations. Which one
depends on your train, and it matters, because they are far apart.

| Station | Code | Where | Good for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| KSR Bengaluru City (Majestic) | SBC | The centre, beside the main bus stand | Buses to almost everywhere, including BTM; the metro interchange |
| Yesvantpur Junction | YPR | North-west | Rajajinagar, Vijayanagar, Malleshwaram |
| SMVT Bengaluru (Baiyappanahalli) | SMVB | East | Whitefield, Marathahalli, KR Puram side |

SMVT did not exist as a big terminal when I went; it opened in 2022, and some
long-distance trains that used to go to the city station now go there. Check
which station your train ends at, and whether it stops at another one earlier
that is closer to your area.

For BTM Layout, KSR Bengaluru City (Majestic) is usually the easiest: buses from
the Majestic bus stand run towards BTM and Silk Board all day. Check the current
BMTC and metro routes from your station on Google Maps before you arrive.

## What to pack

::tabs
:::tabs-item{label="Clothes"}
- Two formal shirts and one pair of formal trousers for walk-ins and the institute
- One pair of formal shoes and one pair of comfortable ones
- Four or five sets of daily clothes
- A light jacket; Bangalore mornings and the monsoon months are cooler than
  most of north India
- An umbrella, because it rains when you least expect
:::
:::tabs-item{label="For daily life"}
- A padlock for your PG cupboard
- A bedsheet, a pillow cover and a thin blanket (many PGs give a mattress only)
- A steel bottle and a small tiffin box
- A power bank and a spare charger
- A small bag of common medicines, and any prescription you take
:::
:::tabs-item{label="For studying"}
- Two long notebooks and pens; you will fill them
- An aptitude book if you already have one
- Earphones, for YouTube lectures in a shared room
:::
::

## The documents folder

This is the part that matters most. Every walk-in desk I sat at asked for some
of these, and some asked for all of them. Keep them in one waterproof folder.

| Document | Originals | Photocopies | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Class 10 marksheet and certificate | Yes | 5 | Age proof and percentage checks |
| Class 12 marksheet | Yes | 5 | Percentage cut-offs at walk-ins |
| All semester marksheets | Yes | 5 sets | Aggregate and backlog checks |
| Degree or provisional certificate | Yes | 5 | Proof of degree |
| Aadhaar card | Yes | 5 | ID at walk-ins, PG, SIM, bank |
| PAN card | Yes | 3 | Bank account, and your first salary |
| Passport-size photographs | | 20 | Forms at every walk-in and the PG |
| Resume, printed | | 10 | Carry more than you think |

::caution
**Never hand over an original certificate** to a company or an agency, for any
reason, including "we will keep it until your bond ends". Show originals for
verification and take them back. Keep scans of every document in your email and
in a cloud folder, so a lost folder is a bad day and not a disaster.
::

## A worked example

Somebody leaving on 1 July might plan like this:

::steps
### Early May
Make the IRCTC account. Book a sleeper ticket as soon as the window opens.

### June
Collect originals. Get photocopies and photographs done in your town, where
they are cheaper. Scan everything to your email.

### A week before
Pack using the tabs above. Print ten copies of your resume, even if it is thin.

### The day before
Check the PNR status. Download the e-ticket to your phone and keep a screenshot
in case there is no signal.
::

## Common mistakes

- **Booking late** and travelling on a waiting-list ticket without a backup.
- **Getting off at the last stop by habit** when an earlier stop was closer to
  your PG.
- **Leaving originals at home** "to be safe". The first walk-in that asks for
  them, you cannot sit.
- **Only one copy of the resume.** Print ten.
- **Packing only casual clothes.** Walk-ins expect formals.

## How to practise

Before you book, open IRCTC and search your route on three different dates.
Note the trains, the stations they end at, the journey time and the sleeper
fare. Ten minutes, and you will book the real ticket calmly.
