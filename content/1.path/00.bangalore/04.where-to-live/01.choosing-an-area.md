Bangalore is not one place. This page answers which part of it to live in, as a
job seeker, and why I lived in BTM Layout.

## Why it matters

Where you live decides three things you feel every day:

- **Rent.** The same shared room can differ by several thousand rupees a month
  between two areas a few kilometres apart.
- **The commute to the institute.** An hour each way in traffic is two hours a
  day you are not studying. Over three months that is about 150 hours, roughly
  the length of a whole course.
- **Who is around you.** Living among other job seekers means walk-in tips at
  breakfast, somebody to do mock interviews with, and not feeling like the only
  person without a job.

## Why BTM Layout

I lived in a PG in BTM Layout, in the south of the city, because that is where
job seekers live. It is not the nicest area in Bangalore. It is the most
practical one for somebody in my position.

Many of the big Java, testing and web institutes had branches in BTM or a short

## The areas, compared

These are the areas job seekers most often live in. The rent figures are for a
shared room in a PG with food. The "then" column is my memory of 2018 to 2019;
the "roughly now" column is a rough guide. **Check current listings before you
decide.**

| Area | Why people live there | Shared PG with food, then | Roughly now | Commute notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **BTM Layout** | The biggest institute cluster, cheap food, PGs everywhere | ₹5,500 to ₹7,000 | ₹9,000 to ₹13,000 | Close to Silk Board junction, which is famous for traffic; metro now runs nearby |
| **Madiwala** | Next to BTM and Koramangala, often a little cheaper | ₹5,000 to ₹6,500 | ₹8,500 to ₹12,000 | Same traffic as BTM |
| **Marathahalli** | A second institute cluster, near the Outer Ring Road tech parks | ₹5,500 to ₹7,000 | ₹9,000 to ₹14,000 | Good for east-side walk-ins, heavy traffic on the Ring Road |
| **Electronic City** | Large IT campuses; cheaper rooms | ₹4,500 to ₹6,000 | ₹8,000 to ₹12,000 | Far from the centre and from most institutes |
| **HSR Layout** | Startups, cleaner and quieter | ₹6,500 to ₹8,500 | ₹11,000 to ₹16,000 | Close to BTM, more expensive |
| **Koramangala** | Startups, restaurants, central | ₹7,000 to ₹9,000 | ₹12,000 to ₹18,000 | Central, but you pay for it |
| **Whitefield** | Big tech parks in the east | ₹5,500 to ₹7,500 | ₹9,000 to ₹14,000 | Far from the southern institute cluster; the metro now reaches it |
| **Rajajinagar and Vijayanagar** | The west-side institute cluster, older residential areas | ₹5,000 to ₹6,500 | ₹8,500 to ₹12,000 | Good metro on the Purple Line; far from the east-side tech parks |

::warning
These rents are approximate and vary street by street, by room size, by whether
there is AC, and by month. July to September, when a new batch of graduates
arrives, is when prices are highest and good rooms go fastest.
::

## What to do

Choose in this order.

::steps
### Choose the institute cluster first
You will go to the institute every day for three months. Walk-ins happen a few
times a week. Live close to the thing you do daily.

### Then choose within walking distance
Ideally you can walk to the institute in fifteen minutes. That saves bus fare
and, more importantly, time and energy.

### Then check the budget
If the area near your institute is over your budget, move one area over (BTM to
Madiwala, for example) rather than one side of the city over.

### Then visit, in the evening
Walk the area between 7 and 9 in the evening. See the food, the street lights,
how busy it is, and how you feel walking back.
::

## Common mistakes

- **Choosing by the name of the area.** The fashionable areas cost more and give
  a job seeker nothing extra.
- **Living near the tech parks** before you have a job there. You do not work
  there yet. Live near where you learn.
- **Ignoring Silk Board.** If your route to anywhere crosses Silk Board junction
  at 9 in the morning, add half an hour.
- **Deciding from Google Maps alone.** Walk it once in the evening.

## How to practise

Open Google Maps. Search for the institutes you are considering. Drop a pin on
each, then look at which areas are within two kilometres of them. That list is
your shortlist of areas, and it will probably be one or two names.
