You have an area. This page is how to find a PG in it that feeds you, lets you
sleep and study, and gives your deposit back when you leave.

A **PG** (paying guest accommodation) is a building of shared rooms, usually
with meals, rented by the month. You get a bed, a cupboard, food and Wi-Fi. You
do not get a lease or a kitchen of your own. It is how most job seekers in
Bangalore live, and how I lived.

## Why it matters

You will spend more hours in your PG than anywhere else for the next six months
or more. A bad one costs you in ways that do not show up in the rent: food that
makes you ill, Wi-Fi that dies when you need to watch a lecture, a roommate who
plays music at 2am, and a deposit you never see again.

The deposit alone is often one or two months' rent. Losing it is losing a month
of your budget.

## How to find one

| Method | How it works | Good for | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Walking around** | Walk the streets of your area. PGs put boards up on the building. Ring and ask to see a room | The cheapest options and the most choice | Takes a day or two |
| **Word of mouth** | Ask seniors, friends, people at the institute | PGs somebody has lived in and can vouch for | Your friend's standards may not be yours |
| **NoBroker and similar apps** | Listings with photos, filterable by area and price | Seeing what is available before you arrive | Photos can be old or flattering. Always visit |
| **Managed PG chains** (Stanza Living, Zolo and others) | Company-run PGs with standard rooms, app payments and formal agreements | Clearer rules, cleaner rooms, fewer surprises | Usually more expensive, and lock-ins and notice periods can be strict |
| **Brokers** | Somebody shows you rooms for a fee | Almost never worth it for a PG | Many PGs do not charge brokerage. Do not pay a stranger |

In BTM, walking works better than anything else, because there are so many.

## What to check

On every visit, check these. The order is the order in which they will hurt you
if you get them wrong.

::steps
### The food
Ask to see the menu for the week, and if you can, eat a meal there. Ask what
time breakfast is served; if it is after your institute starts, it is useless to
you. Ask whether food is included on Sundays.

### Water and bathrooms
How many people share a bathroom? Is there hot water in the morning, and for how
long? Is the drinking water filtered? Turn on a tap.

### The room
How many beds share the room? Is there a cupboard that locks? A fan that works?
Enough light to read by at night? Is there a window?

### Wi-Fi
Ask for the password and run a speed test on your phone. Watch a YouTube video
in the room. Ask whether it is shared across the whole building.

### Money: deposit, lock-in, notice
How much is the deposit, and in what cases is it not refunded? Is there a
lock-in (a minimum stay, say three months, before you can leave without losing
money)? How many days' notice do you need to give before leaving?

### Distance
Walk from the PG to your institute. Time it.
::

::callout{icon="i-lucide-receipt"}
Get the deposit amount, the refund conditions, the lock-in and the notice period
**in writing**, even if it is a handwritten line on the receipt with the owner's
signature. Take a photo of it. Most deposit arguments I saw were about a rule
that nobody had written down.
::

## Red flags

::caution
Walk away if you see any of these.

- The owner wants the deposit **before** you have seen the room.
- There is no receipt for money paid.
- The refund conditions change when you ask twice.
- Nobody living there will talk to you, or they look away when you ask about
  the food.
- The "attached bathroom" is shared with the next room.
- A lock-in of six months or more on a job seeker; you may need to move when you
  get a job.
- The owner asks to keep your original documents.
::

## A worked example

Two PGs on the same road, both "₹8,000 with food".

::compare{caption="The cheaper-looking one was the expensive one."}
:::compare-side{label="PG A" verdict="wrong"}
₹8,000. Deposit ₹16,000, "refundable". Lock-in of six months. Breakfast at
9:30. No Sunday dinner. The Wi-Fi is shared by forty people. Nothing in writing.
:::
:::compare-side{label="PG B" verdict="right"}
₹8,500. Deposit ₹8,500, refund within seven days of leaving, written on the
receipt. One month's notice, no lock-in. Breakfast at 7:30, three meals a day
including Sunday. A resident says the food is plain but consistent.
:::
::

PG B costs ₹500 more a month. PG A will cost you Sunday meals, missed
breakfasts, and very likely a deposit when you leave for your first job before
month six.

## Common mistakes

- **Taking the first one** because you are tired.
- **Not eating the food.** It is the thing you will complain about most.
- **Not asking a resident.** They will tell you the truth in thirty seconds.
- **No receipt.** Every rupee you pay gets a receipt, every time.
- **Forgetting that you will leave.** You are here until you get a job. Check
  what leaving costs.

## How to practise

Before your first visit, practise the questions out loud: "What is the deposit,
and when is it refunded? Is there a lock-in? How many days' notice? Can I see
the menu? Can I eat a meal here?" Owners respect a tenant who asks, and give
straighter answers.
