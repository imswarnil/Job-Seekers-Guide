You have a bed and a receipt. This page is about everything else in a day:
getting around, eating, washing clothes, staying well, and getting through the
evenings when home feels very far away.

## Why it matters

The job search is a long run, not a sprint. Most people who go home early do
not go home because Java defeated them. They go home because they were ill for
two weeks, ran out of money on autos, stopped eating properly, or were so lonely
that the plan stopped making sense.

Daily life is boring to plan. It is also what decides whether you are still
there at month six.

## Getting around

| Option | What it is | Good for | Rough cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **BMTC ordinary buses** | The city's public buses | Daily travel and walk-ins; they go almost everywhere | The cheapest option. Daily and monthly passes save money if you travel a lot |
| **BMTC Vajra (AC buses)** | Air-conditioned buses on longer routes | Long trips to the tech parks, in comfort | More than ordinary buses |
| **Namma Metro** | Bangalore's metro | Crossing the city quickly, without traffic | Distance-based fare; a smart card is cheaper than tokens |
| **Autos and bike taxis** | Booked on an app or stopped on the road | When you are late, or at night | Far more than a bus. Avoid for daily use |
| **Walking** | | Anything inside your area | Nothing |

::note
The metro has grown a great deal since I lived there. When I was job-hunting it
did not reach BTM at all; a newer line now runs past it towards Electronic City.
Check the current network on the official Namma Metro map, and check BMTC pass
prices, which change. The fares here are left out on purpose because mine are
out of date.
::

::tip
Before every walk-in, look up the route the night before on Google Maps, with
the departure time set to when you will actually leave. Bangalore at 8:30am and
Bangalore at 11am are different cities.
::

## Laundry

Most PGs have a washing machine, or a place to wash by hand and a terrace to dry
clothes. Some include laundry in the rent; others charge per load. There are
also laundry and ironing shops in every area, which charge per piece.

Do one thing without fail: **keep one formal shirt ironed and clean at all
times.** Walk-ins get announced the night before, and you do not want to be
ironing at 7am.

## Staying healthy

::steps
### Sleep at the same time
Seven hours. A PG is noisy; earplugs cost very little and help.

### Drink clean water
Drink from the PG's filtered water or buy it. A stomach infection from the wrong
water is two weeks lost.

### Walk
Walk to the institute. Walk in the evening. It is free, and it clears your head
after a day of classes.

### Eat three meals
Skipping breakfast to save time or money is the most common habit I saw, and it
shows up by 11am as an inability to think.

### Know where the nearest clinic is
Find a clinic and a pharmacy near your PG in week one. Ask your PG owner. Keep
their numbers in your phone.
::

## Loneliness and homesickness

This is the section I most wish somebody had written for me.

::pull-quote
The hardest part of my year in Bangalore was not Java. It was the evenings after
a rejection, in a room with people I did not know yet, a very long way from
home.
::

It is normal. It happens to almost everyone, and it is worst in the first month
and after a bad walk-in. Some things that helped me and the people around me:

- **Call home on a fixed day.** A Sunday call you can look forward to is better
  than calling in a panic after a rejection.
- **Study with somebody.** A friend from the institute or the PG. Do aptitude
  together, do mock interviews together. Company makes the work lighter.
- **Keep one thing that is yours.** For me it was the plan to be a YouTuber one
  day. I was not doing it yet, but I had not given it up. It was queued.
- **Write down one line a day.** What you studied, what went well. On a bad
  night, read the last two weeks back.
- **Go outside on Sunday.** Lalbagh, Cubbon Park, a walk anywhere. A few hours
  that are not about jobs.

::caution
If the sadness stops lifting, if you are not sleeping or eating, or if you have
thoughts of hurting yourself, tell someone today: a friend, your family, or a
doctor. Asking for help is not failing at the plan. It is part of the plan.
Save a national mental health helpline number in your phone in week one, before
you need it; check the current number online.
::

## A worked example

An ordinary weekday that works, once you have settled. The exact class times
depend on your batch:

| Time | What |
| --- | --- |
| 6:30 | Up, bath before the queue for hot water |
| 7:30 | Breakfast at the PG |
| 8:00 | Walk to the institute |
| Morning to afternoon | Classes |
| Afternoon | Lunch, then the institute lab or revision |
| Evening | Aptitude and CS subjects |
| 20:00 | Dinner at the PG |
| After dinner | English practice, one YouTube lecture |
| 23:00 | Sleep |

The full weekly timetable, with the subjects, is in the *How to learn* chapter.

## Common mistakes

- **Taking autos because the bus is confusing.** Learn two bus routes in week
  one. That is all most people need.
- **Living on snacks.** Cheap now, ill later.
- **Not telling anyone you are struggling.** Everyone around you is struggling
  too. Say it first.
- **Working seven days a week.** You will burn out by month three. Take Sunday
  afternoon off.

## How to practise

This week, learn the bus route from your PG to the institute, find the clinic
nearest your PG, and set a fixed day and time for calling home. Three small
things, and they will carry you through month three.
