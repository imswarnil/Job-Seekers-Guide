Some of the listings you find will ask you for money, or for your original
certificates, or for a signature on a contract you have not read. This page is
the scams and traps that target job seekers, and how to check a company before
you give it anything.

## Why it matters

Scams aimed at freshers work because of timing. They find you in month five,
when the money is thin, the rejections are many, and somebody offers you a way
out that only costs ₹20,000. The loss is not only the money. It is weeks of
hope, and sometimes your original documents.

There is one rule that catches most of them:

::callout{icon="i-lucide-shield-alert" color="error"}
**A real employer does not ask you to pay to get a job.** Not a registration
fee, not a training fee before joining, not a security deposit for the offer
letter, not a laptop deposit. If money flows from you to them before your first
salary, stop and check everything.
::

## The common scams

| Scam | What it looks like | What is really happening |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Pay for a job** | "Guaranteed placement at a top company for ₹30,000", often through an agent or consultancy | Nobody can guarantee a job at another company. You pay and wait, and the calls stop |
| **Registration or processing fee** | A walk-in or a call asks for a "small, refundable" fee to process your application | Real companies do not charge candidates. The fee is the business |
| **Fake offer letters** | An offer from a famous company, by email from a Gmail or odd domain, asking for a fee for "verification" or "background check" | The letter is fake. The company's name is being used |
| **Training-fee companies** | "We will hire you after our paid training", with the fee due up front, sometimes with a loan arranged for you | Some are institutes dressed as employers. The job, if it exists, may never come |
| **Document grabs** | The company asks to keep your original degree or marksheets "until the bond ends" | It traps you in the job. Never hand over originals |
| **Task and work-from-home scams** | Easy online tasks for money; pay a little to unlock bigger earnings | You pay more and more, and cannot withdraw |

::warning
Scammers copy real company names, logos and HR names from LinkedIn. A familiar
name on a letter proves nothing. Check through the company's own channels, never
through contact details the offer gives you.
::

## Bonds: a trap, or a normal clause?

A **bond** in an Indian job offer is a clause saying that if you leave before a
fixed period (often one to three years), you must pay the company a sum of
money. Companies say it covers the cost of training you.

Bonds are common for freshers, and not every bond is a scam. My own first job had
one. But some are harsh, and you should read one properly before you sign.

::pros-cons
---
title: A bond in a fresher offer
pros:
  - Common in the industry for freshers, especially where the company trains you
  - A modest bond with real training behind it can be a fair trade
  - It sometimes comes from companies that will take a chance on someone with no experience
cons:
  - Large sums can trap you in a bad job for years
  - Some companies use the bond as the product, with little training at all
  - Holding your original certificates as security is a serious red flag
  - Terms can be written to punish leaving for any reason, including being mistreated
---
::

## How to verify a company

::steps
### Check the official website and careers page
Is the drive or the job listed there? Is the HR email on the company's own
domain, not Gmail or Yahoo?

### Look it up on the government register
Search the company's name on the Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA) website.
A registered Indian company has a CIN (corporate identification number), a
registered address and a date of incorporation. A "company" with no record is
not one.

### Read employee reviews
Search the company on AmbitionBox and Glassdoor. A handful of reviews tells you
about salary delays, bonds and how people are treated.

### Find real employees on LinkedIn
Search the company on LinkedIn and look at the people who work there. A company
claiming 500 staff with four profiles is worth doubting.

### Visit the office, in daylight
If you are asked to go to an interview, look the address up first. A real office
with a reception is a good sign. A flat, a café or a "branch" that keeps moving
is not.

### Ask people
Your institute placement cell and your PG will often know the name. Ask.
::

## A worked example

You get this email, from `hr.recruitment.infotech@gmail.com`:

::compare{caption="The same email, read two ways."}
:::compare-side{label="Month five, tired and hopeful" verdict="wrong"}
"Congratulations, you are selected for Software Trainee at a well-known IT
company, CTC ₹4.2 LPA. Pay ₹8,500 refundable for background verification within
24 hours to confirm your seat." It names a real company. I did not even
interview there, but perhaps my resume was shortlisted. I pay.
:::
:::compare-side{label="The same email, checked" verdict="right"}
Gmail address, not the company's domain. Selected without an interview. Money
requested. A 24-hour deadline to stop you thinking. The company's careers page
says in plain words that it never charges candidates. Delete, and warn the PG
group.
:::
::

Four red flags in one email: wrong domain, no interview, a fee, and a deadline.
Any one of them is enough to stop.

## If you have already paid

- **Stop paying.** Scammers ask again, for a "second verification" or a "tax".
- **Report it** on the national cybercrime portal (cybercrime.gov.in) or the
  cybercrime helpline, and at a police station. Keep screenshots of every
  message and payment.
- **Tell your bank** straight away if you paid by card or bank transfer.
- **Tell people.** Post in your PG and institute groups. You will not be the
  only one who got that email.

::note
I am not a lawyer, and nothing here is legal advice. If a contract, a bond or a
payment dispute is serious, get it read by somebody qualified. Many cities have
free legal aid services; your institute or a working senior may know where.
::

## Common mistakes

- **Believing the logo.** Anyone can copy a logo.
- **Paying because it is "refundable".** Refundable by whom?
- **Handing over originals** "only for verification" and leaving without them.
  Show them, take them back.
- **Signing on the spot.** Ask to take the offer and the bond home overnight. A
  real employer will let you.
- **Keeping quiet out of embarrassment.** Scammers depend on that.

## How to practise

Pick three companies from this week's walk-in listings. For each, check the
careers page, the MCA register, AmbitionBox and LinkedIn. Ten minutes each. By
the tenth company, you will do it without thinking, and that habit is what
protects you in month five.
