Here is what the roles and events from the last lesson look like when they are
your calendar. One ordinary day, day six of a ten-day sprint, on a team that
looks after a university's student portal.

## Why this matters in a job

Because the interview question is rarely "define a stand-up". It is "have you
worked in an Agile environment?" or "walk me through how you would pick up a
task". A fresher cannot claim years of Scrum, but a fresher who can describe a
ticket's journey from backlog to done sounds ready to start on Monday.

## The ticket's journey

Work in a Scrum team lives on a **board**, usually in **Jira** (the most common
tool in Indian service companies) or Azure DevOps. Each story is a card that
moves across columns. Think of it as the file that moves from desk to desk in a
college office until the principal signs it.

::flow{caption="One story, left to right. The board's columns vary by team; these are typical."}
  :::flow-step{label="To do" icon="i-lucide-circle"}
  In the sprint backlog, unassigned.
  :::
  :::flow-step{label="In progress" icon="i-lucide-loader"}
  You assigned it to yourself and are building it.
  :::
  :::flow-step{label="Code review" icon="i-lucide-git-pull-request" highlight}
  A pull request is open and a colleague is reading your code.
  :::
  :::flow-step{label="QA" icon="i-lucide-bug"}
  A tester runs the test cases on the test environment.
  :::
  :::flow-step{label="Done" icon="i-lucide-check"}
  Meets the Definition of Done. Shown at the sprint review.
  :::
::

## How the day goes

::timeline
  :::timeline-item{date="9:30" title="Check the board" state="done"}
  Open Jira. Your story, **STU-214: show attendance percentage on the student
  dashboard**, is in progress. A tester left a comment on yesterday's story.
  :::
  :::timeline-item{date="10:00" title="Daily stand-up, 15 minutes" state="done"}
  "Yesterday I finished the attendance query and raised the PR for STU-209.
  Today I am building the dashboard card for STU-214. I am blocked on test data:
  the test environment has no attendance rows for semester 5." The Scrum Master
  takes the blocker away to solve.
  :::
  :::timeline-item{date="10:20" title="Fix the tester's comment" state="done"}
  The receipt date shows in US format. A 20-minute fix, pushed to the same PR.
  :::
  :::timeline-item{date="11:00" title="Build" state="current"}
  Most of the day is here. Branch, code, run it locally, write the unit test.
  :::
  :::timeline-item{date="14:30" title="Backlog refinement, 1 hour" state="todo"}
  The PO walks through next sprint's stories. The team asks questions and
  estimates with planning poker.
  :::
  :::timeline-item{date="16:00" title="Review a colleague's PR" state="todo"}
  Read somebody else's code and leave comments. Reviewing is part of the job,
  not a favour.
  :::
  :::timeline-item{date="18:00" title="Commit, push, update the ticket" state="todo"}
  Move STU-214 to Code review and note anything the tester needs to know.
  :::
::

The sprint's other events land on fixed days: planning on day one, review and
retrospective on day ten. Refinement usually happens once mid-sprint.

## Real use case

A good stand-up update names the ticket, the progress and the blocker. A bad
one is a diary.

::compare{caption="Same work, two updates."}
  :::compare-side{label="The diary" verdict="wrong"}
  "Yesterday I worked on the dashboard thing, had some issues with the data,
  looked into it, also had a call, today I will continue with it."
  :::
  :::compare-side{label="The update" verdict="right"}
  "STU-209 is in review. Today STU-214, the attendance card. Blocked: no
  semester 5 attendance data in the test environment. I need somebody with
  access to load it."
  :::
::

::real-life{title="The first week, honestly" source="Accenture, 2019"}
Nobody expects a fresher to estimate well or to speak much in planning. What
they notice is whether your ticket moves across the board, whether your
stand-up update is clear, and whether you say "I am blocked" on day one instead
of day four. The last one matters most. A fresher who stays silently stuck for
three days costs the team more than one who asks a "silly" question on the
first morning.
::

## What confuses people here

- **"The stand-up is a status report to the manager."** It is for the team to
  coordinate. Speak to your colleagues, not to the Scrum Master.
- **Moving a card is not finishing it.** A story is done when it meets the
  Definition of Done, including testing, not when your code compiles.
- **"I have no Agile experience."** As a fresher, describe the process you
  know, and any team project where you split work and met regularly. Do not
  claim sprints you never ran.

## Interview questions

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="Have you worked in an Agile environment?"}
  Be honest and specific. "Not in a company yet. In my training batch four of us
  built a small project and split it into tasks on a Trello board, met for ten
  minutes every morning, and showed it to the trainer every Friday. I know the
  Scrum roles and events, and I expect my first team to use Jira." Honest and
  concrete beats a claimed title.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="What would you do if you were blocked on a task?"}
  Try for a fixed time (an hour or two), write down what I tried, then raise it
  in the stand-up or directly with a teammate. Blockers are the one thing the
  stand-up exists to surface, and the Scrum Master's job is to remove them.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="What is a sprint backlog and who can change it?"}
  The set of stories the team committed to for this sprint, plus their plan. The
  development team owns it. New work mid-sprint is negotiated with the Product
  Owner, and usually waits for the next sprint.
  :::
  :::accordion-item{label="What is Jira?"}
  A tool from Atlassian for tracking work: stories, bugs and tasks as tickets on
  a board, grouped into sprints, with reports such as the burndown chart. Most
  Scrum teams in Indian service companies use it or Azure DevOps.
  :::
::
