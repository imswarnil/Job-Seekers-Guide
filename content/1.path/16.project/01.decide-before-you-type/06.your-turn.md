Five lessons of deciding, and the next chapter opens an empty repository. Before
it does, make these five decisions yourself and write them down, because a plan
you read is not a plan you own.

::note
Every one of these produces a file in `docs/`. That folder is the thing you open
in an interview when somebody asks how you approached it, and it is worth more
than the code.
::

## 1. The re-application rule

The schema says a person can apply to the same programme in a later intake, and
cannot apply twice on the same day.

A parent asks: my child applied in June, was rejected, and wants to apply again
in the July intake for the same programme. Can they?

Write the answer as a rule a database could enforce.

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="What a good answer contains" icon="i-lucide-check"}
  The `UNIQUE (applicant_id, program_id, applied_on)` constraint only stops two
  applications on the **same date**, which is a duplicate-click guard rather
  than a policy. June and July are different dates, so as written: yes, they
  can.

  Is that what the university wants? That is a question for the registrar, not
  for you, and the good answer says so. If the policy is "one attempt per
  intake", the schema needs an `intake_id` and the constraint becomes
  `UNIQUE (applicant_id, program_id, intake_id)`.

  The reason this exercise is first: you have just discovered that a constraint
  you wrote encodes a policy nobody stated. That happens on every project, and
  noticing it before the data exists is worth weeks.
  :::
::

## 2. The status that goes backwards

An admin moves an application from `REJECTED` back to `UNDER_REVIEW`, because
the rejection was a mistake.

Should the system allow it? Design the rule.

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="What a good answer contains" icon="i-lucide-check"}
  Yes, allow it, and this is the interesting part: **allow it and record it**.

  A system that forbids the correction forces the office to work around it, and
  the workaround is always worse. They will create a second application, and now
  your data has two rows for one person and the funnel metrics are wrong
  forever.

  The rule: any transition is permitted, `ApplicationEvent` records `from_status`,
  `to_status`, who and when, and a backwards transition **requires a note**. The
  note is not decoration. It is the sentence somebody reads out to a parent on
  the phone.

  The principle worth naming: prefer recording the unusual thing to forbidding
  it. Forbidden operations do not stop happening; they stop being visible.
  :::
::

## 3. The number that will be wrong

The dashboard shows "average score of shortlisted applicants". Some
applications have a null `score`, because the written test has not been marked
yet.

What does the number say, and what should the screen say?

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="What a good answer contains" icon="i-lucide-check"}
  `AVG(score)` ignores nulls and divides by the count of non-null scores. That
  is arithmetically correct and it is not what the label claims.

  If 400 are shortlisted and 120 are marked, the screen says "average score of
  shortlisted applicants: 71.4" and it means "average of the 120 we have
  marked". In an admissions meeting, somebody will make a cutoff decision on
  that number.

  The fix is not in the query. It is on the screen: **71.4, from 120 of 400
  marked.** Same query, one extra `COUNT(score)`, and a label that cannot
  mislead.

  This is the whole "correct, not just runnable" discipline in one example, and
  it is the answer that gets remembered in an interview, because everybody else
  says "handle nulls".
  :::
::

## 4. The screen you would cut

You have four weeks, not eight. Twenty screens is too many.

Pick the five you build first and say what the system can do with only those.

::accordion
  :::accordion-item{label="What a good answer contains" icon="i-lucide-check"}
  There is no single right five, and the reasoning is what is being marked. A
  defensible set:

  1. `/enquire` because nothing exists until data arrives.
  2. `/admin` (the pipeline) because an enquiry nobody can read is useless.
  3. `/admin/applications/:id` because the pipeline needs to be actionable.
  4. `/staff/:subject/attendance` because it is the most-used screen and the
     one that proves the system gets adopted.
  5. `/me` because a student with no portal is a student who rings the office.

  What it can do: take an enquiry, move it through the funnel to admission, and
  mark attendance. That is a complete **loop**, and the test of a good cut is
  whether the remaining screens form a loop or a collection.

  What you sacrifice, said out loud: no results, no fees, no reports. Say that
  rather than pretending the cut was free.
  :::
::

## 5. The decision record

Write `docs/decisions.md`. Four entries from the architecture lesson, in this
format, and nothing longer than five lines each.

```md [docs/decisions.md]
## 2. Server-rendered, not a client-side application

**Decided:** pages are rendered on the server; interactive pieces are islands.

**Rejected:** a single-page application talking to a JSON API.

**Because:** the portal is mostly reading, on phones, often on slow campus
connections, and authorisation must happen where a student cannot reach it.

**This stops being right when:** the interface becomes mostly editing rather
than reading, or an offline mode is required.
```

::tip
The fourth line is the one that matters and the one nobody writes. A decision
with no stated expiry is a belief. A decision that names the condition under
which it becomes wrong is engineering, and an interviewer hears the difference
immediately.
::

## The cheat-sheet for this chapter

| Artefact | Where it lives | The question it answers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Requirements | `docs/requirements.md` | What must it do, and what is out |
| Decision record | `docs/decisions.md` | Why is it shaped this way |
| Schema | `db/schema.sql` | What facts can it hold |
| Access matrix | `docs/access.md` | Who may do what |
| Policies | `db/policies.sql` | What enforces that |
| Screen list | `docs/screens.md` | What does a person touch |

Six files, no code, and every one of them will still be true in a year.
